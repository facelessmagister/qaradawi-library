وَلَقَدۡ كَرَّمۡنَا بَنِيٓ ءَادَمَ "We have honoured the sons of Adam; provided them with transport on land and sea; given them for sustenance things good and pure; and conferred on them special favours, above a great part of Our creation." — Surah al-Isra' (17:70)

1. Man in the Sight of Materialists

What is man? According to materialists, he is part of the sand of this earth — created from earth, walking on it, eating from it, and buried in it.

"He is a creature of no value or privilege over the other creatures. He is only one of the numerous living beings on this earth; rather, he belongs to the species of pests, insects, reptiles, and apes — he simply 'evolved' and became a man!" — Qaradawi summarizing the materialist worldview

A startling illustration: if you break down a 140-pound human body into its chemical elements, you get enough fat for seven bars of soap, enough carbon for seven pencils, enough phosphorus for 120 match heads, enough iron for one medium pin, and enough water to fill a ten-gallon barrel — all purchasable for about fifty piasters.

A contemporary Arab atheist put it bluntly: "We are only an outcome of nature, just like insects. We equal nothing more than ourselves, as do insects."

Darwin, in The Origin of Species, asserted that all living organisms — including man — pass through stages of modification and change, making man indistinguishable from plants, animals, or insects in principle.

"Is there anything more uninspiring to the human soul than this? It makes man look at himself as a base creature, an animal, a clot of clay or mud. It urges him to indulge in indecency and iniquity — rather it makes purity, decency, sacrifice, and seeking Allah's Pleasure seem strange and unnatural."
❦

2. Man in the Sight of the Believers

In the sight of the believers, man is a creature honoured by Allah — created in the best of molds, shaped beautifully by His own Hand, breathed into with His Spirit, and before whom the angels were commanded to prostrate.

Divine Address

"O son of Adam! I created you for Myself and created all things for you. So, as it is My right over you, do not let yourself be distracted by that which I created for you from that which I created you for. O son of Adam! Ask for Me and you will find Me, and if you find Me you will have found everything — but if you lose Me you will have lost everything."

While materialists see man as an atom in an endless desert of geological time, believers are certain that death is not the end — it is a transitional stop to eternity.

ٱدۡخُلُوهَا بِسَلَـٰمٍۚ ذَٰلِكَ يَوۡمُ ٱلۡخُلُودِ "Peace be upon you! Enter ye here, to dwell therein." — Surah az-Zumar (39:73)

The very first revelation — the opening verses of Surah al-'Alaq — establishes man's relationship with his Lord through the twin themes of creation and teaching. The word "Rabb" (Lord) encompasses nurturing, caring, promotion, and perfection.

ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَـٰنَ مِنۡ عَلَقٍ "Proclaim! (or Read!) in the name of thy Lord and Cherisher, Who created — created man, out of a (mere) clot of congealed blood: Proclaim! And thy Lord is Most Bountiful — He Who taught (the use of) the Pen — taught man that which he knew not." — Surah al-'Alaq (96:1–5)
❦

3. Man's Position Before Allah

وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌۖ "When My servants ask thee concerning Me, I am indeed close (to them): I listen to the prayer of every supplicant when he calleth on Me." — Surah al-Baqarah (2:186)

This nearness eliminates the need for mediators and those who seek profit through religion — those who made themselves "doormen" at the gates of Allah's mercy.

"I am as My servant thinks I am, and I am with him whenever he remembers Me: if he remembers Me in himself, I remember him in Myself; if he remembers Me in a group, I remember him in a better group; if he draws near to Me a hand's span, I draw near to him an arm's length; and if he comes to Me walking, I go to him at speed." — Hadith Qudsi, narrated by al-Bukhari

This is man's position in the sight of Allah.

4. Man's Position in Respect to the Angels

"Behold, Thy Lord said to the angels: 'I will create a vicegerent on earth.' They said: 'Wilt Thou place therein one who will make mischief and shed blood? — whilst we do celebrate Thy praises and glorify Thee?' He said: 'I know what ye know not.' And He taught Adam the names of all things..." — Surah al-Baqarah (2:30–33)

Allah commanded the angels to greet this new creature by bowing before him in respect and glorification. Iblis alone refused — motivated by envy and conceit — and thus became man's sworn enemy.

5. Man's Position in This Material World

Man stands as a disposing master to whose use all things have been subjected — as if the cosmos were "woven" for him and "cut out" to suit his size.

"Do ye not see that Allah has subjected to your (use) all things in the heavens and on earth, and has made His bounties flow to you in exceeding measure, (both) seen and unseen?" — Surah Luqman (31:20)

But why was man given this supreme position, when the universe contains things far larger? It is the secret of the light derived from Allah's Light, the breath from His Spirit — preparing him for vicegerency and the Great Trust that the heavens, earth, and mountains refused to bear.

"We did indeed offer the Trust to the Heavens and the Earth and the Mountains; but they refused to undertake it, being afraid thereof: but man undertook it..." — Surah al-Ahzab (33:72)
❦

6. The Scholars of Islam Extol Man's Position

"Allah the Almighty has never created a better creature than man, for He created him as living, knowledgeable, mighty, able to speak, able to see, able to dispose of his affairs, wise — and these are the attributes of the Lord (Glorified and Exalted be He)." — Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi

Imam al-Ghazali, in his Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Din, explained that the harmony between man's entity and the Divine Attributes — knowledge, beneficence, benevolence, mercy, guidance — draws man near to Allah. The saying "Adopt Allah's morals" points to this profound connection.

"Allah favoured man with special gifts among His creatures. He honoured, preferred, and dignified him; created him for Himself and created all things for him; gave him out of His knowledge, love, nearness, and honouring that which He did not give to anyone else; subjected all things in the heavens and on earth to his use; used even His angels for his benefit..." — Imam ibn al-Qayyim, Madarij as-Salikin

7. The Honor of Iman Comes After the Honor of Humanity

These are the meanings of dignity from the viewpoint of being a "man." Yet as a believer, one feels deeper meanings — iman elevates man to a position that can never be reached by normal means.

"But honour belongs to Allah and His Messenger, and to the believers." — Surah al-Munafiqun (63:8)

The believer feels protected, guided, and dignified — knowing that "Allah is the Protector of those who believe." (Muhammad: 11)

The Believer's Motto

"I am a master in this universe
and a servant of Allah alone."

This is why a black slave like Bilal ibn Rabah could stand above the arrogant masters of Quraysh, and why an illiterate Bedouin like Rib'i ibn 'Amir could stand before Rustum, the general of Persia, and declare:

"We are people sent by Allah to lead people away from the worship of creatures, to the worship of the Lord of creatures, alone; from the life of hardship to another life of ease; and from the injustice of doctrines to the justice of Islam." — Rib'i ibn 'Amir, addressing Rustum of Persia
❦

8. Man Between Islam and Materialism

The contradiction between the two worldviews rests on three essential matters:

🧬 Materialist View

Man is a "wild plant" that emerges from non-existence by itself, lives by itself, dies by itself — an "evolved animal" with no roots before existence and no extension after death.

🕌 Islamic View

Man is Allah's vicegerent on earth, assigned to establish truth, serve goodness, and spread beauty — with the angels protecting him and the Lord of all with him.

9. The Nature of Man — The Dual Reality

Man's nature is one of the most dangerous areas one may ever approach, for people inevitably go astray when dealing with it. Man is neither pure lust nor pure mind, neither pure body nor pure soul — his constitution contains both.

أَلَا يَعۡلَمُ مَنۡ خَلَقَ وَهُوَ ٱللَّطِيفُ ٱلۡخَبِيرُ "Should He not know, He that created? And He is the One that understands the finest mysteries (and) is well acquainted (with them)." — Surah al-Mulk (67:14)

Islam holds the middle ground. It has never neglected the spirit for the sake of clay, nor neglected the clay for the sake of spirit — but combines both in harmonious, congruous unity. The Prophet ﷺ himself fasted and broke fast, prayed at night and slept, married and had children.

Three paths have historically erred:

🕉️ Brahminism & Celibacy

Denied the body, tormented the flesh to "purify" the spirit.

⚛️ Materialism

Denied the soul, reduced man to animalistic instincts and chemical elements.

Islam is the middle path — moderation without extremism.

10. The Goal of Man

Man was neither created in jest, nor was he left without purpose. He was created for a goal and a wisdom — and not just for himself.

أَفَحَسِبۡتُمۡ أَنَّمَا خَلَقۡنَـٰكُمۡ عَبَثٗا وَأَنَّكُمۡ إِلَيۡنَا لَا تُرۡجَعُونَ "Did ye then think that We had created you in jest, and that ye would not be brought back to Us (for account)? Therefore exalted be Allah, the King, the Reality." — Surah al-Mu'minun (23:115–116)

Man was created to know Allah, worship Him, and be His vicegerent on earth — to undertake the greater Trust of duties and responsibilities, to go through trials and obligations, and thus be prepared for another life of eternity.

The Core Difference

Materialists say: "Man lives for himself and for worldly pleasures."
Believers say: "Man lives for his Lord, the Most High, and for the next, everlasting life."

Al-Qaradawi closes with a devastating comparison: the existentialist is like a dog running in circles chasing its own tail — a game dogs play when they have nothing to do. The Qur'an presents the same parable for those who abandon Allah's verses and follow their own vain desires (al-A'raf: 175–177).

"Those who reject Allah will enjoy (this world) and eat as cattle eat; and the Fire will be their abode." — Surah Muhammad (47:12)
