# Sunnah Methodology Across Contexts — Fiqh, Ethics, and Education

**Comparison Domain:** Hadith methodology as applied by Yusuf al-Qaradawi across three disciplinary contexts  
**Primary Source:** [[approaching-the-sunnah-overview|Approaching the Sunnah: Comprehension and Controversy]]  
**Secondary Sources:** [[fiqh-al-zakah-overview|Fiqh al-Zakah]], [[ethics-in-islam-overview|Ethics in Islam]], [[education-economy-sunnah-overview|Education and Economy in the Sunnah]]

---

## Introduction

Al-Qaradawi's hadith methodology is not monolithic. While a single set of principles governs his approach — verify authenticity (taḥarrī ṣiḥḥat al-sunnah), understand in context (al-iṭṭilāʿ ʿalā fahm al-sunnah), and ensure freedom from contradiction by stronger evidence (salāmah al-naṣṣ min muʿāraḍat mā huwa aqwā minhu) — the *application* of these principles shifts substantially depending on the domain in which he is writing. In fiqh, hadith function as legal evidence (*ḥujjah*) for establishing or refuting obligations; in ethics, they furnish moral exempla and character-shaping narratives; in education, they model Prophetic pedagogy. This comparison traces how a single author adapts a unified methodology to three distinct contexts, and what each context reveals about the methodology's flexibility and limits. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

---

## 1. The Unified Framework: Approaching the Sunnah (Chapters 1–3)

[[approaching-the-sunnah-ch-01|Chapter 1]] establishes the Sunnah's constitutional characteristics: it is comprehensive (*shāmil*), balanced (*mutawāzin*), integrative (*jāmiʿ*), and realistic (*wāqiʿī*). These are not merely descriptive labels but *methodological controls*: any hadith that, if taken at face value, would produce extremism, one-sidedness, or a narrowing of Islam's broad scope is ipso facto suspect. The famous hadith "Whoever prefers [something else] above my sunnah is not one of mine" is read not only as a prohibition of asceticism but as a hermeneutical principle: authentic Prophetic guidance cannot contradict the Sunnah's own character. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

[[approaching-the-sunnah-ch-02|Chapter 2]] addresses the Sunnah's legislative authority. Al-Qaradawi insists that all jurists, whether of the school of hadith or the school of opinion (rā'y), accept the Sunnah as a binding source. The distinctive contribution is his insistence that jurists must master hadith science and hadith specialists must master fiqh — a "long-standing gap" that produces weak rulings on one side and legally uninformed transmissions on the other. He catalogs the consequences: fiqh textbooks contain weak, fabricated, or even baseless (muʿallaq) hadiths, some with inverted meanings (the "laysa" corruption of "There *is* a duty on wealth besides zakah"). He calls for systematic source-critique (*taḥrīj*) of the entire fiqh inheritance. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

[[approaching-the-sunnah-ch-03|Chapter 3]] develops the Qur'an-first principle for resolving hadith contradictions. A sound hadith cannot genuinely contradict the Qur'an; where apparent conflict arises, either the hadith is unsound, its understanding is flawed, or the conflict is merely apparent. The lead case study is zakah on agricultural produce: the Qur'anic verse (Q 6:42) imposes a due on *all* crops, yet some jurists restricted liability to four staple categories. Al-Qaradawi demonstrates that the restricting hadith ("There is no ṣadaqah on vegetables") is weak per al-Tirmidhī, and that Abū Bakr ibn al-ʿArabī's survey of Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, and Ḥanbalī positions finds all three wanting — with Abū Ḥanīfah's broader reading closest to the Qur'anic text. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

These three chapters articulate a methodology that is:
- **Hierarchical:** Qur'an > sound Sunnah > scholarly consensus > analogy
- **Critical:** Weak hadiths cannot establish obligations (ḥukm)
- **Contextual:** Sunnah must be understood in light of its own general characteristics
- **Revisionist:** The fiqh inheritance must be re-examined against authenticated hadith

## 2. Hadith Methodology in the Fiqh Context: Fiqh al-Zakah

### 2.1 The Functional Role of Hadith in Legal Derivation

In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, hadith function as *legal evidence* — the second source after the Qur'an for establishing obligations, thresholds, rates, and exemptions. The methodology shifts from the theoretical stance of *Approaching the Sunnah* to a practical, case-by-case engagement with specific hadiths as they bear on particular legal questions.

The pattern across ten chapters is consistent:

1. **Establish the Qur'anic general principle** first (e.g., "Out of their wealth take ṣadaqah" at Q 9:103; "Render the dues that are proper on the day that the harvest is gathered" at Q 6:141).
2. **Identify relevant Prophetic hadiths** that specify the general principle (nisab thresholds, rates, exempt categories).
3. **Survey all four madhhab positions** — Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī, plus Zāhirī and Zaydī where relevant.
4. **Evaluate each position against the primary texts** using the criteria articulated in *Approaching the Sunnah*.

^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-02.txt]

### 2.2 From Principle to Practice: The "General Text" Rule

The single most consequential methodological rule in *Fiqh al-Zakah* is what Qaradawi calls the "adoption of general texts" — general Qur'anic verses and Prophetic sayings "should be received and taken as general, except when their scopes are narrowed by another text that is correct, authentic and clear in its meaning." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

This rule, which originates in his *uşūl* training, drives his most distinctive rulings:

| Issue | Restricting Hadith | Qaradawi's Assessment | His Ruling |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|------------|
| Agricultural zakah scope | "There is no ṣadaqah on vegetables" (al-Tirmidhī) | Weak chain; contradicts Q 6:141 | All crops are zakatable |
| Mineral zakah scope | Shāfiʿī limitation to gold/silver | No sound text restricts general Qur'anic wording | All extractable minerals are zakatable |
| Exploited assets | Classical enumerations of zakatable categories | Enumerations are illustrative, not exhaustive | Rental income, factories, and capital goods are zakatable |
| Salary zakah | No classical category | No textual restriction on *māl* (wealth) as a general term | Professional earnings are zakatable |

^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-05.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-07.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-08.txt]

### 2.3 Chain Criticism Applied to Fiqh Hadiths

Where *Approaching the Sunnah* theorizes the need for source-critique, *Fiqh al-Zakah* performs it. The most striking example is Qaradawi's demolition of the hadith evidence for the passage-of-year (*ḥawl*) condition in zakah on earnings:

| Narrator | Defect | Source |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Al-Ḥārith al-A'war | Known fabricator | ʿAlī hadith on ḥawl |
| ʿĀṣim bin Ḏamūrah | Poor memory | Same chain |
| Ismāʿīl bin ʿAyyāsh | Narrations weak outside Syria | Anas hadith |
| Ibn al-Rajjāl | Graded unreliable | ʿĀ'ishah hadith |

Since all four narrations for the ḥawl condition have *serious* chain defects, Qaradawi argues that the condition rests on consensus (*ijmāʿ*) rather than textual proof, and therefore allows modification for modern salary structures. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-09.txt]

Similarly, in [[fiqh-al-zakah-ch-05|the agricultural zakah chapter]], the hadith restricting zakatable crops to four categories (wheat, barley, dates, raisins) is evaluated and found wanting, leading Qaradawi to prefer the broad Qur'anic reading. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-05.txt]

### 2.4 When Hadith and Ijmāʿ Collide

In his chapter on gold and silver ([[fiqh-al-zakah-ch-03|Ch. 3]]), Qaradawi presents *seven arguments* against raising the zakah rate on monetary wealth above 2.5%. The first argument is that it contradicts authentic Sunnah; the second, that it violates centuries of *ijmāʿ*. Here, hadith and consensus reinforce each other. But when hadith and ijmāʿ point in different directions — as with the ḥawl condition — Qaradawi is willing to set aside a hadith with a defective chain even when centuries of jurists have relied on it. The relative weight of evidence determines the outcome, not the weight of consensus alone. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

### 2.5 Ijtihad as Hadith Methodology in Practice

*Fiqh al-Zakah* demonstrates that Qaradawi's hadith methodology is not merely critical (removing weak hadiths) but also constructive (using ijtihad and qiyās to extend rulings to new categories). He explicitly states: "The rates, wherever there is no text, are subject to ijtihad." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-02.txt]

The *method* of ijtihad in zakah is:
1. Identify the *effective cause* (*ʿillah*) of the ruling in the original text — e.g., *growth* (*namā'*) as the rationale for zakatability.
2. Extend the ruling by analogy to categories sharing that *ʿillah* — e.g., from agricultural produce to rental income, from gold/silver to stocks and bonds.
3. Prefer the analogy most consistent with Qur'anic general texts and Prophetic practice.

This is not free-wheeling innovation; it is analogical reasoning anchored in authenticated texts. But it reveals a tension: Qaradawi the hadith critic is willing to discard weak hadiths that restrict zakah's scope, while Qaradawi the jurist uses analogy to expand it. Both moves share the same *uşūl* principle — the primacy of general Qur'anic texts — but the first prunes and the second extends. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt] ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

## 3. Hadith Methodology in the Ethics Context: Ethics in Islam

### 3.1 From Legal Evidence to Moral Exempla

In *Ethics in Islam*, the function of hadith shifts fundamentally. Here, hadith serve not as *legal evidence* establishing obligations but as *moral exempla* shaping character. The Prophet's hadith "I was sent to perfect good morals" is the book's foundation, but the term "morals" (*akhlaq*) operates in a different register than "obligation" (*ḥukm*).

This shift produces three methodological differences:

**First, the authentication threshold is lower.** While *Approaching the Sunnah* insists that weak hadiths cannot support legal injunctions (*ḥukm*), *Ethics in Islam* draws heavily on hadiths about moral character, many of which fall in the *ḥasan* category. Qaradawi's own position, articulated in *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 1, is that "there is scholarly agreement that hadiths used in legal injunctions must be *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan*" — and *ḥasan* is the lower boundary, not *ṣaḥīḥ* alone. However, he warns against using fabricated hadiths even in moral exhortation, rebuking preachers who circulate spurious material under the pretext of encouraging virtue. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

**Second, the interpretive lens shifts from legal reasoning to moral philosophy.** In *Ethics in Islam* Ch. 3, the hadith "Actions are judged by intentions" (*innamā al-aʿmāl bi-al-niyyāt*) is not deployed to establish a fiqh ruling but to ground the philosophical principle that moral worth resides in intention. The same hadith, in a fiqh manual, would be discussed in terms of the conditions for valid worship. In the ethics context, Qaradawi uses it to argue that Islamic morality is neither arbitrary nor purely duty-based — it is intention-infused, linking the outward act to the inner disposition. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-03.txt]

**Third, the Qur'an-first priority is present but operates differently.** In fiqh, Qaradawi uses the Qur'an to *override* hadiths that restrict general Qur'anic commands. In ethics, the Qur'an and hadith work in *harmony* rather than in a corrective hierarchy. The moral values Qaradawi identifies — truthfulness, justice, freedom — are anchored in both sources simultaneously, and the "contradiction" scenario that drives the methodology of *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 3 rarely arises. When a hadith on moral character appears inconsistent with Qur'anic ethics, the problem is resolved not by discarding the hadith but by correct interpretation. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-01.txt] ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-02.txt]

### 3.2 The Rejection of Fabricated Hadiths in Moral Discourse

A crucial intersection point between the two methodologies is Qaradawi's treatment of fabricated hadiths in preaching and moral instruction. *Ethics in Islam* Ch. 2 includes a sustained rebuttal of Sheikh Khalid Mohammed Khalid's claim in *Either This or the Flood* that secular ethics can replace religious ethics. Qaradawi demonstrates that Khalid "conflates cultural practices with Islamic teachings, misrepresents the scope of divine revelation, and fails to distinguish between authentic Prophetic tradition and fabricated hadiths." ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-02.txt]

This is not merely a scholarly disagreement about ethics; it is an application of the hadith methodology from *Approaching the Sunnah* in a new context. The principle is the same — fabricated hadiths must be identified and excluded — but the purpose has shifted: here, it protects the moral authority of the Sunnah from being undermined by spurious material that misrepresents Islamic ethics.

### 3.3 Hadith as Proof of the Religion–Ethics Unity

In *Ethics in Islam* Chs. 1 and 4, hadith are used to demonstrate the inseparability of faith and moral conduct: "One who cannot be trusted cannot be described as faithful." This is a different use than the fiqh deployment of hadith as legal proof-texts. In ethics, hadith function as *integrative proofs* — showing that moral character is not an appendage to faith but constitutive of it. The verification requirement still applies, but the standard of "sufficient evidence" is calibrated to the *moral* rather than the *legal* domain. A *ḥasan* hadith that would be insufficient to establish a criminal penalty (*ḥadd*) may be entirely adequate to illustrate a moral principle. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-01.txt] ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

## 4. Hadith Methodology in the Educational Context: Education and Economy in the Sunnah

### 4.1 Hadith as Pedagogical Model, Not Legal Evidence

In *Education and Economy in the Sunnah*, the function of hadith shifts again — now they serve primarily as *instances of Prophetic pedagogy*. The methodology is descriptive and illustrative rather than prescriptive and legal.

[[education-economy-sunnah-ch-01|Chapter 1]] catalogs nineteen educational principles derived from Prophetic practice: the obligation of seeking knowledge, sincerity of intention, gradual instruction, employing similitudes and stories, using questions to engage learners, and — centrally — the Prophet's practice of *individualizing instruction*. Qaradawi documents that the Prophet "gave different advice to different questioners, different answers to the same question depending on the questioner's condition, varied his manner toward Bedouins versus close Companions, tailored obligations to each person's ability, and accepted from some what he rejected from others." ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

This last point is methodologically significant. In the fiqh context, the same Prophetic practice — giving different answers to the same question — is a recognized source of apparent hadith contradiction that requires *jtimāʿ* (harmonization) or *tarjīh* (preference). In the educational context, it is presented as a *feature* of the Sunnah's wisdom, not a problem to be resolved. The hadith methodology of *Approaching the Sunnah* provides the theoretical apparatus for understanding why the Prophet varied his answers (accounting for individual, environmental, and temporal variations); the educational context *celebrates* this variation rather than seeking to resolve it.

### 4.2 Authentication Discipline: Sahih and Hasan Only

Notably, Qaradawi's educational work restricts itself to "Sound (*Ṣaḥīḥ*) and Good (*Ḥasan*) hadith traditions" — explicitly stated in the chapter summary. This is consistent with the principle from *Approaching the Sunnah* that weak hadiths cannot serve as the basis for *obligations*, but it also reflects the specific rigor of the educational context: since Prophetic pedagogy is being presented as a *model* for emulation, the evidence must be reliable enough to attribute to the Prophet with confidence. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

This is a narrower standard than the ethics context (which occasionally draws on *ḥasan* hadiths for moral illustration) and a parallel standard to the fiqh context (which insists on *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan* for legal rulings).

### 4.3 The Comprehensiveness Principle Applied to Education

The "comprehensive" (*shāmil*) characteristic of the Sunnah from *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 1 is applied directly in the educational context. The Prophetic model of education is not limited to religious instruction; it encompasses all dimensions of human development — physical, intellectual, moral, and spiritual. Qaradawi argues that these educational principles "constitute an advanced educational philosophy that recognizes the diversity of human aptitudes, environments, and developmental stages, offering a model of differentiated instruction rooted in revelation and Prophetic practice rather than in secular modern theory." ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

The "realistic" (*wāqiʿī*) characteristic also appears: the Prophet did not treat learners as angels but as human beings with varying capacities — and his pedagogy reflected this realism by adjusting difficulty, pacing, and content to the learner's condition.

### 4.4 Economy as a Hadith Domain

[[education-economy-sunnah-ch-02|Chapter 2]] extends the Sunnah's comprehensiveness into the economic domain, citing hadiths on property protection, market ethics, agricultural activity, usury prohibition, debt obligations, and public funds. The methodology here mirrors the fiqh approach — hadiths are cited as evidence for economic norms — but the chapter also uses hadiths that are *supplicatory* (seeking refuge from poverty and debt) or *moral* (prohibiting deception in trade) rather than strictly legal. This blending of hadith categories — legal, moral, and supplicatory — in a single chapter reflects the Sunnah's integrative character as described in *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 1. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

## 5. Comparative Analysis: Three Contexts, One Methodology, Three Applications

### 5.1 Authentication Standards

| Criterion | Fiqh Context | Ethics Context | Education Context |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Minimum grade for obligation** | *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan* | *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan* | *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan* |
| **Minimum grade for moral illustration** | N/A (legal domain) | *ḥasan* (lower than fiqh threshold) | *ṣaḥīḥ* or *ḥasan* |
| **Fabricated hadiths** | Absolutely rejected | Absolutely rejected; identification used to rebut secular critics | Absolutely rejected |
| **Weak hadiths for preaching (*tarġhīb/tarhīb*)** | Not permitted for legal rulings | Qaradawi is stricter than many preachers; prefers *ṣaḥīḥ/ḥasan* alternatives | Excluded from the educational model |

### 5.4 The Hadith–Fiqh Gap

One of Qaradawi's most distinctive contributions — articulated in *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 2 and demonstrated throughout *Fiqh al-Zakah* — is the insistence that jurists and hadith specialists each need the other's discipline. The applications differ by context:

- **In fiqh:** The gap produces *legal consequences* — weak hadiths in fiqh textbooks lead to incorrect obligations. The remedy is source-critique (*taḥrīj*) and systematic re-examination. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt] ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-09.txt]
- **In ethics:** The gap produces *moral consequences* — fabricated hadiths misrepresent Islamic moral teachings, as Qaradawi demonstrates in his rebuttal of Khalid Mohammed Khalid. The remedy is distinguishing authentic Prophetic ethics from cultural accretions. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-02.txt]
- **In education:** The gap produces *pedagogical consequences* — attributing educational methods to the Prophet that he did not actually practice. The remedy is restricting the educational model to authenticated hadiths. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## 6. Key Tensions and Resolutions

### 6.1 The Expansion–Restriction Dynamic

In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi *expands* the scope of zakah by discarding restricting hadiths and applying general Qur'anic principles — from four zakatable crops to all crops, from four types of zakatable wealth to all growing wealth. In *Ethics in Islam* and *Education and Economy in the Sunnah*, the dynamic is the *opposite*: hadiths are *retained* and *celebrated* rather than restricted or overridden, because their function is illustrative rather than limiting.

This is not a contradiction but a consequence of the same methodology applied to different domains: in fiqh, restricting hadiths are critical evidence for limiting obligations, and their weakness justifies expansion. In ethics and education, hadiths expand the moral and pedagogical scope, and there is no incentive to restrict them.

### 6.2 The Individualized Answer Problem

*Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 1 identifies the Prophet's practice of giving different answers to different questioners as a characteristic of the Sunnah. *Education and Economy in the Sunnah* Ch. 1 celebrates this as pedagogical wisdom. But in *Fiqh al-Zakah*, the same practice creates the *uşūl* problem of apparently contradictory hadiths that require harmonization or preference (*tarjīh*). Qaradawi's resolution — that different circumstances justify different rulings — is itself a Prophetic teaching method, but one that is deployed differently in each context:

- **Fiqh:** The variation is evidence of context-dependent rulings, legitimizing ijtihad for new circumstances.
- **Ethics:** The variation demonstrates the Sunnah's capacity to address diverse moral situations.
- **Education:** The variation is the content itself — Prophetic pedagogy that differentiates instruction.

### 6.3 The Role of Ijtihad

Ijtihad occupies different structural positions in the three contexts:

- **Fiqh:** Ijtihad is the *primary engine* of the argument in *Fiqh al-Zakah*. Qaradawi's distinctive rulings (zakatable salary income, rental income, minerals beyond gold/silver, exploited assets) all depend on analogical reasoning from authenticated texts to modern circumstances. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]
- **Ethics:** Ijtihad operates at the *philosophical* level — arguing that Islamic ethics is comprehensive, non-derivative (neither secular liberalism nor blind imitation), and rooted in both revelation and reason. The hadith methodology is primarily citatory rather than analogical. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-01.txt]
- **Education:** Ijtihad is virtually absent from the hadith methodology; the Prophetic educational model is presented as self-sufficient and directly imitable, requiring interpretation but not analogical extension. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## 7. Conclusion: One Methodology, Three Modalities

Qaradawi's hadith methodology, as theorized in *Approaching the Sunnah*, provides a single framework — verify authenticity, understand context, and check against stronger evidence — that manifests in three distinct modalities depending on the disciplinary context:

1. **In fiqh**, the methodology is *critical and constructive*: it identifies weak hadiths that distort legal obligations, overrides them with Qur'anic general texts, and extends authenticated principles through ijtihad to new asset categories. Hadith function as legal evidence; the fiqh–hadith gap is a practical problem requiring systematic source-critique.

2. **In ethics**, the methodology is *defensive and integrative*: it defends the moral authority of authenticated hadiths against fabrication, secular appropriation, and cultural misrepresentation. Hadith function as moral proof-texts and character models; the primary threat is not weak chains but misattribution and misinterpretation.

3. **In education**, the methodology is *descriptive and imitative*: it restricts itself to authenticated hadiths, presents Prophetic pedagogy as a comprehensive model, and draws out principles without requiring analogical extension. Hadith function as instances of teaching practice; the primary task is faithful description rather than critical reconstruction.

The underlying unity is Qaradawi's conviction that the Sunnah is, as he describes it in *Approaching the Sunnah* Ch. 1, a comprehensive, balanced, integrative, and realistic pattern — and that each discipline must engage with it according to its own canons of evidence while remaining faithful to these general characteristics. The result is a methodology that is flexible without being relativistic, rigorous without being rigid, and contextually sensitive without being contradictory.
