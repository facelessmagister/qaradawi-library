# Module 3.2 — Affection, Mercy, and Mutual Rights

**Phase:** 3 — Branches: The Family Unit
**Week:** 14
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that marriage is built on affection (mawaddah) and mercy (rahmah) — divine gifts, not human inventions — and that kindness to one's spouse is a Qur'anic command. The session establishes that the Qur'an's use of the plural pronoun ("between you") reveals that marital love flows outward to the entire family and society.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that the love between spouses is not just a human emotion — it is a divine sign. Allah placed affection and mercy between husband and wife, and this love is meant to flow from the couple to the whole family.

> وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُم مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا لِّتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُم مَّوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً
>
> "And among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may dwell in tranquility with them, and He has placed between you affection (mawaddah) and mercy (rahmah)."
>
> — **Surah ar-Rum (30:21)**

### Verse 2 — Live with Them in Kindness

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَن تَرِثُوا النِّسَاءَ دَفْتًا ۖ وَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ إِلَّا أَن يَأْتِينَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ مُّبَيِّنَةٍ ۚ وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۚ فَإِن كَرِهْتُمُوهُنَّ فَعَسَىٰ أَن تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَيَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ خَيْرًا كَثِيرًا
>
> "O you who have believed, it is not lawful for you to inherit women by compulsion. And do not make difficulties for them in order to take [back] part of what you gave them unless they commit a clear immorality. **And live with them in kindness.** For if you dislike them — perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good."
>
> — **Surah an-Nisa' (4:19)**

### Verse 3 — Do Not Take Back What You Gave

> وَإِنْ أَرَدتُّمُ اسْتِبْدَالَ زَوْجٍ مَّكَانَ زَوْجٍ وَآتَيْتُمْ إِحْدَاهُنَّ قِنطَارًا فَلَا تَأْخُذُوا مِنْهُ شَيْئًا ۚ أَتَأْخُذُونَهُ بُهْتَانًا وَإِثْمًا مُّبِينًا
>
> "But if you want to replace one wife with another and you have given one of them a great amount [in gifts], do not take [back] anything from it. Would you take it in injustice and clear sin?"
>
> — **Surah an-Nisa' (4:20)**

### Verse 4 — Rights Similar to Rights Against Them

> وَلَهُنَّ مِثْلُ الَّذِي عَلَيْهِنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

> "And for them [women] are rights similar to those [of men] over them, according to what is equitable."
>
> — **Surah al-Baqarah (2:228)**

> هُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ وَجَعَلَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا لِيَسْكُنَ إِلَيْهَا
>
> "It is He who created you from one soul and created from it its mate that he might dwell in tranquility with her."
>
> — **Surah al-A'raf (7:189)**

### Verse 6 — Kind Agreement Is Best

> وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خَافَتْ مِن بَعْلِهَا نُشُوزًا أَوْ إِعْرَاضًا فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا أَن يُصْلِحَا بَيْنَهُمَا صُلْحًا ۚ وَالصُّلْحُ خَيْرٌ
>
> "If a woman fears from her husband contempt or evasion, there is no sin upon them if they make terms of kind agreement between them — and kind agreement is best."
>
> — **Surah an-Nisa' (4:128)**

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — If He Dislikes One Trait, He Will Be Pleased with Another

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "A believing man should not hate a believing woman; if he dislikes one of her personal traits, he will be pleased with another trait."
>
> — **Narrated by Muslim (1469)** on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

### Hadith 2 — Each One of You Is a Caretaker

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Each one of you is a caretaker (ra'i) and is responsible for those under his care."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** on the authority of Ibn Umar

### Hadith 3 — Wasting Sustenance Is Sufficient Sin

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Wasting the sustenance of his dependants is sufficient sin for a man."
>
> — **Narrated by Ibn Hibban** in his Sahih

### Hadith 4 — Allah Will Ask Every Caretaker

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Allah will ask every caretaker about what He entrusted him to take care of, whether he has done the care-taking well or otherwise, and every man will be asked concerning the people of his household."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim**

### Hadith 5 — The Prophet Never Raised His Hand

Qaradawi notes in Ethics in Islam (section 4.3): "We have never heard of the Prophet (PBUH) hitting a woman, a servant, or even an animal. The Prophet (PBUH) has even urged men to be like him and never raise a hand to a woman."

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.3: "The Ethics of the Family" (pp. 364-370); **The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam** (الحلال والحرام), Chapter 3 (pp. 212-216). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Qur'anic Foundation: Mawaddah and Rahmah

Qaradawi identifies the Qur'anic verse 30:21 as the foundational text for understanding marriage:

"Among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may dwell in tranquility with them, and He has placed between you affection (mawaddah) and mercy (rahmah)."

He draws attention to a crucial linguistic detail: **the Qur'an uses the second-person PLURAL pronoun** — "He placed between YOU (plural)" — not the dual pronoun that would refer to just the two spouses. This is because:

> "The affection and mercy move from the nucleus of the married couple **to the rest of their family and across society**."

The love between husband and wife is not a private emotion — it is a force that radiates outward. When parents love each other, children feel secure. When the home is filled with mawaddah and rahmah, the extended family, the neighbours, and the community all benefit. Marital love is not just for the couple — it is the engine of social cohesion.

### 2. Tranquility (Sukun) — The Purpose of Marriage

Qaradawi emphasises that the Qur'an describes the purpose of marriage as **sukun** — tranquility, peace, rest. "That you may dwell in tranquility with them" (30:21). The home is meant to be a place of rest — not a battlefield. When the husband returns from work, he should find peace. When the wife is at home, she should feel security. When children grow up in this environment, they develop emotional stability.

This means: a home filled with arguments, shouting, and tension is not fulfilling the Qur'anic purpose of marriage. The goal is not merely to coexist but to create **tranquility** — a home where everyone feels safe, loved, and at peace.

### 3. Live with Them in Kindness

The Qur'an commands: "Live with them in kindness" (4:19). Qaradawi explains that this is not a suggestion — it is a divine command. The Arabic phrase *'āshirūhunna bil-ma'rūf* means "associate with them in that which is recognized as good" — in other words, treat your wife according to the best standards of your culture and religion.

The verse continues: "If you dislike them — perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good." Qaradawi comments: this is a remarkable teaching. The Qur'an acknowledges that a person may dislike something about their spouse — but then reveals that **Allah may have placed hidden good in that very thing you dislike**. The annoying habit, the physical trait, the personality difference — it may contain a blessing you cannot see. Patience reveals the hidden good.

### 4. Mutual Rights — "Like Upon Like"

The Qur'an states: "And for them [women] are rights similar to those [of men] over them, according to what is equitable" (2:228). Qaradawi treats this as the foundational principle of marital equality in Islam.

While Islam assigns different roles to husbands and wives (the husband is responsible for financial maintenance, the wife for the household), the RIGHTS are mutual. Each spouse has rights over the other. The husband is not a tyrant and the wife is not a servant. They are partners with reciprocal obligations.

Qaradawi cites the Prophet: "Each one of you is a caretaker (ra'i) and is responsible for those under his care." The husband is a caretaker of his wife and children — not their master. He is accountable to Allah for how he treats them. "Allah will ask every caretaker about what He entrusted him to take care of" (al-Bukhari, Muslim).

### 5. Patience with Imperfection

Qaradawi addresses a crucial reality: no spouse is perfect. The Prophet said: "A believing man should not hate a believing woman; if he dislikes one of her personal traits, he will be pleased with another trait" (Muslim).

In Ethics in Islam (section 4.3), Qaradawi writes: "One needs to be patient with his wife because nobody is perfect. Rather, people should be realistic in their expectations and appreciate the good qualities of their spouses. Just as men are ordered to be patient with their wives, women are also ordered to be patient with their husbands."

The Qur'an: "If a woman fears from her husband contempt or evasion, there is no sin upon them if they make terms of kind agreement between them — and kind agreement is best" (4:128). Both spouses are called to patience and reconciliation.

### 6. Guarding Secrets

Qaradawi highlights a critical aspect of marital trust: guarding the private intimacy between spouses. In *The Lawful and the Prohibited* (pp. 215-216), he cites the Prophet's severe warning:

"Among those who will occupy the worst position in the sight of Allah on the Day of Resurrection is the man who has intercourse with his wife and she with him, and then he spreads her secret."

The Prophet asked: "Is there among you any man who comes to his wife, closes the door, and draws the curtain, and then goes out and speaks about it?" Then he asked the women the same question. A woman said: "Yes, by Allah, the men talk about it and the women, too." The Prophet said: "Do you know what those who do this are like? They are like a male and female devil who meet each other in the road and satisfy their desire while the people look on."

Qaradawi comments: "This emphatic way of expressing the matter should be sufficient to turn the Muslim away from such ill-considered and degraded behavior." The intimacy between spouses is sacred — it must never be shared with friends, family, or the public.

### 7. Financial Responsibility

The Prophet said: "Wasting the sustenance of his dependants is sufficient sin for a man" (Ibn Hibban). Qaradawi explains: providing for the family is not optional generosity — it is a religious obligation. A man who fails to provide, or who wastes what should go to his family, commits a sin.

But the Qur'an also balances this: "Allah does not charge a soul except [according to] what He has given it" (65:7). The husband is required to provide according to his means — not beyond them. The wife should not demand what the husband cannot afford. Both must practice contentment (connecting to Module 2.5).

In *Ethics in Islam* (section 4.3), Qaradawi adds a beautiful historical detail: "In the old days, a wife would pray for her husband as he left for work: 'Don't bring back ill-gotten money. We would rather go hungry than eat from forbidden money. We can be patient while hungry, but nobody can be patient with hell fire.'" This is the standard: the family prefers honest poverty to dishonest wealth.

### 8. The Prophet as Marital Model

Qaradawi emphasises: "We have never heard of the Prophet (PBUH) hitting a woman, a servant, or even an animal. The Prophet (PBUH) has even urged men to be like him and never raise a hand to a woman."

The Prophet's marriage is the model. He was kind, patient, helpful at home (as established in Module 2.3 — he cleaned his own shoes and helped his family). He listened to his wives, consulted them, and treated them with dignity. His marriage to Khadijah was a partnership of mutual respect and love. His example is the standard against which all Muslim marriages should be measured.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — What Does "Affection and Mercy" Look Like?

> The Qur'an says Allah placed "affection and mercy" between spouses. What does mawaddah (affection) look like in daily married life? What does rahmah (mercy) look like?

**Facilitator guidance:** Mawaddah = warm love, the desire to be near, the joy of each other's company. Rahmah = mercy, compassion, forgiving mistakes, being gentle when the other is struggling. In daily life: mawaddah is the smile when your spouse comes home. Rahmah is not bringing up past mistakes during an argument.

### Question 2 — The Plural Pronoun — Love That Radiates

> Qaradawi notes that the Qur'an uses the plural "between you" — not the dual "between you two." This means marital love flows from the couple to the whole family. How does the love between parents affect the children?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the key insight. When parents love each other, children feel secure. When parents fight, children feel anxious. The love between spouses is not a private matter — it is the emotional foundation of the entire household. Ask: "When we (parents) are kind to each other, how do the children feel? When we argue, how do they feel?"

### Question 3 — Dislike Something, Find Good in It

> The Qur'an says: "If you dislike them, perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good." Have you ever disliked something about a family member and later discovered it was actually a good thing?

**Facilitator guidance:** This verse teaches patience with imperfection. The annoying trait may be connected to a virtue: the stubborn person may also be determined; the quiet person may also be thoughtful; the emotional person may also be compassionate. The Qur'anic promise: Allah has placed good in the very thing you dislike — if you are patient enough to see it.

### Question 4 — Guarding Secrets

> The Prophet said the worst position on the Day of Resurrection belongs to the person who shares their spouse's intimate secrets. How does this apply to our family? Are there things we should never share outside the home?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is about trust. Spouses must never share intimate details with friends or family. Parents must not share children's private struggles with others. Children must not share family problems at school. The home is a trust — what happens inside stays inside. This is the Islamic standard of privacy.

### Question 5 — Each One Is a Caretaker

> The Prophet said: "Each one of you is a caretaker and is responsible for those under your care." Who is each person in our family a caretaker of? What does that responsibility look like?

**Facilitator guidance:**
- Father: caretaker of the entire family — responsible for their physical, emotional, and spiritual well-being
- Mother: caretaker of the home and children — responsible for their upbringing, education, and emotional security
- Older sibling: caretaker of younger siblings — responsible for setting an example and protecting them
- Each child: caretaker of their own actions and their relationships with family members

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Mercy Challenge"

Each family member does ONE act of mercy for another family member this week — without being asked and without expecting thanks.

**Examples:**
- Husband: doing a chore his wife normally does, so she can rest
- Wife: preparing her husband's favourite meal without being asked
- Older sibling: helping a younger sibling with homework
- Younger sibling: making a card for an older sibling
- Child: cleaning up without being told

**At the next halaqah:** Share what you did and how it felt. Discuss:
- Did the person notice?
- How did it feel to show mercy without being asked?
- How does this relate to "Allah placed between you affection and mercy"?

**Goal:** To make rahmah (mercy) a lived, daily practice — not just a concept. The family that practices mercy toward each other is building the Qur'anic model of marriage and family.

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **the plural pronoun in 30:21.** "He placed between YOU (plural) affection and mercy" — not "between you two." This means marital love is not a private emotion but a force that shapes the entire family. When parents love each other, children thrive. When parents fight, children suffer. ### The Prophet as Model
The Prophet never hit a woman, servant, or animal. He cleaned his own shoes and helped at home. He was patient with his wives. He consulted them. This is the standard. Any man who claims religious authority to dominate his wife is placing himself ABOVE the Prophet — and that is a form of arrogance (connecting to Module 2.3).
