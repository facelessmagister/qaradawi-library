# Module 4.5 — Cleanliness: Half of Faith

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 23
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that cleanliness is a form of worship — "half of faith" — and develop concrete hygiene habits based on the Sunnah. The session establishes that Islam links cleanliness to worship (wudu before prayer), that the first verses revealed include "purify your clothing," and that Allah loves those who purify themselves.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that cleanliness is not just a good habit — it is half of our faith. The Prophet taught us that Allah is clean and loves cleanliness, and that purifying our bodies is an act of worship.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Cleanliness" (pp. 338-340). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Cleanliness Plays a Significant Role in Faith

Qaradawi writes: "Cleanliness plays a significant role in faith, because faith is cleansing oneself from evil and developing noble qualities."

This is a profound insight: faith itself is a form of cleanliness — cleansing the HEART from evil. Physical cleanliness is the outward expression of this inner purification. The two are connected: a clean body reflects a clean heart, and a clean heart produces a clean body.

"Indeed, Allah loves those who are constantly repentant and loves those who purify themselves" (2:222). The verse pairs repentance (spiritual cleansing) with purification (physical cleansing) — both are loved by Allah.

### 2. Cleanliness Linked to Worship

Qaradawi notes: "One cannot pray unless they are physically clean." This is a unique Islamic feature — no other major religion makes physical purity a precondition for prayer. In Islam, wudu (ablution) is not optional; it is required. You cannot stand before Allah in prayer with an impure body.

"Almost all books on Islamic jurisprudence start with a chapter on cleanliness." This is not accidental — it reflects the priority Islam gives to physical purity. Before you learn about prayer, fasting, or zakat, you learn about cleanliness. It is the foundation of all other practices.

### 3. The First Revelation: Purify Your Clothing

Qaradawi notes that one of the very first verses of the Qur'an to be revealed is: "And your clothing purify" (74:4). This is extraordinary — among the first words Allah spoke to the Prophet through revelation was a command about cleanliness. It was not about theology, not about law, not about society — it was about purifying one's clothing. This establishes cleanliness as foundational to the Islamic message.

### 4. Personal Hygiene Practices from the Sunnah

Qaradawi catalogues the personal hygiene practices the Prophet taught:

- **Brushing teeth (miswak):** "The Miswak cleanses and purifies the mouth and pleases Allah"
- **Washing hands before and after meals**
- **Removing excess hair from the armpits and the groin**
- **Trimming one's nails**
- **If you have hair, honor it** — caring for one's hair
- **Bathing after sexual intercourse or a menstrual period** (ritual purification)
- **Ablution (wudu) before every prayer** — washing hands, mouth, nose, face, arms, head, and feet five times a day

These practices make cleanliness a HABIT — not an occasional activity but a daily, repeated practice woven into the rhythm of worship.

### 5. Cleanliness of the Environment

Qaradawi extends cleanliness beyond the individual to the environment:

- "Allah is clean, and He loves cleanliness. Clean your courtyards" — the Prophet commanded clean courtyards, not just clean bodies
- "Removing harm from the road is a form of charity" — keeping streets clean is an act of worship
- Mosques should be clean — they are houses of Allah
- Homes should be clean — they are where the family lives and worships

The principle: cleanliness is not only personal — it is communal. The Muslim who is clean personally but litters the street has not understood the full scope of Islamic cleanliness. The Muslim who keeps their home spotless but neglects their neighbourhood has missed the point.

### 6. Adornment with Fine Clothes and Perfumes

Qaradawi notes that Islam goes beyond cleanliness to adornment:

"Allah says: 'O children of Adam, take your adornment at every masjid' (7:31); and 'Say: Who has forbidden the adornment of Allah which He has produced for His servants and the good [lawful] things of provision?' (7:32)."

The Prophet: "Allah is beautiful, and He loves beauty" (Muslim, via Ibn Mas'ud).

Islam encourages not only being clean but also being well-presented: wearing fine clothes (especially at Hajj and Eid), using perfumes, keeping one's hair groomed. The Muslim should be recognisable by their cleanliness and presentation — not by their dishevelment.

### 7. Cleanliness of the Heart

Qaradawi connects physical cleanliness to spiritual cleanliness:

"Faith is cleansing oneself from evil and developing noble qualities." The physical act of wudu — washing the hands, face, arms, and feet — is an outward symbol of an inward reality: the believer is cleansing their heart from sin through repentance, just as they cleanse their body from dirt through water.

The Qur'an pairs: "Allah loves those who are constantly repentant and loves those who purify themselves" (2:222). Repentance = spiritual cleansing. Purification = physical cleansing. Both are loved by Allah. Both are "half of faith."

### 8. Cleanliness as a Family Practice

For the family, cleanliness becomes a shared practice:

| Practice | Family Application |
|---|---|
| Wudu before prayer | Teach children to make wudu from a young age — make it a habit |
| Miswak/toothbrush | Brush teeth as a family after meals |
| Washing hands | Before and after every meal — a Sunnah practice |
| Clean home | Assign cleaning tasks to all family members — cleanliness is worship |
| Clean clothes | Dress well for prayer, for Eid, for Friday — "take your adornment" |
| Perfume | Use fragrance, especially for Friday prayer and gatherings |
| Clean environment | Pick up litter as a family — "removing harm from the road is charity" |

The home that is clean is a home that pleases Allah. The family that practices cleanliness together is practicing "half of faith" together.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Half of Faith

> The Prophet said: "Cleanliness is half of faith." What does that mean? How can something as simple as being clean be HALF of our faith?

**Facilitator guidance:** Faith = cleansing the heart from sin + cleansing the body from dirt. One half is spiritual (repentance, taqwa, good character), the other half is physical (wudu, hygiene, clean environment). Both are essential. A person with a pure heart but a dirty body is incomplete. A person with a clean body but a corrupt heart is incomplete. Both halves together = complete faith.

### Question 2 — Allah Loves Those Who Purify Themselves

> The Qur'an says Allah LOVES those who purify themselves. When you make wudu, brush your teeth, or take a shower — you are doing something Allah loves. How does this change how we think about these daily habits?

**Facilitator guidance:** Daily hygiene habits become worship when done with the awareness that Allah loves purification. Brushing teeth is not just a health practice — it pleases Allah. Wudu is not just a prayer requirement — it is loved by Allah. This transforms mundane activities into spiritual practices.

### Question 3 — The First Revelation Was About Cleanliness

> One of the very first verses Allah revealed was: "And your clothing purify" (74:4). Why do you think Allah started with cleanliness?

**Facilitator guidance:** Cleanliness is the foundation. Before you can pray, you must be clean. Before you can learn, you must be clean. Before you can represent Islam to the world, you must be clean. Allah started with cleanliness because it is the precondition for everything else.

### Question 4 — Cleanliness of the Environment

> The Prophet said "clean your courtyards" and "removing harm from the road is charity." Cleanliness is not just personal — it's communal. How does our family treat our environment?

**Facilitator guidance:** This extends cleanliness beyond the home: picking up litter, keeping the neighbourhood clean, not throwing trash from the car, reporting hazards. "Removing harm from the road is charity" — every piece of litter picked up is sadaqah.

### Question 5 — Cleanliness in Our Home

> What is one area of cleanliness our family needs to improve? Personal hygiene? Home cleanliness? Environmental cleanliness?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let each family member identify one area. Common issues: children not brushing teeth consistently, teenagers not keeping rooms clean, parents not keeping the car clean, the family not picking up litter. Agree on ONE concrete change.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Cleaning Day — as Worship"

Everyone cleans one area of the home together. Frame it NOT as a chore but as worship.

**Before starting, say together:**
> "Allah is clean and He loves cleanliness. We are cleaning our home to please Allah."

**Assign areas:**
- One person cleans the prayer area
- One person cleans the kitchen
- One person cleans the bathroom
- One person cleans the living area
- (Adjust based on family size)

**After cleaning, make du'a together:**
> "اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَنَا فِي بَيْتِنَا — O Allah, bless our home."

**Discuss:**
- How did it feel to clean as worship rather than as a chore?
- The Prophet said "cleanliness is half of faith" — what half did we just practice?
- What would change if we always cleaned with this intention?

**Goal:** To transform cleaning from a mundane task into an act of worship — by changing the INTENTION (niyyah, connecting to Module 2.4). Cleaning with the awareness that "Allah loves cleanliness" makes the same physical act a spiritual practice.
