# Module 5.1 — Surrender and Trust: The Fruit of Faith

**Phase:** 5 — Fruit: Trust, Hope & Eternal Vision
**Week:** 26
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that tawakkul (trust in Allah) is not passive fatalism — it is active reliance after doing one's part. The session establishes Qaradawi's treatment of reliance on Allah as one of the divine ethics, the Prophetic model ("Tie your camel and rely on Allah"), and the balance between fear and hope that prevents both despair and complacency.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn the fruit of faith — trusting Allah completely while doing our part. This is the harvest of everything we have planted: roots of faith, trunk of character, branches of family, leaves of daily life. The fruit is surrender to Allah and trust in His plan.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — Tie Your Camel and Rely on Allah

A Bedouin asked the Prophet (peace be upon him):

> "O Messenger of Allah! Shall I tie my camel and rely on Allah, or let it loose and rely on Allah?" The Prophet replied: **"Tie it and rely on Allah."**
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi** on the authority of Anas ibn Malik — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.1

### Hadith 2 — What Do You Think of Two Men in the Company of Allah?

During the Hijrah, when the pursuers reached the cave, Abu Bakr said: "O Messenger of Allah! If any of them looks down, he will definitely see us." The Prophet replied:

> "O Abu Bakr! What do you think of two men in the company of Allah? Have no fear, for Allah is with us."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.1

### Hadith 3 — Strange Are the Ways of the Believers

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Strange are the ways of the believers, for there is good in all their affairs. This is not the case with anyone else except the believers: they thank Allah in good times, thus gain blessings; and in hard times, they endure patiently, thus they gain blessings."
>
> — **Narrated by Muslim (6792)** on the authority of Suhayb — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.1

### Hadith 4 — Whoever Approaches Me, I Approach Him

The Prophet (peace be upon him) narrated from Allah:

> "Whoever approaches Me by one span, I will approach him by one cubit; and whoever approaches Me by one cubit, I approach him by one fathom."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (7405)** and **Muslim (2675)** on the authority of Abu Hurayrah — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.1

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.1: "Divine Ethics — Reliance on Allah" (pp. 316-318); **Faith and Life** (الإيمان والحياة), Chapter 3 (Iman and Love) and Chapter 4 (Iman and Hope). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Reliance on Allah (Tawakkul) — The Foundation

Qaradawi defines tawakkul: "To rely on Allah is to have full trust in Him and to depend on Him to overcome all hardships. This reliance stems from firm belief in Allah's words."

The Qur'anic foundations:
- "If Allah should aid you, no one can overcome you" (3:160)
- "Whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him" (65:3)
- "Allah is sufficient as Trustee" (4:81)

Qaradawi writes: "Those who seek refuge in Allah are never defeated, for He is the Powerful, and those who submit to His plans are never misguided, for He is the Wise. This is the message of all the prophets who faced the most brutal of tyrants."

### 2. Tawakkul Is NOT Passive Fatalism

Qaradawi is emphatic: "Reliance on Allah is sometimes misunderstood as a form of passive fatalism. The truth about reliance on Allah is explained in the Sunnah."

The definitive hadith: A man asked whether he should tie his camel and trust Allah, or let it loose and trust Allah. The Prophet said: **"Tie it and rely on Allah."**

This single hadith demolishes the misconception of passive tawakkul. The Prophet did NOT say "let it loose and trust Allah." He said: DO the action (tie the camel) AND trust Allah. Tawakkul is not a substitute for effort — it is the spiritual dimension OF effort. You do your part, and you trust Allah for the result.

### 3. The Prophet as Model of Active Tawakkul

Qaradawi presents the Hijrah as the ultimate example:

"When it was time for him to emigrate from Mecca to Medina, he prepared all the things needed for the journey. He also used various strategies to evade those who pursued him. He had everything well prepared: the camels, the company, and the guide before he set off. He even chose an uncommonly taken route, a cave where they could hide, and trusted people to bring provisions and hide their traces."

The Prophet did EVERYTHING humanly possible:
- Prepared the camels
- Chose a companion (Abu Bakr)
- Hired a guide
- Took an uncommon route
- Hid in a cave
- Arranged for provisions
- Had someone hide their tracks

AND THEN the pursuers reached the cave. Despite ALL the preparation, the situation seemed hopeless. This is when Abu Bakr panicked — "If any of them looks down, he will definitely see us" — and the Prophet responded with the words of tawakkul: "What do you think of two men in the company of Allah? Have no fear, for Allah is with us."

The sequence is critical: **maximum human effort → trust in Allah → divine intervention.** Tawakkul does not replace effort — it completes it. You do everything you can, and then you surrender the result to Allah.

### 4. The Balance of Fear and Hope

Qaradawi presents a crucial balance: fear (khawf) and hope (raja').

"Hope for forgiveness and mercy does not allow fear to take over, which could result in resignation and despair. At the same time, fear does not allow hope to take over to the point of taking mercy and forgiveness for granted."

The two extremes:
- **Too much fear → despair:** "No one despairs of relief from Allah except the disbelieving people" (18:87)
- **Too much hope → complacency:** "No one feels secure from the plan of Allah except the losing people" (7:99)

The balance: "Is one who is devoutly obedient during the night, prostrating and standing [in prayer], fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord?" (39:9). The believer stands between fear and hope — like a bird with two wings. Remove one wing, and the bird cannot fly.

### 5. Allah's Mercy Is Greater Than Any Sin

Qaradawi presents the verse: "Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins" (39:53).

He notes: "In this verse, Allah addresses those who commit sins as 'O My servants,' for they are still His servants despite their sins."

This is one of the most beautiful insights in Qaradawi's treatment: even the sinner is still Allah's servant. Allah does not disown them. He calls them "My servants" — even while they are sinning — and then invites them back: "do not despair." The door is always open. The mercy is always greater.

The Prophet: "By the One in Whose Hand my soul is! If you do not commit sins, Allah would replace you with a people who would commit sins and seek His forgiveness; and Allah will certainly forgive them." And: "All humans sin, and the best of those who sin are the ones who repent."

### 6. Gratitude and Patience — The Two Wings

Qaradawi pairs gratitude and patience as complementary virtues:

"Life has both hard and good times. During good times, one needs to be grateful for Allah's blessings, whereas in hard times, one needs to patiently endure."

The Prophet: "Strange are the ways of the believers, for there is good in all their affairs... they thank Allah in good times, thus gain blessings; and in hard times, they endure patiently, thus they gain blessings."

The believer is always in a state of blessing — either gratitude (in good times) or patience (in hard times). Both produce good. Both are rewarded. The believer's life is never wasted — every moment is either gratitude or patience, and both are worship.

### 7. Allah Approaches Those Who Approach Him

Qaradawi cites the hadith qudsi: "Whoever approaches Me by one span, I will approach him by one cubit; and whoever approaches Me by one cubit, I approach him by one fathom."

The message: Allah's response is always GREATER than our effort. We take a small step — Allah takes a big step. We reach out a little — Allah reaches out a lot. The math of tawakkul: our effort is small, Allah's response is vast. This is the fruit of faith — the confidence that no effort toward Allah is ever wasted.

### 8. Being a Slave to Allah Is True Freedom

Qaradawi addresses the orientalist critique that Islamic submission is "humiliating":

"Appreciating the nature of this relationship frees the individual from slavery and servitude to all others, whether people, ideologies, or oneself. What ruined the human experience is their enslaving each other and choosing to be slaves to each other."

The paradox: being a slave to Allah is the ONLY true freedom — because it frees you from slavery to everything else. The person who submits to Allah is no longer slave to wealth, status, fashion, social media, peer pressure, or their own desires. They serve One Master — and that service liberates them from all other masters.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Tie Your Camel and Rely on Allah

> The Prophet said: "Tie it and rely on Allah." How does this one sentence destroy the idea that tawakkul means doing nothing? What does real tawakkul look like?

**Facilitator guidance:** Real tawakkul = maximum effort + trust in Allah for the result. You study hard AND trust Allah for the grade. You work hard AND trust Allah for the income. You raise your children with effort AND trust Allah for their character. You do your part — and you let go of the outcome. This is the balance: not lazy fatalism, not anxious control — active trust.

### Question 2 — The Prophet in the Cave

> The Prophet prepared everything for the Hijrah — camels, guide, route, cave, provisions. AND THEN he said: "Have no fear, for Allah is with us." What does this sequence teach us?

**Facilitator guidance:** The sequence is: do everything humanly possible → then trust Allah. The Prophet didn't sit in Mecca and say "Allah will handle it." He prepared meticulously. But when human effort reached its limit, he didn't panic — he trusted. This is the model: prepare like everything depends on you, and trust like everything depends on Allah.

### Question 3 — Fear and Hope: Two Wings

> Qaradawi says the believer stands between fear and hope "like a bird with two wings." What happens if you have only fear? Only hope? What does the balance look like?

**Facilitator guidance:**
- Only fear → despair ("I'm too sinful, Allah will never forgive me")
- Only hope → complacency ("Allah is merciful, I don't need to change")
- Balance → motivation ("Allah is merciful, so I will try — but I am accountable, so I must try")

### Question 4 — Even the Sinner Is "My Servant"

> Allah calls sinners "O My servants" — even while they are sinning. He doesn't disown them. What does this tell us about Allah's love? How should we treat family members who struggle?

**Facilitator guidance:** Allah does not disown the sinner. He calls them "My servant" and invites them back. This is the model for families: when a child sins, when a spouse struggles, when a family member goes astray — we do not disown them. We say: "You are still ours. Come back. The door is open." We model Allah's mercy.

### Question 5 — Gratitude and Patience: Always in Blessing

> The Prophet said: the believer is blessed in good times (gratitude) AND in hard times (patience). Both produce good. Is there any situation where the believer is NOT in a state of blessing?

**Facilitator guidance:** No. The believer is ALWAYS in a state of blessing — either gratitude (good times) or patience (hard times). Both are rewarded. Both are worship. This means: there is no "wasted" moment in the believer's life. Even hardship is productive — it produces patience, which produces reward. The question is not "why is this happening?" but "am I responding with gratitude or patience?"

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Tawakkul in Action"

Each family member identifies ONE situation this week where they need to practice "tie your camel and rely on Allah."

| Person | The Situation | What I Will Do (Tie the Camel) | What I Will Trust Allah For |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [exam at school] | [study hard, prepare well] | [the result] |
| [Name] | [job application] | [prepare CV, apply] | [the outcome] |
| [Name] | [child's behavior] | [set rules, be patient, make du'a] | [the child's character] |
| [Name] | [health concern] | [see doctor, follow advice] | [healing] |

**Process:**
1. Each person fills in their row
2. Share with the family
3. At the next halaqah: share what happened. Did you "tie the camel"? Did you trust Allah? What was the result?

**Goal:** To make tawakkul a lived practice — not a theoretical concept. The family that practices tawakkul together learns that effort + trust = peace. You do your best, and you leave the rest to Allah.
