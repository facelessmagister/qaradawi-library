# Module 1.6 — Tawakkul: Trust in Allah's Plan

**Phase:** 1 — Roots: Faith as the Foundation
**Week:** 6
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that tawakkul (reliance on Allah) is active trust — not passive fatalism. The session establishes the Prophetic principle: "Tie your camel and rely on Allah" — the believer takes every reasonable action AND trusts Allah completely. Tawakkul is the culmination of Phase 1: faith produces dignity, happiness, love, hope, taqwa — and finally, surrender to Allah's plan.

---

## Opening (3 min)

Begin with the standard opening:

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn what it truly means to trust Allah's plan — that trust is not laziness, and effort is not reliance on oneself. We learn to do both: tie the camel AND rely on Allah.

### Hadith 1 — Tie Your Camel and Rely on Allah

The Prophet's companion Anas ibn Malik narrated:

> A man asked: "O Messenger of Allah! Shall I tie my camel and rely on Allah, or let it loose and rely on Allah?" The Prophet responded: **"Tie it and rely on Allah."**
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi** (Kitab Sifat al-Qiyamah) on the authority of Anas ibn Malik. Also narrated by Abu Nu'aym in Hilyat al-Awliya'.

Abu Bakr, who accompanied the Prophet on the Hijra journey, said:

> "O Messenger of Allah! If any of them looks down, he will definitely see us."
>
> The Prophet responded calmly: **"O Abu Bakr! What do you think of two men in the company of Allah? Have no fear, for Allah is with us."**
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** (Kitab Tafsir al-Qur'an) and **Muslim**

### Hadith 3 — Allah Is With You Wherever You Are

Allah says in the Qur'an:

> "He is with you wherever you are. And Allah, of what you do, is Seeing."
>
> — **Surah al-Hadid (57:4)**

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.1: "Divine Ethics: Human Morality Toward The Divine" (pp. 314-318). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. What Is Tawakkul?

Qaradawi defines tawakkul as "to have full trust in Allah and to depend on Him to overcome all hardships." It is a divine ethic — a moral value that governs the individual's relationship with Allah.

Tawakkul stems from firm belief in Allah's words:

- "If Allah should aid you, no one can overcome you" (3:160)
- "Whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him" (65:3)
- "Allah is sufficient as Trustee" (4:81)
- "Whoever relies upon Allah — then indeed, Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise" (8:49)

The core of tawakkul: **those who seek refuge in Allah are never defeated, for He is the Powerful; and those who submit to His plans are never misguided, for He is the Wise.**

### 2. Tawakkul Is Not Passive Fatalism

Qaradawi explicitly warns against a common misunderstanding:

> "Reliance on Allah is sometimes misunderstood as a form of passive fatalism."

This is the most critical distinction in the entire module. Passive fatalism says: "If Allah wants something to happen, it will happen. I don't need to do anything." Tawakkul says: "Allah wants me to take action, make effort, use my mind and body — AND trust that the outcome is in His hands."

Qaradawi explains the truth of tawakkul through the Sunnah:

> "One of the Prophet's companions asked him, 'O Messenger of Allah! Shall I tie my camel and rely on Allah, or let it loose and rely on Allah?' The Prophet responded: **'Tie it and rely on Allah.'**"

This single hadith contains the entire Islamic understanding of tawakkul:

1. **Tie your camel** — take action, prepare, use your intelligence, do everything within your power
2. **And rely on Allah** — trust that the outcome is in Allah's hands, not yours

Both parts are essential. Tying without trusting is anxiety — you think the outcome depends on your knot. Trusting without tying is laziness — you expect Allah to do what you should do yourself. Tawakkul is BOTH: complete action + complete trust.

### 3. The Prophet as Role Model of Tawakkul

Qaradawi presents the Prophet's Hijra (emigration from Makkah to Madinah) as the ultimate demonstration of active tawakkul:

**The Prophet took every possible precaution:**
- He prepared camels for the journey
- He chose a companion (Abu Bakr)
- He hired a guide
- He chose an uncommonly taken route
- He hid in a cave
- He used trusted people to bring provisions
- He had people hide their traces

**Despite ALL these preparations**, the unbelievers who pursued him made it to the cave. Abu Bakr said: "O Messenger of Allah! If any of them looks down, he will definitely see us."

The Prophet responded with the calmness of perfect tawakkul: **"O Abu Bakr! What do you think of two men in the company of Allah? Have no fear, for Allah is with us."**

Qaradawi draws the lesson: The Prophet did everything humanly possible — he tied his camel in every conceivable way — and then, when the situation was beyond human control, he trusted Allah completely. He did not say "I don't need to prepare, Allah will protect me." He prepared everything. And he did not say "I'm afraid, my preparations might fail." He trusted that Allah was with them.

### 4. The Relationship Between Tawakkul and Other Divine Ethics

Qaradawi places tawakkul within a series of divine ethics that build on each other:

1. **Sincerity (Ikhlas)** — all actions are for Allah alone
2. **Observing Allah (Muraqaba)** — being aware of Allah while acting
3. **Self-Accountability (Muhasaba)** — reviewing actions after completing them
4. **Tawakkul** — trusting Allah with the outcome of all actions

This sequence reveals the architecture of tawakkul:

- **Before action**: Sincerity (why am I doing this? For Allah.)
- **During action**: Observing Allah (am I doing this in the best way? Allah sees me.)
- **After action**: Self-accountability (did I do it well? What can I improve?)
- **Beyond action**: Tawakkul (the result is in Allah's hands. I trust Him.)

Tawakkul is the final link — it is what happens when you have done everything you can and you hand the result to Allah.

### 5. Tawakkul in the Face of Hardship

Qaradawi emphasizes that tawakkul is most needed — and most tested — during hardship:

> "This is the message of all the prophets who faced the most brutal of tyrants."

He cites Surah Ibrahim (14:12): "Why should we not rely upon Allah while He has guided us to our ways? And we will surely be patient against whatever harm you should cause us."

The prophets faced tyranny, persecution, and seemingly impossible situations. Their tawakkul was not passive resignation — it was active trust that combined perseverance with faith:

- Prophet Musa at the Red Sea: took his people out of Egypt (action), stood before the sea (impossible situation), said "My Lord is with me!" (tawakkul) — and Allah parted the sea
- Prophet Muhammad in the cave: took every precaution (action), faced discovery (impossible situation), said "Allah is with us" (tawakkul) — and Allah protected them

### 6. "Allah Is With You Wherever You Are"

The Qur'an declares: "He is with you wherever you are. And Allah, of what you do, is Seeing" (57:4).

Qaradawi draws on this verse to explain that tawakkul is not a momentary act — it is a **constant state**. The believer who has tawakkul lives with the awareness that Allah is present in every situation, every location, every moment. This awareness:
- Removes fear of people (Allah is greater than any threat)
- Removes anxiety about outcomes (Allah controls the result)
- Removes pride in success (Allah caused the success)
- Removes despair in failure (Allah can turn failure into success)

### 7. Tawakkul and the Family

For the family, tawakkul has a specific and beautiful application:

**Parents**: You do everything you can for your children — feed them, educate them, guide them, protect them. But you cannot guarantee their future. Tawakkul means: do your absolute best AND trust Allah with the outcome. You are not responsible for the result — you are responsible for the effort.

**Children**: You study hard, do your homework, prepare for exams. But you cannot guarantee the grade. Tawakkul means: study with full effort AND trust Allah with the result.

**Spouses**: You are kind, patient, loving. But you cannot guarantee the other person's happiness. Tawakkul means: be the best spouse you can be AND trust Allah with the relationship.

**In all things**: "Tie your camel and rely on Allah." Do everything. Then let go.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — What Does "Tie Your Camel" Mean for You?

> The Prophet said: "Tie it and rely on Allah." What does "tying your camel" look like for a student? For a parent? For a child?

**Facilitator guidance:** Make this concrete for each family member:
- Student: tying the camel = studying, doing homework, preparing for the exam. Relying on Allah = trusting the grade is in His hands.
- Parent: tying the camel = working, providing, guiding, praying for children. Relying on Allah = trusting their future is in His hands.
- Child: tying the camel = being kind, doing chores, helping siblings. Relying on Allah = trusting that Allah sees and rewards.

### Question 2 — When Have You Done Your Best and Allah Solved It?

> Have you ever worried about something, done your best, and then Allah solved it in a way you didn't expect? What happened?

**Facilitator guidance:** This question makes tawakkul personal. Everyone has experienced a moment where they did everything they could and then the situation resolved in a way they couldn't have planned. These are tawakkul moments — evidence that Allah's plan is better than ours.

**Qaradawi's framework:** "Whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him" (65:3).

### Question 3 — What Is the Difference Between Trusting Allah and Being Lazy?

> If tawakkul means trusting Allah, why can't we just say "Allah will provide" and not work? What's the difference?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is THE question that distinguishes true tawakkul from false fatalism. The answer: the Prophet said "Tie it AND rely on Allah" — not "Let it loose and rely on Allah." The camel that is not tied will run away. The person who does not work will not eat. Tawakkul does not replace effort — it completes effort. You do your part, Allah does His part.

### Question 4 — The Prophet in the Cave

> The Prophet prepared everything — camels, companion, guide, route, cave, provisions. Then the enemy arrived at the cave. The Prophet said: "Have no fear, for Allah is with us." How can our family apply this when we face fear?

**Facilitator guidance:** The Prophet's tawakkul was not "I don't need to prepare, Allah will protect me." He prepared everything. Then, when preparation was exhausted and the situation was beyond human control, he trusted. For the family: when you face a health scare, financial difficulty, or crisis — first do everything you can (appoint the best doctor, make a financial plan, seek advice). Then, when you've done all you can, say: "Allah is with us."

### Question 5 — What Is Our Family's "Camel" Right Now?

> What is one thing our family is worrying about right now? How can we "tie the camel" (take action) AND "rely on Allah" (trust the outcome)?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the practical application. Identify one real family concern. Make a concrete action plan together — what can each person do? Then make du'a together, asking Allah to handle what you cannot. This IS tawakkul: action + trust, together, as a family.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Tawakkul Action Plan"

Materials: paper, pens.

**Step 1:** As a family, identify ONE current challenge or concern (school, work, health, finances, relationships).

**Step 2:** Write "Tie the Camel" on one side of the paper. List 3 concrete actions the family will take to address this challenge:
- What will each person do?
- When will they do it?
- How will they know they've done it?

**Step 3:** Write "Rely on Allah" on the other side of the paper. Write a du'a that the family will recite together every day until the challenge is resolved:

> اللَّهُمَّ تَوَفَّنِي مُسْلِمًا وَأَلْحِقْنِي بِالصَّالِحِينَ
>
> "O Allah, cause me to die as a Muslim and join me with the righteous." (variant — adapt to your specific need)

**Step 4:** Agree to review the plan weekly. When the challenge is resolved — whether the way you expected or a different way — thank Allah together.

**The teaching:** Tawakkul is the COMBINATION of Step 2 (action) and Step 3 (trust). Neither alone is sufficient. Together, they are tawakkul.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Use the camel analogy literally — draw a camel with a rope. On the rope, write "We do our best." On the ground, write "Allah takes care of the rest." This makes tawakkul visual and memorable.

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-04-01.txt` (the "Reliance On Allah" subsection, approximately lines 152-196)
- Also re-read the Prophet in the cave story from Faith and Life Chapter 4 (the same story appears in both sources)

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
This module's most important teaching is the **BOTH/AND** of tawakkul:

| False Tawakkul (Fatalism) | True Tawakkul (Active Trust) |
|---|---|
| "Allah will provide, so I don't need to work" | "Allah provides THROUGH my work" |
| "If Allah wants it, it will happen" | "Allah wants me to take action, and He controls the result" |
| "Praying is enough, I don't need to prepare" | "Praying IS preparation, AND I also study/plan/work" |
| Letting the camel loose | Tying the camel AND relying on Allah |

The Prophet's hadith — "Tie it and rely on Allah" — contains the entire Islamic understanding in five words. Neither part is optional. Tying without trusting = anxiety. Trusting without tying = laziness. Both together = tawakkul.

### The Prophet's Hijra as the Perfect Example
The Hijra story is the most powerful illustration because it shows BOTH parts in action:

**Action (tying the camel):**
- Prepared camels
- Chose a companion (Abu Bakr)
- Hired a guide
- Took an uncommon route
- Hid in a cave
- Used trusted people for provisions
- Had tracks hidden

**Trust (relying on Allah):**
- When the tracker arrived at the cave — the moment ALL preparation was exhausted — the Prophet said: "Allah is with us"

The lesson: the Prophet did not skip a single precaution. He did not say "Allah will protect me, so I don't need to hide." He hid. He did not say "Allah will provide, so I don't need provisions." He arranged provisions. But when all human effort was exhausted and the situation was beyond human control, he did not panic. He trusted.

### The Sequence of Divine Ethics
This is the final module of Phase 1. It completes the sequence of divine ethics that Qaradawi presents in Ethics in Islam section 4.1:

1. **Sincerity (Ikhlas)** — why am I doing this? For Allah alone
2. **Observing Allah (Muraqaba)** — am I doing this in the best way? Allah sees me
3. **Self-Accountability (Muhasaba)** — did I do it well? What can I improve?
4. **Tawakkul** — the result is in Allah's hands. I trust Him.

This sequence provides the framework for the entire family halaqah course:
- Phase 1 (Roots) established the foundation: faith, dignity, happiness, love, hope, taqwa
- Tawakkul is the culmination: having done all that faith requires, we surrender the outcome to Allah
- Phase 2 (Trunk) will build on this: character and inner purification
