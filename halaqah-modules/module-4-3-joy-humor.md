# Module 4.3 — Joy, Humor, and Celebration in Family Life

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 21
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that joy and laughter are part of faith, following the Prophet's example. The session establishes that the Prophet jested without lying, played with his grandchildren, encouraged celebration at Eid and weddings, and that "laughter distinguishes human beings from other species."

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that Islam is not a religion of gloom — the Prophet smiled, laughed, played with children, and celebrated. Joy is part of faith, not opposed to it.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — The Prophet Jested Without Lying

Zayd ibn Thabit, the Prophet's neighbour, said:

> "I was his neighbour. When the Revelation descended on him, he sent for me and asked me to write down the revelations. He used to share our interests. He used to participate in our conversations about life, the Hereafter and food."
>
> His Companions described him as "one of the most humorous men."
>
> — **Cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7**

### Hadith 2 — The Prophet and His Grandsons

> The Prophet bent down so that his grandsons (al-Hasan and al-Husayn) would ride on his back. When a companion saw this and said: "You ride the back of the best of men," the Prophet replied: **"They are the best of riders!"**
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** — cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7

### Hadith 3 — The Old Woman and Paradise

> The Prophet jested with an old woman who asked him to supplicate for her to enter Paradise. He said: "O my dear, no old woman enters Paradise!" When she began to cry, he consoled her: he meant she would enter it as a young woman, not an old one. He then recited: "Perfectly We have created them, and We made them virgins" (56:35-36).
>
> — **Cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7**

### Hadith 4 — Aisha and Sawda's Food Fight

> Aisha narrated: "Allah's Prophet and Sawda bint Zam'a were at my place so I cooked Hareera (flour cooked with milk or fat). I offered it to them and said to Sawda: 'Go on eat.' She said: 'I do not like it.' I said: 'By Allah you will eat it, otherwise I will smear your face with it.' She said: 'I will not even taste it.' So I took some of it from the dish and smeared her face with it while the Prophet sat in between. The Prophet lowered his bent knees so that she would be able to get even with me. Thus, she took some of the dish and smeared my face with it. **The Prophet stood there laughing.**"
>
> — **Cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7**

### Hadith 5 — Let Them Sing, It Is Feast Day

> When Abu Bakr showed discontent with the singing of two female singers at the Prophet's home on Eid, the Prophet said: **"O Abu Bakr! Let them sing for it is feast day."** In another version: "So that the Jews will know that we have latitude in our religion."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** — cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7

### Hadith 6 — Haven't You Got Any Amusement?

> The Prophet said to Aisha: "Haven't you got any amusement for the marriage ceremony? The Ansar like amusement." He even suggested specific songs: "We are here especially for you; greet us so that we can greet you."
>
> — **Narrated by Ibn Majah** — cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7

### Hadith 7 — The Difference Between Lawful and Unlawful

> The Prophet said: **"The difference between what is lawful and what is unlawful is the duff (tambourine) and the voice at weddings."**
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** — cited by Qaradawi in Diversion and Arts, Chapter 7

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Diversion and Arts in Islam** (الترفيه والفنون في الإسلام), Chapter 7: "Comedy, Humor, Games and Conclusion." All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Life Is Struggle — People Need Relief

Qaradawi opens Chapter 7 with the Qur'anic declaration: "Indeed, We have created mankind in struggle" (90:4). Life is hard — full of "painful calamities." The believer faces five enemies: "a Muslim who is full of envy, a hypocrite who detests him, a disbeliever who fights him, a Satan who leads him astray, and his baser self which incites him to evil."

Given this struggle, Qaradawi argues: "People are in desperate need of a sanctuary which provides them with a sense of relief and appeasement. They are in need of a break away from the struggle of monstrous life. Their thirst for diversion and entertainment is one of the facts of life. They need to laugh, rejoice and have fun. We cannot let them fall prey to grief and sadness which will ultimately ruin their lives."

Joy is not a luxury — it is a necessity. Islam recognizes this and provides lawful channels for it.

### 2. Laughter Distinguishes Humans

Qaradawi writes: "Laughter distinguishes human beings from other species. Animals cannot laugh because laughter is the outcome of the comprehension of words or certain situations which raise laughter."

He adds: "Since Islam is the religion of instinct, it cannot possibly repress the human urge to laugh and have a good time. On the contrary, it embraces everything which contributes to the enjoyment and joys of life. It urges man to be optimistic and cheerful. It reprimands the gloomy and pessimistic attitude to life."

This is a powerful statement: **Islam "embraces everything which contributes to the enjoyment and joys of life."** The religion that prohibits harmful pleasure also embraces wholesome joy. The gloomy, sullen Muslim who never smiles is NOT more religious — they are missing a dimension of the Sunnah.

### 3. The Prophet as the Model of Joy

Qaradawi presents a cascade of Prophetic examples:

**He jested without lying:** "The Prophet used to jest without resorting to lies. He lived a normal life with his companions. He shared their laughters, joys and jests. He also shared their pain, grief and trials."

**He played with his grandchildren:** He bent down so Hasan and Husayn could ride on his back. He did not feel embarrassed when a companion saw him. "They are the best of riders!" — a playful response that shows the Prophet's delight in his grandchildren.

**He jested with an old woman:** "No old woman enters Paradise!" — then explained she would enter as a young woman. The jest was gentle, the correction was kind, and the teaching (about Paradise) was embedded in the humor.

**He laughed at Aisha and Sawda's food fight:** Aisha smeared Sawda's face with food. The Prophet didn't scold them — he lowered his knees so Sawda could get even. Then "the Prophet stood there laughing." This is a remarkable scene: the Prophet of Allah, laughing at a playful food fight between his wives. Joy was part of his household.

**His companions described him as "one of the most humorous men"** — this is not how most people imagine the Prophet. But it is how his companions — who knew him best — described him.

### 4. Celebration at Eid and Weddings

Qaradawi emphasises that the Prophet actively encouraged celebration:

**Eid:** When Abu Bakr reprimanded two female singers at the Prophet's home on Eid, the Prophet said: "O Abu Bakr! Let them sing for it is feast day." And: "So that the Jews will know that we have latitude in our religion." The Prophet wanted Eid to be joyful — visibly, audibly joyful. He wanted the world to know that Islam has "latitude" — room for celebration.

**Weddings:** The Prophet asked Aisha: "Haven't you got any amusement for the marriage ceremony? The Ansar like amusement." He even suggested specific songs. He wanted weddings to be celebratory — not sombre affairs.

**The duff at weddings:** "The difference between what is lawful and what is unlawful is the duff and the voice at weddings." The duff (tambourine) and singing are the markers of lawful celebration — distinguishing a wedding (halal) from a gathering of sin (haram).

### 5. "At Home, He Used to Jest and Have Fun with His Wives"

Qaradawi writes: "At home, he used to jest and have fun with his wives. He even listened to their short stories." The Prophet's home was not a solemn place — it was a place of warmth, humor, and play. He listened to his wives' stories — not because they were "important" but because they were his companions in life, and sharing joy is part of companionship.

This is the model for the Muslim home: a place where the family laughs together, plays together, and shares joy. A home where the father is not a distant, stern figure but a warm, playful presence. A home where the mother is not a burdened servant but a cherished companion.

### 6. The Conditions for Lawful Entertainment

While Qaradawi strongly supports joy and celebration, he also establishes conditions — consistent with his wasatiyyah framework:

1. **Content must be lawful** — no obscene, vulgar, or sinful content in songs, jokes, or entertainment
2. **No lying in jest** — the Prophet "jested without resorting to lies"; jokes that deceive are prohibited
3. **Moderation** — "excessiveness in diversion is forbidden even if moderate diversion is basically allowed"
4. **No encroachment on obligations** — entertainment must not replace prayer, family duties, or work
5. **Appropriate context** — celebration at Eid and weddings is encouraged; celebration during mourning or crisis is inappropriate

### 7. Comedy and Humor as Psychological Necessity

Qaradawi cites political jokes, riddles, anecdotes, and traditional comic characters (like Goha/Juha) as examples of lawful humor. He notes that humor serves psychological functions: "consolation," "alleviating bitterness," and "ameliorating the status quo."

He even traces the history of comedy in Egyptian culture — caricature, puppet shows (Al Aragouz), shadow plays (Khayal Al Zel), and riddles (Fawazir) — as examples of wholesome entertainment that conform to Islamic values.

For families: humor is not a distraction from faith — it is a form of relief that sustains faith. The family that laughs together stays together. The home that has no laughter is missing a Sunnah.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Was the Prophet Serious All the Time?

> The Prophet's companions described him as "one of the most humorous men." He played with his grandchildren, laughed at a food fight, and jested with an old woman. Does this match your image of the Prophet? Why or why not?

**Facilitator guidance:** Many Muslims imagine the Prophet as always solemn, always serious. The hadiths Qaradawi cites paint a different picture: a man who laughed, played, and celebrated. The question is: if the Prophet was joyful, why are some Muslims gloomy? Is gloom a sign of piety — or a misunderstanding of piety?

### Question 2 — The Prophet Stood There Laughing

> Aisha smeared Sawda's face with food. The Prophet didn't scold them — he helped Sawda get even, and then laughed. What does this tell us about the atmosphere in the Prophet's home?

**Facilitator guidance:** The Prophet's home was filled with play, warmth, and laughter. He didn't see playful behavior as disrespect — he saw it as joy. For families: is our home filled with laughter? Or with tension? The Prophet's home is the model.

### Question 3 — Let Them Sing, It Is Feast Day

> The Prophet defended singing at Eid: "Let them sing for it is feast day." And: "So that the Jews will know that we have latitude in our religion." How does our family celebrate Eid? Is it joyful enough that others can see "we have latitude"?

**Facilitator guidance:** The Prophet wanted Eid to be visibly, audibly joyful — not just internally. He wanted the world to see that Islam has room for celebration. Ask: "When our neighbours see our Eid, do they see joy? Or do they see solemnity?" The Prophetic Eid included singing, food, games, and community.

### Question 4 — Jest Without Lying

> The Prophet "jested without resorting to lies." What kind of humor is Islamic? What kind is not? How do we know the difference?

**Facilitator guidance:** The line is: does the joke involve deception? Does it humiliate anyone? Does it involve haram content? The Prophet's jest with the old woman ("no old woman enters Paradise") was technically untrue — but it was a setup for a beautiful revelation (she would enter as a young woman). The humor was gentle, kind, and led to joy — not to deception or humiliation.

### Question 5 — Laughter in Our Home

> Qaradawi says "Islam embraces everything which contributes to the enjoyment and joys of life." How much laughter is in our home? What is one thing we can do to bring more joy?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the practical application. Ideas: family game night, telling stories, watching a wholesome comedy together, celebrating small milestones, playful teasing (within limits), outdoor activities, cooking together. The goal: a home where the Prophet would feel at home — a home with laughter.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Joy Night"

Plan a family joy night for this week — an evening of wholesome entertainment and laughter.

**Elements to include:**
1. **A meal together** — something everyone enjoys
2. **A game** — board game, card game, or physical game
3. **Storytelling** — share funny family memories, or tell the story of the Prophet and his grandchildren (the "best of riders")
4. **No phones** — everyone puts devices away
5. **Du'a of gratitude** at the end: "Thank You, Allah, for the joy of family"

**At the next halaqah:** Share how it went. Was it hard to put phones away? Did everyone enjoy it? How did it feel to laugh together as a family?

**Goal:** To experience what Qaradawi teaches — that "Islam embraces everything which contributes to the enjoyment and joys of life." The family that plays together, prays together, and laughs together is following the Sunnah in its fullness.

### Key Distinction
The MOST important teaching: **the Prophet laughed and played.** This is not optional information — it is essential to understanding who the Prophet was. A Muslim who only knows the Prophet's rules but not his joy has an incomplete picture. The Prophet was the most devoted worshipper AND the most joyful companion. Both are Sunnah.
