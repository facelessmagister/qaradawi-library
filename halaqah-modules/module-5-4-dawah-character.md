# Module 5.4 — Dawah Through Character: Being a Light

**Phase:** 5 — Fruit: Trust, Hope & Eternal Vision
**Week:** 29
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that dawah (inviting to Islam) is not just speaking — it is living. The session establishes that the most powerful dawah is character, that "whoever shows the way to good has the same reward as the one who does it," and that the family is the first and most important audience for dawah.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that the best dawah is not words — it is character. Our family's behaviour IS our dawah. When people see our kindness, our justice, our cleanliness, our joy — they see Islam. When they see our anger, our dishonesty, our stinginess — they see the opposite.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.3.2: "The Ethics of Society — Expanding Good Deeds" (pp. 376-377); **Faith and Life** (الإيمان والحياة), Chapter 3: "Iman and Love" (dawah through love and tolerance). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Mandate: A Community Inviting to Good

Qaradawi cites the Qur'anic mandate: "Let there be among you a community inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong" (3:104).

He notes: "Muslims are not only required to do good deeds, but also to encourage others to do the same." This is the dual nature of dawah: DO good AND ENCOURAGE good. It is not enough to be good privately — the believer must also be a source of goodness for others.

"Whoever shows the way to doing good has the same reward as someone who actually does it." This means: if you teach someone to pray, you get the same reward as their prayer — every time they pray. If you teach someone to give charity, you get the same reward as their charity — every time they give. The dawah multiplier: one act of teaching produces a lifetime of shared reward.

### 2. Dawah Through Character — The Most Powerful Form

Qaradawi emphasizes throughout his works that the most powerful dawah is not speeches or books — it is CHARACTER. Aisha's description of the Prophet — "His character was the Qur'an" — is the ultimate statement of dawah through being.

The Prophet did not just preach Islam — he WAS Islam. His kindness, his honesty, his patience, his generosity, his cleanliness, his humor, his love for his family — these were the living Qur'an. People saw Islam in him before they heard it from him.

For families: the most powerful dawah your family can give is not what you SAY about Islam — it is how you LIVE. When your neighbours see:
- Your family praying together
- Your children being kind and respectful
- Your home being clean and welcoming
- Your generosity to the community
- Your patience in hardship
- Your joy in celebration
- Your justice among your children

...they see Islam. No words needed. Character is the loudest dawah.

### 3. "Whoever Shows the Way" — The Dawah Multiplier

The Prophet: "Whoever shows the way to doing good has the same reward as someone who actually does it."

Qaradawi comments: "Thus, if one cannot do good deeds, they should at least encourage others to do it, and if they can, they should at least have the intention to do it."

This creates a hierarchy of participation:
1. **Do good** — the highest level
2. **Show the way to good** — equal reward
3. **Encourage good** — also rewarded
4. **Intend good** — rewarded even without action

No one is excluded from dawah. Even the person who cannot do anything can INTEND good — and that intention is rewarded. The gate is wide open.

### 4. Dawah Through Love

In Faith and Life (Chapter 3), Qaradawi writes: "Islam is the greatest call for love and the best confirmation of its meanings. It is also the strongest fighter against hatred, animosity, and envy."

He cites the Prophet: "By Him in Whose Hand is my soul, you will not enter Paradise unless you truly believe, and you will not truly believe unless you love one another."

And: "Three qualities, whoever possesses them finds the joy of faith: to have Allah and His Prophet dearer to him than anything else, **to love others only for Allah's sake**, and to hate reverting to disbelief as much as one hates to be thrown into the fire."

Dawah through love means: we don't invite people to Islam by argument — we invite them by love. When people see that Muslims love each other for Allah's sake — not for money, not for status, not for advantage — they are drawn to that love. Love is the most persuasive dawah.

### 5. Tolerance Is Part of the Creed

Qaradawi writes: "Tolerance is a part of the creed." This is a remarkable statement — tolerance is not just a social virtue but part of the Islamic CREED.

The Qur'an: "Allah does not forbid you from being righteous toward those who do not fight you because of religion" (60:8). Dawah to non-Muslims includes being righteous and just toward them — regardless of their faith.

For families: this means teaching children that dawah to their non-Muslim friends, classmates, and neighbours is not through argument or pressure — it is through kindness, justice, and good character. The child who is kind to a non-Muslim classmate is giving dawah. The family that helps a non-Muslim neighbour is giving dawah. No words needed — just character.

### 6. The Family as the First Dawah Audience

The most important audience for dawah is not outsiders — it is the family itself. Parents are the first daiyah (caller to Allah) for their children. The family halaqah IS dawah — teaching, modelling, and practicing Islam within the home.

The Prophet: "Each one of you is a caretaker and is responsible for those under his care" (al-Bukhari, Muslim). The parent's dawah to their children is not optional — it is a religious obligation. And the method is not lecturing — it is modelling. Children learn Islam by SEEING it, not just hearing it.

The 30-week halaqah course this family has been completing IS dawah — each module, each discussion, each activity is a call to Allah. The family that completes this course together has given dawah to each other for 30 weeks.

### 7. The Best of People Benefit Others

The Prophet: "The best of people are those who are most beneficial to people."

Qaradawi connects this to the broader Islamic principle: the measure of a person's worth is not their wealth, their status, or their knowledge — it is their BENEFIT to others. The best Muslim is the one who helps the most people.

For families: this reorients the family's purpose. The family is not just for itself — it is for the community. The family that isolates itself, that doesn't help neighbours, that doesn't contribute to society — is not fulfilling the Islamic purpose. The family that is a source of benefit — through charity, through kindness, through service, through example — is the "best nation produced for mankind" (3:110).

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — His Character Was the Qur'an

> Aisha said: "His character was the Qur'an." People saw Islam in the Prophet before they heard it from him. What do people see when they look at our family? Do they see Islam?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a reflection question. When neighbours see your family — at the grocery store, at school, at community events — what do they see? Do they see kindness? Patience? Cleanliness? Joy? Or do they see arguing, rudeness, and stress? Character is the loudest dawah — and bad character is the loudest anti-dawah.

### Question 2 — The Dawah Multiplier

> The Prophet said: "Whoever shows the way to good has the same reward as the one who does it." If you teach your friend to pray, you get the same reward as their prayer — every time they pray. What does this mean for our family?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the power of dawah — one act of teaching produces a lifetime of shared reward. For children: "If you teach your friend to say Bismillah before eating, you get the same reward every time they say it!" For adults: "If you teach someone to give charity, you share their reward every time they give." Dawah is the best investment — it keeps producing reward long after you're gone.

### Question 3 — Dawah Through Love

> The Prophet said: "You will not enter Paradise unless you believe, and you will not believe unless you love one another." How does love function as dawah? Can love alone bring people to Islam?

**Facilitator guidance:** Love doesn't replace teaching — but it makes teaching believable. People will not listen to your words if your character contradicts them. But when they see genuine love — love for Allah, love for family, love for neighbours, love for humanity — they are drawn to the source of that love. The Prophet attracted people through his love before he taught them his message.

### Question 4 — Our Family's Dawah

> What is one way our family can give better dawah — to our neighbours, our community, and each other? Through character? Through service? Through kindness?

**Facilitator guidance:** Practical ideas: invite neighbours for a meal, help a neighbour with a task, volunteer at a community event, donate to a local charity, be the family that is known for kindness. For children: be the child who helps classmates, who is kind to the new student, who doesn't gossip. "The best of people are those who are most beneficial to people."

### Question 5 — The Family as First Dawah

> The most important dawah is not to outsiders — it is to our own family. Parents are the first daiyah for their children. How has this halaqah course been a form of dawah for our family?

**Facilitator guidance:** Reflect on the 30-week journey. Each module has been dawah — teaching faith, character, family, daily life, and now trust and hope. The family that has completed this course together has been giving dawah to each other for 30 weeks. This IS dawah — the most important kind.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Dawah Plan"

Create a simple plan for how the family will be a source of good in the community.

**Step 1:** Identify 3 ways the family can benefit others:

| Who | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| Neighbours | [invite for a meal / help with a task] | [this month] |
| Community | [volunteer / donate / participate] | [this quarter] |
| Extended family | [call / visit / help] | [this week] |
| School/workplace | [kindness / help / good example] | [daily] |

**Step 2:** Each family member commits to ONE act of dawah through character this week:
- "I will be kind to [person] who I usually ignore"
- "I will help [person] with [task]"
- "I will smile at [person] who I usually avoid"
- "I will invite [neighbour] for a meal"

**Step 3:** At the next halaqah, share what happened. Discuss:
- How did people respond?
- Did they notice the kindness?
- How does this connect to "the best of people are those who benefit others"?

**Goal:** To make dawah a lived family practice — not through lectures and arguments, but through character, kindness, and service. The family that benefits others is the "best nation produced for mankind" (3:110).
