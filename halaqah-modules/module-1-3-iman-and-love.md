# Module 1.3 — Iman and Love

**Phase:** 1 — Roots: Faith as the Foundation
**Week:** 3
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that love — for Allah, for family, for people, for nature, and for life itself — is a fruit of faith (iman), not separate from it. The session establishes the Prophetic principle that true belief is impossible without love for one another, and that the believer's heart is free from envy, hatred, and rancor.

---

## Opening (3 min)

Begin with the standard opening:

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that love in its fullest, purest form is possible only for the believer — and that faith and love are inseparable.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — No Paradise Without Love

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "By Him in Whose Hand is my soul, you will not enter Paradise unless you truly believe, and you will not truly believe unless you love one another."
>
> — **Narrated by Muslim** (Kitab al-Iman) on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

### Hadith 2 — The Prophet's Love for Uhud

Anas ibn Malik, the Prophet's servant, narrated:

> "Once the Prophet (peace be upon him) left for Khaybar and I accompanied him to be in his service. On his way back, (the mountain of) Uhud came into his view and he said, 'This is a mountain that loves us and that we love.'"
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** on the authority of Anas ibn Malik

### Hadith 3 — None of You Truly Believes Until...

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "None of you will have true faith till he wishes for his (Muslim) brother what he likes for himself."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** on the authority of Anas ibn Malik

### Hadith 4 — A Believer Supports Another Believer

Abu Musa al-Ash'ari narrated:

> "A believer to another believer is like a building whose different parts support each other." The Prophet then clasped his hands with the fingers interlaced to assert this meaning.
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** (Kitab al-Adab) and **Muslim** (Kitab al-Birr was-Silah) on the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari

### Hadith 5 — Perfect Iman

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Whoever loves for (the sake of) Allah, hates for (the sake of) Allah, gives for (the sake of) Allah, and retains for (the sake of) Allah, has been granted perfect Iman."
>
> — **Narrated by Abu Dawud** on the authority of Abu Umamah

### Hadith 6 — The Du'a for All Creatures

The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught the morning supplication:

> "O Allah! Whatever blessing that I or any of Your creatures has entered upon the morning with, is from You alone; there is no partner with You, so (all) praise and gratitude is due to You!"
>
> — **Cited in Faith and Life, Chapter 3**

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Faith and Life** (الإيمان والحياة), Chapter 3: "Iman and Love." All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Love as the Gravitational Force of Human Relations

Qaradawi opens with a magnificent comparison. Love, he says, "is the spirit of existence, the elixir of the hearts, and a source of safety for mankind."

He draws an analogy to physics: "Just as the law of gravity grasps the earth and prevents planets from colliding with one another lest they should fall down or burn and fade away, **the law of love protects human relations from clashing with one another** lest they should burn and waste away."

Love is not an optional sentiment — it is the force that holds human relationships together. Without it, relationships collide, burn, and are destroyed — just as planets would without gravity.

Qaradawi quotes an ascetic poet: "Love changes bitterness into sweetness, sand into gold, turbidity into clarity, pain into a remedy, prison into a garden, illness into a blessing, and oppression into mercy. It softens iron, melts stone, resurrects the deceased, and breathes life into it."

A journalist wrote about searching for a "guiding star" in life — is it wisdom? caution? work? property? He concluded: "Love is the only essence that grants us safety, stability, and peace... Can anyone have this kind of love? If the answer is yes, he will be an angel."

Qaradawi answers: "Only one kind of person can have this kind of love: **the believer**. Iman alone is the spring of pure eternal love."

### 2. Love for Allah

The believer's love begins with Allah. Qaradawi writes: "Whoever believes in the creed of Islam has penetrated the mystery of existence. Thus a Muslim loves Allah, the provider of life and the sole source of creation, security, origination, and provision."

This love has three dimensions:

**a) Love for beauty:** "He loves Him as man loves beauty, since he has seen in His universe signs of perfect creativity." The Qur'an: "No want of proportion will you see in the Creation of Allah Most Gracious" (67:3). The believer sees Allah's beauty reflected in creation and loves the Source of that beauty.

**b) Love for perfection:** "He loves Him as man loves perfection." All observable perfection in the universe "are only atoms which are derived from Him and in need of Him."

**c) Love for beneficence:** "He loves Him as man loves beneficence and good-doing, for it is part of the nature of the soul to love whoever does good to it." The greatest benefactor is the One "who created man, fashioned him into a human being, appointed him as His vicegerent on earth, and subjected the whole universe to his use" (2:29; 31:20).

This love exceeds all other loves: "the believer loves Allah to such an extent that exceeds man's love for his parents, his sons, and even for his own self."

The Prophet (peace be upon him) invoked: "O Allah! Please grant me Your love and the love of those who love You and make Your love dearer to me than cool water!"

### 3. Love for Nature

The believer also loves nature. Qaradawi writes: "The believer, under the shade of Islam, loves nature and the whole of existence just as he loves Allah. Nature is an effect of the Grace of his Lord."

Nature is not an enemy — it is "a creation that has been subjected to his use in order to help him accomplish his mission as Allah's vicegerent on earth." The Qur'an declares: "The seven heavens and the earth, and all beings therein, declare His glory: there is not a thing but celebrates His praise" (17:44).

Qaradawi argues against the Manichaean philosophy that sees the world as evil: "The world is not an evil that should be eliminated... Rather, it is a book of Allah that is open for both readers and non-readers where the verses of His Might, Mercy, Greatness, and Grace are recited."

The most beautiful example: the Prophet's love for the mountain of Uhud — the place where Muslims suffered defeat. Anas ibn Malik narrated: "On his way back, Uhud came into his view and he said, 'This is a mountain that loves us and that we love.'" Even the mountain that witnessed defeat is loved, because it is part of Allah's creation.

### 4. Love for Life

The Muslim loves life — but not as an end in itself. "He considers it a mission that should be fulfilled and a blessing that he should thank Allah for."

The Prophet said: "The best of people is whoever is granted a long lifetime and does good therein." And: "No one of you should wish for death before it comes. A believer's lifetime adds nothing but goodness to him."

Qaradawi clarifies: the believer loves life because through it he fulfills Allah's rights on earth — worship, vicegerency, service. But he does not cling to life out of fear of death.

### 5. Love for Death

The believer also loves death — not out of despair, but because it takes him to his Lord. Qaradawi cites:

- The Prophet: "Allah loves to meet whoever loves to meet Him."
- When given the choice between this world and the next, the Prophet said: "I choose the highest companions!"
- `Ali ibn Abi Talib, when struck by his assassin, said: "I have won, by the Lord of the Ka'bah!"
- Bilal, dying, said to his weeping wife: "You should rather say, 'How joyful I am!' Tomorrow I shall meet my beloved ones: Muhammad and his Companions!"
- Khalid ibn al-Walid wrote to Persian and Roman leaders: "I will crash you with men who love death as much as you love life!"

### 6. Love for People

Qaradawi writes: "The believer loves all people because they are servants of Allah and his brothers in humanity, as they are all sons of Adam."

The Qur'an establishes the kinship of all humanity: "O mankind! Revere your Guardian-Lord, Who created you from a single Person, created, of like nature, his mate, and from them twain scattered countless men and women" (4:1).

The variations in languages and colors "are only a proof of Allah's Might and His Greatness" (30:22) — not a basis for discrimination.

Qaradawi cites the Prophet's daily du'a after prayer: "O Allah! Our Lord and the Lord of all things! I bear witness that all servants are brothers."

The Prophet also said: "None of you will have true faith till he wishes for his brother what he likes for himself." And Abu Musa's hadith: "A believer to another believer is like a building whose different parts support each other" — the Prophet interlaced his fingers to demonstrate.

### 7. The Believer Does Not Envy

"The minimal fruit of affection that Iman inculcates in the heart of the believer, is that it protects him from hatred and envy."

Qaradawi explains: "The believer does not envy because he wishes goodness for all the servants of Allah and does not oppose his Lord in His disposition of the affairs of His creatures or distribution of His provisions for them."

He quotes al-Hasan al-Basri: "O son of Adam! Why should you envy your brother? If what he possesses has been given to him because Allah wanted to honor him, then why should you envy one whom Allah has honored? And if it is not so, why should you envy one whose destiny is the Fire?"

And Ibn Sirin: "I have never envied anyone because of any worldly matter. If he is one of the residents of Paradise, how should I envy him for anything pertaining to this worldly life, which is valueless if compared to Paradise, and if he is one of the residents of the Fire, how should I envy him for any worldly matter while his destiny is the Fire?"

The Prophet said: "There is no envy except in two: a man whom Allah has given wealth and he spends it in the right way, and a man whom Allah has given wisdom and he judges according to it and teaches it." This is not destructive envy (hasad) but constructive admiration (ghibtah) — the desire to achieve similar good.

### 8. Altruism (Ithar) — The Highest Degree of Love

Qaradawi presents altruism — giving preference to others over oneself — as the highest expression of love: "The highest degree of love is that man gives preference to his brother over his own self by giving him something that he himself needs. He may remain hungry to satisfy his brother, work hard to make him rest, and stay awake at night to let him sleep."

He cites the historical example of the Muhajirun and Ansar in Madinah. `Abd ar-Rahman ibn `Awf narrated: "When we came to Madinah, the Prophet joined me and Sa'd ibn ar-Rabi' — one of the Ansar — as brothers. Then Sa'd said to me, 'I am one of the most wealthy men among the Ansar so I may give you half my wealth, and I will also divorce any of my two wives whom you like, and when she finishes her waiting period you can marry her.'" `Abd ar-Rahman responded with modesty: "May Allah bless your wives and wealth for you, but just guide me to the market."

Allah praised this in the Qur'an: "They give them preference over themselves, even though poverty was their own lot" (59:9).

Qaradawi cites Dr. Muhammad `Abdullah Draz: "Religion to the community is like the heart to the body." The spiritual unity created by faith "continues without any of the other forms of unity and creates a very strong and close relationship between individuals, although they may have different races, genders, dialects, nationalities, and interests."

### 9. Tolerance as Part of the Creed

Qaradawi closes with a remarkable section on tolerance. The believer's hatred is restricted — not directed at people, but at Satan and falsehood. Even this hatred "is mixed with pain and pity for them, wishing them good, and invoking Allah to guide them."

Two principles make the Muslim tolerant:

**a) Divine Will includes difference:** "If your Lord had so willed, He could have made mankind one people: but they will not cease to dispute, except those on whom your Lord has bestowed His Mercy" (11:118-119). The believer accepts that diversity of belief is part of Allah's wisdom — he cannot resist Allah's Will.

**b) Judgment belongs to Allah:** "Allah will judge between you on the Day of Judgement concerning the matters in which you differ" (22:68-69). The believer's role is to call, not to compel: "Allah is our Lord and your Lord: for us is the responsibility for our deeds, and for you for your deeds. There is no contention between us and you" (42:15).

Qaradawi's concluding summary: "This is the believer according to the creed of Islam: he loves the whole of existence, loves Allah and nature, loves life and death, loves destiny whether bitter or sweet, and loves all people. If he is to hate, he hates Satan and his party, yet his hatred is joined with mercy, pity, and wishing goodness for all people."

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — What Is Love?

> Who do you love most? How does your faith connect to that love? Is loving someone a feeling, a choice, or both?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a warm opening question. Let each person answer honestly — children will say parents and siblings; adults may say spouse, children, Allah. The key teaching: in Islam, all love flows from iman. The believer loves Allah first, and from that love flows love for everything else.

**Qaradawi's framework:** "Iman alone is the spring of pure eternal love and the believer alone is the one who can love everything, even catastrophes."

### Question 2 — No Paradise Without Love

> The Prophet said: "You will not enter Paradise unless you truly believe, and you will not truly believe unless you love one another." What does that mean for our family? Can faith exist without love?

**Facilitator guidance:** This hadith makes love a condition of faith — not an optional add-on. If our family has prayer, fasting, and Qur'an recitation but lacks love, something is fundamentally incomplete. Ask: "If a stranger watched our family for a day, would they see love?"

### Question 3 — Love Like Gravity

> Qaradawi compares love to gravity — the force that keeps planets from crashing into each other. What happens to relationships when love is missing? What happens to our family when we forget to love each other?

**Facilitator guidance:** This analogy is accessible to all ages. Without gravity, planets would collide and be destroyed. Without love, family members "clash with one another lest they should burn and waste away." The practical question: when was the last time our family "collided"? Could love have prevented it?

### Question 4 — Can You Love Someone You Envy?

> The believer does not envy. Al-Hasan al-Basri said: "Why should you envy your brother? If Allah honored him, why envy one whom Allah has honored?" How does envy destroy love?

**Facilitator guidance:** Envy (hasad) is the opposite of love. The envious person wants what the other has AND wants them to lose it. The believer, Qaradawi says, wishes "goodness for all the servants of Allah." Ask: "Have you ever felt jealous of someone in our family? How did it feel? What would help us replace jealousy with happiness for each other?"

### Question 5 — The Ansar and Altruism

> Sa'd offered `Abd ar-Rahman half his wealth and one of his wives. `Abd ar-Rahman said, "Just guide me to the market." What does this story teach us about love in the family? How can we put each other first?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the highest example of love — giving what you need to someone else. In the family, altruism looks like: letting someone else choose the movie, giving the last piece of food, doing a chore so someone else can rest, listening when you're tired. It does NOT mean the extreme of the Ansar — but it means the spirit of the Ansar.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Love Charter"

Together, write a **family love charter** — 3 commitments about how family members will treat each other this week. Each commitment should be concrete and specific.

**Structure:**
- "In our family, we will..."
- "In our family, we will not..."
- "In our family, when we disagree, we will..."

**Examples:**
- "In our family, we will speak kindly to each other, even when we are angry."
- "In our family, we will not use hurtful names or bring up past mistakes."
- "In our family, when we disagree, we will listen first and speak second, and we will remember that Allah loves those who reconcile."

Write the charter on paper, have everyone sign it, and post it on the refrigerator or family board. Review it at next week's halaqah.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of writing, have children draw a picture of the family doing something loving together. Frame it as "This is what love looks like in our family."

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-03.txt`
- The chapter is approximately 28 pages. Key sections: "Love for Allah" (line 131), "Love for people" (line 387), "The believer does not envy" (line 542), "Altruism" (line 683), "Tolerance" (line 860).

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
Qaradawi makes a crucial point about the SOURCE of love. Love is not a human invention — it is a FRUIT of iman. "Iman alone is the spring of pure eternal love and the believer alone is the one who can love everything, even catastrophes."

This means:
- Without faith, love is incomplete — it can be selfish, conditional, or temporary
- With faith, love becomes universal, unconditional, and eternal
- The believer loves even trials because "they awaken the ability of resistance"
- The believer loves even enemies in a sense — his hatred is "mixed with pain and pity for them"

### The Gravity Analogy
This is the most teachable concept in the chapter. Love = gravity. Without gravity, planets crash. Without love, relationships crash. Every family conflict is a "gravity failure" — love was not strong enough to hold the relationship together when pressure came.

### The Story of Sa'd and `Abd ar-Rahman
This story is the most powerful example of altruism in Islamic history. Sa'd offered:
1. Half his wealth
2. One of his two wives (to divorce her so `Abd ar-Rahman could marry her)

`Abd ar-Rahman's response — "May Allah bless your wives and wealth for you, but just guide me to the market" — shows that love does not require taking everything offered. It requires APPRECIATING the offer and responding with dignity.

**For children:** Simplify — "Sa'd loved `Abd ar-Rahman so much he offered to give him half of everything he owned. `Abd ar-Rahman said thank you but just showed him where to work. Both of them were being loving — Sa'd by offering, `Abd ar-Rahman by not taking too much."

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "love for Allah" and "love for family." Use the gravity analogy simply: "Love is like an invisible glue that holds our family together." Skip the envy and tolerance sections. |
| 7–12 | Tell the story of Sa'd and `Abd ar-Rahman as a friendship story. Ask: "Would you give half your toys to a friend who had none?" Tell the Uhud story: "The Prophet loved a mountain!" |
| 13–17 | Engage with the envy discussion: "Social media shows everyone's best moments. How can we feel happy for others instead of jealous?" Connect tolerance to living in a diverse society. |
