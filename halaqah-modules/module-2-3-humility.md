# Module 2.3 — Humility and Honor

**Phase:** 2 — Trunk: Character and Inner Purification
**Week:** 9
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that humility is strength, not weakness, and that it coexists with honor. The session establishes the Prophetic model: the Prophet cleaned his own shoes and helped at home, yet stood before kings with dignity. Humility means doing away with pride and arrogance — the traits that lead to Hell — while maintaining the honor that faith confers.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that true strength is humble, and that the Prophet — the greatest of men — cleaned his own shoes, mended his clothes, and helped at home. We learn to be humble with each other and honorable before the world.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Humility" (pp. 335-336). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. What Is Humility?

Qaradawi defines humility as "doing away with one's pride and arrogance, which are considered evil traits that lead to hell." Humility is the opposite of *kibr* (arrogance) — the quality that caused Iblis to refuse Allah's command to prostrate before Adam.

The Qur'an warns repeatedly against pride and arrogance:
- "Is there not in Hell a residence for the arrogant?" (39:60)
- "Thus Allah seals over the hearts of the arrogant and the tyrants" (40:35)
- "I will turn away from My signs those who are arrogant upon the earth without right" (7:146)
- "Do not walk upon the earth exultantly. Indeed, you will never tear the earth apart, and you will never reach the mountains in height" (17:37)
- "Do not turn your cheek in contempt toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like the self-deluded and the boastful" (31:18)

The Prophet said: "He who has in his heart the weight of a mustard seed of pride shall not enter Paradise" (Muslim, via Ibn Mas'ud). A mustard seed — one of the smallest seeds known — is the threshold. Even a tiny amount of pride blocks Paradise.

### 2. The Prophet as Role Model of Humility

Qaradawi presents the Prophet as the ultimate model of humility:

> "The Prophet (PBUH) was a role model of humbleness. He was a man with considerable social power, but he cleaned his shoes, mended his clothes, and milked his sheep himself. He helped his family and servants with their chores."

This is extraordinary: a man who was the leader of a nation, the messenger of Allah, the commander of armies — and he cleaned his own shoes. He mended his own clothes. He milked his own sheep. He helped at home. He did not consider these tasks "beneath" him.

Qaradawi adds: "When he met with his companions, nobody could tell which one the prophet was." The Prophet did not sit on a throne. He did not wear a crown. He did not dress differently from his companions. He did not demand special treatment. He sat among his people as one of them.

When a man came to see the Prophet and began shaking with awe, the Prophet said kindly: **"Let your heart be at ease. I am not a king, but a man whose mother ate dried meat."** This single sentence captures the Prophet's humility: he identified himself not as a ruler but as an ordinary human being — the son of a mother who ate simple food.

### 3. Humility and Honor Are Not Opposites

One of Qaradawi's most important arguments: **humility and honor are not contradictions.** They coexist in the believer.

The Qur'an describes the believers as "humble toward the believers, powerful against the disbelievers" (5:54). The same person is BOTH humble AND powerful — the humility is directed toward fellow believers, the strength toward external threats.

Allah says: "Honor belongs to Allah and His Messenger, and to the believers" (63:8). The believer has honor — but this honor comes from faith, not from arrogance. The believer does not seek honor through power over others, but through nearness to Allah.

Qaradawi clarifies: "Honor here should not be confused with pride, or power either. Allah says: 'Whoever desires honor [through power] — then to Allah belongs all honor' (35:10)."

The distinction:
| Arrogance (Kibr) | Honor (Izzah) |
|---|---|
| Looking down on others | Knowing your worth comes from Allah |
| Demanding special treatment | Serving others with dignity |
| "I am better than you" | "I am a servant of Allah" |
| Leads to Hell | Granted by Allah to believers |
| The trait of Iblis | The trait of the Prophet |

### 4. Humility Toward Parents

Qaradawi notes that the Qur'an specifically mentions humility in the context of parents: "And lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy" (17:24). The metaphor of "lowering the wing" comes from a bird protecting its young — the parent bird lowers its wing to shelter and care for its chicks. This is the attitude a believer should have toward aging parents: protective, gentle, humble.

This is the ONLY place in the Qur'an where the specific word for humility (*dhull*) is used in a positive sense — and it is used for parents. All other uses of humility are toward Allah. This elevates the treatment of parents to a uniquely honored position.

### 5. Humility Does Not Mean Accepting Humiliation

Qaradawi makes a crucial clarification: humility does not mean accepting humiliation or oppression.

The Qur'an says: "Indeed, those whom the angels take [in death] while wronging themselves — [the angels] will say: 'In what [condition] were you?' They will say: 'We were oppressed in the land.' The angels will say: 'Was not the earth of Allah spacious [enough] for you to emigrate?'" (4:97).

Allah rejects those who passively accept oppression — EXCEPT those who genuinely cannot resist: "Except for the oppressed among men, women, and children who cannot devise a plan nor are they directed to a way — for those, Allah may pardon them" (4:98-99).

The principle: humility before fellow believers and before Allah is virtuous. Accepting humiliation from oppressors without any ability to resist is excused. But passively accepting oppression when you CAN resist is condemned. Humility is not self-degradation.

### 6. Humility in the Family

The Prophetic model has direct family applications:

**For parents:** the Prophet cleaned his own shoes and helped at home. A father who does household chores is not "unmanly" — he is following the Sunnah. A mother who serves her family is not "subservient" — she is modeling the Prophet's humility. When parents do not demand special treatment from their children but serve alongside them, they teach humility by example.

**For children:** "Allah has revealed to me that you should humble yourselves to one another. One should not hold himself above another" (Muslim, via Iyad ibn Hammam). Siblings who look down on each other — the older thinking they're better than the younger, the stronger thinking they're better than the weaker — are violating this command.

**For spouses:** humility means not considering yourself "above" serving your spouse. The Prophet helped his family. A husband who won't help at home because "it's not a man's job" is claiming to be above the Prophet himself.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Humble vs. Low Self-Esteem

> What is the difference between being humble and having low self-esteem? Can you be humble and still confident?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the key question. The answer: humility is knowing that your worth comes from Allah, not from yourself. Low self-esteem is thinking you have no worth. The Prophet was the most humble person AND the most confident — because his confidence was in Allah, not in himself.

**The formula:** Arrogance = "I am great (by myself)." Humility = "Allah made me great, so I serve." Low self-esteem = "I am worthless." The believer rejects both arrogance and self-deprecation.

### Question 2 — The Prophet Cleaned His Own Shoes

> The Prophet — the leader of a nation, the messenger of Allah — cleaned his own shoes, mended his clothes, and helped at home. What does that tell us about leadership in a family?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the most practical question in the module. If the Prophet — who had more "reason" to be proud than anyone — cleaned his own shoes and helped his family, then no family member is "above" any task. The father who won't wash dishes, the teenager who won't clean their room, the mother who won't accept help — all are placing themselves above the Prophet's example.

### Question 3 — Can You Be Humble and Still Stand Up for Yourself?

> The Qur'an says believers are "humble toward believers, powerful against disbelievers." Can you be humble and still defend yourself? How?

**Facilitator guidance:** Yes — the Prophet was both. Humility is about HOW you treat people, not about whether you defend yourself. You can be humble (not looking down on others) AND assertive (standing up for your rights). The difference: the arrogant person looks down on others while defending themselves. The humble person defends themselves while respecting others.

### Question 4 — How Can We Show Humility to Our Parents?

> The Qur'an says "lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy" — to parents. What does "lowering the wing" look like in daily life?

**Facilitator guidance:** "Lowering the wing" = the bird protecting its young. It means being gentle, protective, and caring with parents — especially as they age. Practical examples: speaking softly to parents, not interrupting them, helping them with technology, visiting them regularly, asking for their advice, not rolling eyes when they repeat stories.

### Question 5 — Nobody Could Tell Which One Was the Prophet

> When the Prophet sat with his companions, "nobody could tell which one the prophet was." In our family, do we act like we're "above" certain tasks or certain people? How can we be more like the Prophet?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a challenging question. In many families, there are unspoken hierarchies — the father who won't do "women's work," the teenager who won't play with younger siblings, the mother who won't let the father cook. The Prophet had no such hierarchy. He was indistinguishable from his companions. Ask: what would change in our family if we truly believed no one is "above" any task?

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Humble Service Week"

Each family member chooses ONE household task they normally don't do — something they consider "not my job" or "below me" — and does it quietly all week without asking for recognition or thanks.

**Examples:**
- Father: washing dishes every night
- Mother: taking out the trash
- Older sibling: playing with the younger sibling for 30 minutes daily
- Teenager: cleaning a shared space without being asked
- Child: making their bed without being told

**Rule:** Do it quietly. Do it without announcing it. Do it without expecting thanks.

**At the next halaqah:** Reveal what each person did. Discuss:
- How did it feel to do a task you normally avoid?
- Did anyone notice? Did it matter if they didn't?
- How does this relate to the Prophet cleaning his own shoes?
- What would our family be like if everyone did this permanently?

**Goal:** To experience the Prophet's humility — the man with "considerable social power" who cleaned his own shoes and helped at home. The activity makes humility physical: your hands do what your mouth says.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of choosing a task, have children "help the Prophet" — tell them: "The Prophet used to help at home. Let's be like the Prophet!" Give them a simple task (putting away toys, helping set the table) and frame it as following the Sunnah.

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important distinction: **humility ≠ low self-esteem.** These are fundamentally different:

| Humility | Low Self-Esteem |
|---|---|
| "My worth comes from Allah" | "I have no worth" |
| Confident in Allah, not in self | Not confident in anything |
| Serves others out of strength | Serves others out of fear |
| The Prophet's trait | Not the Prophet's trait |
| Leads to Paradise | Leads to passivity |

The Prophet was the most humble person AND the most confident — because his confidence was in Allah. He didn't need to prove himself to anyone, so he could afford to be humble. Arrogance is actually a sign of insecurity — the arrogant person needs to put others down to feel big. The humble person is already secure in Allah's estimation.

### The Prophet's Household Example
This is the most practically applicable teaching in the module. The Prophet:
- Cleaned his own shoes
- Mended his own clothes
- Milked his sheep
- Helped his family and servants with chores
- Was indistinguishable from his companions

If the Prophet did all this, then:
- No father is "above" washing dishes
- No mother is "above" accepting help
- No teenager is "above" playing with younger siblings
- No child is "above" cleaning their room
- No one is "above" any task that serves the family

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "the Prophet cleaned his own shoes — can you clean your own room?" Use the "Humble Service Week" activity with simple tasks. |
| 7–12 | Tell the story of the man who shook before the Prophet: "The Prophet said 'I am not a king, but a man whose mother ate dried meat.' What does that mean?" Ask: "Are there things you think you're 'too good' for?" |
