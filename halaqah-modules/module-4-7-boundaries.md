# Module 4.7 — Protecting the Family: Boundaries and Halal/Haram

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 25
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that Islam sets boundaries to protect the family and that these boundaries come from Allah's wisdom. The session establishes the three approaches to the sexual drive, the prohibition of khalwah (seclusion), the command to lower the gaze for both men and women, and the principle that chastity means lawful enjoyment — not celibacy.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that Allah's boundaries are not restrictions — they are protections. The limits Allah sets around the family are like walls around a garden: they keep what is precious safe.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam** (الحلال والحرام), Chapter 3: "The Physical Appetites" and "Khalwah" (pp. 168-174). All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Three Approaches to the Sexual Drive

Qaradawi opens with the same framework presented in Module 3.1 — the three historical approaches:

1. **Free sex** — "satisfy one's sexual need freely with whomever is available, without any restraints." This "would result in the destruction of the family structure and of all society."

2. **Suppression** — "suppress and try to annihilate the sexual drive." This is "contrary to Allah's plan and purpose" and "in conflict with the course of the natural order."

3. **Regulation** — "regulate the satisfaction of this urge, allowing it to operate within certain limits, neither suppressing nor giving it free rein." **This is the stand of the revealed religions, and in particular Islam.**

Islam's position: the sexual drive is from Allah — it is natural and good. But like all drives, it must be channeled through lawful marriage. Islam does not suppress desire — it REGULATES it. The regulation protects the family, preserves lineage, and prevents social chaos.

### 2. Do Not Come NEAR Zina

The Qur'an's command is remarkable in its precision: "And do not come near zina" (17:32). Not "do not commit zina" — but "do not come NEAR zina."

Qaradawi writes: "When Islam prohibits something, it closes all the avenues to it. This is achieved by prohibiting every step and every means leading to the haram. Accordingly, whatever excites passions, opens ways for illicit sexual relations between a man and a woman, and promotes indecency and obscenity, is haram."

The principle: Islam does not wait for the sin — it blocks every path that leads to the sin. This is called *sadd al-dhara'i* — "blocking the means." The goal is not to make life difficult but to make sin difficult.

### 3. Khalwah: The Prohibition of Seclusion

Qaradawi presents the prohibition of khalwah — a man and a non-mahram woman being alone together:

The Prophet: "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day must never be in privacy with a woman without there being a mahram of hers with her, for otherwise Satan will be the third person."

Qaradawi explains: "The reason for this is not a lack of trust in one or both of them; it is rather to protect them from wrong thoughts and sexual feelings which naturally arise within a man and a woman when they are alone together without the fear of intrusion by a third person."

This is not about suspicion — it is about wisdom. Human attraction is natural. Seclusion creates vulnerability. The prohibition protects BOTH the man and the woman from situations that could lead to compromised judgment.

**Special warning about in-laws:** "The in-law is death." Qaradawi explains: relatives of the husband (brother, cousin, nephew) have easier access to the wife's quarters — "something concerning which no one would question him." This easy access creates vulnerability that outsiders don't have. The Prophet's warning is especially strong because people are "quite negligent in this regard, sometimes with disastrous consequences."

### 4. Lowering the Gaze — Both Men and Women

Qaradawi notes that the Qur'an commands BOTH men AND women to lower their gazes (24:30-31). This is not a command only for women — it is equally for men.

"'Lowering the gazes' does not mean that in the presence of the opposite sex the eyes should be shut or that the head should be bowed toward the ground... Here 'lowering the gazes' means to avert one's gaze from the faces of the passers-by and not to caress the attractive features of the members of the opposite sex with one's eyes."

The Prophet to Ali: "Do not let a second look follow the first. The first look is allowed to you but not the second." The first look is accidental — it happens before you can control it. The second look is a CHOICE — it is deliberate, and therefore accountable.

The Prophet: "The eyes also commit zina, and their zina is the lustful look." The lustful look is "zina of the eye" because it "gives sexual pleasure and gratification in an unlawful way."

### 5. Chastity (Iffah) — Not Celibacy

In Ethics in Islam (section 4.2), Qaradawi defines chastity (*iffah*):

"Chastity also involves refraining from all unlawful sexual acts, which range from lustful looks to committing adultery."

But critically: "It is important to note that chastity does not mean celibacy, which is a form of monasticism that Islam rejects. Islam prohibits unlawful sexual relations only and celebrates lawful ones."

The believers are those who "safeguard their private parts — except from their wives" (23:5-7). Chastity is NOT abstinence — it is regulation. The chaste person enjoys intimacy WITHIN marriage and abstains OUTSIDE marriage. Islam "celebrates lawful" relations — marriage is the halal channel, and what is within it is not merely permitted but blessed.

### 6. Protection in the Digital Age

While Qaradawi's text predates the internet, his principles apply directly:

| Prophetic Principle | Digital Application |
|---|---|
| "Do not come near zina" | Avoid websites, apps, and content that lead to haram |
| "Satan is the third" | Online chat with non-mahram in private = digital khalwah |
| "Do not give a second look" | When inappropriate content appears, look away immediately |
| "Lower the gaze" | Do not scroll through content that provokes lust |
| "The in-law is death" | Be cautious with private messaging, even with acquaintances |
| "The eyes commit zina" | Watching inappropriate content = zina of the eye |

The principle is the same: Islam blocks every avenue that leads to sin. In the digital age, the avenues are more numerous — but the principle is unchanged.

### 7. Boundaries Are Protection, Not Restriction

Qaradawi's overall framework: Allah's boundaries are like the walls of a garden. The garden is the family — precious, beautiful, worth protecting. The walls (boundaries) are not there to imprison the family but to keep it safe.

| Without Boundaries | With Boundaries |
|---|---|
| Free access to haram content | Filters, supervision, self-regulation |
| Unrestricted interaction with non-mahram | Modesty, lowering the gaze, no khalwah |
| No protection from temptation | Prophetic guidelines that block the paths to sin |
| Family vulnerable to external threats | Family protected by Allah's commands |
| "Freedom" that leads to destruction | Boundaries that preserve what is precious |

The believer does not see Allah's boundaries as burdens — they see them as protections. "And do not come near zina" is not a restriction on freedom — it is a protection for the family.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Do Not Come NEAR

> The Qur'an says "do not come NEAR zina" — not just "do not commit zina." What's the difference? Why does Islam block every path that leads to sin?

**Facilitator guidance:** The difference is proactive prevention vs. reactive prohibition. Islam doesn't wait for the sin — it blocks the path TO the sin. This is wisdom: it is easier to avoid the path than to resist the temptation once you're on it. For families: this means not putting ourselves in situations where temptation is strong.

### Question 2 — Satan Is the Third

> The Prophet said: "Satan is the third person" when a man and woman are alone together. How does this apply in the age of private messaging and online chat?

**Facilitator guidance:** Digital khalwah = private chat with a non-mahram person of the opposite sex. The same principle applies: seclusion (even digital) creates vulnerability. The solution: keep conversations public, involve others, maintain professional boundaries online. For teenagers: "Private DMs with someone you're not related to — is that 'Satan being the third'?"

### Question 3 — Do Not Give a Second Look

> The Prophet told Ali: "The first look is allowed, the second is not." How does this apply to scrolling on phones? What is the "first look" and the "second look" online?

**Facilitator guidance:** The first look = when an inappropriate image or video appears in your feed accidentally. You scroll past it. The second look = going back to look again, or deliberately seeking similar content. The first is forgiven; the second is a choice. For families: teach children to scroll past inappropriate content immediately, without a second look.

### Question 4 — Chastity Is Not Celibacy

> Qaradawi says: "Chastity does not mean celibacy. Islam prohibits unlawful sexual relations only and celebrates lawful ones." What does this mean? How does Islam view intimacy within marriage?

**Facilitator guidance:** Islam is not anti-sex — it is anti-ZINA. Intimacy within marriage is celebrated, blessed, and a form of worship. The boundary is not between sex and no-sex — it is between lawful and unlawful. This is a balanced, healthy view that avoids both extremes: promiscuity (everything is allowed) and puritanism (nothing is allowed).

### Question 5 — Family Boundaries

> What is one boundary our family needs to strengthen? Phone rules? Internet filtering? Modesty at home? Interaction with non-mahram?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let the family discuss concrete boundaries. Common areas: phone-free zones or times, internet filtering, rules about private messaging, modesty within the home (especially regarding non-mahram family members or guests), rules about who enters which rooms. Agree on ONE new boundary or ONE existing boundary to strengthen.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Media Agreement"

Discuss and agree on 3 family rules for phones, internet, and social interactions. Write them down. Everyone signs.

**Example rules:**
1. "No phones in bedrooms after [time] — devices charge in the living room"
2. "No private messaging with non-mahram people without family awareness"
3. "When inappropriate content appears, we scroll past immediately — no second look"
4. "We use internet filters on all family devices"
5. "We don't share private family photos or details on social media"
6. "When guests visit, we maintain modest dress and behavior"

**Process:**
1. Brainstorm rules together — let children suggest rules too
2. Agree on 3-5 final rules
3. Write them on paper, everyone signs
4. Post in a visible family space
5. Review monthly

**Goal:** To make the family's boundaries explicit and shared. The Prophetic principle: "Do not come near zina" — block the paths before the temptation arrives. A family media agreement is the contemporary application of this principle.

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-03.txt` (lines 1-200, "The Physical Appetites" and "Khalwah" sections)
- Read Ethics in Islam section 4.2 (the "Chastity" subsection, approximately lines 347-400)

### Key Distinction
Boundaries = protection, not restriction. The Qur'an says "do not come near zina" — not to imprison the family but to protect it. The family that sees boundaries as a burden will resist them. The family that sees boundaries as protection will embrace them.

### Sensitivity Note
This module deals with sensitive topics (sexual ethics, digital content, private messaging). The facilitator should adapt the language to the family's ages and cultural context. For families with young children, focus on modesty and screen-time boundaries rather than the detailed khalwah discussion. For families with teenagers, the digital applications are essential.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "Allah wants us to be safe." Teach basic modesty. Skip the khalwah and zina discussions entirely. |
| 7–12 | "Do not give a second look" — teach children to look away when they see something inappropriate on a screen. "Lowering the gaze" applies to everyone. |
| 13–17 | The full module — digital khalwah, private messaging, screen content, the "second look" principle. The media agreement is essential. |
| Adults | All sections. The boundary-as-protection framing. The family media agreement. Discussion of how to model boundaries for children. |

### If Time Is Short
Reduce to sections 2 (do not come near), 4 (lowering the gaze), and 7 (boundaries as protection). Keep Questions 1 and 5. The media agreement is essential.
