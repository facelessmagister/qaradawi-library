# Module 5.5 — The Hereafter: Living with Eternal Vision

**Phase:** 5 — Fruit: Trust, Hope & Eternal Vision
**Week:** 30
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that living with awareness of the Hereafter transforms every decision — "work for this world as if you will live forever; work for the Hereafter as if you will die tomorrow." The session completes the 30-week course by connecting all five phases to the eternal perspective, and closes with a family commitment to continue the halaqah.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family for the final module of our 30-week halaqah course. We learn that everything we have studied — faith, character, family, daily life, trust — leads to one destination: the meeting with Allah. Living with eternal vision transforms how we see everything.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.1: "Reverence" and "Fear of Allah" (pp. 319-324); **Faith and Life** (الإيمان والحياة), Chapter 4: "Iman and Hope" (the Hereafter as the believer's destination); **Approaching the Sunnah** (كيف نتفهم السنة), Chapter 1 (the balance of this world and the Hereafter). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Reverence (Taqwa) — The Best Provision

Qaradawi writes: "All the moral values we have discussed thus far can be combined in revering Allah, which is a sense of awe and devotion."

The Qur'an: "Take provisions, but indeed, **the best provision is reverence**" (2:197). Life is a journey — and the best provision for that journey is taqwa (reverence, God-consciousness).

Qaradawi: "Revering Allah is not about words that people can utter or rituals they can perform. It is all about the heart being at peace with itself and Allah." The Prophet: "Allah does not look at your figures, or at your attire, but He looks at your hearts." He pointed to his chest three times: "Reverence is here."

The rewards of reverence:
- "Whoever reveres Allah — He will grant him a criterion" (8:29) — the ability to distinguish right from wrong
- "He will make for him a way out and provide for him from where he does not expect" (65:2-3)
- "He will make for him of his matter ease" (65:4)
- "He will remove for him his misdeeds and make great for him his reward" (65:5)

### 2. The Balance of This World and the Hereafter

In Approaching the Sunnah (Chapter 1), Qaradawi presents the wasatiyyah between this world and the next:

**The extreme of excess (ifrāṭ):** Monastic withdrawal — rejecting the world entirely, living in seclusion, treating the body as an enemy.

**The extreme of negligence (tafrīṭ):** Worldly absorption — living only for this world, forgetting the Hereafter, treating pleasure as the only goal.

**The wasatiyyah:** "Work for this world as if you will live forever; work for the Hereafter as if you will die tomorrow."

This single principle balances both:
- **Work for this world AS IF you will live forever** — plant trees, build institutions, raise children, contribute to society, plan for the future. Don't abandon the world.
- **Work for the Hereafter AS IF you will die tomorrow** — pray, fast, give charity, repent, prepare your soul. Don't delay what must be done NOW.

The believer holds both: they are active in this world AND prepared for the next. They don't escape the world (monasticism) and they don't drown in it (materialism). They live in it — fully, actively, responsibly — while keeping their eyes on the destination.

### 3. The Four Questions

Qaradawi cites the hadith: "One's feet will not move on the Day of Resurrection before he is asked about:
- **His life** — how he spent it
- **His youth** — how he wore it out
- **His wealth** — how he earned it and how he spent it
- **His knowledge** — how he used it"

These four questions are the framework for the entire 30-week halaqah:

| Question | Phase That Addressed It |
|---|---|
| How did you spend your LIFE? | Phase 4.6 — Wise Use of Time; Phase 5.1 — Surrender and Trust |
| How did you use your YOUTH? | Phase 1 — Roots of Faith; Phase 4.1 — Sabr; "five before five" |
| How did you earn and spend your WEALTH? | Phase 2.5 — Contentment; Phase 3.3 — Simplifying Weddings; Phase 5.2 — Justice and Charity |
| How did you use your KNOWLEDGE? | Phase 3.6 — Prophetic Pedagogy; Phase 5.4 — Dawah Through Character |

The 30-week course has been preparation for these four questions. Every module has been building the answers.

### 4. Remember the Destroyer of Pleasures

The Prophet: "Frequently remember the destroyer of pleasures" — death.

This is not morbid — it is clarifying. Remembering death does not make life gloomy; it makes life PURPOSEFUL. When you know that life is finite, you:
- Don't waste time on meaningless things
- Prioritize what matters
- Treat each day as a gift
- Forgive quickly (you may not have another chance)
- Give charity now (you can't give from the grave)
- Pray now (you can't pray after death)
- Say "I love you" to your family (you may not have tomorrow)

Qaradawi: "Be in this world as if you are a stranger or a traveler passing through." The traveler doesn't invest in decorating the hotel room — they prepare for the destination. The believer doesn't over-invest in this world — they prepare for the Hereafter.

### 5. Whoever Loves to Meet Allah

The Prophet: "Whoever loves to meet Allah, Allah loves to meet him."

Qaradawi treats this as the ultimate expression of faith. The believer does not fear death as an end — they see it as a MEETING. Death is not the loss of life — it is the beginning of the real life, the meeting with the Beloved.

The believer who has:
- Faith in Allah (Phase 1)
- Good character (Phase 2)
- A strong family (Phase 3)
- Daily Islamic practices (Phase 4)
- Trust, hope, and eternal vision (Phase 5)

...does not fear the meeting. They look forward to it. They have prepared for it. They have lived for it. "Whoever loves to meet Allah, Allah loves to meet him."

### 6. The 30-Week Journey — The Tree Complete

The tree metaphor is now complete:

| Phase | Metaphor | What Was Planted |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Roots | Faith — iman, taqwa, tawakkul, love, hope, dignity |
| Phase 2 | Trunk | Character — akhlaq, modesty, humility, truthfulness, contentment, tazkiyah |
| Phase 3 | Branches | Family — marriage, affection, simplicity, parents, children, pedagogy |
| Phase 4 | Leaves | Daily Life — sabr, wasatiyyah, joy, beauty, cleanliness, time, boundaries |

The roots feed the trunk. The trunk supports the branches. The branches hold the leaves. The leaves produce the fruit. And the fruit contains the seeds — which will plant new trees.

### 7. The Seed: Continuing the Halaqah

The 30-week course is complete — but the journey is not over. The fruit contains seeds. The seeds are the family's commitment to continue practicing what they have learned.

The halaqah should not end here. It should continue — in a new form:
- **Weekly family discussion** — review one module per week
- **Daily family practices** — the du'as, the self-review (muhasabah), the sabr tracker
- **Monthly family activities** — the justice audit, the simplicity plan, the joy night
- **Annual review** — revisit the course each year as a family

The Prophet: "Convey from me, even if it is one verse." The family that has completed this course now has the knowledge and the tools to teach others — to plant new trees in their extended family, their community, and their neighbourhood.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — The Four Questions

> On the Day of Judgment, you will be asked: how did you spend your life, your youth, your wealth, and your knowledge? After 30 weeks of halaqah, how would you answer these questions today? What would you change?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the culminating reflection. Let each family member reflect on all four questions in light of the 30-week course. The course has been preparation for these questions. The answers should reflect what was learned: faith, character, family, daily life, and trust.

### Question 2 — Work for Both Worlds

> "Work for this world as if you will live forever; work for the Hereafter as if you will die tomorrow." How does this balance change how we live? Are we balanced — or do we lean too much toward one world?

**Facilitator guidance:** The wasatiyyah of this world and the next. Some families lean too much toward this world (career, money, comfort) and neglect the Hereafter. Some lean too much toward the Hereafter (excessive worship) and neglect family and society. The balance: active in this world, prepared for the next.

### Question 3 — Remember the Destroyer of Pleasures

> The Prophet said: "Frequently remember the destroyer of pleasures" — death. Is remembering death depressing — or is it clarifying? How does it change our priorities?

**Facilitator guidance:** Remembering death is NOT depressing — it is CLARIFYING. It strips away the trivial and reveals the essential. When you remember death, you don't argue about small things. You don't waste time on grudges. You prioritize family, worship, and charity. Death awareness is not morbidity — it is wisdom.

### Question 4 — Whoever Loves to Meet Allah

> The Prophet said: "Whoever loves to meet Allah, Allah loves to meet him." Do you look forward to meeting Allah? Or does the thought frighten you? What would make you more ready?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the deepest question. The believer who has prepared — through faith, character, family, daily practice, and trust — looks forward to the meeting. The believer who has not prepared fears it. The way to become ready: continue the practices learned in this course. Prayer, charity, kindness, patience, repentance. Each act of preparation makes the meeting sweeter.

### Question 5 — The 30-Week Journey

> We have completed 30 weeks of halaqah together — 5 phases, 30 modules, from the roots of faith to the fruit of eternal vision. What was the most impactful thing you learned? What will you carry with you?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let each family member share the one teaching that most impacted them. This is the harvest — the fruit of 30 weeks of learning together. The sharing itself is a form of gratitude to Allah for the journey.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Covenant and Continuation Plan"

**Part 1: Covenant**

The family writes a covenant — a commitment to continue the practices learned in the 30-week course. Each person writes ONE practice they commit to continue:

| Family Member | Practice I Commit To Continue |
|---|---|
| [Name] | [e.g., daily muhasabah before sleep] |
| [Name] | [e.g., the sabr tracker] |
| [Name] | [e.g., lowering the gaze online] |
| [Name] | [e.g., justice among children] |
| [Name] | [e.g., daily du'a for parents] |

**Part 2: Continuation Plan**

Agree on how the halaqah will continue:
- **Weekly:** Family halaqah every [day] at [time] — review one module or discuss a Qur'an verse
- **Daily:** Family du'a after Fajr or Maghrib — 5 minutes
- **Monthly:** Family activity (joy night, charity project, simplicity review)
- **Annual:** Revisit the full course during Ramadan as a family review

**Part 3: Sign and Display**

Write the covenant on paper. Everyone signs. Post it in a visible family space — as a reminder of the 30-week journey and the commitment to continue.

**Goal:** The 30-week course has been a planting. The covenant is the commitment to keep watering. The tree will continue to grow — insha'Allah — producing fruit for years to come.
