# Module 4.4 — Beauty and Aesthetics in Islam

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 22
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that beauty is a divine attribute ("Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty") and that appreciating beauty is part of faith — not opposed to it. The session establishes the Qur'anic principle of pairing aesthetic appreciation with utilitarian benefit, and that the Prophet corrected the confusion between loving beauty and arrogance.

---

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that beauty is from Allah — and that loving beauty is part of faith, not a distraction from it. "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty."

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Diversion and Arts in Islam** (الترفيه والفنون في الإسلام), Chapters 1 and 2: "Islam and Sense of Reality" and "Beauty in the Quran and Universe." All citations refer to these chapters unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Islam Does Not Reject Art or Beauty — It Channels It

Qaradawi opens Diversion and Arts with a declaration of the middle course between two extremes:

- **Extreme prohibition**: "Muslim society should consecrate solely on the service and worship of Allah and hard work. There is no room for diversion or mirth."
- **Extreme permissibility**: "Life is a fair for fun and games" with no distinction between halal and haram.

Qaradawi chose "a middle course," guided by "sound and authentic texts" and "the torch of Shari'ah and Fiqh." This middle course: **Islam does not reject art or beauty — it channels them**, insisting that content is more important than form and that aesthetic satisfaction must coexist with moral purpose.

### 2. The Qur'an Pairs Beauty with Benefit

Qaradawi demonstrates that the Qur'an consistently pairs aesthetic appreciation with utilitarian benefit:

- **Cattle**: "warmth and benefit" (utility) + "beauty for you" (aesthetics) — 16:5-6
- **Horses**: "to ride" (utility) + "for ornament" (aesthetics) — 16:8
- **The sea**: "fresh flesh" to eat (utility) + "ornaments which you wear" (aesthetics) — 16:14
- **Fruits and gardens**: "Look at their fruits when they bear fruit" — beauty AND nourishment — 6:141

Qaradawi writes: "The Qur'an orientates man towards the assimilation of aesthetic and utilitarian aspects in different fields of life." Beauty is not separated from usefulness — they are paired. This is the Islamic aesthetic: beauty that serves, beauty that nourishes, beauty that is connected to life — not beauty for beauty's sake alone.

### 3. Allah Is Beautiful and He Loves Beauty

The foundational hadith: "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty" (Muslim, via Ibn Mas'ud). Qaradawi treats this as the theological basis for Islamic aesthetics:

- Beauty is a **divine attribute** — Allah Himself is Beautiful
- Loving beauty is **loving what Allah loves** — it is an act of faith
- Creating beauty is **imitating Allah's creation** — which is inherently beautiful
- Appreciating beauty is **recognizing Allah's signs** — "No want of proportion will you see in the Creation of Allah" (67:3)

### 4. Beauty vs. Arrogance — The Critical Distinction

The Prophet was asked about people who like attractive clothes and shoes. He replied: "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty, **but arrogance is different.**"

Qaradawi presents the distinction:

| Beauty (Mahmud) | Arrogance (Mudhmum) |
|---|---|
| Appreciating what Allah created | Claiming you are better than others |
| Wearing nice clothes with gratitude | Wearing nice clothes to show off |
| Enjoying adornment within Allah's boundaries | Using adornment to dominate or impress |
| Acknowledges Allah as the source | Claims personal superiority |

The Prophet defined arrogance precisely: "to deny the truth although one is certain that it is so, and to be thankless for the people who were helpful to one." Arrogance is not about appearance — it is about attitude. A person in simple clothes can be arrogant. A person in fine clothes can be humble. The difference is in the heart.

### 5. The Qur'an as Aesthetic Miracle

Qaradawi devotes significant attention to the Qur'an itself as an aesthetic experience:

"The Qur'an is both an aesthetic and intellectual miracle. It fulfils man's spiritual, intellectual and emotional needs."

The Prophet: "Adorn the Qur'an by reciting it with your nice voices." And: "The attractive audible sweet sounding voice adds to the beauty of the Qur'an."

Qaradawi cites Sheikh Muhammad Abdullah Draz: listening to the Qur'an is not merely an obligation — it is "an aesthetic experience fulfilling intellectual, emotional, and spiritual needs simultaneously."

For families: reciting the Qur'an beautifully is an act of worship AND an act of beauty. Teaching children to recite with nice voices is cultivating both faith and aesthetics.

### 6. Seven Key Principles Governing Art and Beauty

From Qaradawi's concept page on art:

1. **The default is permissibility** — everything is halal unless explicitly forbidden
2. **Content over form** — the moral content matters more than the medium
3. **The middle course** — neither total prohibition nor unlimited permissibility
4. **Art serves human needs** — sports for the body, worship for the soul, knowledge for the intellect, art for the emotions
5. **Art within the Islamic framework** — art that promotes virtue is halal; art that promotes vice is haram
6. **Beauty is a divine attribute** — "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty"
7. **Prevention of idolatry** — the primary concern with imagery is preventing shirk, not suppressing aesthetics

### 7. Beauty in the Family Home

For families, Qaradawi's aesthetics translate into:

- **Keeping the home clean and beautiful** — a tidy, well-maintained home is an act of worship
- **Adorning for prayer and Eid** — "take your adornment at every masjid" (7:31)
- **Reciting Qur'an beautifully** — teaching children to recite with nice voices
- **Appreciating nature together** — "Look at the creation of Allah" as a family activity
- **Dressing well without arrogance** — fine clothes with gratitude, not for show
- **Creating art with a message** — "Discipline and message are the bases of art"
- **Making beauty serve** — beauty that benefits the family, not beauty that distracts

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Allah Is Beautiful and He Loves Beauty

> The Prophet said: "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty." What does this mean? If Allah loves beauty, what should our relationship with beauty be?

**Facilitator guidance:** If Allah loves beauty, then loving beauty is an act of faith — not a distraction from it. Appreciating a sunset, a garden, a beautiful recitation, a well-made object — all of these are acts of recognizing Allah's creation. The Muslim who says "I shouldn't care about beauty because I'm religious" is misunderstanding the religion.

### Question 2 — Beauty Is Not Arrogance

> The Prophet said beauty and arrogance are "different." You can wear nice clothes without being arrogant. How do we tell the difference in ourselves?

**Facilitator guidance:** The test: am I wearing this to feel good (gratitude to Allah) or to make others feel bad (superiority)? Am I decorating my home to create warmth for my family or to impress visitors? The intention — not the object — determines whether beauty is worship or arrogance.

### Question 3 — The Qur'an Pairs Beauty with Benefit

> The Qur'an always pairs beauty with usefulness — cattle for food AND beauty, horses for riding AND ornament, the sea for food AND jewelry. What does this teach us about Islamic aesthetics?

**Facilitator guidance:** In Islam, beauty is not isolated from function. Beautiful things should also be useful. Useful things should also be beautiful. This rejects both "art for art's sake" (beauty without purpose) and "pure utilitarianism" (function without beauty). The Islamic aesthetic: beauty that serves.

### Question 4 — How Can We Make Our Home More Beautiful?

> If "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty," how can we make our home more beautiful in a way that pleases Allah? What is one change we can make?

**Facilitator guidance:** Practical ideas: keeping the home clean and tidy, adding plants, decorating with Qur'anic calligraphy, playing beautiful recitation, keeping a garden, dressing nicely for Eid, making meals visually appealing, teaching children to appreciate nature. The goal: a home that reflects Allah's beauty.

### Question 5 — The Qur'an as an Aesthetic Experience

> The Prophet said: "Adorn the Qur'an with your nice voices." Reciting the Qur'an beautifully is both worship AND art. How can we make Qur'an recitation more beautiful in our family?

**Facilitator guidance:** Ideas: learn tajweed (rules of beautiful recitation), play recitation in the home, have children recite and praise their effort, listen to famous reciters together, make recitation a family activity. The Qur'an is not only a book of rules — it is a book of beauty.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Beauty Walk"

Go outside together as a family. Walk through a park, garden, or natural area. Each person finds **5 beautiful things** in nature and explains why they find them beautiful.

**Structure:**
- "I find [object] beautiful because [reason]"
- Connect to: "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty — and this is His creation"

**Examples:**
- "I find this flower beautiful because of its colors — Allah made the colors"
- "I find this tree beautiful because it's so tall and strong — Allah made it grow"
- "I find this sky beautiful because it changes color — Allah paints it every evening"

**Goal:** To train the family to see beauty as Allah's creation — and to appreciate it as an act of faith. The beauty walk makes the abstract teaching ("Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty") concrete and experiential.

**For homebound families:** Instead of walking, find 5 beautiful things inside the home and discuss why they're beautiful and who created their beauty.
