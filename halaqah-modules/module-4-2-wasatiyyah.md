# Module 4.2 — Wasatiyyah: The Middle Way in Family Life

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 20
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that wasatiyyah (moderation) is not compromise between truth and falsehood but the straight path between two extremes of error. The session establishes wasatiyyah as Qaradawi's master framework — structuring his thought across worship, ethics, lifestyle, and daily family life — and identifies where the family tends to go to extremes.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that Islam is the middle way — not extreme strictness, not extreme laxity, but the balanced path that the Prophet modelled. We learn to identify where our family goes to extremes and how to return to the center.

>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (1975)** and **Muslim (1159)** on the authority of Amr ibn al-As

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** on the authority of Anas ibn Malik

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Love moderately, for you may hate those you love one day. And hate moderately, for you may love those you hate one day."
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi (1997)** on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Eat, drink, and wear [fine] clothes, as long as that does not involve any extravagance or vanity."

> — **Narrated by Ahmed** and an-Nasa'i on the authority of Abdullah ibn Amr

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Moderation" (pp. 340-341); **Diversion and Arts in Islam** (الترفيه والفنون), Chapter 1 (the middle course); **Approaching the Sunnah** (كيف نتفهم السنة), Chapter 1 (the balanced Sunnah). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Wasatiyyah: Qaradawi's Master Framework

Qaradawi's comparison page on wasatiyyah describes it as "the architectonic principle that governs his thought across every domain." Whether in fiqh, ethics, lifestyle, or eschatology, the same pattern recurs: **Islam steers between two extremes, embraces both poles, and refuses to reduce the human being to a single dimension.**

Wasatiyyah is rooted in the Qur'anic description of the Muslim community as "a middle nation" (*ummatan wasatan*, 2:143). The word *wasat* means center, middle, balanced — neither one extreme nor the other.

### 2. Wasatiyyah Is NOT Compromise

Qaradawi is emphatic: wasatiyyah is NOT splitting the difference between truth and falsehood. It is the straight path between two extremes of ERROR.

In Approaching the Sunnah (Chapter 1), he presents the balance as "an integration of two goods that extremes have pitted against each other":

| Polar Pair | Extreme of Excess (ifrāṭ) | Extreme of Negligence (tafrīṭ) | Wasatiyyah Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body / Spirit | Ascetic denial of the body | Materialist neglect of the spirit | The body has rights AND the spirit has rights |
| This World / Hereafter | Monastic withdrawal | Worldly absorption | "Work for this world as if you will live forever; work for the hereafter as if you will die tomorrow" |
| Individual / Community | Isolationist Sufism | Totalitarian collectivism | Integration — prayer, family, community together |
| Reason / Revelation | Rationalist excess | Literalist repudiation of reason | Faith and reason united |

This is crucial: wasatiyyah does not mean "a little bit of truth and a little bit of falsehood." It means refusing the false dichotomy that forces you to choose between two goods when Islam requires BOTH.

### 3. The Prophet Corrected Extremes

Qaradawi cites the famous incident (from Approaching the Sunnah, Chapter 1): three Companions proposed extreme ascetic practices — perpetual fasting, perpetual night prayer, and lifelong celibacy. The Prophet corrected them: "I fast and I break my fast, I offer prayers during the night and sleep, and I marry women. He who turns away from my Sunnah has nothing to do with me."

The corrective is not merely a prohibition of specific acts — it is a declaration that **the Prophetic pattern itself constitutes the center.** Any deviation toward excess (ifrāṭ) or negligence (tafrīṭ) abandons that center. The Sunnah's balanced nature means that any interpretation producing extremism, one-sidedness, or narrowing is suspect on methodological grounds alone.

### 4. Moderation in Worship

The Prophet said: "Your body has its rights, your wife has her rights, and your guest has his rights. Give each their rights."

Qaradawi comments: "Islam condemns all forms of excess, even when it comes to acts of worship, because it always comes at the expense of something else." A person who fasts every day neglects their body. A person who prays all night neglects their family. A person who gives everything to charity neglects their dependants. Worship that harms other obligations is not piety — it is imbalance.

Other religions promoted excess in monasticism and asceticism (Buddhism, Manichaeism, Christianity). Islam emphasizes that "Allah has created pleasure to be enjoyed" and forbidding pleasure is "ingratitude."

The Prophet: "Eat, drink, and wear [fine] clothes, as long as that does not involve any extravagance or vanity."

### 5. Moderation in Emotions

Qaradawi draws particular attention to emotional moderation: "Muslims are required to be moderate even in their emotions, so that feelings do not take over and lead them to error."

The Prophet: "Love moderately, for you may hate those you love one day. And hate moderately, for you may love those you hate one day."

This is profound wisdom:
- The person who loves too intensely will be destroyed when love turns to hatred
- The person who hates too intensely will be unable to reconcile when circumstances change
- The parent who is overly attached to one child will be devastated by that child's independence
- The teenager who is consumed by a friendship will collapse when the friendship ends

Moderation in emotion protects relationships from the extremes of infatuation and animosity.

### 6. Moderation in Spending and Consumption

The Qur'an: "Not excessively or sparingly, but moderate" (25:67). The Prophet: "Do not waste water even if you are on the bank of a flowing river."

Qaradawi: even water — the most abundant resource — should not be wasted. If this is the standard for water, what about food, electricity, clothing, and money? Moderation in consumption is not about deprivation — it is about proportion.

The Qur'an also prohibits the other extreme: "Do not make haram the good things which Allah has made halal for you" (5:87). Over-restriction — forbidding what Allah allowed — is also a form of extremism.

### 7. Moderation in the Family

For families, wasatiyyah applies to every dimension:

| Domain | Extreme Strictness (Ifrāṭ) | Extreme Laxity (Tafrīṭ) | The Middle Way |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen time | No screens at all | Unlimited screens | Reasonable limits with purpose |
| Discipline | Harsh punishment | No discipline at all | Firm but gentle correction |
| Religious practice | Every minute is worship | Never pray or fast | Regular worship + family + rest |
| Social life | Never leave the house | Always out with friends | Family time + community time |
| Food | Strict diet, no treats | Unrestricted junk food | Healthy eating + occasional treats |
| Spending | Never spend on enjoyment | Spend everything on luxuries | Reasonable spending + savings + charity |
| Education | Force 6 hours of study daily | Never check homework | Consistent, moderate study with breaks |

The middle way is not a fixed point — it varies by family, age, and circumstance. But the principle is constant: neither extreme, always balanced.

### 8. The Balance of the Straight Path

Qaradawi cites the Qur'an's image of the balance: "The heaven He raised and imposed the balance; that you not transgress within the balance. And establish weight in justice and do not make deficient the balance" (55:7-9).

The straight path (sirat al-mustaqim) is the balanced path — between the extremes on either side. For the family, the goal is not to find the perfect formula but to constantly adjust: when we tilt toward strictness, lean back toward ease; when we tilt toward laxity, lean back toward discipline. The balance is dynamic, not static.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Wasatiyyah Is Not Compromise

> Qaradawi says wasatiyyah is NOT splitting the difference between truth and falsehood. It is the straight path between two extremes of error. What's the difference? Can you give an example?

**Facilitator guidance:** Compromise = "Let's allow a little bit of lying to be kind." Wasatiyyah = "Be kind AND truthful — find a halal way to be both." The difference: compromise accepts a little falsehood. Wasatiyyah refuses both the falsehood of harsh truth AND the falsehood of lying — and finds the third path: gentle truth.

### Question 2 — Where Does Our Family Go to Extremes?

> What is one area where our family tends to go to an extreme? Too strict? Too lax? What would the middle way look like?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the most practical question. Common extremes: too strict about screen time (causing resentment) or too lax (causing addiction); too strict about religious practice (causing rebellion) or too lax (causing indifference); too strict about academic performance (causing anxiety) or too lax (causing underachievement). Identify ONE area and agree on what the middle way would look like.

### Question 3 — The Prophet Balanced Worship and Life

> The Prophet fasted AND broke his fast. He prayed at night AND slept. He married AND had a family. What does this tell us about the relationship between worship and daily life?

**Facilitator guidance:** The Prophet was the most devoted worshipper AND the most balanced human. He did not sacrifice family for worship, or worship for family. He held both. For families: a parent who is always at the mosque but never at home is imbalanced. A parent who is always at home but never prays is also imbalanced. The middle way: both.

### Question 4 — Love Moderately

> The Prophet said: "Love moderately, for you may hate those you love one day." Does this mean loving less? Or loving wisely?

**Facilitator guidance:** "Love moderately" does NOT mean "love less." It means: love without obsession. Love without losing yourself. Love in a way that can survive disappointment. The person who loves moderately can forgive, reconcile, and continue. The person who loves excessively is destroyed by every disappointment.

### Question 5 — Do Not Waste Water Even at a River

> The Prophet said don't waste water even at a flowing river. If this is the standard for water, what about our family's use of food, electricity, money, and time?

**Facilitator guidance:** This hadith establishes that moderation applies even when resources seem unlimited. A family with a good income should still practice moderation — because the principle is not about scarcity but about balance. Wasting what Allah has provided — even if you can afford it — is a form of ingratitude.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Balance Audit"

List all weekly family activities in a table. For each, mark whether the family is:

- **Too much** (ifrāṭ — excessive)
- **Balanced** (wasatiyyah — moderate)
- **Too little** (tafrīṭ — negligent)

| Activity | Too Much / Balanced / Too Little | One Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Screen time | ? | ? |
| Family meals together | ? | ? |
| Prayer as a family | ? | ? |
| Homework/study time | ? | ? |
| Physical activity | ? | ? |
| Social media use | ? | ? |
| Quality family time | ? | ? |
| Sleep | ? | ? |
| Charity/giving | ? | ? |

**Agree on ONE adjustment:** Pick the most imbalanced area and make one concrete change this week. For example: "We will reduce screen time by 30 minutes and add 30 minutes of family reading." Or: "We will increase family meals from 2 to 4 per week."

**Goal:** Wasatiyyah is not a one-time achievement — it is a constant adjustment. The balance audit is a tool for regular recalibration.

### Key Distinction
Wasatiyyah is NOT "being moderate for the sake of being moderate." It is following the Prophetic model — which IS the center. The Prophet's life IS the balanced path. When we follow him, we are automatically in the center. When we deviate from him — toward excess strictness OR excess laxity — we leave the center.
