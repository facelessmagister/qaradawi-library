# Module 5.2 — Justice and Charity in the Family and Society

**Phase:** 5 — Fruit: Trust, Hope & Eternal Vision
**Week:** 27
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that justice is the cornerstone of Islamic ethics — commanded in every interaction, from family to society to enemies. The session establishes that charity extends beyond money to every good deed, that doing good is not limited to Muslims, and that "helping people reconcile is better than prayer, fasting, and charity."

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that justice is not just a political concept — it begins in the home. Justice between spouses, justice among children, justice with neighbours, justice even with those we dislike. And charity is not just money — every good deed is charity.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.3.2: "The Ethics of Society" (pp. 376-380). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Expanding the Domain of Good Deeds

Qaradawi opens with a striking principle: "Social ethics has such a special status in Islam that the faithful are highly rewarded for their actions that promote social well-being."

He cites:
- "A day in the life of a just leader is more valuable than 60 years of worship"
- "Do you know what is better than praying, fasting, and giving to charity? **Helping people reconcile.**"
- "Whoever shows the way to doing good has the same reward as someone who actually does it"

The message: social good — justice, reconciliation, helping others — is not secondary to worship. It IS worship. In some cases, it surpasses worship. Helping two people reconcile is better than praying, fasting, AND giving charity — because reconciliation heals relationships, prevents conflict, and strengthens the community.

### 2. Justice: The Cornerstone

Qaradawi writes: "Justice is a fundamental moral ideal that serves as the cornerstone of Islamic ethics. In fact, a shared sense of justice is what keeps the peace within society and regulates everyday life."

He presents numerous Qur'anic commands:
- "When you testify, be just" (6:152)
- "When you judge between people, judge with justice" (4:58)
- "Persistently stand firm in justice, even if it be against yourselves or your parents and relatives" (4:135)
- "Do not let the hatred of people prevent you from being just" (5:8)

The scope of Islamic justice:
1. **Justice in the family** — between spouses, among children ("Fear Allah and be just between your children")
2. **Justice in testimony** — even against your own relatives
3. **Justice with non-Muslims** — "Allah does not forbid you from being righteous toward those who do not fight you"
4. **Justice with enemies** — "Do not let the hatred of people prevent you from being just"
5. **Justice in governance** — "A day of a just leader is more valuable than 60 years of worship"

### 3. Justice Even Against Yourself

The verse 4:135 is one of the most powerful in the Qur'an: "Stand firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, **even if it be against yourselves or your parents and relatives.**"

Qaradawi highlights this: justice does not bend for family. If your parent is wrong, you testify to the truth. If your relative committed an injustice, you do not cover for them. If YOU are wrong, you admit it. Justice is not selective — it applies to everyone, including yourself and your loved ones.

For families: this means teaching children that justice is not about loyalty to family over truth. If a family member does wrong, the right response is not to cover it up but to address it with justice and kindness. "Do not follow your personal inclination, lest you be unjust" (4:135).

### 4. Justice Even with Those You Dislike

Verse 5:8: "Do not let the hatred of people prevent you from being just. Be just; that is nearer to righteousness."

Qaradawi: "Muslims are required to observe justice with family, strangers, non-Muslims, and even enemies. At the same time, they are forbidden from unfairly favoring some people over others or from being unfair to those they dislike."

This is a radical teaching: justice does not depend on your feelings. You may dislike someone — but you must still treat them justly. You may disagree with someone's religion — but you must still act righteously toward them (60:8). Justice is not earned by being liked — it is owed to everyone by virtue of their humanity.

### 5. Charity Beyond Money

Qaradawi extends charity far beyond financial giving. The Prophet outlined a hierarchy of charity:

1. **Give from wealth** — if you have it
2. **Work and earn** — if you can't give, work and benefit yourself (so you don't need charity)
3. **Help someone in need** — if you can't work, help others physically
4. **Enjoin good** — if you can't help physically, encourage others to do good
5. **Refrain from evil** — if you can't do anything else, at least don't harm anyone. "That is charity."

The principle: EVERYONE can give charity. Even the poorest person, even the weakest person, even the person who can do nothing at all — they can at least NOT harm others, and that refraining is itself charity.

For families: charity is not just giving money. Helping a sibling with homework is charity. Helping a parent with chores is charity. Smiling at a neighbour is charity. Removing harm from the road is charity (Module 4.5). Refraining from gossip is charity. Every good deed — no matter how small — is charity.

### 6. Doing Good to All Living Things

Qaradawi notes: "Islam extends the domain of doing good deeds to include all living things."

He cites the famous hadith: "The Prophet told of a prostitute who went to heaven for giving water to a thirsty dog, and a man whose sins were all forgiven because he did the same."

The scope of Islamic charity: not just humans, not just Muslims, not just family — ALL living things. Giving water to a thirsty dog is charity. Feeding a stray cat is charity. Planting a tree that birds eat from is charity.

### 7. Justice as the Foundation of Family Life

Qaradawi ties justice back to the family: "We also see justice as a foundation of family life, both among the husband and wife and also among children."

The Prophet: "Fear Allah and be just between your children" (al-Bukhari, Muslim).

This connects directly to Module 3.5 (Children's Rights and Justice) — but here it is framed within the broader principle: justice is not just a family value, it is the CORNERSTONE of all Islamic ethics. The justice you practice in the family is the same justice you must practice in society. If you are just with your children but unjust with your neighbours, your justice is incomplete. If you are just with Muslims but unjust with non-Muslims, your justice is incomplete.

### 8. The Story of the Jew Falsely Accused

Qaradawi presents a powerful example: "Islamic justice is for everyone, regardless of religion. There are nine verses in the Qur'an that were revealed to protect a Jew who was unfairly accused of theft by some Muslims. They even lied to the Prophet in their testimonies."

The Qur'an: "We have revealed to you the Book in truth so that you may judge between people according to that which Allah has shown you. **And do not be for the deceitful an advocate.**" (4:105).

Allah revealed NINE verses to defend a Jewish man against false accusation by Muslims. This is the depth of Islamic justice: Allah Himself defended a non-Muslim against Muslim perpetrators. Justice does not bend for religion, tribe, or loyalty.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Better Than Prayer and Fasting

> The Prophet said: "Do you know what is better than praying, fasting, and giving charity? Helping people reconcile." How can something be BETTER than prayer, fasting, and charity?

**Facilitator guidance:** Reconciliation heals what prayer, fasting, and charity cannot fix: broken relationships. You can pray all night, fast every day, and give all your money — but if your family is broken, your community is divided, and your neighbours are in conflict, your worship has not reached its full purpose. Reconciliation is the social form of worship — it heals the Ummah.

### Question 2 — Justice Even Against Yourself

> The Qur'an says: "Stand firm in justice, even if it be against yourselves or your parents." Have you ever had to admit you were wrong, even when it was embarrassing? How did it feel?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a vulnerability question. Justice starts with honesty — admitting your own mistakes. The parent who admits they were wrong to their child is practicing 4:135. The child who admits they were wrong to their sibling is practicing 4:135. This is the hardest form of justice — justice against yourself.

### Question 3 — Justice with Those You Dislike

> The Qur'an says: "Do not let the hatred of people prevent you from being just." How do we treat people we don't like? Are we still just?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a challenging question. We all have people we dislike — a difficult neighbour, a rude colleague, a problematic family member. The Qur'an says: be just ANYWAY. You don't have to like them. You don't have to be their friend. But you must not wrong them. You must not cheat them. You must not lie about them. Justice is owed to everyone — not just the people you like.

### Question 4 — Charity Is for Everyone

> The Prophet said: even if you can do nothing else, "refraining from evil is charity." What does this mean? How can NOT doing something be charity?

**Facilitator guidance:** Not gossiping = charity (you protect people's reputations). Not littering = charity (you protect the environment). Not arguing = charity (you protect peace). Not looking at haram = charity (you protect your soul). Refraining from harm IS a form of giving — you are giving the absence of harm. Everyone can give this charity, no matter how poor or weak.

### Question 5 — Justice in Our Family

> Where does our family need more justice? Between siblings? Between parents? With neighbours? With non-Muslims in our community?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let the family discuss honestly. Common areas: siblings feeling treated unfairly, parents feeling unappreciated, neighbours being neglected, non-Muslims in the community being excluded. Agree on ONE concrete change.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Reconciliation Circle"

Identify ONE conflict or tension in the family (or with a neighbour/relative) and work toward reconciliation.

**Step 1:** Identify the conflict (without blame): "There has been tension between [X] and [Y] about [issue]."

**Step 2:** Each person shares their perspective (without interruption):
- "I feel [emotion] when [situation] because [reason]"
- Use "I" statements, not "You" statements

**Step 3:** Seek understanding:
- Each person repeats what they heard the other say
- "What I heard you say is: [summary]"

**Step 4:** Seek reconciliation:
- What would resolve this?
- What is each person willing to change?
- Can we agree on a path forward?

**Step 5:** Make du'a together:
> "Allah, reconcile our hearts. Remove the bitterness between us. Make us among those who reconcile."

**Goal:** The Prophet said reconciliation is "better than praying, fasting, and charity." This activity makes reconciliation concrete — not a concept but a practice. The family that reconciles regularly stays healthy.

### Key Distinction
Justice is not selective. It applies to everyone — family, strangers, non-Muslims, enemies. And charity is not financial — it includes every good deed, every act of help, and even refraining from harm. The family that internalizes these two principles (universal justice + broad charity) becomes a force for good in the community.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "being fair to everyone." Use the dog story: "A woman gave water to a thirsty dog, and Allah forgave her sins! Being kind to animals is charity!" |
| 7–12 | The reconciliation teaching: "The Prophet said helping people make up is BETTER than praying and fasting! When you help two friends who are fighting, that's better than extra prayers!" |
| 13–17 | Justice with those you dislike: "You don't have to like everyone. But you must be fair to everyone. Even the person you can't stand — you can't lie about them, cheat them, or wrong them." |
| Adults | The full treatment. Justice against yourself (4:135). Justice with non-Muslims (60:8). The nine verses defending a Jew. Reconciliation as the highest social worship. |
