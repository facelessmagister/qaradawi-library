# Module 2.5 — Contentment and Moderation

**Phase:** 2 — Trunk: Character and Inner Purification
**Week:** 11
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that contentment (qana'ah) is the true wealth and that moderation (wasatiyyah) is the very nature of Islam. The session establishes that the believer is called to enjoy Allah's blessings without excess, to be grateful without complaint, and to find richness in the heart rather than in possessions.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that the richest person is not the one who has the most, but the one who is most content with what Allah has given. We learn the middle way — neither extravagance nor stinginess, but the balanced path that is the nature of Islam.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Moderation" and "Contentment" (pp. 340-342). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Moderation Is the Nature of Islam

Qaradawi establishes that moderation (wasatiyyah) is not a recommended virtue — it is the very NATURE of Islam:

> "Allah has intended for the Muslim community to be the middle-ground community: 'Thus We have made you a just community' (2:143). It is the middle-ground because each individual has a mandate to establish and maintain a balanced approach to all aspects of life."

The Qur'an explicitly commands: "O children of Adam, take your adornment at every masjid, and eat and drink, but be not excessive. Indeed, He likes not those who commit excess" (7:31).

Islam condemns ALL forms of excess — even in worship:
- "Your body has its rights, your wife has her rights" (Bukhari, Muslim) — the Prophet corrected those who vowed to fast every day and pray all night
- Other religions promoted excess in monasticism and asceticism (Buddhism, Manichaeism, Christianity)
- Islam emphasizes that "Allah has created pleasure to be enjoyed" and forbidding pleasure is "ingratitude"

### 2. Moderation in All Things

Qaradawi applies moderation to every domain of life:

| Domain | Extremism (Rejected) | Laxity (Rejected) | The Middle Way (Islam) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worship | Fasting every day, praying all night | Neglecting prayer and fasting | Regular worship + family + rest |
| Spending | Extravagance — wasting wealth | Stinginess — hoarding wealth | "Not excessively or sparingly, but moderate" (25:67) |
| Emotion | Overwhelming love → obsession | Complete detachment → coldness | "Love moderately" (hadith) |
| Eating | Gluttony | Starvation | "Eat and drink, but be not excessive" (7:31) |
| Speech | Loud, boastful | Silent, withdrawn | "Be moderate in your pace and lower your voice" (31:19) |
| Water use | Wasting even at a river | Refusing to use water for wudu | "Do not waste water even at a flowing river" |

The Prophet: "Eat, drink, and wear [fine] clothes, as long as that does not involve any extravagance or vanity."

### 3. Moderation in Emotions

Qaradawi draws particular attention to emotional moderation:

> "Muslims are required to be moderate even in their emotions, so that feelings do not take over and lead them to error."

The Prophet said: "Love moderately, for you may hate those you love one day. And hate moderately, for you may love those you hate one day."

This is profound wisdom: the person who loves too intensely will be destroyed when that love turns to hatred. The person who hates too intensely will be unable to reconcile when circumstances change. Moderation in emotion protects relationships from the extremes of infatuation and animosity.

The Qur'an: "In order that you not despair over what has eluded you and not exult in pride over what He has given you" (57:23). Neither grief over loss nor pride over gain — balance.

### 4. Contentment (Qana'ah): The True Wealth

Qaradawi defines contentment as "being satisfied with what Allah has allotted to us in this world, because He is the one who provides us with talents, wealth, health, and everything we have."

Contentment protects against greed — "which makes some jealous of others, even if they were themselves the richest people on earth. Jealousy sets their hearts on fire, and no matter what they get their hands on, they are never satisfied."

The Prophet said:
- "Richness does not lie in abundance of worldly goods, but richness is the richness of the heart" (Bukhari, Muslim)
- "Be satisfied with what Allah has allotted for you and you shall be the richest person" (Ibn Majah)
- "Whoever begins the day feeling secure with his family and healthy with enough provisions for the day is as though he owns the whole world" (Tirmidhi)

### 5. Satisfaction Is About Quality, Not Quantity

Qaradawi makes a crucial distinction:

> "Satisfaction is never about quantity, but it is about quality. If one has all the riches in the world, it will never be enough without contentment, which is the true treasure."

This means: a person with millions and no contentment is POOR. A person with enough for the day and contentment is RICH. The measure of wealth is not the bank account — it is the heart.

The Prophet: "Look to those below you, and do not look to those above you." Why? Because looking at those above you produces dissatisfaction — "you may belittle Allah's favors upon you." Looking at those below you produces gratitude — you see how much Allah has given you compared to others.

### 6. Moderation Does Not Mean Forbidding Good Things

Qaradawi is careful to balance his teaching: moderation does NOT mean rejecting Allah's blessings. The Qur'an explicitly asks: "Who has forbidden the adornment of Allah which He has produced for His servants and the good [lawful] things of provision?" (7:32). And: "Do not prohibit the good things which Allah has made lawful for you" (5:87).

The Prophet: "Eat, drink, and wear [fine] clothes, as long as that does not involve any extravagance or vanity."

Islam holds the middle ground between:
- Asceticism (rejecting all pleasure) — rejected by Islam
- Hedonism (unlimited pleasure-seeking) — rejected by Islam
- **The middle way: enjoy Allah's blessings with gratitude, within Allah's boundaries, without excess**

### 7. The Straight Path Is the Balance

Qaradawi cites the Qur'an's image of the balance:

> "The heaven He raised and imposed the balance; that you not transgress within the balance. And establish weight in justice and do not make deficient the balance" (55:7-9).

The balance is the metaphor for the entire Islamic way of life: not too much, not too little. The straight path (sirat al-mustaqim) is the balanced path — between the extremes on either side.

For families: this means:
- Not so much screen time that worship and family are neglected
- Not so little recreation that the family becomes tense and joyless
- Not so much spending that debt accumulates
- Not so little spending that the family feels deprived
- The middle way in everything

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Richness of the Heart

> The Prophet said: "Richness does not lie in abundance of worldly goods, but richness is the richness of the heart." What does it mean to be rich in the heart? Can you be rich without money?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the core question. "Richness of the heart" = contentment (qana'ah). It means feeling that you have enough. A person with a million dollars who always wants more is POOR. A person with enough for the day who is grateful is RICH. The measure is internal, not external.

**For children:** "If you have a toy and you're happy with it, you're rich. If you have ten toys and you're crying for an eleventh, you're poor — even if you have lots of toys!"

### Question 2 — The Whole World Is Yours Today

> The Prophet said: "Whoever begins the day feeling secure with his family and healthy with enough provisions for the day is as though he owns the whole world." Do we feel that way? What would it take to feel like we own the whole world right now?

**Facilitator guidance:** This hadith reframes everything. If you have: (1) security with your family, (2) health, (3) enough food for today — you ALREADY own the whole world. Most people in the world don't have all three. Ask: do we have all three right now? Then we already own the world. What are we complaining about?

### Question 3 — Look Below, Not Above

> The Prophet said: "Look to those below you, and do not look to those above you." How does social media make this hard? What happens when we always look at people who seem to have more?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the contemporary application. Social media is a machine for making you look ABOVE — at people with better houses, better cars, better vacations, better bodies. The result: constant dissatisfaction. The Prophetic cure: look below — at people who don't have enough food, shelter, or safety. This is not about feeling superior — it's about feeling grateful.

### Question 4 — Love Moderately

> The Prophet said: "Love moderately, for you may hate those you love one day. And hate moderately, for you may love those you hate one day." What does it mean to love moderately? Does it mean loving less?

**Facilitator guidance:** "Love moderately" does NOT mean "love less." It means: love without obsession. Love without losing yourself. Love in a way that can survive disappointment. The person who loves too intensely is destroyed when the loved one disappoints them. The person who loves moderately can recover, forgive, and continue. This applies to ALL relationships — parent-child, spouse, friends.

### Question 5 — Where Does Our Family Need More Balance?

> What is one area where our family goes to extremes? Too much screen time? Too much spending? Too much work? Too little family time? What is one change we can make?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the practical application. Let each family member name one area where the family is imbalanced. Then agree on ONE concrete change. The goal is not perfection — it's one step toward the middle way.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Contentment Journal"

Each family member gets a small notebook (or a shared family notebook). For one week, each person writes THREE things they are grateful for each day:

1. One thing about Allah (e.g., "Thank You Allah for my health today")
2. One thing about the family (e.g., "I'm grateful Mom made my favorite dinner")
3. One thing about themselves (e.g., "I'm grateful I did well on my test")

**At the next halaqah:** Share highlights from the week. Discuss:
- Did the gratitude practice change how you felt about what you have?
- Was it hard or easy to find three things each day?
- Did you notice things you usually take for granted?

**The teaching:** "Look to those below you, and do not look to those above you." The contentment journal is a daily practice of looking at what Allah has already given — not what He hasn't.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of writing, have children draw one thing they're happy about each day. Ask: "Who gave you this? Allah! So what do we say? Alhamdulillah!"

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-04-02.txt` (the "Moderation" subsection, approximately lines 447-550, and "Contentment" subsection)

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **moderation is not deprivation.** Islam does NOT say "don't enjoy life." Islam says "enjoy life WITHIN BOUNDARIES." The Prophet said: "Eat, drink, and wear [fine] clothes, as long as that does not involve any extravagance or vanity."

| Asceticism (rejected) | Hedonism (rejected) | Islam (the middle way) |
|---|---|---|
| "Don't eat good food" | "Eat as much as you want" | "Eat and drink, but be not excessive" |
| "Don't wear nice clothes" | "Spend everything on fashion" | "Wear fine clothes, without vanity" |
| "Fast every day" | "Never fast" | "Fast some days, break fast others" |
| "Give everything away" | "Keep everything" | "Give regularly, keep what you need" |

The family that teaches children ONLY restraint without teaching enjoyment produces resentment. The family that teaches ONLY enjoyment without teaching restraint produces greed. The middle way teaches BOTH: enjoy AND restrain.

### The Contentment Journal
This is the most practical activity in the module. Three gratitudes per day, for one week, transforms the heart. Research in positive psychology confirms what the Prophet taught 1,400 years ago: gratitude practice increases contentment and reduces anxiety. The difference: in Islam, gratitude is directed to Allah — not just "positive thinking" but active remembrance of the Source of all blessings.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "Alhamdulillah for what we have." Use the contentment journal with drawings. Ask: "What made you happy today?" |
| 7–12 | The "whole world" hadith: "If you have a safe family, health, and food for today, you own the WHOLE WORLD!" Ask: "Do we have all three? Then we're richer than we think!" |
| 13–17 | Social media comparison: "Social media makes you look UP — at people who seem to have more. The Prophet said look DOWN — at people who have less. Try one day of looking down." |
| Adults | Read the full source section. Discuss emotional moderation: "Love moderately" — how does this apply to parenting? To marriage? To friendships? |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching to sections 1 (moderation as the nature of Islam), 4 (contentment = true wealth), and 5 (quality not quantity). Keep the full discussion — Questions 1 and 2 are essential.
