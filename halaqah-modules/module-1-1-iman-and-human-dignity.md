# Module 1.1 — Iman and Human Dignity

**Phase:** 1 — Roots: Faith as the Foundation
**Week:** 1
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that their worth comes from Allah, not from appearance, achievement, or social status. The session establishes the foundational Islamic concept that human beings are Allah's honored vicegerents, and that faith (iman) elevates this honor to its highest station.

---

## Opening (3 min)

Begin with the standard opening du'a for gathering:

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ
>
> "In the name of Allah, and peace and blessings upon the Messenger of Allah."

State the intention (niyyah) for this halaqah: we gather as a family to learn what Allah says about our worth and dignity, so that we may live with the confidence of those who know they are honored by their Creator.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Divine Hadith (Qudsi) on Allah's Proximity

The Prophet (peace be upon him) narrated that Allah said:

> "I am as My servant thinks I am, and I am with him whenever he remembers Me: if he remembers Me in himself, I remember him in Myself; if he remembers Me in a group of people, I remember him in a better group of people; if he draws near to Me a hand's span, I draw near to him an arm's length, and if he draws near to Me an arm's length, I draw near to him a fathom's length; and if he comes to Me walking, I go to him at speed."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** (Kitab at-Tawhid) and **Muslim** (Kitab adh-Dhikr wad-Du'a), on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

### Hadith on the Vicegerency of Adam

The Qur'an records Allah's words to the angels:

> "Behold, thy Lord said to the angels: 'I will create a vicegerent on earth.'"
>
> — **Surah al-Baqarah (2:30)**

Allah then taught Adam the names of all things and commanded the angels to prostrate before him:

> "When I have fashioned him in due proportion and breathed into him of My spirit, fall ye down in obeisance unto him.' So the angels prostrated themselves, all of them together: not so Iblis..."
>
> — **Surah Sad (38:71-74)**

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Faith and Life** (الإيمان والحياة), Chapter 1: "Iman and the Dignity of Man." All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Materialist View of Man

Qaradawi opens with a stark contrast. The materialist philosophy reduces man to "a piece of flesh, blood, nerves, systems, glands, and cells." Mind and reasoning, in this view, "is only a material excreted by the brain, just as the liver excretes bile or as the kidney excretes urine."

He cites a scientist who calculated the chemical value of a 140-pound human body:

- Fat enough for seven bars of soap
- Carbon enough for seven pencils
- Phosphorus enough for 120 match heads
- Iron enough for one medium-sized pin
- Lime enough to paint a chicken coop
- Water enough to fill a ten-gallon barrel

The total market value? "Fifty or sixty Egyptian piasters." This is the materialist assessment of human worth.

Qaradawi quotes a contemporary Arab atheist: "We are only an outcome of nature, just like insects... What then will the cosmos, the sun, or the moon lose if we lose ourselves?"

He traces this to Darwin's *Origin of Species*, where man "is not distinguished from plants, animals, or insects," and to Freud, who reduced human psychology to animal drives. The result: man is "an 'evolved animal' that was evolved from one stage to another." He asks: "Is there anything more uninspiring to the human soul than this?"

### 2. The Islamic View: Man as Allah's Honored Vicegerent

Against this reductive view, Qaradawi presents the Qur'anic vision of man with a cascade of divine actions:

1. **Allah created man in the best of molds** (Q 95:4) — the human form is a masterpiece, not an accident.
2. **Allah created man with His own Hand** — a mark of special creation, distinct from the rest of the universe which was created by the word "Be."
3. **Allah breathed into man of His Spirit** (Q 38:72) — the divine breath that gives man spiritual dimension.
4. **Allah commanded the angels to prostrate before Adam** (Q 38:72-73) — man's position is such that even the angels were ordered to honor him.
5. **Allah made man His vicegerent (khalifah) on earth** (Q 2:30) — man is Allah's deputy, entrusted with the stewardship of creation.
6. **Allah subjected all things in the heavens and earth to man's use** (Q 14:32-34; 31:20; 45:12-13) — the entire universe serves the human being.
7. **Allah gave man knowledge** (Q 2:31-33) — Adam was taught the names of all things, a knowledge even the angels did not possess.

Qaradawi writes: "Allah created man as a physical body and a transparent soul; a body that attracts him to the earth and a soul that looks forward to the heavens; a body that has instincts and lusts and a soul that has hopes and expectations."

This dual nature is not accidental — it is the very design that qualifies man for the vicegerency. Islam holds the middle ground: "The creed of Islam has not neglected the spirit for the sake of the clay, nor has it neglected the clay for the sake of the spirit; but rather it combines between both of them in a harmonious, congruous unity, giving each its own right without exaggeration or negligence."

### 3. The Trust of Responsibility

Qaradawi draws attention to Surah al-Ahzab (33:72):

> "We did indeed offer the Trust to the Heavens and the Earth and the Mountains; but they refused to undertake it, being afraid thereof: but man undertook it."

The "Trust" (amanah) is the responsibility of free will — the capacity to choose between good and evil, to obey or disobey, to accept or reject divine guidance. The heavens, the earth, and the mountains — all massive and powerful — refused this trust because they recognized its weight. Man alone accepted it. This acceptance, Qaradawi explains, is what gives man "choice concerning his destiny, because Allah has facilitated for him all means of guidance and removed all excuses."

### 4. The Honor of Iman: Dignity Beyond Humanity

Qaradawi then makes a crucial distinction. The honor described so far belongs to every human being by virtue of being human (karāmat al-insan). But there is a higher honor — the honor of faith (karāmat al-mu'min) — that elevates the believer to a station beyond mere humanity.

He writes: "In the capacity of his being a member in this nation of Iman, he feels greater dignity and even more honor."

The Qur'an declares:
- "You are the best of peoples, evolved for mankind" (3:110)
- "Thus have We made of you an Ummah justly balanced" (2:143)
- "He has chosen you, and has imposed no difficulties on you in religion" (22:78)
- "But honor belongs to Allah and His Messenger, and to the believers" (63:8)
- "And never will Allah grant to the unbelievers a way to triumph over the believers" (4:141)

The believer feels "that he is protected by Allah, the Beneficent the Merciful, through His help, support, care, and guidance." He knows that "Allah is the Protector of those who have Faith: from the depths of darkness He will lead them forth into light" (2:257).

### 5. Two Historical Examples: Bilal and Ribʿi

Qaradawi illustrates this dignity with two powerful historical examples:

**Bilal ibn Rabah** — a black slave who, through faith, "prided himself above the arrogant 'masters', and raised his head high." He looked at the leaders of Quraysh — Umayyah ibn Khalaf, Abu Jahl ibn Hisham — "in view of his being one endowed with eyesight looking at blind people, and one who is walking in the light at people who are groping in darkness." The Qur'an describes this: "Can he who was dead, to whom We gave life, and a light whereby he can walk amongst men, be like him who is in the depths of darkness?" (6:122).

**Ribʿi ibn ʿAmir** — an illiterate Bedouin from the desert, enlightened by the Islamic creed, who stood before Rustum, the general of the Persian armies. Surrounded by gold, silver, and the splendor of the Persian court, Rustum asked: "Who are you?" Ribʿi answered with the dignity of faith: "We are people sent by Allah to lead people away from the worship of creatures, to the worship of the Lord of creatures, alone; from the life of hardship to another life of ease; and from the injustice of doctrines to the justice of Islam."

### 6. Man's Position Before Allah

Qaradawi emphasizes that man's dignity is not an abstract concept — it is rooted in his direct, unmediated relationship with Allah. The Qur'an states:

- "When My servants ask thee concerning Me, I am indeed close to them: I listen to the prayer of every supplicant when he calleth on Me." (2:186)
- "We are nearer to him than his jugular vein." (50:16)
- "There is not a secret consultation between three, but He makes the fourth among them." (58:7)

The divine hadith quoted above — "I am as My servant thinks I am... if he draws near to Me a hand's span, I draw near to him an arm's length" — establishes that man's relationship with Allah is direct, personal, and immediate. No mediator, no priest, no intercessor stands between the believer and his Lord.

### 7. The Two Views Compared

Qaradawi concludes the chapter with a devastating comparison:

"There is a big difference between the following two men: one who lives with the belief that he is just an 'animal' from a high species that has no roots before his existence nor extension after his death... The other man, however, lives with the belief that he is a vicegerent of Allah on earth, who is assigned by Him to establish the truth, serve as a means to goodness, and spread beauty in this universe. He feels that the whole universe is subjected to his use, that the honorable angels protect him, that the Lord of all is with him, that he is one of those on whom Allah has bestowed His Grace, of the Prophets, the sincere, the martyrs, and the righteous, and that his existence does not end with death, for he was created for a life of eternity."

This, Qaradawi declares, is "one of the essential points in which the Islamic creed contradicts the materialistic ideology."

### 8. The Purpose of Man

Qaradawi closes with man's purpose: "Man was created to know Allah, worship Him, and be His vicegerent on earth. He was created to undertake the greater trust in this short life: the trust of duties and responsibilities, so that he may go through trials and obligations and thus be prepared for another life of eternity."

He quotes the Qur'an: "Did ye then think that We had created you in jest, and that ye would not be brought back to Us for account?" (23:115-116).

"It is truly great that man was not created for himself but to worship Allah. It is also great that he was not created for this perishable, mortal life, but for immortality!"

## Family Discussion (15 min)

The following questions are designed for all ages. Adapt language as needed for young children.

### Question 1 — What Makes a Person Valuable?

> What makes a person valuable? Is it what they look like, what they own, how famous they are — or something deeper?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let each family member answer. Young children may say "being kind" or "being nice." Guide them toward the idea that Allah created everyone in the "best of molds" and that our value comes from Him, not from what people think of us.

**Qaradawi's framework:** The materialist calculates a human body's worth as "fifty or sixty piasters" in chemicals. Islam says: "We have honored the sons of Adam" (17:70). Our value is not in our chemical composition but in the divine breath within us.

### Question 2 — How Does Knowing Allah Honored Us Change How We Treat Each Other?

> If every person we meet is someone Allah has honored — created in the best of molds, breathed into with His Spirit — how should that change the way we treat them? How should it change how we treat each other in this family?

**Facilitator guidance:** This question connects theology to daily family life. If we truly believe that every family member is Allah's honored creation, then teasing, name-calling, belittling, or ignoring take on a different gravity. We are not just hurting a person — we are disrespecting Allah's masterpiece.

### Question 3 — Has Anyone Ever Made You Feel Small?

> Has anyone ever made you feel like you are not important? How does the verse "the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the one with the most taqwa" help?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a vulnerable question. Allow silence. Share your own experience age-appropriately. The key teaching: the world measures us by appearance, wealth, and status. Allah measures us by taqwa — God-consciousness. A person the world considers "nobody" can be the most noble person in Allah's sight.

### Question 4 — Bilal and Ribʿi: Faith That Transforms

> Bilal was a slave, but faith made him stand above the masters of Quraysh. Ribʿi was a desert Bedouin, but faith made him stand before a king without fear. What gives a person that kind of inner strength?

**Facilitator guidance:** The answer Qaradawi leads us to: the knowledge that "I am a master in this universe and a servant of Allah alone." When you know your worth comes from Allah, no one can make you feel inferior. This is the dignity that faith produces.

### Question 5 — What Does "The Most Noble" Mean for Our Family?

> The verse says "the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the one with the most taqwa." What does taqwa look like in our home? Who is the "most noble" person in our family — and what makes them noble?

**Facilitator guidance:** This question should not create competition but reflection. Taqwa is not about who prays the most — it is about who is most aware of Allah in daily life, most sincere, most kind. Children often see what adults miss: "Mom is noble because she always helps us even when she's tired." This IS taqwa in action.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "The Honor of Our Family"

Each family member takes a turn. The others share one thing they appreciate about that person — focusing on **character, not achievement**. Use this structure:

- "I honor [name] because I see in them [character trait], and that reflects how Allah created them in the best of molds."

**Examples for children:**
- "I honor [sister] because she is kind to everyone, and kindness is a gift from Allah."
- "I honor [father] because he is patient with us, and patience is from taqwa."
- "I honor [mother] because she sacrifices for us, and sacrifice is what the best of peoples do."

**Goal:** Each family member hears, from the people who love them most, that their worth is rooted in how Allah created them — not in grades, salary, appearance, or social media.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of speaking, draw a picture of each family member and say one thing you love about them. Frame it as "Allah made [name] special because..."

### Qaradawi's Key Arguments (from Faith and Life, Ch. 1)

1. Materialism reduces man to chemicals worth "fifty piasters" and an "evolved animal"
2. Islam affirms man as Allah's vicegerent: created in the best of molds, breathed with divine spirit, honored by angelic prostration
3. Man's dual nature (body + soul) is the design that qualifies him for the vicegerency
4. The honor of faith (karāmat al-mu'min) surpasses the honor of humanity (karāmat al-insan)
5. Historical examples: Bilal (slave elevated by faith) and Ribʿi (Bedouin confronting a king)
6. Man's purpose: not created in jest, but for worship, vicegerency, and eternity
7. The middle ground: Islam honors both body and soul, neither asceticism nor materialism

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-01.txt`
- The chapter is approximately 42 pages in the original — read at minimum the sections "Man in the sight of the believers" (around line 163) through "The honor of Iman comes after the honor of humanity" (around line 517) and "Man between Islam and materialism" (around line 644)

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
Qaradawi makes a crucial two-level argument:
1. **Level 1 (universal):** Every human being is honored by Allah (karāmat al-insan) — this applies to Muslims and non-Muslims alike
2. **Level 2 (faith-specific):** The believer has an additional honor (karāmat al-mu'min) — faith elevates dignity beyond mere humanity

This distinction matters for family discussion: we respect every person because they are Allah's creation, AND we understand that faith adds a dimension of dignity that transforms how a person sees themselves and the world.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "Allah made you special" — the idea that each person is a unique, honored creation. Use the drawing activity. Skip the materialism discussion. |
| 7–12 | Tell the stories of Bilal and Ribʿi as adventure narratives. Ask: "How did Bilal feel strong even though he was a slave?" |
| 13–17 | Engage with the materialism vs. Islam contrast. Ask: "How does social media make you feel about your worth? How does this verse change that?" |
| Adults | Read the full source chapter beforehand. Discuss the philosophical contrast Qaradawi draws between Darwin/Freud and the Qur'anic vision. |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching section to points 2, 4, and 5 (the Islamic view, the honor of iman, and the Bilal/Ribʿi examples). Keep the full discussion and activity — these are where the learning happens.
