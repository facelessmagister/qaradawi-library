# Module 4.6 — Wise Use of Time

**Phase:** 4 — Leaves: Living Islam in the World
**Week:** 24
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that time is life — "our life is nothing but the time we spend between our birth and our death" — and that we are accountable to Allah for how we spend it. The session establishes the Prophetic framework: "Take advantage of five before five," and the Day of Judgment question: "How did you spend your life?"

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that time is not money — time is LIFE. Every minute we spend is a minute of our life we can never get back. We learn to use our time wisely, as the Prophet taught us.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — Your Feet Will Not Move Until Asked About Your Life

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "One's feet will not move on the Day of Resurrection before he is asked about his life, how he spent it; his youth, how he wore it out; his wealth, how he earned it and how he spent it; and his knowledge, how he used it."
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi** on the authority of Ibn Mas'ud — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.2

### Hadith 2 — Take Advantage of Five Before Five

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Take full advantage of five before five: your youth before your old age, your health before your sickness, your wealth before your poverty, your life before your death, and your free time before your busy time."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Hakim** on the authority of Ibn Abbas — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.2

### Hadith 3 — Whoever Begins the Morning Safe and Healthy

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Whoever begins the day feeling secure with his family and healthy with enough provisions for the day is as though he owns the whole world."
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi** on the authority of Ubaydullah ibn Muhsin al-Ansari — cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.2

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Wise Use of Time" (pp. 344-345). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Qur'an Draws Attention to Time Through Divine Oaths

Qaradawi writes: "The Holy Qur'an draws our attention to the value of time by including several divine oaths that use time periods":
- "By the night when it covers" (92:1)
- "By the dawn" (89:1)
- "By the morning brightness" (93:1)
- **"By time" (103:1)**

When Allah swears by something, it is to draw attention to its importance. The Qur'an swears by time — "By time, indeed, mankind is in loss" — making time itself the subject of a divine oath. The implication: time is so valuable that Allah Himself swears by it.

The conclusion of the oath: all of mankind is in LOSS — except those who (1) believe, (2) do righteous deeds, (3) join together in truth, and (4) join together in patience. Time without these four = loss. Time with these four = profit. Every moment is either profit or loss.

### 2. The Day of Judgment Question

Qaradawi cites the hadith: "One's feet will not move on the Day of Resurrection before he is asked about:
- **His life** — how he spent it
- **His youth** — how he wore it out
- **His wealth** — how he earned it and how he spent it
- **His knowledge** — how he used it"

Four questions about four trusts: life, youth, wealth, knowledge. Each was given by Allah. Each will be accounted for. The believer who is conscious of these questions lives differently — because they know that every hour, every dirham, and every piece of knowledge will be audited.

For families: this means teaching children that their TIME is a trust. The hours they spend on screens, in study, in play, in worship — all will be asked about. Not to create anxiety, but to create AWARENESS: time is not disposable; it is accountable.

### 3. Five Before Five

Qaradawi presents the Prophetic framework: "Take full advantage of five before five":

| What You Have Now | What You Will Lose |
|---|---|
| Youth | Old age |
| Health | Sickness |
| Wealth | Poverty |
| Life | Death |
| Free time | Busy time |

Each pairing is a wake-up call:
- **Youth before old age**: the energy and vitality of youth are temporary — use them before they fade
- **Health before sickness**: the ability to act is temporary — act before illness prevents you
- **Wealth before poverty**: the resources you have are temporary — give and spend before they're gone
- **Life before death**: your very existence is temporary — worship before it ends
- **Free time before busy time**: the leisure you have now will be consumed by obligations — use it wisely

For families: this is especially relevant for children and teenagers. "Your youth is a gift. Your health is a gift. Your free time is a gift. Use them before they're gone." The teenager who wastes years on mindless scrolling will one day wish they had used that time to learn, to grow, to serve.

### 4. Time Is Life

Qaradawi cites two powerful statements:

**Hassan al-Banna:** "Our life is nothing but the time we spend between our birth and our death."

**Imam al-Hasan al-Basri:** "You are nothing but a limited number of days. With every day that passes, part of you is gone."

The traditional saying: "Every day, as dawn breaks, dawn calls on people: 'O children of Adam, I am a new creation and a witness to what you do. Take what you can from me, because once I am gone, I will never be back.'"

This is the Islamic philosophy of time: time = life. Wasting time is not just inefficient — it is literally wasting your life. Every hour spent in pointless activity is an hour of your existence that you can never recover.

### 5. The Qur'an Shows Those Who Regret Wasted Time

Qaradawi cites two Qur'anic passages showing the regret of those who wasted their time:

**The one who delays charity until death:** "My Lord, if only You would delay me for a brief term so I would give to charity and be among the righteous. But never will Allah delay a soul when its time has come" (63:10-11).

**The people of Hell:** "They will cry out therein: 'Our Lord, remove us; we will do righteousness — other than what we were doing!' But did We not grant you life enough for whoever would remember therein to remember, and the warner had come to you?" (35:37).

The message: you HAD time. You had enough time. The warner came to you. But you did not use it. Now it is too late. This is the ultimate tragedy of wasted time — not that you failed to achieve something, but that you cannot get the time back to try again.

### 6. Special Times with Multiplied Rewards

Qaradawi notes: "Muslims should use their time wisely, and focus on acts of worship that are performed at special times, such as dawn, Friday, and Ramadan, as rewards from these times are multiplied more than any other time."

Certain times carry multiplied value:
- **The last third of the night** — Allah descends to the nearest heaven and asks: "Who is calling upon Me?"
- **Friday** — there is an hour when supplications are accepted
- **Ramadan** — the entire month carries multiplied rewards
- **The first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah** — "there are no days in which righteous deeds are more beloved to Allah"

The wise believer recognises these "golden hours" and invests in them — because the return is multiplied.

### 7. Time Management as Worship

For families, Qaradawi's teaching translates into practical time management as a form of worship:

| Time Investment | Return |
|---|---|
| Prayer on time | Spiritual connection + multiplied reward |
| Quality family time | Strong relationships + following the Sunnah |
| Learning and study | Knowledge that benefits + preparation for accountability |
| Serving others | Charity + community building |
| Rest and sleep (with intention) | Renewal for worship + health |
| Meaningless scrolling | Loss — no return, no benefit, accountable |
| Gossip and arguments | Loss — sin + wasted life |

The believer's time management is not about productivity for its own sake — it is about investing the trust of time in what Allah loves.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — By Time, Man Is in Loss

> Allah swears "By time, indeed mankind is in loss." What does it mean that ALL of mankind is in loss — except those who believe and do good? Is time really that serious?

**Facilitator guidance:** Yes — time is that serious. Every moment that passes without faith, good deeds, truth, and patience is a LOSS. The Qur'an does not say "some people are in loss" — it says ALL of mankind is in loss, except a specific category. This makes time management a spiritual issue, not just a productivity issue.

### Question 2 — The Four Questions

> On the Day of Judgment, you will be asked: "How did you spend your life? How did you use your youth? How did you earn and spend your wealth? How did you use your knowledge?" Which of these questions worries you most?

**Facilitator guidance:** Let each person answer honestly. For children: "How are you spending your youth? Are you using your energy for good things?" For adults: "How are you spending your life? Are you using your time for what matters?" The goal is not anxiety but awareness.

### Question 3 — Five Before Five

> The Prophet said: "Take advantage of five before five." Which of the five is most relevant to you right now? Youth? Health? Free time?

**Facilitator guidance:** For children: "You have youth and health and free time — these are gifts that won't last forever. Use them now!" For adults: "You may have health now — use it before sickness comes. You may have wealth now — give before poverty comes." For the elderly: "You have life — worship before death comes."

### Question 4 — Time Is Life

> Hassan al-Banna said: "Our life is nothing but the time we spend between birth and death." If time IS life, what does it mean to "waste time"? What are we actually wasting?

**Facilitator guidance:** Wasting time = wasting life. Every hour of mindless scrolling is an hour of your life that you will never get back. Every hour of gossip is an hour of your life spent on something that has no eternal value. The question is not "am I being productive?" but "am I spending my LIFE well?"

### Question 5 — Our Family's Time

> What is one way our family wastes time? What is one change we can make to use our time better?

**Facilitator guidance:** Common time-wasters: excessive screen time, scrolling social media, watching meaningless content, arguing about trivial things, procrastinating on important tasks. Agree on ONE concrete change: "We will limit screen time to X hours" or "We will have one screen-free evening per week" or "We will read Qur'an together for 15 minutes daily."

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Time Audit"

Track how each person spends ONE full day. Use a simple log:

| Time | Activity | Worship / Benefit / Neutral / Waste |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00-7:00 | | |
| 7:00-8:00 | | |
| ... | | |

**At the next halaqah:** Review together. Categorise each hour as:
- **Worship** (prayer, Qur'an, dhikr)
- **Benefit** (study, work, family time, exercise, service)
- **Neutral** (eating, commuting, necessary rest)
- **Waste** (mindless scrolling, pointless arguments, excessive entertainment)

**Discuss:**
- What percentage of the day was worship? Benefit? Neutral? Waste?
- What is ONE change that would shift more time from "waste" to "benefit" or "worship"?
- How does this connect to "your feet will not move until asked about your life"?

**Goal:** To make the family CONSCIOUS of how they spend time — because consciousness is the first step to change. The time audit is not about guilt — it's about awareness. "You are nothing but a limited number of days. With every day that passes, part of you is gone" (al-Hasan al-Basri).
