# Module 2.2 — Modesty (Haya'): The Distinctive Mark of Islam

**Phase:** 2 — Trunk: Character and Inner Purification
**Week:** 8
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that modesty (haya') is a branch of faith for both men and women, that it begins in the heart, and that it is the distinctive characteristic of Islam. The session establishes that modesty is not weakness or timidity — a modest person can be brave, strong, and assertive while maintaining inner dignity.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that modesty is not just about clothing — it is a quality of the heart that shapes everything: how we dress, how we speak, how we behave online, and how we treat each other.

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — Modesty Is a Branch of Faith

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Faith consists of 60 (or 70) branches. The best of them is to profess that 'There is no Allah but Allah.' The lowest of them is to remove harmful things from the road. **Modesty is also a branch of faith.**"
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (24)** and **Muslim (59)** on the authority of Ibn Amr

### Hadith 2 — Modesty Brings Nothing but Good

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Modesty is part of faith, and it brings nothing but good."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** on the authority of Ibn Amr

### Hadith 3 — The Distinctive Characteristic of Islam

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Every religion has its distinctive characteristic, and **modesty is the distinctive characteristic of Islam.**"
>
> — **Narrated by Ibn Majah (4181)** on the authority of Anas ibn Malik

### Hadith 4 — If You Have No Modesty, Do Whatever You Like

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "One of the sayings of the earlier prophets is: 'If you have no sense of modesty, do whatever you like.'"
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (3483)** on the authority of Abu Masoud al-Ansari

### Hadith 5 — The Prophet Was More Modest Than a Virgin

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri described the Prophet (peace be upon him):

> "He was more modest than a virgin behind her veil."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** on the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Modesty" (pp. 333-334). All citations refer to this section unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. What Is Modesty?

Qaradawi defines modesty (haya') as "being repulsed by immoral acts, and it is manifested in intrinsically rejecting anything that is undesirable from a religious or cultural perspective."

Modesty is an **inner disposition** — a natural aversion to anything shameful, immodest, or morally repugnant. It is not merely an external behavior (like covering the body) — it is an internal quality that produces external behavior. A person with haya' feels discomfort at the thought of doing something wrong, even when no one would find out.

### 2. Modesty as Intelligence in Children

Qaradawi cites the Islamic historian **Ibn Miskawayh** (d. AH 421), who wrote in his book *Tahdhib al-Akhlaq*:

> "The first sign of sharp intellect in children is modesty. It reflects their ability to discern what is good and what is not. Children with a good sense of modesty are fully aware of acceptable behavior. They avoid unacceptable behaviors and fear to be seen acting in an unacceptable way. **If you see a child staring down modestly and avoiding eye contact, you know this is an intelligent child.**"

This is a profound insight: modesty is not a sign of backwardness or shyness — it is a sign of **intelligence**. A child who naturally feels uncomfortable around inappropriate behavior is showing discernment — the ability to distinguish good from bad. This is the beginning of moral character.

### 3. The Prophet as the Model of Modesty

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri describes the Prophet as "more modest than a virgin behind her veil." This comparison is deliberate: a virgin behind her veil represents the pinnacle of natural modesty in Arab culture. The Prophet — a man, a leader, a commander of armies — was MORE modest than this. His modesty was not weakness; it was the natural expression of his inner dignity.

### 4. Modesty Is NOT Weakness

Qaradawi makes a critical distinction:

> "Modesty should not be confused with weakness, cowardice, or timidity. A faithful person has the courage of a lion when it comes to defending what is true and right. Being modest does not in any way mean being passive when one's rights are violated."

This is essential: the Prophet was "more modest than a virgin" AND he stood before the kings of the earth, confronted the leaders of Quraysh, and led armies. Modesty did not make him passive — it made him dignified. He was modest in private conduct and courageous in public duty.

Qaradawi adds: "Modesty should not impede anyone from learning about sciences or religion or from the pursuit of the truth." Allah Himself "does not shy away from presenting an example — that of a mosquito or what is smaller than it" (2:26). Modesty that prevents learning, speaking the truth, or standing for justice is not true modesty — it is false shyness.

### 5. The Poetic Test: Abu Tammam

Qaradawi cites the classical poet Abu Tammam, who captures the essence of modesty in verse:

> *If you do not fear the consequences of what you do at night,*
> *And if you have no sense of modesty, then do as you might.*
> *Nothing good is left in this life, if the beacon of modesty goes dark.*
> *Modest people live in joy, for a tree thrives as long as it has bark.*

The metaphor of the tree is powerful: modesty is like the bark of a tree. The bark protects the tree, keeps it strong, and allows it to thrive. Without bark, the tree is exposed, vulnerable, and eventually dies. Without modesty, a person is exposed, vulnerable, and eventually loses their moral foundation.

### 6. Modesty in Speech, Dress, and Digital Life

While Qaradawi's text was written before the digital age, the principles he establishes apply directly to contemporary life:

| Domain | What Modesty Looks Like |
|---|---|
| Speech | Not speaking in a way that attracts inappropriate attention (Q 33:32); not using crude or vulgar language |
| Dress | Covering what should be covered; not displaying adornment except what is apparent (Q 24:31) |
| Behavior | Not seeking attention through immodest conduct; maintaining dignity in all interactions |
| Digital | Not posting inappropriate photos; not sharing private details publicly; not engaging in inappropriate online conversations |
| Gaze | Lowering the gaze — both men and women (Q 24:30-31); "Do not give a second look" — the first is accidental, the second is a choice |

### 7. Modesty for Men and Women Alike

Qaradawi explicitly states: "Modesty, for men and women alike, is a highly appreciated moral value in Islam." This is crucial — modesty is NOT only for women. The Qur'an commands BOTH men and women to lower their gaze (24:30-31). The Prophet — a man — was "more modest than a virgin." The hadith "modesty is the distinctive characteristic of Islam" applies to the entire community, not one gender.

In the family context:
- Fathers model modesty for sons (how they speak, how they look at others, how they conduct themselves)
- Mothers model modesty for daughters (how they dress, how they interact, how they carry themselves)
- Both parents model modesty in how they interact with each other (not flirting publicly, not crude jokes, not inappropriate conversations in front of children)

### 8. The Loss of Modesty

The hadith "If you have no sense of modesty, do whatever you like" is both a description and a warning. It describes the state of a person who has lost modesty: without the inner compass of shame, there is no internal restraint. Such a person will do anything — because nothing feels wrong.

This is the ultimate danger: when a society loses its collective sense of modesty, external laws cannot substitute. You cannot legislate what people feel in their hearts. Modesty is the internal compass that makes external laws unnecessary — because the person with haya' restrains themselves before anyone else needs to.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — What Does Modesty Look Like?

> What does modesty look like for a boy/man? For a girl/woman? Is modesty only about clothing, or is it more than that?

**Facilitator guidance:** This question opens the discussion. The answer Qaradawi leads us to: modesty is primarily a quality of the HEART — an inner aversion to anything shameful. It manifests in dress, speech, behavior, and digital life — but the source is internal. A person can dress modestly but have no modesty in their heart (if they are arrogant or crude). A person with true modesty will naturally dress, speak, and behave with dignity.

**For children:** "Modesty means feeling uncomfortable when something is not right. Like when someone tells a dirty joke — a modest person doesn't laugh, because it feels wrong."

### Question 2 — Modest But Brave

> The Prophet was "more modest than a virgin behind her veil" but he was also brave enough to stand before kings and lead armies. How can someone be both modest and brave? Are they opposites?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the key distinction Qaradawi makes: modesty ≠ weakness. The Prophet was the most modest of people AND the most courageous. Modesty is about dignity — it means not lowering yourself to inappropriate behavior. Courage is about standing up for what's right — even when it's hard. A person can be both: modest in private conduct, courageous in public duty.

### Question 3 — The First Sign of Intelligence

> Ibn Miskawayh said: "The first sign of sharp intellect in children is modesty." What does that mean? Does being modest make you smart?

**Facilitator guidance:** Ibn Miskawayh's insight: modesty is a form of discernment. A child who naturally avoids inappropriate behavior is showing that they can tell the difference between right and wrong — and that is the beginning of intelligence. A child who has no sense of modesty will do anything, go anywhere, watch anything — because they lack the inner filter that says "this is not for me."

### Question 4 — Modesty and Phones

> How does modesty apply to how we use phones, social media, and the internet? What does it mean to be modest online?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the contemporary application. Online modesty includes:
- Not posting photos that seek inappropriate attention
- Not sharing private family details publicly
- Not following accounts that post immodest content
- Lowering the gaze while scrolling (closing or skipping inappropriate content)
- Not engaging in inappropriate conversations with non-mahram people

The principle: if you wouldn't do it in front of your parents, don't do it on your phone.

### Question 5 — Modest With Each Other

> How can we show more modesty with each other in our family? Are there things we do at home that we should be more modest about?

**Facilitator guidance:** Modesty starts at home. Family members should be modest with each other:
- Knocking before entering bedrooms
- Dressing appropriately even at home (especially in front of non-mahram family members)
- Not discussing inappropriate topics in front of younger children
- Not listening to each other's private conversations
- Respecting each other's privacy

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Modesty Audit"

Together as a family, review ONE day's media consumption (phones, TV, YouTube, games, music). For each item, ask:

1. **Was this modest?** Did it promote dignity, respect, and appropriate behavior? Or did it promote immodesty, crudeness, or inappropriate content?
2. **Would the Prophet watch this?** If the Prophet — "more modest than a virgin" — were sitting with us, would we be comfortable watching/listening to this?
3. **What change should we make?**

**Family agreement:** Write down 3 family rules for media and modesty. Examples:
- "We will not watch content that promotes immodesty"
- "We will lower our gaze and skip inappropriate content when we encounter it"
- "We will not post photos that seek inappropriate attention"
- "We will not follow accounts that make us feel less modest"

**Sign it, post it, review it at next halaqah.**

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of an audit, play "Modest or Not Modest" — name situations and have children say "modest" or "not modest":
- "Looking at something inappropriate on a screen" → Not modest
- "Covering properly when guests come" → Modest
- "Using bad words" → Not modest
- "Knocking before entering a room" → Modest

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-04-02.txt` (the "Modesty" subsection, approximately lines 151-200)

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **modesty is NOT weakness.** The Prophet was the most modest of people AND the bravest. These are not contradictions — they are complementary. Modesty is dignity (not lowering yourself), courage is strength (standing up for what's right). A person can be both.

| False Modesty | True Modesty |
|---|---|
| Shyness that prevents speaking the truth | Dignity that restrains from inappropriate behavior |
| Fear of social situations | Inner compass that says "this is not for me" |
| Passive acceptance of wrong | Active avoidance of wrong |
| Hiding from the world | Engaging with the world with dignity |

### The Bark of the Tree
Abu Tammam's metaphor is the most teachable image: modesty = bark. Without bark, the tree is exposed to disease, insects, and weather — it eventually dies. Without modesty, a person is exposed to moral corruption, peer pressure, and cultural decay — they eventually lose their character.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "modesty means feeling uncomfortable when something is wrong." Use the "Modest or Not Modest" game. Skip the digital life discussion. |
| 7–12 | Tell them: "The Prophet was braver than anyone, but he was also more modest than anyone. You can be both!" Discuss: what does modesty look like at school? |
| 13–17 | The media audit is essential. Ask: "When you scroll through social media, do you lower your gaze? Or do you look at everything?" Connect to the Qur'anic command for BOTH men and women. |
| Adults | Read the full source section. Discuss Ibn Miskawayh's insight about modesty as intelligence. Discuss how to model modesty for children — not through rules but through example. |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching to sections 1 (what is modesty), 4 (not weakness), and 6 (modesty in daily life). Keep the full discussion — Questions 2 and 4 are essential for practical application.
