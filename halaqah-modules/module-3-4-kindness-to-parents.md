# Module 3.4 — Kindness to Parents (Birr al-Walidayn)

**Phase:** 3 — Branches: The Family Unit
**Week:** 16
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that kindness to parents — especially in old age — is second only to worshipping Allah. The session establishes that disobedience to parents is a major sin, that the mother's rights are stressed three times before the father's, and that kindness extends even to non-Muslim parents.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to honour the people who brought us into this world — our parents. We learn that after Allah, no one deserves our kindness more than our parents, and that even "uff" — the smallest expression of annoyance — is prohibited.

>
> — **Surah Luqman (31:14-15)**

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — Your Mother, Three Times, Then Your Father

A man came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and asked:

> "Who is most deserving of my good companionship?" The Prophet replied: "Your mother." He asked: "Who is next?" The Prophet replied: "Your mother." He asked: "Who is next?" The Prophet replied: "Your mother." He asked: "Who is next?" The Prophet replied: "Your father."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

### Hadith 2 — Three Major Sins

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Shall I not inform you about the three major sins?" They said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." He said: **"Associating partners with Allah, disobedience to parents,"** and sitting up from his reclining position, he continued: "and telling lies and false testimony; beware of it."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim**

### Hadith 3 — Punishment in This Life

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Allah defers the punishment of all sins to the Day of Resurrection excepting disobedience to parents, for which Allah punishes the sinner in this life before his death."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Hakim** with a sound chain of transmitters

### Hadith 4 — Strive in Their Service

A man came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and asked permission to go for jihad. The Prophet asked:

> "Are your parents living?" "Yes," he replied. The Prophet said: **"Then strive in their service."**
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** on the authority of Abdullah ibn Amr

### Hadith 5 — Return to Them and Make Them Laugh

A man came to the Prophet and said: "I have come to swear allegiance to you for Hijrah, and I have left my parents weeping." The Prophet said:

> "Return to them and make them laugh as well as you made them weep."
>
> — **Narrated by Abu Dawud**

### Hadith 6 — Cursing Parents

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Among the major sins is a man's cursing his parents." They asked: "How can a man curse his own parents?" He replied: "He insults another man's father, and then the other insults his father, and he insults the other's mother, and the other returns the insult to his mother."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim**

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam** (الحلال والحرام), Chapter 3: "The Relationship Between Parents and Children" (pp. 247-255). All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. Disobedience to Parents: Second Only to Shirk

Qaradawi establishes the gravity of this topic at the outset:

"It is the right of parents that their children should treat them with kindness, obedience, and honor. Devotion to parents is a natural instinct which must be strengthened by deliberate actions."

The Qur'an pairs the command to worship Allah with the command to be kind to parents — in the same verse, with the same divine decree: "Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him **and** that you be kind to parents" (17:23). This pairing is not accidental — it elevates kindness to parents to a level immediately after worship of Allah.

The Prophet declared disobedience to parents as one of the three major sins — second only to shirk (associating partners with Allah): "Associating partners with Allah, disobedience to parents, and telling lies and false testimony."

### 2. The Mother: Three Times Before the Father

Qaradawi highlights the extraordinary emphasis on the mother's rights:

"The rights of the mother are stressed more because of her suffering during pregnancy and childbirth, her suckling of the child, and her role in rearing it."

The hadith is one of the most famous in Islam: "Who is most deserving of my good companionship?" → "Your mother" (three times) → then "Your father." The repetition is deliberate — the mother's sacrifice is three times: pregnancy, childbirth, and nursing. Each "your mother" corresponds to one of these sacrifices.

The Qur'an confirms: "His mother carries him in pain and she gives birth to him in pain, and (the period) of carrying him and weaning him is thirty months" (46:15). The physical, emotional, and spiritual investment of the mother is the basis for her elevated rights.

### 3. Not Even "Uff"

The Qur'an's instruction is breathtaking in its precision:

"If one or both of them attain old age with you, **do not say 'uff' to them** nor repulse them, but speak to them in gracious words."

Qaradawi cites the commentator who said: "If a lesser thing than saying 'uff' to parents, as a sign of ill-treatment to them, were known, Allah would have prohibited it."

"Uff" is the smallest expression of annoyance — the equivalent of a sigh, a grunt, an eye-roll, a "tsk." If even THIS is prohibited, then what about shouting? What about ignoring? What about sarcasm? What about the silent treatment? The prohibition of "uff" establishes a floor — and the floor is already very high.

The verse adds: "in mercy lower to them the wing of humility" — the same phrase used in Module 2.3 for humility. The image is of a bird lowering its wing to protect and shelter its young. Now the roles are reversed: the child shelters the aging parent with the same tenderness the parent once gave the child.

### 4. Old Age: The Real Test

Qaradawi draws attention to a crucial phrase: "**If one or both of them attain old age with you.**"

The command is general — always be kind to parents. But the specific, detailed instruction addresses the situation of old age. Why? Because old age is when kindness is hardest and most needed.

When parents are young and strong, kindness is easy — they don't need much. When they are old and weak, kindness is tested:
- They repeat stories → patience is tested
- They need help with basic tasks → service is tested
- They become forgetful → tolerance is tested
- They become slow → patience is tested
- They become dependent → generosity is tested

The Qur'an acknowledges this: "As their strength fails, they require more attention and care, more consideration of them, and even more sensitive feelings." The command to not say "uff" is specifically for this stage — because this is when "uff" is most tempting.

### 5. Parents' Consent for Jihad

Qaradawi presents one of the most striking examples of parental honor in Islam:

"Pleasing one's parents is considered so important in Islam that the son is forbidden to volunteer for jihad without his parent's permission, in spite of the fact that fighting in the cause of Allah has such great merit in Islam that the merit of a person who spends his nights in prayer and his days in fasting fall short of it."

Multiple hadith confirm this:
- A man asked permission for jihad. Prophet: "Are your parents living?" Man: "Yes." Prophet: "Then strive in their service."
- A man pledged Hijrah and jihad. Prophet: "Are your parents living?" Man: "Both." Prophet: "Then go back and be a good companion to them."
- A man left his parents weeping. Prophet: "Return to them and make them laugh as well as you made them weep."
- A man from Yemen emigrated without permission. Prophet: "Go back and ask their permission. If they agree, go on jihad; otherwise stay and serve them."

The implication: serving parents IS a form of jihad — a form so important that it can replace armed jihad. The Prophet redirected the man's religious zeal from the battlefield to the bedside of his parents.

### 6. Kindness to Non-Muslim Parents

Qaradawi highlights what he calls "one of the beauties of Islam":

"It forbids the Muslim to be disrespectful to [parents] even if they should be non-Muslims who are fanatical to the point of arguing with him and putting pressure on him to renounce Islam."

The Qur'an: "Be grateful to Me and to your parents... But if they endeavor to make you associate with Me that of which you have no knowledge, do not obey them but **accompany them in this world with appropriate kindness**, and follow the way of those who turn back to Me" (31:14-15).

The balance is extraordinary:
- **Do not obey them** in shirk — there is no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator
- **BUT accompany them with kindness** — their disbelief does not cancel their rights as parents
- Leave judgment to Allah — "to Me will be your return"

Qaradawi comments: "Indeed, such tolerant and beneficent teachings are not to be found in any other religion." The Muslim whose parents try to turn them away from Islam must still treat them with honor, kindness, and companionship.

### 7. Cursing Parents: Even Indirectly

The Prophet declared cursing parents a major sin. When asked how someone could curse their own parents, he explained: "He insults another man's father, and then the other insults his father, and he insults the other's mother, and the other returns the insult to his mother."

This means: if you insult someone's parents, you are indirectly causing your own parents to be insulted in return. The chain of insults leads back to your own mother and father. Therefore, insulting anyone's parents is effectively cursing your own.

**For the family:** this teaches children to never insult anyone's parents — not at school, not online, not in arguments. "Your friend's mom is..." → leads to "Your mom is..." → you have just caused your own mother to be insulted.

### 8. Punishment in This Life

The Prophet said: "Allah defers the punishment of all sins to the Day of Resurrection excepting disobedience to parents, for which Allah punishes the sinner in this life before his death."

Qaradawi treats this as a solemn warning: disobedience to parents is the ONE sin whose punishment is not delayed. It begins in this life. The person who mistreats their parents may see the consequences — in their relationships, their children's behavior toward them, their health, their circumstances — before they even reach the Hereafter.

The lesson for the family: how you treat your parents is how your children will learn to treat you. If you are kind to your aging parents, your children will be kind to you when you age. If you are neglectful, your children will learn neglect.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Why After Allah?

> Why does Allah pair the command to worship Him with the command to be kind to parents — in the same verse? What does this tell us about the importance of parents?

**Facilitator guidance:** The pairing elevates kindness to parents to the highest level after worship of Allah. It tells us: after your relationship with Allah, your relationship with your parents is the most important relationship you have. No friend, no spouse, no teacher, no boss comes before parents in the Islamic hierarchy of obligations.

### Question 2 — Your Mother, Three Times

> The Prophet said "your mother" three times before "your father." Why? What does the mother's sacrifice teach us about why she deserves three times the priority?

**Facilitator guidance:** Pregnancy (9 months of physical burden), childbirth (the most intense pain), and nursing (2 years of constant care). Three sacrifices, three "your mother." Ask: "Has anyone here seen what Mom went through during pregnancy? What about childbirth? What about nursing? That's why she gets three."

### Question 3 — Not Even "Uff"

> The Qur'an says don't even say "uff" — the smallest expression of annoyance. What does "uff" look like today? An eye-roll? A sigh? A "tsk"? Are we guilty of saying "uff" to our parents?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a conviction question. Everyone has sighed at a parent's request, rolled their eyes at a repeated question, or responded with annoyance. The Qur'an prohibits even this. Ask: "When was the last time you made a face when your parent asked you to do something? That's 'uff.'"

### Question 4 — Old Age: The Real Test

> The Qur'an specifically mentions old age: "if one or both of them attain old age with you." Why is kindness hardest when parents are old? What does it look like to "lower the wing of humility" to aging parents?

**Facilitator guidance:** Old age is when parents need you most and when kindness is tested most. They repeat stories, need help with technology, move slowly, forget things. The Qur'an says: speak gracious words, lower the wing, say "My Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me." Ask: "How do we treat our elderly parents or grandparents? Do we have the same patience they had when we were children?"

### Question 5 — Jihad or Parents?

> The Prophet told a man who wanted to fight for Allah: "Go back and serve your parents." Serving parents replaced jihad! What does this tell us about the value of caring for parents?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is one of the most striking teachings in Islam. Jihad — the highest merit in Islam — was deferred because the parents needed service. This means: if your parents need you, you do not need to go looking for "greater" acts of worship. Serving them IS the greatest act of worship available to you right now.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Call or Visit Grandparents"

Each family member calls or visits a grandparent this week. During the call or visit, each person:

1. **Asks one question about their life** — "What was your childhood like?" "How did you and Grandma meet?" "What was the happiest moment of your life?"
2. **Listens attentively** — without interrupting, without checking the phone, without rushing
3. **Thanks them for something specific** — "Thank you for praying for me" / "Thank you for teaching me [skill]" / "Thank you for being patient with me"

**At the next halaqah:** Share what you learned. Discuss:
- What did you learn about your grandparent that you didn't know?
- How did it feel to listen without rushing?
- How does this connect to "lower the wing of humility"?

**Goal:** To practice birr al-walidayn (kindness to parents) through the grandparents. The Qur'an says: "My Lord, bestow Your mercy on them as they cherished me when I was little." The call or visit is a practical expression of this du'a.

**For children:** Frame it as "Let's call Grandma/Grandpa and ask them to tell us a story from when they were young!" Children love stories — and grandparents love telling them. This creates a natural bridge between generations.

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **not even "uff."** The Qur'an's precision is breathtaking. It doesn't say "don't hit your parents" (that's obvious). It doesn't say "don't insult your parents" (that's also obvious). It says: don't even say "UFF" — the smallest, most minor expression of annoyance. The sigh. The eye-roll. The grunt.

This establishes that the floor of kindness to parents is already very high. If "uff" is prohibited, then everything above "uff" — shouting, ignoring, sarcasm, impatience — is doubly prohibited.

### The Three "Your Mother"
This is one of the most memorable teachings in all of Islam. The repetition is not redundant — it is proportional. Three sacrifices (pregnancy, childbirth, nursing) → three "your mother." For children: "Mom carried you for 9 months, went through the pain of birth, and fed you for 2 years. That's why she gets three."
