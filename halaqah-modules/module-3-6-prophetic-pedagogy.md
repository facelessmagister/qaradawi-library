# Module 3.6 — Prophetic Pedagogy: Raising and Educating Children

**Phase:** 3 — Branches: The Family Unit
**Week:** 18
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand the Prophet's educational methods and commit to applying them in the home. The session establishes Qaradawi's presentation of 19 Prophetic educational principles — particularly gradual instruction, individual differences, mercy toward students, and the use of questions and stories — as the model for Islamic parenting and education.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn how the Prophet educated his companions — the same principles that apply to how we raise our children. The Prophet was the greatest teacher; his methods are our model.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Source: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Education and Economy in the Sunnah** (التربية والاقتصاد في السنة), Chapter 1: "The Sunnah and Education." All citations refer to this chapter unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Prophet as the Greatest Teacher

Qaradawi opens with a powerful statement:

"The chief and principal teacher of humanity — Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) — was the best to take these factors into consideration from both aspects, theoretical and practical."

He notes that the Prophet, "even though being illiterate, cared about knowledge and praised scholars." The Prophet established educational principles that "anticipate many values attributed to modern pedagogy" — principles that "many people, even among the Muslims, still believe are an outcome of modern contemporary studies that nobody came to know but the West."

### 2. The 19 Educational Principles

Qaradawi catalogues 19 distinct educational guidelines from the Prophet's practice:

1. Learning and related manners
2. What is obligatory for each Muslim to learn
3. Observing the right niyyah (intention)
4. Continuous seeking of knowledge
5. Being patient with the problems associated with seeking knowledge
6. Treating the teacher with dignity and asking questions in a polite manner
7. Caring of the Muslim society about the scholars and stressing their esteem
8. Solidarity of the society in teaching its members
9. Welcoming the student and being cheerful with him
10. Being merciful and sympathetic towards the student
11. Rewarding the good students and praising them
12. Feeling pity towards the mistaken ones
13. Assuming a gradual attitude in teaching and making things easy rather than difficult
14. Taking individual differences and variations into consideration
15. Being moderate in the amount being taught and avoiding putting the student in a state of boredom
16. Making good use of practical situations for the sake of education and guidance
17. Making use of educational aids
18. Choosing the best modes (like similitude, giving examples, telling stories)
19. Attracting the student's attention by asking questions and leading live conversation

### 3. The Core Principle: Individual Differences

Qaradawi selects one principle for special focus: **taking individual differences into consideration.**

"What is right for one person is not necessarily right for another, and what is suitable for a certain environment is not necessarily suitable for the other. What is right for a certain group or sex is not necessarily right for another, also what could be right for a certain period of time or age is not always suitable for other times or ages."

He explains: "A clever teacher is one who gives every human being — whether individually or in groups — his share of knowledge which suits him most and which is right for him to take. This share is also given in a suitable amount and at the time which is most beneficial for the student."

### 4. Evidence: Different Advice to Different People

Qaradawi provides multiple examples of the Prophet applying this principle:

**Different advice to different individuals:**
- To one: "Worship Allah, perform prayers, pay Zakah, keep good relations with relatives."
- To another: "Fear Allah wherever you are, follow up a bad deed with a good one, behave well towards people."
- To another: "Say: I believe in Allah, and thereafter be upright."
- To another: "Do not get angry" (and he added ten more).

The Prophet "took into consideration the condition of the person seeking the advice, and gave each of them what he needed most. So, he dealt with the questioners the same way a doctor would deal with his patients, giving each the kind of medicine he needs most."

**Different answers to the same question:**
The Prophet was asked "Which deed is best?" by different people and gave different answers:
- To Ibn Mas'ud: "Prayer at its proper time" → "Good to parents" → "Jihad"
- To a Khath'ami man: "Belief in Allah" → "Keeping relations with kinship"
- To another: "Hajj mabrur"
- To another: "Jihad in Allah's cause"

Why different answers? Because each person needed to hear a different priority. The person who was struggling with prayer needed to hear "prayer." The person who was neglecting family needed to hear "kinship." The Prophet diagnosed the spiritual need and prescribed the spiritual medicine — just as a physician prescribes different remedies to different patients.

### 5. Gradual Instruction

Principle 13: "Assuming a gradual attitude in teaching and making things easy rather than difficult."

The Prophet did not overwhelm new Muslims with the entire body of Islamic law at once. He introduced teachings gradually — starting with the most essential (tawhid, prayer) and building from there. This is the Prophetic model: start with what the student can absorb, build gradually, and never make the path so difficult that they give up.

For families: this means not expecting a 7-year-old to pray all five prayers perfectly on day one. Start with one prayer. When that becomes habit, add another. Gradual, not overwhelming. Easy, not difficult.

### 6. Mercy and Sympathy Toward Students

Principles 9-12 address the emotional climate of teaching:

- "Welcoming the student and being cheerful with him"
- "Being merciful and sympathetic towards the student"
- "Rewarding the good students and praising them"
- "Feeling pity towards the mistaken ones"

The Prophet was not a harsh teacher. He welcomed questions, smiled at his companions, praised good efforts, and was gentle with mistakes. He never shamed a questioner. He never humiliated a student who made an error. He corrected with mercy, not with anger.

For parents: this means that when a child makes a mistake in prayer, in recitation, in behaviour — the response should be gentle correction, not anger. "Feeling pity towards the mistaken ones" — the child who errs needs compassion, not punishment.

### 7. Questions and Stories

Principle 19: "Attracting the student's attention by asking questions and leading live conversation."
Principle 18: "Choosing the best modes (like similitude, giving examples, telling stories)."

The Prophet taught through questions — "Do you know what is backbiting?" — and through stories — the story of the three men trapped in a cave, the story of the woman and the cat, the story of the man who gave water to a dog. He used similitudes: "A believer to another believer is like a building whose parts enforce each other" — and then interlaced his fingers.

For families: instead of lecturing children for 15 minutes, ASK questions. "What do you think Allah wants us to do in this situation?" "What would the Prophet do?" And TELL stories — the stories of the Prophets, the Companions, and the Prophet's own life. Stories capture the imagination in ways that rules never do.

### 8. Moderation in Teaching

Principle 15: "Being moderate in the amount being taught and avoiding putting the student in a state of boredom."

The Prophet did not teach so much that his companions became exhausted. He paced his teaching to their capacity. He was moderate — not too little (which loses attention) and not too much (which causes boredom).

For families: this means a family halaqah should be 45-60 minutes — not three hours. A child's Islamic education should be paced — not crammed. The goal is sustained interest, not a one-time overload that kills the desire to learn.

### 9. Practical Application

Principle 16: "Making good use of practical situations for the sake of education and guidance."

The Prophet taught in the moment. When a funeral passed, he taught about death. When someone asked a question, he taught the answer AND the principle behind it. When a situation arose — a conflict, a mistake, a success — he used it as a teaching moment.

For families: the best teaching happens in the moment, not in a scheduled lesson. When a child lies, that is the moment to teach about truthfulness. When a sibling fight happens, that is the moment to teach about patience and love. When someone dies, that is the moment to teach about the Hereafter. The Prophetic method is contextual — it seizes the teachable moment.

### 10. Applying These Principles in the Family

Qaradawi's 19 principles translate into a family learning plan:

| Prophetic Principle | Family Application |
|---|---|
| Individual differences | Each child learns differently — tailor your approach |
| Gradual instruction | Start small, build gradually — don't overwhelm |
| Mercy toward mistakes | Correct gently, never shame or humiliate |
| Questions and dialogue | Ask, don't lecture — engage, don't dictate |
| Stories and examples | Tell stories of the Prophets and Companions |
| Moderation | Keep learning sessions short and engaging |
| Practical situations | Use daily events as teaching moments |
| Cheerful reception | Make learning a joy, not a burden |
| Rewarding good | Paise effort, not just results |
| Patience with difficulties | Learning is hard — be patient with the learner |

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — The Prophet as Teacher

> The Prophet was illiterate — he could not read or write. Yet he established educational principles that modern pedagogy only "discovered" centuries later. How is that possible?

**Facilitator guidance:** The Prophet's teaching came from divine guidance, not from academic study. Allah taught him how to teach — just as Allah taught Adam the names of all things. The Prophet's educational wisdom is evidence of his prophethood: an unlettered man producing principles that anticipate modern educational theory.

### Question 2 — Different Answers for Different People

> The Prophet gave different answers to "What is the best deed?" depending on who asked. Why? What does this teach us about how we should teach our children?

**Facilitator guidance:** Each person needs to hear a different priority — "the way a doctor deals with patients." For families: each child is different. The child who struggles with anger needs to hear about patience. The child who is shy needs to hear about courage. The child who is selfish needs to hear about generosity. One-size-fits-all teaching does not work — the Prophet taught us to diagnose the need and prescribe the lesson.

### Question 3 — Mercy Toward Mistakes

> Qaradawi says the Prophet was "merciful and sympathetic towards the student" and felt "pity towards the mistaken ones." How do we respond when our children make mistakes in learning? With mercy or with anger?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a conviction question for parents. The Prophetic model: gentle correction, no shaming, no humiliation, no anger. The common model: "How many times have I told you?" "Why can't you get this right?" Ask: which approach builds a love of learning, and which kills it?

### Question 4 — Questions Instead of Lectures

> The Prophet taught by asking questions and telling stories. How can we use more questions and stories in our family, instead of lectures and rules?

**Facilitator guidance:** Instead of "You should pray on time" → "What do you think happens when we delay prayer? How do you feel when someone keeps you waiting?" Instead of "Don't lie" → Tell the story of the Prophet who was known as al-Amin. Questions engage the mind; stories engage the heart. The Prophet used both.

### Question 5 — Each Child's Learning Plan

> If each child is different — "what is right for one person is not necessarily right for another" — what is the best way to teach each of our children? What works for one that doesn't work for another?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the most practical question. For each child, identify:
- Their temperament (quiet, active, sensitive, bold)
- Their learning style (visual, auditory, kinesthetic)
- What motivates them (praise, challenges, stories, rewards)
- What they struggle with (attention, patience, anger, shyness)

Then: tailor your approach. The quiet child needs gentle encouragement. The active child needs hands-on learning. The sensitive child needs extra reassurance. The bold child needs boundaries. ONE approach for all children is not Prophetic.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Learning Plan"

For each child in the family, parents create a simple learning plan:

| Child | Temperament | What Motivates Them | What They Struggle With | One Islamic Lesson This Week | Best Teaching Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [quiet/active/etc.] | [praise/stories/etc.] | [attention/anger/etc.] | [patience/honesty/etc.] | [story/question/activity] |

**Process:**
1. Parents fill this in for each child (privately first, then discuss with older children)
2. For each child, identify ONE Islamic lesson to teach this week
3. Choose the teaching method based on the child's personality — a story, a question, an activity, a practical situation
4. At the next halaqah, share what worked and what didn't

**Goal:** To move from one-size-fits-all parenting to Prophetic individual-difference parenting. The Prophet "gave every human being his share of knowledge which suits him most." This activity is the family's version of that principle.

**For single-child families:** The learning plan focuses on identifying the child's unique temperament, learning style, and needs — and choosing the best method for them specifically.

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt` (the full chapter, approximately 100 lines of the 19 principles, then the "individual differences" section starting at line 81)

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **each child is different, and the Prophetic method is to tailor teaching to the individual.** "What is right for one person is not necessarily right for another." The Prophet gave different answers to the same question because each questioner needed a different prescription. Parents must do the same — diagnose each child's need and prescribe the appropriate lesson.

### The 19 Principles as a Parenting Toolkit
The 19 principles are not just for schools — they are a toolkit for parenting:

| The Prophet Taught... | Parents Should... |
|---|---|
| Gradually | Not overwhelm children with rules all at once |
| With mercy | Correct mistakes gently, never shame |
| Through questions | Ask "What do you think?" instead of "Do this" |
| Through stories | Tell stories of Prophets and Companions |
| With moderation | Keep sessions short — don't cause boredom |
| In the moment | Use daily events as teaching opportunities |
| Cheerfully | Make learning a joy, not a burden |
| With praise | Reward effort, not just results |
| With patience | Learning is hard — be patient with the learner |
| Individually | Tailor to each child's personality and needs |

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "the Prophet was kind to children." Skip the 19 principles. Use the story of the Prophet letting children sit on his lap during prayer. |
| 7–12 | The "doctor" analogy: "The Prophet treated each person like a doctor treats each patient — giving different medicine to different people. You and your sibling are different — so we teach you differently!" |
| 13–17 | The individual differences discussion: "You are unique. Your Islamic education should be tailored to YOU — your struggles, your strengths, your personality. What do YOU need to work on most?" |
| Adults | The full 19 principles as a parenting toolkit. The family learning plan activity is essential. Discuss how the Prophetic method differs from common parenting approaches (lecturing, one-size-fits-all, anger at mistakes). |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching to sections 3 (individual differences — the core principle), 4 (evidence: different answers), and 6 (mercy toward mistakes). Keep the full discussion — Questions 2 and 5 are essential. The family learning plan activity MUST be introduced.
