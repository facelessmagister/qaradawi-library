# Module 2.6 — Tazkiyah and Ikhlas: Purifying the Soul and Intention

**Phase:** 2 — Trunk: Character and Inner Purification
**Week:** 12
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that spiritual purification (tazkiyah) is an active, daily process — not passive mysticism — and that sincerity (ikhlas) is the criterion by which Allah accepts or rejects all deeds. The session establishes the three-stage sequence of divine ethics: sincerity (before action), observing Allah (during action), self-accountability (after action) — and the principle that "moral life is founded on knowledge and willpower."

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that purifying the soul is not a mysterious spiritual exercise — it is a daily, active process of choosing virtue over vice. And we learn that the most important question about any action is not "what did I do?" but "why did I do it?"

## Hadith (3 min)

### Hadith 1 — Actions Are by Intentions

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "The value of an action depends on the intentions behind it. A man will be rewarded only for what he intends."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (1)** and **Muslim (1907)** on the authority of Umar ibn al-Khattab

### Hadith 2 — The Righteous Are Hidden

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "The righteous are those who are pious and hidden. If they are absent, they are not missed, and if they are present, they are not recognized. Their hearts are beacons of guidance."
>
> — **Narrated by Ibn Majah (3989)** and al-Hakim

### Hadith 3 — A Wise Man Calls Himself to Account

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "A wise man is one who calls himself to account and does noble deeds to benefit him after death. A foolish person is one who subdues himself to his temptations and seeks from Allah the fulfillment of his vain desires."
>
> — **Narrated by at-Tirmidhi (2459)** on the authority of Shaddad ibn Aws

### Hadith 4 — Hold Yourselves Accountable Before You Are Held Accountable

Umar ibn al-Khattab said:

> "Hold yourselves accountable before you are held accountable [before Allah]."
>
> — **Cited by Qaradawi in Ethics in Islam, Section 4.1**, from Ibn Abi Shaybah

### Hadith 5 — Human Perfection Is to Worship Allah as If You See Him

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said (in answer to Gabriel's question about human perfection):

> "Human perfection is to worship Allah as if you see Him. If you do not achieve this state of devotion, then take it for granted that Allah sees you."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (50)** and **Muslim (9)** on the authority of Abu Hurayrah

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.1: "Divine Ethics" (pp. 314-318); **concept-tazkiyah**; **concept-ikhlas**. All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. What Is Tazkiyah?

Tazkiyah (تَزْكِيَة) — "purification, growth, sanctification" — denotes the spiritual discipline of cleansing the soul from vice and cultivating virtue. The foundational verse: "Successful is the one who purifies it, and failed is the one who corrupts it" (91:9-10).

The concept is DUAL, derived from the same Arabic root *z-k-w*:

| Tazkiyat al-Nafs | Tazkiyat al-Mal |
|---|---|
| Purification of the SOUL | Purification of WEALTH |
| Removing blameworthy traits (arrogance, envy, greed) | Removing the portion due to Allah and the poor (zakat) |
| Cultivating praiseworthy traits (patience, gratitude, generosity) | Cultivating gratitude and trust in Allah through giving |
| Internal purification | External purification |

The Qur'an links them: "Take from their wealth a sadaqah that purifies and sanctifies them" (9:103). Zakat is not merely a tax — it is a spiritual purification of wealth that simultaneously purifies the soul of greed and attachment.

### 2. Tazkiyah Is Active, Not Passive

Qaradawi explicitly rejects the notion that tazkiyah is passive mysticism. In Ethics in Islam, he follows the principle that **"moral life is founded on knowledge and willpower."**

Tazkiyah is an ACTIVE, knowledge-driven process:
- **Knowledge**: you must KNOW what is virtuous and what is vicious — this comes from the Qur'an and Sunnah
- **Willpower**: you must CHOOSE virtue over vice — this comes from your own effort
- **Sustained by worship**: prayer, fasting, zakat, dhikr — these strengthen the will and purify the heart

The Qur'an describes the process: "And by the soul and He who proportioned it — And inspired it with discernment of its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it. And he has failed who instills it with corruption" (91:7-10). The soul is CREATED with the capacity for both good and evil — tazkiyah is the conscious choice to develop the good and suppress the evil.

### 3. The Sunnah as the Source for Tazkiyah

Qaradawi asserts that the Sunnah is "the agreed-upon source for the purification of the soul." This means:

- Tazkiyah follows the authentic Prophetic model — not invented practices
- The Prophet's life IS the curriculum for soul purification
- His character ("his manners were the Qur'an" — Aisha) is the standard

This distinguishes Qaradawi's approach from later Sufi developments that introduced practices not found in the Qur'an or Sunnah. Tazkiyah, for Qaradawi, is rooted in revelation, not in mystical innovation.

### 4. The Three-Stage Sequence of Divine Ethics

In Ethics in Islam (Section 4.1), Qaradawi presents divine ethics as a sequence of three stages:

**Stage 1: Sincerity (Ikhlas) — BEFORE action**
- Why am I doing this? For Allah alone.
- The Qur'an: "They were not commanded except to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion" (98:5)
- The Prophet: "Actions are but by intentions" (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Sincerity means: no interest in materialistic objectives (wealth, status, power)
- The righteous are "hidden — if absent, not missed; if present, not recognized"

**Stage 2: Observing Allah (Muraqaba) — DURING action**
- Am I doing this in the best way? Allah sees me.
- The Prophet: "Human perfection is to worship Allah as if you see Him. If you do not achieve this, then know that Allah sees you" (Bukhari, Muslim)
- The Qur'an: "He is with you wherever you are. And Allah, of what you do, is Seeing" (57:4)
- Observing Allah means: performing every action with the awareness that Allah is watching — which naturally produces excellence

**Stage 3: Self-Accountability (Muhasaba) — AFTER action**
- Did I do it well? What can I improve?
- The Prophet: "A wise man is one who calls himself to account and does noble deeds to benefit him after death" (Tirmidhi)
- Umar: "Hold yourselves accountable before you are held accountable"
- Maymun ibn Mahran: "He who fears Allah holds himself accountable more strictly than a tyrant would hold his subjects"
- Self-accountability means: reviewing each day's actions, identifying what was done well and what needs improvement

This sequence — sincerity → observing → accountability — IS tazkiyah. It is the daily, lived process of purifying the soul through conscious awareness of Allah at every stage of action.

### 5. Ikhlas: The Criterion for Acceptance

Qaradawi insists that ikhlas (sincerity) is the criterion by which Allah accepts or rejects deeds. The worth of any action is measured not by its outward magnitude but by the sincerity of the intention.

| What People See | What Allah Sees |
|---|---|
| A large charity donation | The intention: for Allah or for reputation? |
| Long prayers at night | The intention: for Allah or to be called pious? |
| A scholar teaching | The intention: for Allah or for status? |
| A martyr fighting | The intention: for Allah or for glory? |

The Prophet's warning (cited by Qaradawi): the first person judged on the Day of Resolution will be a martyr. Allah will say: "What did you do?" He will say: "I fought for You until I died as a martyr." Allah will say: "You lie. You fought so that people would call you brave — and they did." Then he will be dragged into Hell. The same for the scholar and the generous person — the action was good, the intention was corrupt, the result is Hell.

**For the family:** the question is not only "did you pray?" but "WHY did you pray?" A child who prays because Mom told them to has obedience but not yet ikhlas. A child who prays because Allah told them to has ikhlas. The development from one to the other IS the process of tazkiyah.

### 6. The Greater Jihad: Struggling with the Self

Qaradawi connects tazkiyah to the concept of jihad al-nafs — the struggle against one's own desires. The Qur'an:

- "He who fears the position of his Lord and prevents the soul from [unlawful] inclination — then indeed, Paradise will be his refuge" (79:40-41)
- "Those who strive for Us — We will surely guide them to Our ways" (29:69)

The Prophet described this as the "greater jihad" — more difficult than fighting enemies, because the enemy is within. The soul has its own desires, its own tendencies toward comfort, laziness, pride, and self-interest. Tazkiyah is the daily battle to choose what Allah wants over what the self wants.

But — and this is Qaradawi's emphasis — this is NOT a losing battle. "Moral character can be changed" (Module 2.1). "Dogs and horses can be trained to be gentle, and people too" (al-Ghazali). Tazkiyah is a TRAINING process, not a punishment. Every small victory — choosing patience over anger, honesty over convenience, gratitude over complaint — purifies the soul a little more.

### 7. Tazkiyah in the Family

The family is the primary school of tazkiyah. How?

**Through example:** parents who model sincerity, self-accountability, and struggle with their own flaws teach more than any lecture. A parent who says: "I made a mistake today. I lost my temper. I ask Allah for forgiveness" is teaching tazkiyah by example.

** through practice:** the daily family routine IS the curriculum:
- Praying together → teaches sincerity (doing it for Allah, not for show)
- Eating together → teaches gratitude ("Thank You Allah for this food")
- Resolving conflicts → teaches self-accountability ("What did I do wrong? What can I do better?")
- Giving charity together → teaches purification of wealth and soul

**Through the evening review:** each family member, before bed, reviews the day:
1. What did I do well today? (Thank Allah)
2. What could I have done better? (Identify the flaw)
3. What will I do differently tomorrow? (Commit to improvement)

This is the Prophetic model: "A wise man is one who calls himself to account" — made into a family practice.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — What Does "Purifying the Soul" Mean?

> What does "purifying the soul" mean? Is it something you do once, or every day? Can you think of a "dirt" you need to clean from your heart?

**Facilitator guidance:** Tazkiyah = cleaning the heart, just like cleaning the body. You wash your body every day. You should wash your heart every day too. The "dirt" of the heart is: anger, jealousy, greed, arrogance, laziness, ingratitude. The "soap" is: dhikr, repentance, self-accountability, good deeds.

**For children:** "Your body gets dirty every day, so you take a bath. Your heart gets dirty too — with bad feelings like anger and jealousy. So every day we need to clean our hearts with dhikr and saying sorry to Allah."

### Question 2 — Why Did You Do It?

> The Prophet said: "Actions are but by intentions." Think about something good you did this week. Why did you do it? For Allah? For Mom and Dad? For yourself?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is the core question of ikhlas. The same action — praying, giving charity, helping a sibling — can have different intentions:
- For Allah → full reward
- For parents' approval → some reward (obedience) but not ikhlas
- For show → no reward
- For self-interest → no reward

The goal is not to make children feel bad about mixed intentions — it's to help them BECOME AWARE of their intentions, so they can gradually purify them.

### Question 3 — The Three-Stage Sequence

> Qaradawi teaches three stages: sincerity (before), observing Allah (during), self-accountability (after). Can we practice this with one action this week?

**Facilitator guidance:** Choose one action — like praying Fajr on time:
- **Before**: "I am praying Fajr for Allah alone." (Sincerity)
- **During**: "Allah is watching me pray." (Observing Allah)
- **After**: "Did I pray with focus? Could I have been more attentive?" (Self-accountability)

This three-stage awareness IS tazkiyah in practice.

### Question 4 — The Wise Man and the Foolish Person

> The Prophet said: "A wise man calls himself to account; a foolish person follows his desires and asks Allah for what he wants." Which one are we as a family? How can we be more like the wise man?

**Facilitator guidance:** The wise man reviews his actions and improves. The foolish person just does what he wants and asks Allah for blessings without changing himself. For the family: do we review our day? Do we identify what we could do better? Or do we just live and hope?

### Question 5 — Tazkiyah as Training

> Qaradawi says tazkiyah is like training — "dogs and horses can be trained, and people too." What is one trait you want to train in yourself? How will you practice it this week?

**Facilitator guidance:** This connects back to Module 2.1 (akhlaq is mutable). The training method: practice the trait until it becomes natural. If you want to be more patient: practice patience in one situation each day. If you want to be more grateful: practice saying "Alhamdulillah" ten times each day. Small, consistent practice = tazkiyah.

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Family Self-Accountability Practice"

This is a daily practice, not a one-time activity. Introduce it during the halaqah and commit to doing it every evening for the coming week.

**Each evening, before bed, each family member silently reviews three questions:**

1. **What did I do well today?** (Thank Allah for enabling it)
2. **What could I have done better?** (Identify one specific thing)
3. **What will I do differently tomorrow?** (Make one specific commitment)

**At the next halaqah:** Each person shares (voluntarily) one thing they discovered through self-accountability. Discuss:
- Was it hard or easy to review your day?
- Did reviewing change how you acted the next day?
- Did you notice patterns (the same thing you could have done better, repeating)?

**The teaching:** "A wise man is one who calls himself to account." Self-accountability is the engine of tazkiyah. Without it, the soul drifts. With it, the soul is consciously guided, corrected, and purified — one day at a time.

**Variation for young children (under 7):** Instead of silent review, do it together verbally at bedtime. Ask: "What was the best thing you did today? What could you do better tomorrow?" Keep it simple and positive. End with: "Alhamdulillah for today. May Allah help us tomorrow."

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-04-01.txt` (the full "Divine Ethics" section, lines 1-200)
- Read the concept pages: `/root/qaradawi-library/concepts/concept-tazkiyah.md` and `/root/qaradawi-library/concepts/concept-ikhlas.md`

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **tazkiyah is ACTIVE, not passive.** It is not sitting in meditation waiting for spiritual enlightenment. It is the daily, conscious practice of:
1. Checking your intention before acting (sincerity)
2. Being aware of Allah while acting (observing Allah)
3. Reviewing your actions afterward (self-accountability)

This three-stage sequence is the PRACTICAL method of tazkiyah. It can be applied to ANY action — from prayer to eating to working to playing with children.

### The Self-Accountability Practice
This is the most important activity in the entire course so far. If the family adopts ONLY one practice from all 12 modules, it should be this: the daily evening review.

**Three questions before bed:**
1. What did I do well? (Gratitude)
2. What could I have done better? (Self-awareness)
3. What will I do differently tomorrow? (Commitment)

This practice, sustained over months and years, IS tazkiyah. It is the engine of spiritual growth. Without it, the soul drifts with its desires. With it, the soul is consciously guided, corrected, and purified.

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "cleaning the heart" like cleaning the body. Use the bedtime review as a family ritual: "What was the best thing today? What can we do better tomorrow?" Keep it short and warm. |
| 7–12 | The three-stage sequence can be taught as a simple practice: "Before you do something, ask WHY. While you do it, remember Allah is watching. After you do it, ask: could I have done better?" |
| 13–17 | The jihad al-nafs discussion: "The hardest battle is with yourself. Every time you choose what Allah wants over what YOU want, you win a battle. The war lasts your whole life." |
| Adults | Read the full source section. Discuss the sequence: sincerity → observing → accountability. Commit to the daily review as a family practice. Discuss how to model self-accountability for children — by openly admitting mistakes and asking for forgiveness. |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching to sections 1 (what is tazkiyah — dual meaning), 4 (the three-stage sequence), and 5 (ikhlas as criterion). Keep the full discussion — Questions 2 and 3 are essential. The self-accountability activity MUST be introduced, even if briefly.
