# Module 3.5 — Children's Rights and Justice Among Children

**Phase:** 3 — Branches: The Family Unit
**Week:** 17
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that children have God-given rights — to life, a good name, sustenance, education, and proper care — and that parents are accountable to Allah for how they raise them. The session establishes the Prophetic command for justice and equality among children, the Islamic position on orphans, and the principle that parental responsibility is a divine trust.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that children are a trust from Allah — not our possession. Parents are caretakers, accountable to Allah for every child's rights. And children must be treated with justice — no favouritism, no discrimination.

>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim** on the authority of Ibn Umar

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Wasting the sustenance of his dependants is sufficient sin for a man."
>
> — **Narrated by Ibn Hibban** in his Sahih

### Hadith 3 — Allah Will Ask Every Caretaker

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Allah will ask every caretaker about what He entrusted him to take care of, whether he has done the care-taking well or otherwise, and every man will be asked concerning the people of his household."
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim**

### Hadith 4 — Do Justice Among Your Sons (3x)

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Do justice among your sons," repeating it three times.
>
> — **Narrated by Muslim, Ahmad, and Abu Dawud** — the story of Bashir ibn Sa'd and his son an-Nu'man

### Hadith 5 — Your Children Have the Right of Equal Treatment

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Do not ask me to be a witness to injustice. Your children have the right of receiving equal treatment, as you have the right that they should honor you."
>
> — **Variant narration** — narrated by Abu Dawud

### Hadith 6 — I and the One Who Raises an Orphan

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "I and the one who raises an orphan will be like these two in Paradise," and he pointed with his middle and index fingers with a slight gap between the two.
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari** and **Muslim**

### Hadith 7 — The Greatest Sin After Shirk

Ibn Mas'ud asked the Prophet: "What is the greatest sin?" He replied: "To ascribe divinity to someone other than Allah." "What is next?" he asked. The Prophet replied: **"To kill your child out of fear that it will share your food."**
>
> — **Narrated by al-Bukhari (4761)** and **Muslim (86)** on the authority of Ibn Mas'ud

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam** (الحلال والحرام), Chapter 3: "The Relationship Between Parents and Children" (pp. 245-250); **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.3.1 (pp. 367-368). All citations refer to these sources unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Child's Right to Life

Qaradawi establishes that the first and most fundamental right of a child is the right to life. Islam abolished the pre-Islamic practice of killing children — especially girls — out of fear of poverty or disgrace.

The Qur'an: "And do not kill your children out of fear of poverty. We shall provide for them and for you. Truly, the killing of them is a great sin" (17:31).

And: "When the female child who was buried alive is asked: for what crime she was killed" (81:8-9).

The Prophet ranked this as the SECOND greatest sin after shirk: "To kill your child out of fear that it will share your food." In Qaradawi's words (Ethics in Islam 4.3): "Pre-Islamic Arabs had a shameful tradition of killing their children because of their poverty."

While the literal killing of children is (thankfully) rare in most Muslim societies today, Qaradawi's teaching extends to the modern equivalent: **anti-natalism** — the view that children are a burden to be avoided. Islam sees children as Allah's provision, not a financial liability. "Nobody should think of children as a financial burden because they come to this world with the provisions that Allah has allotted to them."

### 2. The Child's Right to a Good Name

Qaradawi notes: "It is the right of a child in relation to his parents that they should give it a good name, not one which will cause it disturbance when it grows older. It is haram to give a name which denotes that it is a worshipper of someone other than Allah, as for example 'Abd an-Nabi (servant of the Prophet), 'Abd al-Masih (servant of Christ)."

A name is a child's first identity — it stays with them for life. Parents must choose names that:
- Are meaningful and dignified
- Do not associate servitude to anyone other than Allah
- Will not cause embarrassment or bullying

### 3. The Child's Right to Sustenance, Education, and Care

Qaradawi lists the practical rights: "A child has a right to sustenance, education, and proper care. The parents are not permitted to neglect the child's needs nor to abuse them."

The Prophet said:
- "Each one of you is a caretaker and is responsible for those under his care" (al-Bukhari, Muslim)
- "Wasting the sustenance of his dependants is sufficient sin for a man" (Ibn Hibban)
- "Allah will ask every caretaker about what He entrusted him" (al-Bukhari, Muslim)

Qaradawi comments: providing for children is not optional generosity — it is a religious obligation. A father who fails to provide, or who wastes what should go to his family, commits a sin. The word "sufficient sin" (in the hadith) means: this ONE sin is enough to condemn a man — he doesn't need any other sins.

But the Qur'an also balances: "Allah does not charge a soul except [according to] what He has given it" (65:7). The father is required to provide according to his means — not beyond them. The family should not demand what the father cannot afford.

### 4. The Right to Lineage

Qaradawi affirms Islam's strict protection of lineage: "Just as Islam prohibits a father to deny his paternity of his own child without a justifiable reason, it likewise forbids the child to claim a lineage other than his own, or to claim as father someone who is not his real father."

The Prophet: "The one who claims descent from someone other than his real father... are cursed by Allah, His angels, and all people."

And: "If someone claims a man as his father while knowing that he is not, Paradise will be forbidden to him."

Lineage protection is why Islam prohibits:
- Zina (fornication/adultery) — which confuses parentage
- Artificial insemination by a donor other than the husband — "classified in the same category as zina" (citing Sheikh Shaltut)
- Formal adoption that attributes the child to a non-biological father — Islam abolished this practice

### 5. Equal Treatment of Children — "Do Justice Among Your Sons"

Qaradawi presents one of the most practically important teachings in the chapter:

"It is obligatory for a father to treat all his children equally, especially in the matter of giving gifts. Accordingly, he is prohibited to bestow on some of his children more favors than on others without any necessity or valid reason, since this will produce jealousy and may even arouse enmity and hatred among them. This applies equally to the mother."

The hadith: Bashir ibn Sa'd gave a gift to one son (an-Nu'man) but not to his other children. The Prophet asked: "Does he have brothers?" "Yes." "Did you give the same to each of them?" "No." The Prophet said: **"This is not correct, and I never bear witness to other than what is just."**

In another version: "Do not ask me to be a witness to injustice. **Your children have the right of receiving equal treatment, as you have the right that they should honor you.**"

And: "Fear Allah and treat your children with equal justice."

Qaradawi notes: Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal permitted preferential treatment ONLY for valid reasons — if a child is handicapped, or engaged in studies, or has special needs. But without a valid reason, equal treatment is obligatory.

The connection: "this will produce jealousy and may even arouse enmity and hatred among them." Favouritism doesn't just hurt the unfavoured child — it destroys sibling relationships, and those destroyed relationships can last a lifetime.

### 6. Inheritance: Allah's System, Not Ours

Qaradawi addresses the Islamic inheritance system: "It is haram for a father to deprive his children of inheritance, as for example, to deprive the females or the children of a wife who is not a favourite to him."

The Qur'an: "Those are the limits of Allah, and whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, He will admit him to Gardens... But whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and transgresses His limits, He will admit him to Fire" (4:12-14).

The principle: the distribution of inheritance is established by Allah Himself — "out of His knowledge, wisdom, and justice." It is not for any human — parent, ruler, or judge — to alter Allah's distribution. Depriving a child (especially a daughter) of inheritance is a transgression of Allah's limits.

### 7. Kindness to Orphans

Qaradawi presents the Islamic position on orphans alongside children's rights:

The Prophet: "I and the one who raises an orphan will be like these two in Paradise" — and he pointed with his two fingers.

A foundling (laqit) is regarded as an orphan. Sponsoring an orphan — feeding, clothing, educating, loving — is a meritorious act rewarded with Paradise. The Prophet's two-finger image shows how close the orphan-sponsor will be to the Prophet in Paradise.

But the flip side: "Those who devour the property of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies" (4:10). Taking an orphan's wealth is one of the gravest sins — literally described as eating fire.

### 8. Children Are Not a Burden

In Ethics in Islam (section 4.3), Qaradawi counters the cultural attitude that children are a financial burden:

"Children are the joy of life, and nobody should think of children as a financial burden because they come to this world with the provisions that Allah has allotted to them."

The Prophet: "Marry the one who is fertile and loving, for I will boast of your great numbers." Islam encourages having children — not limiting them out of economic fear. The Qur'an's promise: "We provide for them and for you" (17:31).

The pre-Islamic practice of killing children out of poverty is condemned. The modern equivalent — treating children as an economic liability to be avoided — is equally condemned. Children are Allah's trust, Allah's provision, and Allah's joy.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — Children as a Trust

> The Prophet said: "Each one of you is a caretaker and is responsible for those under your care." What does it mean that children are a "trust" from Allah? Are they our possession?

**Facilitator guidance:** A trust (amanah) is something you are responsible for but do not own. Children are not the parents' property — they are Allah's creation entrusted to the parents for care, guidance, and protection. The parents are accountable to Allah for how they raise them. This changes the entire frame: parenting is not about control — it is about stewardship.

### Question 2 — Do Justice Among Your Sons

> The Prophet refused to witness an unequal gift and said: "Your children have the right of receiving equal treatment." Have you ever felt treated unfairly compared to a sibling? How did it feel?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a vulnerable question. Many adults still carry childhood wounds of perceived favouritism. Let family members share age-appropriately. The teaching: the Prophet refused to bless unequal treatment. Equal treatment is not a suggestion — it is a RIGHT of the children. Parents, ask yourselves: do I favour one child over another? In gifts? In attention? In praise? In time?

### Question 3 — Wasting Sustenance Is Sufficient Sin

> The Prophet said: "Wasting the sustenance of his dependants is sufficient sin for a man." What does "wasting sustenance" look like today? Is it only about money?

**Facilitator guidance:** "Wasting sustenance" includes:
- Spending on luxuries while the family lacks necessities
- Spending on habits (smoking, gambling) instead of family
- Not providing education when it is affordable
- Not providing emotional sustenance — time, attention, presence
- Working so much that children never see their parent

The word "sufficient" means: this alone is enough to condemn. A father who provides material food but is never emotionally present may also be "wasting" the sustenance of his dependants.

### Question 4 — Children Are Not a Burden

> Qaradawi says: "Nobody should think of children as a financial burden because they come with the provisions Allah has allotted to them." How does this differ from the modern view of children as expensive?

**Facilitator guidance:** Modern culture often treats children as a cost — "How much does it cost to raise a child?" Islam says: children come WITH their provision. Allah provides for them. The Qur'an: "We provide for them and for you" (17:31). This doesn't mean parents don't work — it means the RESULT of their work is Allah's provision, not their own achievement. The attitude shifts from "I can't afford children" to "Allah will provide if I do my part."

### Question 5 — The Orphan in Our Community

> The Prophet said: "I and the one who raises an orphan will be like these two in Paradise." Is there an orphan in our family or community that we can support? How?

**Facilitator guidance:** This is a practical question. Orphan support can include:
- Financial sponsorship (monthly contribution)
- Including orphaned children in family activities
- Checking on widowed mothers in the community
- Volunteering at orphan support organisations
- Teaching children to be kind to classmates who have lost a parent

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Justice Audit"

Parents audit their treatment of children. Each child (if there are multiple) answers (privately or written):

1. Do you feel you get the same amount of attention as your siblings?
2. Do you feel gifts and treats are distributed fairly?
3. Is there anything you feel is unfair in how you're treated compared to your siblings?
4. Is there something you wish Mom/Dad would do more with you?

Parents review the answers privately first, then discuss together as a family:
- Acknowledge any unfairness
- Commit to specific changes
- Ask children for forgiveness where needed

**Goal:** The Prophet said: "Fear Allah and treat your children with equal justice." This activity is a practical application of that command — making justice visible and actionable in the family.

**For single-child families:** The justice audit can focus on: "Do you feel you get enough time and attention? Is there something you wish was different?" Even one child needs to feel fairly treated — in the sense of being seen, heard, and valued.

## Notes for the Facilitator

### Before the Session
- Read the full source text: `/root/qaradawi-library/raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-03.txt` (lines 3500-3700, "Adopting a Child" through "Equal Treatment of Children")

### Key Distinction to Emphasize
The MOST important teaching: **children are a trust, not a possession.** "Each one of you is a caretaker and is responsible for those under his care." This reframes parenting entirely:
- You do not OWN your children — you are entrusted with them
- You are ACCOUNTABLE to Allah for how you raise them
- Your children have RIGHTS over you — to life, a name, sustenance, education, equal treatment
- Your authority comes from Allah — and it is limited by Allah's commands

This means: the parent who favours one child over another is violating the child's right. The parent who fails to provide is accountable. The parent who is present physically but absent emotionally is "wasting sustenance."

### The Justice Audit
This may be the most challenging activity in the course. Parents may discover that their children feel unfairly treated in ways they never realised. The facilitator (usually a parent) should model vulnerability: "I know I sometimes favour [X] because [reason]. I ask Allah for forgiveness and I commit to doing better." This models humility (Module 2.3) and self-accountability (Module 2.6).

### Adapting for Age

| Age Group | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Under 7 | Focus on "Allah gave you to us as a gift. We take care of you because Allah asked us to." Use the orphan story: "The Prophet said if you take care of a child who doesn't have parents, you'll be right next to him in Paradise!" |
| 7–12 | The justice hadith: "A dad gave a gift to one son but not the others. The Prophet said NO! That's not fair! Your children have the right to be treated equally." Ask: "Do you ever feel things are unfair in our family?" |
| 13–17 | The trust concept: "You are not your parents' property. You are Allah's trust placed with your parents. They are accountable to Allah for how they raise you. And YOU are accountable to Allah for how you respond." |
| Adults | The full weight: you are a caretaker. Allah will ask you about every child in your household. Are you providing? Are you just? Are you present? The justice audit is essential. |

### If Time Is Short
If 60 minutes is not available, reduce the teaching to sections 1 (right to life), 5 (equal treatment), and 7 (orphans). Keep the full discussion — Questions 2 and 3 are essential. The justice audit MUST be introduced.
