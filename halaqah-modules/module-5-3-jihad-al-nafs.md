# Module 5.3 — Jihad al-Nafs: The Greater Struggle

**Phase:** 5 — Fruit: Trust, Hope & Eternal Vision
**Week:** 28
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes

---

## Learning Objective

Family members understand that the greatest jihad is the struggle against the self (jihad al-nafs) — the daily battle to purify the heart, resist temptation, and hold oneself accountable. The session establishes Qaradawi's treatment of self-accountability, the mixed nature of the human being (body + spirit), and the Prophetic model of constant repentance.

---

## Opening (3 min)

> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ

State the niyyah: we gather as a family to learn that the greatest battle is not outside — it is inside. The struggle against our own desires, our own weaknesses, and our own lower self is the jihad that never ends and never stops being rewarded.

## Teaching: Qaradawi's Treatment (15 min)

*Sources: Yusuf al-Qaradawi, **Ethics in Islam** (أخلاق المسلم), Section 4.1: "Divine Ethics — Self-Accountability and Repentance" (pp. 315-323); Section 4.2: "Individual Ethics — Struggling with Desire" (pp. 342-343). All citations refer to these sections unless otherwise noted.*

### 1. The Mixed Nature of the Human Being

Qaradawi presents a profound anthropology: "We are not angels, and we do err, because of our mixed nature that has a physical element that pulls us down to the earth and a spiritual element that wants us to rise to the sky. This mixed nature leaves us constantly torn between the push and pull of our instinctive needs for materialism and spirituality, hence it is only human to err."

This is the starting point for jihad al-nafs: the human being is NOT an angel. We have a body (pulling toward earth) and a spirit (pulling toward heaven). The tension between them is not a flaw — it is the design. The jihad is not to eliminate the body — it is to discipline it so that the spirit leads and the body follows.

The Prophet: "All humans sin, and the best of those who sin are the ones who repent." Sin is human. Persistence in sin is the problem. Repentance is the solution.

### 2. Self-Accountability (Muhasabah)

Qaradawi presents self-accountability as a core divine ethic:

"Intentions precede actions, whereas observing Allah takes place while performing actions. **Holding oneself accountable starts immediately when an action is completed.**"

The Prophet: "A wise man is one who calls himself to account and does noble deeds to benefit him after death. A foolish person is one who subdues himself to his temptations and desires and seeks from Allah the fulfillment of his vain desires."

Umar: "Hold yourselves accountable before you are held accountable."

Maymun ibn Mahran: "He who fears Allah holds himself accountable more strictly than a tyrant would hold his subjects accountable and more so than a selfish businessman holding his partners accountable."

Qaradawi comments: "Holding oneself accountable motivates people to do their very best in everything they do. It also helps them reflect on their actions and seek new ways to improve themselves. Perhaps, the most important function of self-accountability is that it drives people to pursue human perfection and it discourages vanity and pride."

He adds: "Today, we see a lot of the literature on self-improvement using the terms 'self-evaluation', which is just a synonym for self-accountability. The issue is not that the term is being promoted in modern psychology. Rather, the issue is that many among us think it is a new concept imported from the West, and forget that it is the same concept the Holy Qur'an and Sunnah have been promoting for centuries."

### 3. The Stain on the Heart

Qaradawi warns about the spiritual consequence of unchecked sin:

"When sins increase, our hearts become rusty and resistant to faith. Allah says: 'No! Rather, the stain has covered their hearts of that which they were earning' (83:14)."

The image is of rust (rayn) covering the heart. Each sin adds a layer. Over time, the heart becomes so covered that faith cannot penetrate it. This is why jihad al-nafs is urgent — not because every sin is catastrophic, but because accumulated sin dulls the heart's sensitivity to Allah.

The solution: constant repentance. "Allah's door is always open for us to repent. He extends His hand during the night for those who sin in the day to repent, and during the day for those who sin in the night to repent."

### 4. The Prophet's Model: Repentance 100 Times a Day

Qaradawi presents the Prophet as the model of constant repentance:

"O people! Repent to Allah. I repent to Him a hundred times every day."

This is extraordinary: the Prophet — whose past and future sins were forgiven — repented a hundred times daily. If the Prophet repented 100 times a day, how often should we repent?

And: "By the One in Whose Hand my soul is! If you do not commit sins, Allah would replace you with a people who would commit sins and seek His forgiveness; and Allah will certainly forgive them."

The paradox: Allah does not want perfection — He wants RETURN. The person who never sins (if such a person existed) would be replaced by people who sin AND repent — because repentance is a higher act than sinlessness. Repentance demonstrates humility, need, and love for Allah. Sinlessness demonstrates nothing.

### 5. Sincere Repentance

Qaradawi defines sincere repentance:

"Repenting sincerely comes with an overwhelming sense of regret for what happened and determination not to do it again. Sincere repentance renews faith and wipes away all sins: 'Those who repent, believe, and do righteous work, for them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good ones' (25:70)."

Three components of sincere repentance:
1. **Regret** — feeling sorrow for the sin (not just getting caught)
2. **Determination** — resolving not to repeat it
3. **Action** — doing righteous deeds to replace the evil ones

The promise: "Allah will replace their evil deeds with good ones" (25:70). Repentance doesn't just erase the sin — it TRANSFORMS it into good. The sinner who repents becomes better than they were before the sin — because the sin led to repentance, and repentance led to renewal.

### 6. Struggling with Desire (Jihad al-Nafs)

In section 4.2, Qaradawi addresses the struggle with desire directly:

"Allah says: 'He who fears the position of his Lord and prevents the soul from [unlawful] inclination — then indeed, Paradise will be his refuge' (79:40-41)."

The jihad al-nafs is the daily struggle to:
- Resist the temptation to sleep instead of praying Fajr
- Resist the temptation to look at what is haram
- Resist the temptation to gossip, lie, or cheat
- Resist the temptation to anger when provoked
- Resist the temptation to spend on luxuries when charity is needed
- Resist the temptation to eat excessively (connecting to Module 4.2 — wasatiyyah)

The Prophet: "The mujahid is the one who strives against his own soul in obedience to Allah." The greatest jihad is not on the battlefield — it is within.

### 7. The Example of the Prophets

Qaradawi notes: "All prophets were role models of patience" — and of jihad al-nafs:

- **Ismail**: patient when his father told him Allah commanded his sacrifice — "O my father, do as you are commanded. You will find me patient, if Allah wills" (37:102)
- **Muhammad**: "Be patient, as were those of determination among the messengers" (46:35)
- **The prophets to their peoples**: "We will surely be patient against whatever harm you should cause us" (14:12)

The prophets faced external enemies, but their greatest struggle was internal: maintaining faith, patience, and obedience in the face of exhaustion, rejection, and temptation. Their jihad al-nafs is the model for ours.

### 8. When an Impulse Touches You from Satan

Qaradawi cites the verse: "Indeed, those who revere Allah — when an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember Him and at once they have insight" (7:201).

The mechanism of jihad al-nafs: when temptation comes (and it WILL come), the believer remembers Allah — and the remembering itself gives them insight (tafsir) to see the temptation for what it is. The remembrance of Allah is the weapon in the internal battle. Not willpower alone — but willpower powered by the remembrance of Allah.

## Family Discussion (15 min)

### Question 1 — The Greatest Jihad

> The Prophet said: "The mujahid is the one who strives against his own soul." The greatest jihad is not on the battlefield — it is inside. What does this mean? What does your internal jihad look like?

**Facilitator guidance:** Each person's jihad al-nafs is different. For a child: resisting the temptation to hit a sibling. For a teenager: resisting the temptation to scroll instead of studying. For an adult: resisting the temptation to snap at a spouse. For a parent: resisting the temptation to favour one child. The greatest battle is the one you fight every day against your own weaknesses.

### Question 2 — The Wise Man Calls Himself to Account

> The Prophet said: "A wise man calls himself to account... a foolish person subdues himself to his desires." Do you review your day? Do you reflect on what you did well and what you need to improve?

**Facilitator guidance:** Self-accountability (muhasabah) is the practice of reviewing your actions daily. Before sleep: "What did I do today? What was good? What needs improvement? What should I repent for?" This is the Prophet's definition of wisdom — not intelligence, not knowledge, but SELF-AWARENESS.

### Question 3 — 100 Repentances a Day

> The Prophet repented 100 times a day — and his sins were already forgiven. If he repented that much, how often should we? What does this tell us about the role of repentance in daily life?

**Facilitator guidance:** Repentance is not a once-a-year event — it is a daily practice. "Astaghfirullah" should be on our tongues constantly. The Prophet's 100 daily repentances show that repentance is not about major sins only — it is about the small shortcomings, the missed opportunities for good, the moments where we could have done better.

### Question 4 — The Stain on the Heart

> Qaradawi says: "When sins increase, our hearts become rusty and resistant to faith." What does a "rusty heart" feel like? Have you ever felt distant from Allah? What caused it?

**Facilitator guidance:** A rusty heart feels: prayer is mechanical, Qur'an is boring, worship feels heavy, sin feels normal. The cause: accumulated sin without repentance. The solution: repentance cleans the rust. "Sincere repentance renews faith and wipes away all sins" — it doesn't just erase, it TRANSFORMS evil into good (25:70).

### Question 5 — The Body and the Spirit

> Qaradawi says we are "torn between the push and pull of materialism and spirituality." Which way do you feel pulled more? What can you do to let the spirit lead more often?

**Facilitator guidance:** The human being is body + spirit. The body pulls toward comfort, pleasure, and ease. The spirit pulls toward worship, service, and meaning. Jihad al-nafs is not killing the body — it is disciplining it so the spirit leads. Practical: when the body says "sleep," the spirit says "pray Fajr." When the body says "eat more," the spirit says "share with others." When the body says "scroll," the spirit says "read Qur'an."

## Family Activity (10 min)

### Activity: "Daily Self-Review (Muhasabah)"

Introduce a simple daily self-review practice. Each evening before sleep, each family member asks themselves:

1. **What did I do well today?** (gratitude)
2. **What could I have done better?** (accountability)
3. **What do I need to repent for?** (repentance)
4. **What will I try to do better tomorrow?** (intention)

**Track for one week:** Use a simple notebook or journal. Each night, write 1-2 sentences for each question.

**At the next halaqah:** Share (voluntarily) what you discovered:
- Was it hard to review your day?
- Did you notice patterns? (Same mistakes repeatedly?)
- Did the self-review change your behaviour the next day?
- How does this connect to "a wise man calls himself to account"?

**Goal:** To make muhasabah a daily family practice — the internal jihad that never ends. "Hold yourselves accountable before you are held accountable" (Umar). The family that practices self-review together is a family that grows together.

### Key Distinction
Jihad al-nafs is not about being perfect — it is about RETURNING. The Prophet repented 100 times a day. "All humans sin, and the best of those who sin are the ones who repent." The goal is not sinlessness — the goal is a heart that turns back to Allah every time it stumbles.

| Age Group | Adaptation |
