# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam: Introduction

## Summary

This Introduction explains the origin and methodology of the book. Al-Azhar's Department of Islamic Culture commissioned the author to write an accessible English-language work on the lawful (halal) and prohibited (haram) in Islam for Muslims in Europe and America and for non-Muslims interested in Islam.

The author identifies two extremes among contemporary writers on Islam: (1) those overawed by Western civilization who permit what Islam prohibits and frown upon what it permits, and (2) those frozen in rigid opinions who declare haram anything about which they are uncertain. He rejects both, committing himself instead to independent reasoning (ijtihad) across the schools of jurisprudence, citing Imam Malik ("The word of any person other than the Prophet is sometimes acceptable and sometimes objectionable") and Imam Shafi'i ("My opinion is correct with the possibility of its being erroneous").

The chapter establishes key contextual definitions: **al-Halal** (the lawful) — that which is permitted with no restriction; **al-Haram** (the prohibited) — that which the Law-Giver has absolutely prohibited, carrying both worldly and hereafter punishment; **al-Makruh** (the detestable) — that which is disapproved but not strongly, less in degree than haram. The author notes that earlier revealed religions had temporary or punitive prohibitions, but Islam came with a complete, comprehensive, and eternal Shariah for all mankind. The book's purpose is to fill a gap in the contemporary Muslim library by clarifying what is permitted, what is prohibited, and the reasons for each ruling.

### Key Topics

Author's Preface — Al-Azhar commission, purpose, and methodology; definitions of halal, haram, and makruh; critique of two extremes (Western apologetics vs. rigid conservatism); principle of independent ijtihad; Islam as the final and complete Shariah

## Key Passages

- **Two extremes rejected**: One group overawed by the West, the other frozen in fixed opinions (pp. 15–17).
- **Imam Shafi'i on ijtihad**: "My opinion is correct with the possibility of its being erroneous. An opinion different from mine could be erroneous and could be correct." (p. 17)
- **Imam Ali on truth**: "Truth is not to be learned on the basis of authority. Learn the truth and then you will know who the truthful ones are." (p. 17)
- **Qur'anic criteria for halal/haram**: Al-A'raf 32–33 — "Say: What my Lord has indeed prohibited are shameful deeds, whether open or secret, and sin and rebellion without just cause, and that you associate with Allah that for which He has sent down no authority, and that you say concerning Allah that about which you do not know."
