## Summary

This chapter examines the contemporary condition of the Muslim ummah as both a diagnosis of its ailments and a proof of its内在 capacity for renewal. Al-Qaradawi first catalogues the "diseases and blights" of the present time: religion weakened between extremism and neglect; monotheism distorted between grave-worship and palace-worship; the spiritual life eroded by abandoned prayers and deviant Sufism; social mores corrupted by imported Western materialism and inheritedrigid traditionalism; the issue of women torn between extreme concealment and immodest exposure; the Islamic intellect stagnated, having abandoned ijtihad for blind imitation; hypocrisy, despotism, and economic inequality rampant across the Muslim world; and political life ruled by tyranny rather than shura. Yet he argues that this sick state cannot persist indefinitely, because it contradicts the inherent nature of Islam and its community, which are divinely protected from collective error. The chapter's crucial turning point insists that the very visibility of these crises is itself an auspice: the Islamic awakening visible across the Muslim world — the return to mosques, the revival of hijab, the spread of Islamic publishing and education, the independence movements that freed Muslim lands from colonial occupation — all demonstrate that Islam possesses an irrepressible inner vitality. Al-Qaradawi concludes that the present malaise, however severe, is a temporary phase that cannot extinguish the intrinsic power of the faith.
