## Summary

Chapter 5 addresses the fiqh of visual representation — painting, picture-making, and sculpture — which is among the most debated topics in Islamic art law. Al-Qaradawi begins with the Qur'anic affirmation that Allah is "the Shaper" (al-Musawwir) and that human creation reflects divine artistry, then turns to the numerous hadiths that criticize picture-makers and warn of severe punishment. He identifies four distinct reasons for prohibition: (1) idolatry — the historical trajectory from memorial images to worship, as with the people of Noah; (2) making symbols of other religions, such as the cross; (3) imitating Allah's creation with the intention of rivaling the Creator; and (4) luxury and conspicuous consumption, as when images serve as status symbols in the home. Drawing on Imam al-Nawawi's commentary, he argues that the most severe warnings target those who make images for idolatrous purposes, not all picture-making categorically. The chapter demonstrates that the prohibition is graduated: images for worship are the gravest sin, images made to rival divine creation are a serious offense, images of luxury are disliked (makruh), and images of inanimate objects are permissible. This layered approach allows al-Qaradawi to preserve the spirit of the hadith prohibitions while accommodating the varied contexts of visual art.
