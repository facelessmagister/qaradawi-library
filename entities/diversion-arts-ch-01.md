## Summary

This opening chapter establishes the foundational Islamic approach to diversion and the arts by steering a middle course between two extremes: ascetic rejection of all entertainment and licentious indulgence. Al-Qaradawi argues that Islam embraces a realistic worldview that recognizes legitimate human needs — physical, intellectual, and emotional — and that aesthetic appreciation is woven into the Qur'anic vision of creation. He cites numerous Qur'anic passages demonstrating that Allah created the world with both utilitarian and aesthetic dimensions: cattle provide warmth and food yet also beauty; horses and mules serve as transport and ornamentation; the sea yields both sustenance and adornments. The chapter highlights the Qur'anic principle that beauty and provision are divine blessings for which gratitude is due, and underscores that Islam forbids neither the ornaments of God nor the good things He has provided (Sura 7:31-32). By grounding the discussion in Qur'anic ontology, the chapter sets the theological stage for the specific rulings on art forms that follow in subsequent chapters.
