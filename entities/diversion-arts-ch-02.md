## Summary

Chapter 2 continues the argument for Islam's affirmation of beauty by examining the believer's innate susceptibility to aesthetic experience and the Qur'an's own status as an aesthetic miracle. Al-Qaradawi draws on Qur'anic verses that celebrate the beauty of the sky, gardens, animals, and the human form (Suras 50:6, 15:16, 27:60, 16:6, 64:3, 82:7-8) to show that appreciating creation's beauty is an act of spiritual awareness, not worldly vanity. He then invokes the Prophetic hadith "Allah is Beautiful and He loves beauty" to distinguish love of beauty from arrogance, noting that the Prophet clarified this distinction for companions who feared that aesthetic appreciation might lead to pride. The chapter's second half focuses on the Qur'an as both a rhetorical and intellectual miracle, citing hadiths that encourage reciting the Qur'an with a melodious voice and the scholarly position that the beauty of recitation enhances its spiritual impact. Al-Qaradawi references Sheikh Muhammad Abdullah Draz's argument that listening to the Qur'an is not merely an obligation but an aesthetic experience that fulfills intellectual and emotional needs alongside spiritual ones.
