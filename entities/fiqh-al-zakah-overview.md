## Overview

Fiqh al-Zakah is Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi's magnum opus on the jurisprudence of zakat, the third pillar of Islam. Spanning two volumes and ten chapters, the work provides the most comprehensive contemporary treatment of zakat law in English, examining every dimension of this obligatory wealth tax — from the definition of zakatable assets to the categories of recipients entitled to receive it.

The book opens by establishing the Qur'anic and Prophetic foundations of zakah. While the Qur'an commands zakah in general terms and mentions certain categories of zakatable wealth — gold and silver, crops and fruits, trade earnings, and minerals — it leaves the detailed percentages, thresholds (nisab), and procedural rules to the Sunnah. Qaradawi systematically derives the principles that govern which assets are subject to zakah and which are exempt, centering his analysis on core conditions such as undivided and absolute ownership and actual or potential growth.

A distinguishing feature of the work is Qaradawi's engagement with all four Sunni schools of jurisprudence — Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali — alongside Zaydi and Zahiri positions. Rather than merely cataloguing divergent opinions, he evaluates them against the primary texts and the underlying objectives (maqasid) of zakah: to purify wealth, support the needy, and circulate prosperity without impoverishing the payer. He frequently offers his own reasoned rulings (ijtihad), such as his position on doubtful debts, retirement savings, and unlawfully acquired wealth.

The treatise also addresses contemporary questions that classical jurists could not have anticipated — including the zakatability of employee retirement funds and the treatment of modern financial instruments — making it an essential bridge between traditional fiqh and modern economic life. By synthesizing classical scholarship with original ijtihad on new problems, Fiqh al-Zakah stands as both a reliable reference on established rulings and a methodological model for extending Islamic fiscal law to new circumstances.
