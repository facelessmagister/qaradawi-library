## Summary

This chapter argues that the immutable divine laws (Sunan Allah) governing creation necessarily favor Islam's eventual ascendancy. Al-Qaradawi grounds his case in the Qur'anic principle that Allah's sunnah never changes (Fatir 35:43) and then identifies several specific laws that work in Muslims' favor. The first is the law of alternating fortunes: "So are the days, We give to men by turns" (Al 'Imran 3:140) — periods of decline are cyclical, not terminal, and the turn of prosperity must come. Drawing on world-historical patterns, he traces the torch of civilization passing from Eastern empires to Greece and Rome, then back to the Islamic civilization, then to a materialistic West, and argues that by this same cyclicality leadership must return to the Islamic East. The second law is that hardship is always followed by ease (Ash-Sharh 94:5-6; At-Talaq 65:7). The third is the law of change: those who relapse from good into evil inevitably lose their prosperity, a principle stated in Surat Al-Anfal after the fate of Pharaoh's people. The fourth is the law that civilization cannot endure without morality, and morality cannot thrive without faith — and since Western materialism has severed the bond between faith and public life, its collapse is structurally inevitable. Al-Qaradawi cites the sudden dissolution of the Soviet Union as a recent proof that military and material power cannot compensate for internal spiritual decay. Together, these divinely ordained laws constitute auspices that the Muslim world's current weakness is a phase, not a permanent condition.
