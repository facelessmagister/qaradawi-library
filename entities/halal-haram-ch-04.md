# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam: The Lawful and Prohibited in Dress, Adornment, and Social Relations

## Summary

This chapter covers the lawful and prohibited in the daily life of the Muslim, addressing beliefs and customs, dealings and transactions, play and recreation, social relationships, and relations with non-Muslims.

**Beliefs and Customs**: Faith (īmān) and tawhid are the foundation. Islam commands respect for Allah's laws in the universe and wages war on superstitions: soothsaying, divination with arrows (istiqsām), magic (siḥr), charms and amulets (tamīm/hamalāt), and evil omens (ṭiyarah). All of these constitute shirk or lead to it. The Prophet declared: "He who goes to a soothsayer and believes in what he says has denied what was revealed to Muhammad." Magic is a major sin, and some jurists consider it kufr. Amulets and charms worn for protection are likewise shirk — "The one who wears it is a polytheist." Divination with arrows, sand, seashells, cards, and palms are all prohibited as forms of istiqsām. Omens (ṭiyarah) are called "shirk" by Ibn Mas'ud.

**Jahili Customs**: Islam forbade chauvinism (ʿaṣabiyyah), boasting of tribal lineage, and imitating non-Muslims in religious practices. The Prophet said: "He who calls others to group chauvinism does not belong to us." Vows made in disobedience (nadr al-maʿṣiyah) are not to be fulfilled. Swearing by anything other than Allah (e.g., by one's father, life, or honor) is prohibited.

**Dealings and Transactions**: The chapter discusses the prohibition of begging — one who begs while having enough is committing a sin, and charity given to such a person is wasted. It is haram to waste wealth excessively (isrāf), though moderation in spending is enjoined. Hoarding (iḥtikār) of essential commodities is prohibited. Fraud, cheating, and concealment of defects in trade are haram. The chapter also addresses the prohibition of selling haram items and the obligation of fairness in wages and contracts.

**Play and Recreation**: Islam permits wholesome recreation that strengthens the body, refreshes the mind, and does not divert from obligations. Prohibited recreation includes gambling, lotteries, and games of chance. The chapter addresses music and singing with nuance — listening to frivolous or sexually provocative music that distracts from dhikr is discouraged, but permissible songs that inspire chivalry, courage, or religious feeling are acceptable. Chess is discussed by the author as makrūh at minimum; some jurists declare it haram due to its association with gambling, though the author is more lenient if it does not lead to neglecting obligations. Photography and images are discussed with the distinction between images used for veneration (haram) and those used for identification/memory (permissible).

**Social Relationships**: Kindness to neighbors is strongly enjoined. The chapter discusses the rights of guests, the obligation of returning salām, and the prohibition of spying and backbiting (ghībah). Lying, false testimony, and slander are major sins.

**Relations with Non-Muslims**: Muslims may exchange gifts and eat with the People of the Book. Muslim men may marry chaste women from the People of the Book. Visiting the sick among them and offering condolences are encouraged. However, Muslims must not participate in their religious festivals or adopt their distinctive religious customs. Friendship (muwālāh) that implies loyalty against the Muslim community is prohibited, while general kindness and justice toward non-Muslims who are not hostile to Islam is commanded.

### Key Topics

Superstitions; soothsaying; magic (siḥr); amulets; omens (ṭiyarah); chauvinism (ʿaṣabiyyah); vows; oaths; begging; wasteful spending; hoarding; fairness in trade; gambling; music and singing; chess; images and photographs; neighborliness; backbiting; lying; relations with non-Muslims; muwālāh

## Key Passages

- **Knowledge of the Unseen belongs to Allah alone**: "Say: No one in the heavens and the earth knows the Unseen except Allah…" (An-Naml: 65)
- **Magic as a destructive sin**: "Avoid the seven destructive things: associating partners with Allah, magic, taking a life which Allah has made sacred except in the course of justice, devouring riba, appropriating the property of an orphan, fleeing from the battlefield, and slandering chaste, indiscreet, and believing women." (Hadith, Al-Bukhari and Muslim)
- **Amulets as shirk**: "The one who wears it is a polytheist." (Hadith, reported by Ahmad)
- **Chauvinism prohibited**: "He who calls others to group chauvinism does not belong to us; he who fights for the sake of group chauvinism does not belong to us; and he who dies upholding group chauvinism does not belong to us." (Hadith, Muslim)
- **Kindness to the People of the Book**: "Today whatever is good is made lawful to you. And the food of those who were given the Scripture is permitted to you…" (Al-Ma'idah: 5)
