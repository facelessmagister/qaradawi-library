## Summary

Chapter 4 is the book's most fiqh-heavy section, systematically examining the Islamic ruling on singing and musical instruments. Al-Qaradawi surveys the spectrum of scholarly disagreement: some permitted all music, others prohibited all of it, and still others drew distinctions between vocal singing and instrumental accompaniment. He establishes the foundational principle that everything is permissible unless explicitly prohibited by Qur'an, Sunnah, or consensus. The chapter then analyzes the proofs cited by opponents of music — Sura 31:6 ("vain talk"), Sura 28:55 ("nonsense"), and various hadiths — and refutes each through the methodology of Ibn Hazm, arguing that the Qur'anic verses target disbelief and mockery, not music per se. Al-Qaradawi contends that singing without instruments, when free of obscene content and not sung by a woman before unrelated men, is lawful. He introduces the crucial concept of intention (niyya): good intention transforms diversion into an approach to Allah, while evil intention corrupts even good deeds. The chapter lays the groundwork for a nuanced, conditions-based approach that distinguishes between content, context, accompaniment, and purpose, moving beyond blanket prohibitions to a calibrated fiqh ruling.
