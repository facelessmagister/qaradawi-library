# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam

## Overview

**The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam** (الحلال والحرام في الإسلام) is Yusuf al-Qaradawi's most widely read work, originally commissioned by Al-Azhar's Department of Islamic Culture in the late 1950s as an accessible primer on Islamic legal ethics for Western audiences. It systematically addresses what Islam permits and prohibits across five domains: (1) the foundational principles of permissibility, (2) food, drink, and economic transactions, (3) marriage and family life, (4) dress, adornment, and social relations, and (5) relations between Muslims and non-Muslims.

The book's central thesis is the principle of *al-aṣl fī al-ashyāʾ al-ibāḥah* — that the default state of all things is permissibility, and prohibition requires explicit divine evidence. Qaradawi builds this from eleven sub-principles (the right of Allah alone to legislate halal and haram, the prohibition of declaring the permissible forbidden, the rule that what leads to haram is itself haram, the principle of necessity, etc.), then applies them across every domain of Muslim life.

Translated into dozens of languages and continuously in print since 1960, it remains the standard reference on the subject — praised for its moderate wasatiyyah approach and criticized by stricter Salafi scholars for its permissiveness on music, photography, and women's participation in public life.

## Distinctive Contributions

- **The eleven principles** of halal and haram — a systematic fiqh framework unique in the literature
- **Graduated rulings** — not binary permit/prohibit but a spectrum from obligatory to prohibited
- **Wasatiyyah methodology** — consistently steers between ascetic restriction and libertine permissiveness
- **Contemporary relevance** — addresses photography, chess, insurance, birth control, and other modern questions
- **Evidence-based argumentation** — every ruling is grounded in Qur'anic verse, authenticated hadith, or scholarly consensus
