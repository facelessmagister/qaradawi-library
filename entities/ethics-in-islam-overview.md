## Overview

_Ethics in Islam_ (الأخلاق في الإسلام) is Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi's comprehensive treatise on Islamic moral philosophy — a project he first announced forty years before its publication, completed through a collaboration between the Qaradawi Center for Islamic Reform and the Research Center for Islamic Legislation and Ethics (2015). The book argues that Islam is fundamentally a message of moral values: the Prophet was sent "to perfect good morals and conduct," and faith itself is inseparable from ethical conduct — one who cannot be trusted cannot be described as faithful, and worship divorced from moral transformation is rendered void.

Qaradawi traces his own intellectual formation through the works of Al-Ghazali, Ibn Taymiya, Ibn Al-Qayyim, and especially Professor Mohammed Abdallah Draz, whose Sorbonne dissertation _La Morale du Koran_ and _The Principles of Moral Ethics_ serve as primary resources. He critiques the Peripatetic Islamic philosophers (Al-Kindi, Al-Farabi, Ibn Sina) as essentially students of Greek philosophy rather than authentic Islamic moralists, and distinguishes his own approach as firmly rooted in the Qur'an and trusted Sunnah.

The book opens with a rigorous examination of the Arabic term _akhlāq_ (plural of _khuluq_), exploring its linguistic connection to _khalq_ (creation) and surveying definitions from Al-Tawhidi, Al-Raghib Al-Asfahani, the Arabic Language Academy, Al-Ghazali, and Dr. Draz. Qaradawi adopts a definition that treats moral character as the individual's inner self with its properties and traits — distinguishing moral traits (which involve willful choice) from intellectual and aesthetic capacities. He then addresses the dichotomy of innate versus acquired ethics, Al-Ezz Ibn Abd Al-Salaam's four-category classification of moral traits, Al-Ghazali's defense of moral changeability, and the interplay of heredity and environment in shaping character.

Across its four chapters, the work moves from definition and philosophy (Ch. 1) to comparative ethical research (Ch. 2), the foundations of Islamic moral philosophy (Ch. 3), and applied ethics (Ch. 4) — establishing that Islam's moral system governs every sphere of human life, from worship and family relations to economics, politics, and warfare, with no separation between the religious and the ethical.
