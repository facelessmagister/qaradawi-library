## Summary

Chapter 6 tackles photography as a modern innovation not directly addressed by classical fiqh. Al-Qaradawi presents the pivotal fatwa of Shaykh Muhammad Bakheet, the Mufti of Egypt, who distinguished photography from picture-making on the grounds that a photograph captures a reflection of existing reality rather than creating something from imagination — the photographer is a "reflector," not a creator imitating Allah's creative act. This distinction is central to the chapter's argument. The chapter then provides a detailed nine-point graduated ruling on pictures and picture-making, ranging from the most strictly forbidden (images made for worship, constituting disbelief if done knowingly) through various degrees of prohibition and dislike, down to the permissible (pictures of inanimate objects, photographs taken for lawful purposes). Al-Qaradawi concludes that photographs are essentially permissible unless they serve a forbidden purpose — such as glorifying tyrants, depicting nudity, or promoting disbelief. The chapter also addresses errant scholarly interpretations that either imposed blanket prohibitions or, conversely, used Solomon's jinn-crafted statues as carte blanche for all images, insisting that authentic hadith texts cannot be overridden by arbitrary interpretation.
