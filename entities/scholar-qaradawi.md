# Shaykh Yusuf al-Qaradawi (يوسف القرضاوي)

## Biography

Yusuf Mustafa Abdallah al-Qaradawi was born on 9 September 1926 in the village of Saft Turab (صفط تراب) in the Nile Delta province of Gharbia, Egypt — the only child of a poor peasant family of devout Sunni Muslims. His father died when he was two years old, and he was raised by his uncle. He memorised the entire Qur'an by age nine, an achievement that marked him for scholarly life from childhood.

He enrolled at the Al-Azhar Institute in Tanta in 1939, progressing through its secondary and then university-level curriculum in Cairo. He earned the following degrees from Al-Azhar University:

- **1953** — Alimiyya degree ( Bachelor's equivalent), Faculty of Usul al-Din (Theology/Religious Fundamentals), with honours
- **1958** — Post-graduate diploma in Arabic Language Studies from the Institute of Advanced Arabic Studies
- **1960** — Master's degree in Qur'anic Studies (thesis on the Qur'anic approach to zakah)
- **1973** — PhD in Islamic Jurisprudence (fiqh) with First Merit, for his dissertation _Zakah and Its Effect on Solving Social Problems_ — which later became the foundation of his magnum opus, *Fiqh al-Zakah*

During the late 1940s and early 1950s, Qaradawi joined the Muslim Brotherhood and was detained several times under King Farouq (1949) and then three more times under President Gamal Abdel Nasser, before the organisation was banned in 1954.

In 1961–62, Al-Azhar University sent him to Qatar as part of a scholarly exchange to head the Qatari Secondary Institute of Religious Studies. This exile became permanent: he would not return to live in Egypt until after the 2011 revolution, spending over four decades in Qatar.

In 1977, he laid the foundation for the Faculty of Shari'ah and Islamic Studies at the University of Qatar, becoming its first dean. In the same year, he founded the Centre of Seerah and Sunnah Research. He also held teaching positions at King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah.

He became the chairman of the **International Union of Muslim Scholars** (IUMS), founded in 2004, which became one of the principal organs of his global intellectual influence. He was also the host of the immensely popular Al Jazeera programme *Al-Shari'ah wa al-Hayat* (Sharia and Life), which ran from 1996 and reached tens of millions of viewers across the Arabic-speaking world.

He authored over 170 books and booklets and issued hundreds of fatwas across virtually every domain of Islamic law and ethics. He died on 26 September 2022 in Doha, Qatar, at the age of 96, and was buried at Mesaimeer Cemetery. His funeral was attended by thousands.

## Intellectual Methodology

Qaradawi's scholarly methodology is defined by several distinctive principles:

### Wasatiyyah (The Middle Way)
The organising principle of his entire corpus. He consistently steers between two extremes: ascetic rigourism that declares all pleasure haram, and libertarian permissiveness that dissolves all boundaries. His *Halal and Haram* opens by stating that Islam is "the religion of the middle community" (*ummatan wasatan*), and every subsequent work applies this balance.

### Ijtihad over Taqlid
Qaradawi advocates reasoned, evidence-based juristic independence rather than uncritical following of a single madhhab. While respectful of the four Sunni schools, he evaluates divergent opinions against primary texts and maqasid (higher objectives), frequently offering his own rulings (e.g., on zakatability of retirement funds, the status of photography, and doubtful debts).

### Qur'an-First, Context-Aware
He treats the Qur'an as the primary reference, the Sunnah as its practical exegesis, and demands that both be understood in context. In *Approaching the Sunnah*, he insists that hadith must be comprehended holistically — understanding the Prophet's balanced, realistic, and merciful pattern rather than extracting isolated rulings without regard for the original circumstances.

### Maqasid al-Shari'ah (Higher Objectives)
Across all his works, Qaradawi appeals to the overarching purposes of Islamic law — preservation of life, intellect, religion, property, and lineage — as the lens through which specific rulings are interpreted. This allows him to derive contemporary positions that are textually grounded yet adaptable to modern financial instruments, medical technology, and social arrangements.

### Taysir (Facilitation and Mercy)
Echoing the Prophetic principle "Make things easy, not difficult," Qaradawi consistently favours facilitation where the evidence permits it. He argues that the original state of all things is permissibility (*al-asl fi al-ashya' al-ibahah*) and that prohibition requires compelling proof. This principle underlies his most widely cited positions on photography, music, and women's participation in public life.

## Key Positions

### Fiqh (Jurisprudence)
- **Original permissibility** (*al-asl fi al-ashya' al-ibahah*): the default state of all things is lawful; prohibition requires explicit textual evidence
- **Zakah on modern assets**: zakah is due on employee retirement funds, stocks, and modern financial instruments if they meet nisab and haul conditions
- **Doubtful debts**: partial zakah on debts unlikely to be recovered
- **Graduated rulings**: not binary halal/haram but a spectrum from obligatory to prohibited, with degrees in between

### Hadith Methodology
- **Sunnah comprehension over literalism**: hadith must be understood holistically, in context, and in light of maqasid
- **Rejection of both hadith dismissal and hadith literalism**: the Sunnah is constitutionally authoritative, but must be applied with comprehension, not mechanical citation
- **Individualised application**: the Prophet's practice of giving different answers to different questioners should guide juristic flexibility

### Ethics
- **Faith and ethics inseparable**: one who lacks moral conduct cannot be described as faithful; worship divorced from ethical transformation is void
- **Moral character is changeable**: against deterministic views, Qaradawi follows Al-Ghazali's position that moral traits are acquired and can be reformed
- **Islam governs all spheres**: no separation between the religious and the ethical; economics, politics, and warfare fall under moral law

### Economics
- **Poverty is a societal disease**: requiring systemic remedies, not merely individual charity
- **Islam guarantees a dignity-floor**: through zakat, sadaqah, and institutional obligations on the wealthy
- **Wealth is a divine trust**: held by the rich on behalf of the community, not an absolute private right

### Lifestyle and Culture
- **Music, photography, and visual arts are broadly permissible** with content-based restrictions — against Salafi blanket prohibitions
- **Islam validates beauty and recreation**: the Qur'an pairs the utilitarian with the aesthetic; worship, knowledge, sport, and art each serve a legitimate human need
- **Women's participation in public life**: broadly permissive where textual evidence does not explicitly restrict

## Legacy and Influence

### Global Reach
- Over **170 books and booklets** spanning fiqh, usul, hadith methodology, ethics, economics, da'wah, and spirituality
- Translated into dozens of languages; *The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam* alone has been translated into more than 30 languages and has remained continuously in print since 1960
- His Al Jazeera programme *Al-Shari'ah wa al-Hayat* (1996–) reached tens of millions of Arabic-speaking viewers, making him arguably the most visible Islamic scholar of the early 21st century

### Institutional Founding
- **Founder and Dean**, Faculty of Shari'ah and Islamic Studies, University of Qatar (1977)
- **Founder**, Centre of Seerah and Sunnah Research, University of Qatar
- **Chairman**, International Union of Muslim Scholars (IUMS, founded 2004)
- **Head**, Qatari Secondary Institute of Religious Studies (from 1962)

### Awards
- **King Faisal International Prize** for Islamic Studies (co-awarded)

### Controversies
- **Muslim Brotherhood affiliation**: lifelong association led to multiple imprisonments in Egypt (1949, 1954 era) and political controversy
- **Political fatwas**: support for Palestinian resistance, opposition to the 2013 Egyptian coup, and positions on the Arab Spring drew intense criticism from several governments
- **Interior Ministry travel ban**: added to Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, and Bahrain terror watch lists in 2017
- **Permissiveness critiques**: Salafi scholars criticised his lenient rulings on music, photography, and women's public participation

### Intellectual Legacy
Qaradawi's most enduring contribution is the systematic application of **wasatiyyah** — the Islamic middle way — as a coherent juristic and ethical methodology. His works on zakah and halal/haram remain the standard references in their fields, and his insistence on ijtihad grounded in maqasid continues to shape contemporary Islamic legal thought worldwide.
