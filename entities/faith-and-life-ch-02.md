## Summary

Chapter 2 examines the relationship between *iman* (faith) and happiness, arguing that true happiness cannot be attained through material prosperity, wealth, or worldly pleasure alone. Al-Qaradawi opens by posing the universal question—"Where is happiness?"—and systematically evaluates the most common candidates: material luxury, having children, experimental science, and inward psychological states. Drawing on the case of Sweden, he notes that despite comprehensive social welfare—universal healthcare, free education, generous subsidies—Swedish society suffers from widespread worry, solitude, and soaring suicide rates, a phenomenon attributed to the deprivation of faith. Similarly, American material prosperity has failed to deliver contentment. The chapter then presents the Islamic thesis: happiness is realized when one's intention (*niyyah*) is directed toward the Hereafter, for which the Prophet's hadith on intention serves as a key proof-text. The believer whose heart is oriented toward Allah receives inner richness, tranquility, and preordained worldly provision, while the one whose aim is solely this world suffers perpetual dissatisfaction and disunity of self. Al-Qaradawi draws on Ibn al-Qayyim's definition of spiritual suffering and on Qur'anic verses warning that wealth and children may themselves become divine punishment for the hypocrites. The chapter thus demonstrates that *iman* is the indispensable precondition for authentic happiness, integrating material well-being under the governance of spiritual purpose.
