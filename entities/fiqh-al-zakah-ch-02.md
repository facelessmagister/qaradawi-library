## Summary

Chapter 2 examines the zakah obligation on livestock, an asset category of paramount importance in the Arabian economy at the time of the Prophet. Al-Qaradawi opens by grounding the discussion in Qur'anic verses that highlight the blessings God bestowed through cattle—providing warmth, meat, transport, milk, and materials for dwellings. Since gratitude for these blessings is enjoined by Scripture, the zakah on livestock constitutes a practical expression of thankfulness. The chapter establishes four general conditions for zakatability of livestock. First, the nisab minimum: five camels and forty sheep by unanimous agreement, with varying thresholds for cows depending on the school. Second, the passage of a full lunar year, based on authenticated practice of the Prophet and his successors. Third, the livestock must be pastured naturally on cost-free grazing for most of the year; the chapter provides detailed hadith evidence that artificially fed livestock are exempt, though Rabi'ah, Malik, and al-Layth dissent from this view. Fourth, working animals used for cultivation, carrying loads, or riding are excluded from zakah—supported by reports from Ali, Jabir, and the majority of jurists—though Malik again differs. The chapter then proceeds to a systematic treatment of zakah on camels, providing the detailed schedule of rates established by ijma and authenticated narrations, from five camels upward through progressively larger holdings, with specific age categories of female camels owed at each threshold.
