## Overview

_Economic Security in Islam_ (الأمن الاقتصادي في الإسلام) presents Islam's comprehensive framework for social welfare economics, arguing that poverty is a societal disease requiring systemic remedies — not merely an individual misfortune to be endured. Dr. al-Qaradawi opens by surveying six competing views on want and poverty — world-desertion (ascetic withdrawal), Jabariyah (predestarian quietism), individual charity without structural obligation, laissez-faire capitalism, Marxist socialism/communism — and refutes each as either resigned to suffering or coercive and materialistic. Islam, by contrast, treats wealth as a divine blessing held in trust, mandates structured redistribution (zakat), encourages voluntary giving (sadaqah), and guarantees a dignity-floor for every member of society. The book then develops the Islamic position in detail: poverty corrodes faith, morality, family stability, and social cohesion — the Prophet ﷺ himself sought refuge from it — and the Qur'an condemns female infanticide driven by economic fear (6:152, 17:31). Across seven chapters, the work moves from diagnosing competing ideologies to constructing the Islamic alternative: guaranteed basic needs, institutional obligations on the wealthy, and a moral economy that prizes neither destitution nor hoarding.
