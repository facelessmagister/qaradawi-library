## Summary

This chapter opens the evidentiary programme by grounding the promise of Islam's victory in Qur'anic revelation. Al-Qaradawi cites the recurring Qur'anic formula — appearing in Surat At-Tawbah (9:32-33), As-Saff (61:8-9), and Al-Fath (48:28 — in which Allah declares that He will perfect His Light and make Islam superior over all religions despite the aversion of disbelievers. The exegetical tradition is marshaled through Ibn Kathir, who explains that the "religion of truth" encompasses both genuine faith and righteous deeds, and that its superiority means its eventual prevalence over every competing system. Prophetic narrations corroborate the Qur'anic promise: the hadith of the earth being folded for the Messenger, the account of 'Ady bin Hatim witnessing the prophesied conquest of Khosrau's treasures, and the narration of Tamim al-Dari about Islam reaching every dwelling. The chapter also addresses the eschatological nuance that the ultimate perfection of Islam's light does not preclude a period in which belief is nearly extinguished — a temporary ebb before the final triumph — drawing on 'A'ishah's hadith about the pleasant wind that will take the lives of true believers. Al-Qaradawi thus establishes the Qur'an as the primary auspice: a divine promise that is categorical, repeated, and immune to abrogation.
