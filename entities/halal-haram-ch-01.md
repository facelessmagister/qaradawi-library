# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam: Islamic Principles Pertaining to the Lawful and the Prohibited

## Summary

This chapter lays out the foundational Islamic principles (uṣūl) governing the lawful and the prohibited. Before Islam, nations erred in either extreme asceticism (Brahmanism, Christian monasticism) or absolute permissiveness (Mazdak philosophy). The pre-Islamic Arabs exemplified this confusion — permitting alcohol, usury, and female infanticide while prohibiting wholesome foods without authority from Allah. Islam came to establish clear, just criteria, creating a "middle Ummah" (Al-Baqarah: 143).

The chapter presents eleven core principles:

1. **The original principle is permissibility (al-aṣl fī al-ibāḥah)**: Everything Allah created is permissible unless a sound, explicit nass prohibits it. This applies both to objects and to human actions (living habits/muʿāmalāt) — not to acts of worship (ʿibādāt), where the principle is limitation (tawqīf). Cites Ibn Taymiyah: in worship, nothing is affirmed except through the Shariah; in worldly matters, the principle is freedom of action.

2. **To make lawful and to prohibit is the right of Allah alone**: Neither rabbis, priests, kings, nor sultans may permanently prohibit something to Allah's servants. The Qur'an censures the People of the Book for taking their rabbis and priests as lords beside Allah (At-Tawbah: 31). Early jurists like Abu Yusuf and Imam Ahmad avoided the terms "halal" and "haram" for matters not explicitly stated in the Qur'an.

3. **Prohibiting the halal and permitting the haram is similar to committing shirk**: The Prophet warned, "The zealots will perish." Cites the pre-Islamic categories of bahīrah, sā'ibah, wasīlah, and ḥām — animals arbitrarily declared sacred without divine authority.

4. **Prohibition is due to impurity and harmfulness**: Only what is impure and harmful is prohibited.

5. **What is halal is sufficient; what is haram is superfluous**: Allah never prohibits something without providing a permissible alternative.

6. **Whatever is conducive to the haram is itself haram**: Islam blocks the avenues leading to sin.

7. **Falsely representing the haram as halal is prohibited**: One may not use legal tricks (ḥiyal) to circumvent divine prohibitions.

8. **Good intentions do not make the haram acceptable**: The end does not justify the means.

9. **Doubtful things are to be avoided**: The famous hadith of the halal, the haram, and the dubious — "whoever avoids the doubtful clears his religion and his honor."

10. **The haram is prohibited to everyone alike**: No preference or privilege; prohibitions apply universally.

11. **Necessity dictates exceptions**: The well-known legal maxim — al-ḍarūrāt tubīḥ al-maḥẓūrāt — necessity permits the prohibited, but only to the extent of the need and without craving the prohibited item.

### Key Topics

Eleven foundational principles; al-aṣl fī al-ibāḥah; divine right to legislate; shirk analogy for prohibiting halal; blocking the means (sadd al-dharā'iʿ); necessity permits the prohibited; Ibn Taymiyah on worship vs. custom

## Key Passages

- **Principle 1 — Permissibility as the default**: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is halal and what He has prohibited is haram, and that concerning which He is silent is allowed as His favor." (Hadith, Al-Hakim)
- **Worship vs. custom (Ibn Taymiyah)**: "Peoples' saying and actions are of two kinds: acts of worship … and customary practices … In acts of worship, nothing is affirmed except through the Shariah. In worldly activities, the principle is freedom of action; nothing may be restricted except what Allah has restricted." (Al-Qawāʿid an-Nūniyyah, pp. 112–113)
- **Principle 2 — Allah's exclusive right**: "They have taken their rabbis and priests as lords besides Allah…" (At-Tawbah: 31); the Prophet explained: "They prohibited to the people what was halal and permitted them what was haram, and the people obeyed them. This is how they worshipped them."
- **Principle 11 — Necessity exceptions**: "But if one is compelled by necessity, neither craving it nor transgressing, there is no sin on him." (Al-Baqarah: 173)
