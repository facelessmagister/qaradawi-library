## Summary

The final chapter examines the relationship between *iman* and hope, presenting hope as a defining spiritual disposition of the believer and despair as its antithesis. Al-Qaradawi opens by affirming that hope is a source of security and tranquility, a motivating force that drives the farmer, the merchant, the student, the soldier, and the believer toward perseverance and righteous action. He draws on Ibn Mas'ud's dictum—associating despair with self-satisfaction as twin destroyers of human striving—and al-Ghazali's commentary that happiness requires persistent pursuit, which despair precludes. The chapter then establishes a fundamental theological link between despair and disbelief (*kufr*), citing Qur'anic verses (Q. 12:87, 15:56) that equate despairing of Allah's mercy with straying. Despair manifests most sharply during hardship: the Qur'an repeatedly censans those who blaspheme when mercy is withdrawn and those who turn away when blessed. The chapter illustrates the virtue of patient hope through the story of Ayyub (Job), who endured the successive loss of wealth, children, health, and companions without forfeiting his trust in Allah, and was ultimately restored. The argument culminates in the assertion that *iman* produces hope precisely because the believer trusts in Allah's wisdom, justice, and mercy, while the disbeliever, cut off from the divine, sees only meaninglessness. The chapter and the book conclude by affirming that hope is not mere wishful thinking but a rational, faith-grounded confidence that animates every dimension of the believer's life.
