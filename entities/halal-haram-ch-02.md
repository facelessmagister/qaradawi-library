# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam: The Lawful and Prohibited in Eating, Drinking, and Economic Life

## Summary

This chapter covers what is lawful and prohibited in eating, drinking, clothing/adornment, the home, and work — the major spheres of daily material life. It is the longest chapter in the book.

**Food and Drink**: Islam permits all wholesome foods. Vegetarian foods are halal unless they intoxicate. For animal-derived foods, prior religions took extreme positions — Brahmanism forbade all meat, Christianity permitted even pork. The Qur'an specifies four broad categories of prohibited animal food (Al-Baqarah 173, Al-An'am 145, Al-Ma'idah 3): (1) carrion (dead animals), (2) flowing blood, (3) pork, and (4) that dedicated to other than Allah — expanded to ten detailed categories including strangled, beaten, fallen, gored, and partly-eaten animals, plus animals sacrificed on idolatrous altars. Sea food and locusts are exempted from the carrion prohibition. Necessity (ḍarūrah) permits consuming haram food to the extent of saving one's life, without craving or excess.

**Islamic Slaughtering**: Land animals must be slaughtered by a sharp instrument cutting the throat (windpipe, gullet, jugular veins), with Allah's name invoked, and by a Muslim or Person of the Book. The wisdom is to minimize suffering. The People of the Book's slaughtered food is halal per Al-Ma'idah: 5, unless a name other than Allah is invoked at slaughter. Hunting regulations and the rules for game animals are discussed.

**Intoxicants (Khamr)**: All intoxicants are haram, defined by 'Umar as "that which befogs the mind." This includes alcohol, marijuana, opium, cocaine, and all drugs impairing reason. Drinking, serving, selling, buying, transporting, and gifting khamr are all prohibited. Exceptions for medical necessity are narrow and strictly conditional. The Prophet cursed ten categories of people related to khamr.

**Clothing and Adornment**: Gold and pure silk are haram for men but halal for women. The chapter discusses the dress of the Muslim woman, the prohibition of artificial alteration of features (taghyīr khalq Allāh), including hair extensions, tattoos, tooth-filing, and cosmetic surgery for vanity.

**The Home**: Gold and silver utensils are haram for eating/drinking. Statues (ṣuwar) are prohibited in the sense of images venerated or that could lead to shirk; photographs are discussed with nuance.

**Work and Trade**: Islam encourages trade and commerce, but prohibits riba (usury/interest), gambling (maysīr), fraud in transactions, hoarding (iḥtikār), and Najsh (artificial price inflation). Riba is warred against by Allah and His Messenger. Every form of gambling is haram regardless of purpose. The chapter closes with prohibited professions: selling khamr, making idols/crosses, and prostitution.

### Key Topics

Food prohibitions (carrion, blood, pork, idol-dedicated meat); Islamic slaughtering; hunting; khamr and intoxicants; gold and silk; women's dress; tattooing and cosmetic alteration; statues and photographs; usury (riba); gambling (maysīr); prohibited trade; halal earning

## Key Passages

- **Four haram food categories**: "Forbidden to you are the flesh of dead animals and blood and the flesh of swine, and that which has been dedicated to any other than Allah…" (Al-Ma'idah: 3)
- **Sea food exemption**: "Its water is pure and its dead is lawful." (Hadith, narrated by Ahmad)
- **Tanning purifies**: "The tanning of the skin is its slaughtering." (Prophetic hadith)
- **Necessity exception**: "But if one is compelled by necessity, neither craving it nor transgressing, there is no sin on him." (Al-Baqarah: 173)
- **Ten cursed for khamr**: The Prophet cursed ten categories — drinker, server, buyer, seller, presser, carrier, one to whom carried, host, consumer of its price, one who transports it. (Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah)
- **Riba**: "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden interest." (Al-Baqarah: 275)
- **Gambling**: "They ask thee concerning wine and gambling. Say: In them is great sin and some benefit…" (Al-Baqarah: 219)
