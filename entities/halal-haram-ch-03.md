# The Lawful and the Prohibited in Islam: The Lawful and Prohibited in Marriage and Family Life

## Summary

This chapter addresses the lawful and prohibited in marriage and family life, covering sexual ethics, the regulation of desire, marriage, spouse relations, birth control, divorce, and parent-child obligations.

**The Physical Appetites**: Islam occupies a middle position between unrestrained free sex (which destroys the family) and ascetic suppression of desire (which contradicts nature). It recognizes the sexual drive, channels it through lawful marriage, and prohibits zina (fornication/adultery) as well as celibacy. The Qur'an commands: "And do not come near zina; indeed, it is an abomination and an evil way" (Al-Isra': 32). Islam not only prohibits zina itself but blocks every avenue leading to it.

**Khalwah (Privacy with a Non-Mahram)**: A man and a non-mahram woman must not be alone together — "Satan will be the third person." The Prophet warned especially about in-laws: "The in-law is death."

**Lowering the Gaze and Dress**: Both men and women are commanded to lower their gaze and guard their private parts (An-Nur: 30–31). A woman's 'awrah before a non-mahram is her entire body except face and hands. Women must not display their adornments except "that which is apparent" — which the author, following the Companions and early jurists, interprets as the face and hands and their ordinary adornments (kohl, rings). However, in times of widespread immorality, covering the face is recommended as added precaution. Twelve categories of mahram men before whom a woman may display her concealed adornments are listed from An-Nur: 31.

**Marriage**: Marriage is the sunnah of the Prophets and the foundation of Muslim society. The chapter discusses the prohibited degrees of marriage (muḥarramāt) in detail — blood relatives, milk-relatives, and in-laws, as enumerated in An-Nisa': 22–23. Polygyny is permitted with the strict condition of justice between co-wives; if one fears injustice, monogamy is required. The chapter also addresses the woman's right to choose her husband and the mahr (dowry).

**Relations between Spouses**: The chapter affirms that spouses may enjoy each other in all ways except anal intercourse, which is explicitly prohibited. During menstruation, only vaginal intercourse is prohibited; other forms of closeness remain permissible.

**Birth Control and Coitus Interruptus ('Azl)**: 'Azl is discussed with differing juristic views; the author permits it with the wife's consent, citing the hadith of Jabir. Abortion after ensoulment (120 days) is prohibited except to save the mother's life.

**Divorce**: While permitted, divorce is described as "the most hated of permissible things" to Allah. The chapter details the rules of ṭalāq, the 'iddah (waiting period), and the rights of both parties.

**Parent-Child Relations**: Kindness to parents, especially in old age, is strongly enjoined. Children's obedience to parents is an obligation except when they command disobedience to Allah.

### Key Topics

Sexual ethics; zina; khalwah; lowering the gaze; hijab and 'awrah; marriage prohibitions (muḥarramāt); polygyny; mahr; spousal relations; menstruation; 'azl and birth control; abortion; divorce (ṭalāq); parent-child relations

## Key Passages

- **Prohibition of zina and its avenues**: "And do not come near zina; indeed, it is an abomination and an evil way." (Al-Isra': 32)
- **Khalwah prohibition**: "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day must never be in privacy with a woman without there being a mahram with her, for otherwise Satan will be the third person." (Hadith, narrated by Ahmad)
- **Lowering the gaze**: "Tell the believing men that they should lower their gazes and guard their sexual organs… And tell the believing women that they should lower their gazes and guard their sexual organs, and not display their adornment except that which is apparent of it…" (An-Nur: 30–31)
- **Polygyny with justice**: "…then marry other women of your choice, two or three or four. But if you fear that you will not deal justly, then only one…" (An-Nisa': 3)
- **The most hated permissible thing**: "Of all the lawful acts, the most detestable to Allah is divorce." (Hadith, Abu Dawud)
