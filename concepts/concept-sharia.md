# Sharia (شَرِيعَة)

**Arabic:** شَرِيعَة (*al-Sharī`a*) — literally: "the path to a watering hole" or "the clear way"
**Domain:** Usul al-Fiqh, Islamic Ethics, Halal/Haram, Aqeedah

## Definition

Sharia (شَرِيعَة) is the divinely ordained comprehensive path of life revealed by Allah through the Qur'an and the Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH). The Arabic root ش-ر-ع connotes a clear, well-trodden path to water — the source of life — and by extension, the way of life that leads to salvation. In Islamic jurisprudence, Sharia encompasses the totality of Allah's legislative guidance: beliefs (*aqeedah*), acts of worship (*ibadat*), transactions (*mu'amalat*), moral conduct (*akhlaq*), and the principles governing lawful (*halal*) and prohibited (*haram*) matters.

Qaradawi describes Sharia as a guiding torch: **"My argument depends on sound and authentic texts and is guided by the torch of Shariah and Fiqh."**^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-01.txt] He elsewhere refers to his own work *Madkhal li-Dirāsat Al-Sharī`a Al-Islāmiya* (Introduction to Islamic Jurisprudence) as a foundational treatment of the subject.^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

## Qaradawi's Position

### 1. The Principle of Original Permissibility (Al-Asl fi al-Ashya' al-Ibahah)

A defining feature of Qaradawi's approach to Sharia is his emphasis on the principle that **everything is originally permissible unless explicitly prohibited**. He articulates this in *Diversion and Arts in Islam*:

> "The Muslim scholars are of the opinion that everything is essentially allowed for Allah, the Most High, states: 'God is the One Who created for you all that is on the earth' (Qur'an 2:29)."^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

He supports this with the Prophetic hadith: **"What Allah made permissible in His Book is lawful (Halal), and what He forbade is unlawful (Haram), and what He deliberately did not mention is forgiven."**^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt] The three legitimate sources of prohibition are the Qur'an, the Sunnah, and the consensus (*ijma'*) of Muslim scholars. What Allah has left unmentioned is a mercy, and Muslims are warned not to investigate it further — "He is never forgetful" (Qur'an 19:64).

This principle underpins Qaradawi's middle-way methodology: neither the extreme of total prohibition nor the extreme of total license, but a balanced adjudication based on authentic texts.

### 2. Sharia's Intrinsic Tolerance and Middle Ground (Wasatiyyah)

Qaradawi consistently presents Sharia as characterized by tolerance, balance, and a middle course. In his engagement with al-Ghazali's legal methodology for the permissibility of singing, he praises **"the Sharia's intrinsic tolerance, middle ground, and feasibility."**^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

He contrasts two extremist camps:
- **Excessive restricters:** Those who forbid all diversion, impose personal austerity on the community, and widen the scope of prohibition beyond what the texts warrant.
- **Licentious indulgers:** Those who erase all boundaries between lawful and unlawful, treating the halal/haram distinction as non-existent.^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-01.txt]

Qaradawi writes: **"Avoiding the two extremes, I have chosen to follow a middle course. I have exerted my utmost effort to reach a just and unbiased conclusion. My argument depends on sound and authentic texts and is guided by the torch of Shariah and Fiqh."**^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-01.txt]

Similarly, on the question of jest and recreation, he affirms: **"The middle course of action is at the very core of Islam. It distinguishes the Muslim nation from other nations."**^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-07.txt]

### 3. The Objectives of Sharia (Maqasid al-Sharia)

Qaradawi draws on the classical Maqasid framework articulated by Imam al-Raghib al-Asfahani in *Al-Dharī`a ilā Maqāṣid Al-Sharī`a* (The Book of Means to the Noble Qualities of Sharia). He identifies three interconnected objectives of Sharia for humankind:

1. **To worship Allah:** "And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me" (Qur'an 51:56).
2. **To succeed Allah on earth:** "Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority" (Qur'an 2:30).
3. **To develop the earth:** "He has produced you from the earth and settled you in it" (Qur'an 11:61).^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

Qaradawi explains that these three objectives are interconnected: "Developing the earth with the intention to fulfill one's duty toward Allah is an act of worship, whereas worship, broadly defined, involves succession and developing the earth. Work in this context means doing good deeds, which include all actions that aim to achieve and maintain the well-being of the individual and the community."^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

### 4. Sharia as a Unifying Bond of the Ummah

In *Ethics in Islam*, Qaradawi describes Sharia as one of the bonds uniting the Muslim community: **"Muslims, in particular, are all brothers and sisters. They share the same faith, the same scripture, the same prophet, and the same Shari'a."** He further elaborates that the Muslim society "is united in faith, rituals, worldview, moral ideals, traditions, and Shari'a. The Muslim society is based on a shared goal that connects this world with the afterlife, a shared moral system that balances idealism with realism, and shared sources for jurisprudence and creed."^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

### 5. Sharia and the Ethics of Animal Welfare

Qaradawi demonstrates that Sharia's concern extends even to animal welfare. Citing his earlier work *Madkhal li-Dirāsat Al-Sharī`a Al-Islāmiya*, he writes that **"moral behavior toward animals is a practical application for moral values, both in their own right and in accordance to the Sharia. Animals are particularly vulnerable, because mistreating them does not have immediate or direct consequences that could deter people from abusing them."** Kindness toward animals, he explains, is "a purely moral act motivated by our desire to serve Allah."^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

### 6. Sharia's Abrogation of Earlier Dispensations

Qaradawi invokes the principle that Sharia can abrogate earlier religious dispensations. In the context of images and statues, he notes that some scholars argued for permissibility based on Solomon's precedent, but **"they did not mention that the Sharia abrogated this privilege"** — meaning that what was permitted under a previous prophetic dispensation may have been superseded by the final Sharia of Muhammad (PBUH).^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-06.txt]

### 7. Sharia and Obedience to Rulers

Qaradawi discusses the limits of political obedience within Sharia. While the Qur'an commands obedience to those in authority (Qur'an 4:58-59), this obedience is conditional: **"This obedience is not absolute or blind, for people are to obey their leaders only if the leaders obey Allah. If the leaders order anything that contradicts Allah's rule, they should not be obeyed."** When the people and their rulers disagree, "they should follow the Holy Qur'an and the Sunnah to settle their disputes."^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt] He references Ibn Taymiyya's treatise *Al-Siyāsa Al-Shar`iyya* (Politics According to Sharia) in this context.^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

### 8. Sharia in the Context of Zakah and Islamic Economics

In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, the reference to Qaradawi as "Dean of Shariah College at University of Qatar, and member of several Academies of Islamic Jurisprudence and Shariah Advisory Bodies to Islamic financial institutions"^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/full.txt] underscores that his juristic methodology is grounded in Sharia and serves the practical implementation of Islamic economic law. Zakah itself is a pillar of Sharia with "far-reaching economic consequences" that demands the attention of "shariah scholars who would elaborate and analyze the law of zakat in a contemporary manner."^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/full.txt]

### 9. Ghazali's Methodology as a Model for Sharia-Based Reasoning

Qaradawi highlights Imam al-Ghazali's approach in *Ihya' Ulum al-Din* as exemplary of Sharia reasoning. Ghazali "presented a liberal fiqh which rejected the fetters of religious schools… He began to investigate Sharia from a different perspective," examining the conditions under which permissible things become impermissible. Ghazali identified five prerequisites for the prohibition of otherwise permissible things, demonstrating Sharia's nuanced approach to halal/haram boundaries.^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

## Evidence Base

### Quranic Verses
- **Qur'an 2:29** — "God is the One Who created for you all that is on the earth" (basis for original permissibility)
- **Qur'an 6:119** — Allah has explained what He has forbidden (limits of prohibition)
- **Qur'an 6:145** — Explicit prohibitions are limited
- **Qur'an 51:56** — "I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me" (first Maqsad)
- **Qur'an 2:30** — Succession on earth (second Maqsad)
- **Qur'an 11:61** — Developing the earth (third Maqsad)
- **Qur'an 4:58-59** — Obedience to those in authority (conditional on Sharia compliance)
- **Qur'an 19:64** — "Your Lord is never forgetful" (what Allah omits is deliberate mercy)
- **Qur'an 21:107** — "We have not sent you except as a mercy" (Sharia as mercy)
- **Qur'an 25:67** — The middle way in spending (wasatiyyah)

### Prophetic Hadiths
- **"What Allah made permissible in His Book is lawful (Halal), and what He forbade is unlawful (Haram), and what He deliberately did not mention is forgiven"** — Reported by al-Hakim on the authority of Abu al-Darda'; authenticated by al-Bazzar and elevated to *hasan* by al-Nawawi^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]
- **"Allah enjoined certain duties (Fara'id) which you should follow, and boundary limits (Hudud) which you should not violate. There are things which He deliberately did not mention by Mercy from Him"** — Reported by al-Daraqutni on the authority of Abu Tha'laba^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]
- Hadith of the two slave girls singing (al-Bukhari and Muslim) — demonstrating the Sharia's tolerance of wholesome entertainment^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]
- Numerous hadiths on animal welfare cited in *Madkhal li-Dirāsat Al-Sharī`a Al-Islāmiya*^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

### Classical Scholarly References
- **Al-Raghib al-Asfahani**, *Al-Dharī`a ilā Maqāṣid Al-Sharī`a* — Maqasid framework^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-04.txt]

Qaradawi's distinctive contribution is his consistent application of the **original permissibility principle** across all domains of contemporary life — arts, entertainment, economics, and social ethics — while maintaining firm boundaries where Sharia texts are explicit. His approach combines Ghazali's nuanced methodology with a commitment to wasatiyyah (the middle way), making Sharia accessible and practical for modern Muslims without diluting its core obligations.

## Contemporary Relevance

1. **The halal/haram debate in modern life:** Qaradawi's principle of original permissibility provides a framework for evaluating new technologies, financial instruments, and cultural forms (social media, entertainment, AI) — what is not explicitly prohibited remains permissible, but must be assessed for its moral content and consequences.

2. **Extremism vs. license:** In an age of both religious extremism (expanding prohibition beyond what texts warrant) and moral license (erasing all boundaries), Qaradawi's wasatiyyah methodology offers a principled middle path grounded in authentic texts.

3. **Maqasid-based ethics:** The three objectives of Sharia — worship, succession, and development — provide a framework for addressing contemporary challenges from climate stewardship (developing the earth) to economic justice (succession on earth requires equitable resource distribution).

4. **Animal welfare and environmental ethics:** Qaradawi's demonstration that Sharia mandates kindness even to animals positions Islamic ethics as an ally of contemporary environmental and animal welfare movements.

5. **Political authority and accountability:** The principle that obedience to rulers is conditional on their compliance with Sharia remains a touchstone in debates about governance, rule of law, and human rights in Muslim-majority societies.

6. **Sharia scholarship in modern institutions:** Qaradawi's role in Academies of Islamic Jurisprudence and Sharia Advisory Bodies for Islamic financial institutions illustrates how Sharia methodology continues to shape modern Islamic economics and finance.
