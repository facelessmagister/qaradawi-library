# Responsibility / Mas'uliyyah (مَسْؤُولِيَّة)

## Definition

**Mas'uliyyah** (مَسْؤُولِيَّة) — "responsibility, accountability" — refers to the Islamic principle that every individual bears moral and legal accountability before Allah for their actions, beliefs, and trusts. The Qur'an declares: "Every soul earns not [blame] except against itself, and no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another" (Q 6:164). Responsibility in Islam operates on multiple planes: individual accountability before God (*taklif*), communal responsibility for welfare and order, and institutional responsibility — including the state's obligation to collect and distribute zakat. Qaradawi emphasizes that the Prophetic pattern is "a comprehensive pattern" that integrates "freedom and responsibility" without reducing one to the other, and that the "weaker ones among the people" carry a specific claim on the responsibility of those in power. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Individual responsibility is non-transferable.** Qaradawi grounds responsibility in the Qur'anic principle that each soul bears only its own burden. No person can carry the sin of another; no community can discharge the individual's obligation of prayer, fasting, or zakat on their behalf. ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-01.txt]

2. **Communal responsibility complements individual duty.** The Islamic concept of responsibility is not atomistic. The state bears responsibility for collecting and distributing zakat; the wealthy bear responsibility for the poor; scholars bear responsibility for guidance. Qaradawi notes that in *Approaching the Sunnah*, the Prophetic model integrates "freedom and responsibility, individualism and collectivism." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **Responsibility extends to the trust of power.** Those in positions of authority — rulers, judges, scholars — bear a heightened accountability. The Qur'an's warning that "the heavens and the earth and the mountains" refused the trust (*amanah*) of responsibility, and "man bore it" (Q 33:72), underscores the gravity of this charge. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]
