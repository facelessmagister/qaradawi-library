# Fasting (صَوْم)

## Definition

**Ṣawm** (صَوْم) — literally "abstention" — is the fourth pillar of Islam, the obligatory act of refraining from food, drink, and sexual relations from dawn (*fajr*) to sunset (*maghrib*) during the month of Ramadan, as well as supererogatory fasts observed on other days. The Qur'an prescribes: **"Fasting is decreed upon you as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous"** (Q 2:183). The Arabic root *(s-w-m)* signifies refraining or holding back, and in the legal context it designates the deliberate, worshipful abstention from specific bodily pleasures during a defined period.

Fasting in Islam is not mere hunger or thirst; it is an act of worship (*ʿibādah*) whose primary purpose is the cultivation of **taqwā** (God-consciousness). The Qur'anic phrase *laʿallakum tattaqūn* ("that you may become righteous") makes the moral and spiritual aim explicit — the physical discipline serves the soul's discipline.

## Qaradawi's Position

Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi treats fasting across multiple works, emphasizing its moral-spiritual dimension and its role in the wasatiyyah (middle path) of Islam:

1. **Fasting and moral character are inseparable.** The hadith Qaradawi cites makes this plain: "It may be that a fasting person gains nothing from his fast but hunger" (Ahmad, Ibn Mājah) and "Whoever does not give up falsehood and acting upon it, Allah is in no need for his abandoning food and drink" (al-Bukhārī). Fasting that does not transform conduct is formally valid but spiritually void. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-00.txt]

2. **Fasting exemplifies Islam's middle path between asceticism and indulgence.** The Prophet (peace be upon him) "used to fast some days and break his fast other days, to perform prayers at night and also to sleep part of the night. He was married and had children." When ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr vowed to fast every day and pray every night, the Prophet corrected him: "Fast one day and leave it the other day, and that is the fasting of the Prophet David, which is the best fasting… There is no better fasting than that." Islam neither enjoins monastic renunciation nor permits unbridled gratification. ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-03.txt]

3. **Crescent-sighting vs. calculation for Ramadan.** Qaradawi has for years called for the adoption of definitive astronomical calculation — at least in the negation of crescent-sighting, if not its affirmation — to reduce the chronic differences among Muslim communities over the start and end of Ramadan. He argues that the Prophetic hadith "Fast upon sighting the crescent and stop fasting upon sighting it; if it is hidden from you, estimate it" has both an **objective** (completing the fast of Ramadan correctly) and a **means** (visual sighting, which was the accessible method at the time). When calculation becomes more reliable and less prone to error than eyewitness testimony, the higher objective (*maqṣid*) of the law is served by adopting it. He cites Taqī al-Dīn al-Subkī (d. 756 AH) who ruled that "if calculation negates the possibility of sighting, the qāḍī is obliged to reject the testimony of witnesses, because calculation is definitive and testimony is conjectural." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

4. **Concessions in fasting reflect Islam's principle of ease.** The Prophet made concessions for the sick, the traveller, the pregnant, and the nursing woman — "There is no virtue in fasting while traveling" under strenuous conditions. This illustrates the broader Sunnah principle that "God desires for His community ease and He does not desire for them hardship" (Q 4:28) and "God loves that you act according to His indulgence, just as He loves that you act according to His decrees." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

5. **Fasting and fatwā adaptability.** Qaradawi cites the Prophetic hadith of the young man and the old man who each asked whether they could kiss their wives while fasting — and received opposite answers — as a proof that legal opinions (*fatāwā*) can differ according to the circumstances and conditions of the questioner. The young man was told "No" and the old man "Yes," because "an old man has better self-control." ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Evidence Base

### Qur'anic Verses

- **"Fasting is decreed upon you as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous"** (Q 2:183) — establishes fasting as obligatory and its purpose as *taqwā*. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-00.txt]
- **"And do not kill yourselves — surely God is ever-merciful to you"** (Q 4:29) — cited by ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ to justify tayammum in extreme cold instead of ghusl, illustrating the principle of concession. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### Prophetic Hadiths

- **"It may be that a fasting person gains nothing from his fast but hunger"** (Ahmad, Ibn Mājah, al-Ḥākim) — fasting without moral transformation is void of spiritual benefit. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-00.txt]
- **"Whoever does not give up saying and doing falsehoods, Allah is in no need for his abandoning food and drink"** (al-Bukhārī, Ahmad, Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Mājah) — fasting must effect moral change. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-01.txt]
- **"Fast upon sighting the crescent and stop fasting upon sighting it; if it is hidden from you, estimate it"** (al-Bukhārī, Muslim) — the foundational text on crescent-sighting, which Qaradawi interprets as having both an objective and a means. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]
- **"There is no virtue in fasting while travelling"** — for journeys that are strenuous. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]
- Abdullah ibn ʿAmr's hadith on the fasting of Dāwūd: **"Fast one day and leave it the other day, and that is the fasting of Prophet David, which is the best fasting"** (al-Bukhārī). ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-03.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

### On Crescent-Sighting vs. Calculation

| Position | School/Scholar | View |
|----------|---------------|------|
| **Sighting-only** | Majority of classical fuqahā' (all four madhhabs) | Visual sighting (*ru'yah*) is the sole legitimate method; calculation may not replace it. |
| **Calculation permitted** | Ibn al-Shāfiʿī (some reports), Ibn Surayj, al-Subkī | Astronomical calculation may be used, at least to negate impossible sightings. |
| **Qaradawi** | — | Advocates calculation at minimum in the negation of sightings (i.e., if definitive astronomy shows the crescent could not have been seen, testimony must be rejected). Goes further than the majority but aligns with al-Subkī's principle that "the conjectural cannot contradict the definitive." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt] |

### On Concessions and Ease

| Position | School | View |
|----------|--------|------|
| **Broadest concessions** | Ḥanafī | Permits combining prayers and breaking the fast while travelling more freely. |
| **Conditional breaking** | Mālikī, Shāfiʿī | Breaking the fast while travelling is permissible (not obligatory) if the journey qualifies. |
| **Qaradawi** | — | Emphasizes the Prophetic principle that "God loves that you act according to His indulgence" and treats concessions as a feature, not a flaw, of the Sharīʿah. |

## Key Sub-Topics

### Obligatory Fasting (Ṣawm al-Farḍ)

- **Ramadan fasting** is fard ʿayn (individual obligation) upon every sane, adult, resident Muslim.
- The sick, the traveller, the pregnant, and the nursing woman are permitted to defer — and must make up the missed days later (Q 2:184–185).
- **Zakat al-Fiṭr** (*ṣadaqat al-fiṭr*) is a separate obligatory payment linked to the completion of Ramadan, discussed in [[concept-zakat]].

### Supererogatory Fasting

- The Prophet's preferred supererogatory fast was that of Dāwūd: fasting every other day.
- Fasting three days per month (the "white days" — *al-ayyām al-bīḍ*) is recommended.
- **ʿĀshūrā'** (10 Muḥarram) — recommended but not obligatory.

### Fasting and Moral Transformation

The link between fasting and moral improvement is axiomatic in Qaradawi's ethical framework. Acts of worship that do not translate into moral behavior lose their value: prayer "prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Q 29:45); fasting produces *taqwā* (Q 2:183); charity "purifies and causes increase" (Q 9:103). If worship and ethics are decoupled, the worship is barren. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-01.txt]

## Contemporary Relevance

### Moon-Sighting Unity
The annual disagreement over the start and end of Ramadan — sometimes producing differences of up to three days between Muslim-majority countries — is a recurring source of communal distress. Qaradawi's position on using astronomical calculation to at least negate impossible sightings provides a workable resolution that respects the text of the hadith while honoring its underlying purpose: accurate observance of Ramadan and communal unity.

### Moderation vs. Extremism
Islam's refusal to enjoin perpetual fasting or monastic celibacy (the hadith of ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr) is central to Qaradawi's wasaṭiyyah framework. Extremism in worship is as dangerous as neglect: the middle path is the Prophetic path.

### Fatwā Flexibility
The differing answers given to the young and old man about kissing while fasting illustrate that Islamic rulings are not rigid formulas but context-sensitive judgments — a principle Qaradawi frequently applies across all areas of fiqh.

### ʿĪd al-Fiṭr
The feast marking the end of Ramadan was instituted by the Prophet as a wholesome替代 for pre-Islamic pagan festivals, giving Muslims "better ones, namely the Day of Adha and the Day of Fiṭr on which Muslims break their thirty-day fast of Ramadan." ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]
