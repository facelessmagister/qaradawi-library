# Quran / The Qur'an (قُرْآن)

## Definition

**The Qur'an** (قُرْآن) — "the recitation" — is the supreme source of Islamic law, theology, and guidance: the preserved, uncreated word of Allah revealed to the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) through the Angel Jibril over twenty-three years. Qaradawi describes it as "the supreme sign and the greatest miracle of Muhammad (SAAS), the preserved everlasting Book, into which falsehood cannot enter from any direction. Its permanency from first to last makes it the primary fixed source validating all the sources of Islam and its further secondary proofs — one never argues from the latter to validate it." The Qur'an is not merely a book of law but a comprehensive guide: "And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification of everything" (Q 16:89). In Qaradawi's jurisprudential framework, all other sources — Sunnah, ijma', qiyas, ijtihad — are subordinate to and checked against the Qur'anic text. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **The Qur'an is the primary, immutable source.** All other evidence must agree with the Qur'an; any hadith or ruling that contradicts a definitive Qur'anic text is rejected. "Only one thing limits all scholars. That is the unmistakable words of the Book of God." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

2. **The Qur'an establishes general principles; the Sunnah provides particulars.** Qaradawi follows the classical usul principle: the Qur'an states the general commands (e.g., "establish prayer," "pay zakat"), while the Sunnah gives their specific details (times of prayer, rates of zakat). The two are inseparable. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **The Qur'an's general texts must be adopted unless restricted by sound specific text.** This is Qaradawi's methodological principle for extending zakat to new categories: the Qur'an's general references to "wealth" (*amwal*) and "earnings" apply to all forms, unless a specific authentic text restricts them. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]
