# Riya / Showing Off and Ostentation (رِيَاء)

## Definition

**Riya** (رِيَاء) — "showing off, ostentation, performing acts of worship for social recognition rather than for Allah" — is the antithesis of *ikhlas* (sincerity) and one of the most strongly condemned spiritual diseases in Islam. The Prophet warned: "The thing I fear most for you is minor shirk (*al-shirk al-asghar*). When asked what it was, he replied: riya." By qualifying it as "minor shirk," the Prophet placed it in the gravest moral category because the *riya*' performer directs worship to people rather than to Allah, thereby violating *tawhid*. Qaradawi addresses riya in the context of zakat expenditure — where "prideful showing off" must be avoided — and in the broader framework of halal/haram, where the sincerity of one's intention determines the moral quality of even permissible actions. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Riya nullifies the spiritual reward of acts of worship.** The Qur'an warns those who spend "to be seen by people" (Q 107:6; Q 4:142). Qaradawi echoes this in *Fiqh al-Zakah*, where he requires that zakat be paid "out of sincerity on the part of the believer for the sake of God" — not from the desire for recognition or social prestige. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

2. **Avoidance of showing off governs even permissible actions.** In *The Lawful and the Prohibited*, Qaradawi notes that acts done with "prideful showing off" lose their permissibility even when the underlying action is halal. The prohibition of extravagance in dress and adornment is partly motivated by the avoidance of riya. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

3. **Riya and its opposite (ikhlas) define the spiritual axis of Islamic ethics.** Qaradawi frames the struggle between riya and ikhlas as the internal dimension of every moral choice. Even charitable spending, when contaminated by the desire for reputation, becomes spiritually barren — a theme that appears throughout *Ethics in Islam* and *The Lawful and the Prohibited*.
