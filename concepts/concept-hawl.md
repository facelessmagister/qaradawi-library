# Hawl / Zakat Fiscal Year (حَوْل)

## Definition

**Hawl** (حَوْل) — "year, circuit, revolution" — denotes the completion of one lunar year of ownership of wealth that has reached the nisab threshold, after which zakat becomes due. It is one of the four essential conditions for zakatability alongside complete ownership (*milk kamil*), growth or potential for growth (*nama'*), and reaching the nisab. The hawl principle ensures that zakat is an annual obligation, not a continuous drain on the same capital. Agricultural produce is the notable exception: zakat on crops is due at harvest time, not after a year, because the Prophetic precedent specifies "the day that the harvest is gathered" (Q 6:141). Qaradawi discusses hawl extensively in *Fiqh al-Zakah*, especially regarding earned income and fiscal-year accounting. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-09.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Hawl applies to monetary and trade wealth, not to crops or minerals.** The hawl condition is specific to gold, silver, trade goods, and other continuously held assets. Agricultural produce is zakated at harvest, and rikaz (buried treasure) at discovery, because the precedent for these categories does not require a year's passage. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

2. **For earned income, an established fiscal year governs.** When a person has an established fiscal year for zakat, earned income that accrues during that year is zakated at the end of the fiscal year, not from the date each portion was earned. Makhul's opinion, which Qaradawi cites, is that income spent before the fiscal year-end is not zakatable, but income saved is. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-09.txt]

3. **Hawl reflects the principle of ease, not hardship.** Requiring zakat on the same wealth multiple times within a year would constitute double taxation. The hawl principle embodies the Shariah's concern that obligations not become oppressive.
