# Wasatiyyah / The Middle Way (وَسَطِيَّة)

## Definition

**Wasatiyyah** (وَسَطِيَّة) — "moderation, middle course, balance" — is the Islamic principle that the community of believers is a "middle nation" (*ummatan wasatan*, Q 2:143), charged with avoiding both the excess of over-strictness and the deficiency of laxity. Qaradawi treats wasatiyyah as a defining characteristic of the Prophetic Sunnah, which is "a moderate pattern for a moderate society, with neither overdoing in it nor doing too little." The Qur'an invokes the principle directly: "That you do not exceed the measure, but establish the measure with equity, and do not fall short of the balance" (Q 55:8-9). Qaradawi's entire juristic methodology — the middle path between taqlid and unbridled innovation, between over-restriction and permissiveness, between individualism and collectivism — is an expression of wasatiyyah. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **The Prophetic model is the embodiment of wasatiyyah.** Qaradawi cites the hadith of the three men who resolved to fast perpetually, pray all night, and abstain from marriage — and the Prophet's response: "Whoever prefers [something else] above my sunnah is not one of mine." The Prophet then enjoined balance: "Your body owns a right over you, your eyes own a right over you, your family own a right over you." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

2. **Wasatiyyah applies to fiqh as well as spirituality.** In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi's middle position between expanding zakat beyond the classical categories (progressive ijtihad) and restricting it to the four traditional categories (conservative taqlid) is itself an exercise in wasatiyyah. Similarly, his rejection of both the closure of ijtihad and reckless pseudo-ijtihad is a moderate position. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **Islamic moderation balances freedom and responsibility.** In *Ethics in Islam*, Qaradawi describes "the Islamic moderation, which is a balance between extremism and secularism," arguing that Islam achieves justice by regulating freedom and keeping desire in check. Wasatiyyah is not compromise between truth and falsehood, but the straight path between two extremes of error. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-03.txt]
