# Isnad / Hadith Chain of Transmission (إِسْنَاد)

## Definition

**Isnad** (إِسْنَاد) — "support, linking" — is the chain of narrators through which a hadith is traced back to the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH). It is the distinctive methodological device of Islamic hadith science, unique among historical-critical traditions in its systematic documentation of transmission pathways. Each narrator in the chain must be identified by name, evaluated for trustworthiness (*tawthiq*) and precision (*dabt*), and scrutinized for continuity and consistency. Qaradawi devotes extensive attention to isnad analysis in *Approaching the Sunnah*, treating it as the essential mechanism by which the Muslim community preserves the authenticity of Prophetic tradition and guards against fabrication. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Isnad is the indispensable guarantor of hadith authenticity.** Qaradawi quotes the famous statement: "We are strict about the isnads of the sunnahs and the injunctions; then we relax on the isnads" of exhortatory reports. This differential rigor — strict for legal rulings, more lenient for virtues — reflects a nuanced methodology. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

2. **A weak isnad does not necessarily invalidate a hadith's legal value** when other supporting chains exist. Qaradawi follows the methodology of grading hadiths according to the collective strength of their chains, not a single narrator in isolation. He evaluates isnads in *Approaching the Sunnah* with a concern for the substance of the report alongside its formal chain. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

3. **The "golden chain" represents the highest standard.** Qaradawi notes the famous "golden chain" of narrators — from Malik from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar from the Prophet — as an exemplar of isnad excellence that scholars have consistently elevated above weaker transmissions. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]
