# Usury (رِبَا)

**Arabic:** رِبَا
**Transliteration:** Riba
**Domain:** Fiqh Muamalat — Financial Transactions

## Definition

Riba (رِبَا) literally means "increase" or "excess." In Islamic jurisprudence, it refers to any unjustified increment in an exchange or loan — pre-eminently, the charging of interest on money lent. Riba encompasses two principal categories:

1. **Riba al-Nasi'ah** (رِبَا النَّسِيئَة): A time-based excess — charging an additional amount on a loan in return for deferred payment. This is the interest charged on money lent, where the borrower must repay more than the principal.
2. **Riba al-Fadl** (رِبَا الفَضْل): An excess in a spot exchange of commodities of the same genus — e.g., trading gold for gold in unequal quantities, even if hand-to-hand.

The prohibition of riba is one of the most emphatic and clear-cut rulings in Islam, declared in the Qur'an and Sunnah with categorical force. Denying the prohibition of riba amounts, by scholarly consensus, to apostasy from Islam.

## Qaradawi's Position

### Riba as the Antithesis of Zakat

Qaradawi frames riba and zakat as the dual pillars of Islamic economic ethics — one prohibited, the other obligatory. In his masterwork *Fiqh al-Zakah*, he states:

> "There are two main subjects in Islamic economics that deserve to be studied in this time because of their relevance. One is an essential obligation or a pillar of Islam. The other is a prohibition, a major sin. The first subject is zakah, the second riba (interest). There is no disagreement among Muslims about the obligatory nature of the first, nor the prohibition of the other, for denying this obligation or that prohibition amounts to apostasy." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/full.txt]

God draws an explicit comparison in Surah al-Rum (30:39) between the apparent increase of riba and the real increase through zakat:

> "That which you lend in riba for increase throughout the poverty of other people will have no increase with God, but that which you lay out for zakah, seeking the countenance of God, will increase — it is they who will get the recompense multiplied." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/full.txt]

### Prohibition and Its Severity

In *Economic Security in Islam*, Qaradawi presents riba prohibition within the broader framework of Islamic economic security:

1. **Islam forbids usury** to eradicate the evil of concentration of wealth. While Islam encourages fair distribution through inheritance and zakat, it categorically prohibits riba, which concentrates wealth in the hands of lenders at the expense of borrowers. ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/full.txt]

2. **The usurer is at war with Allah and His Messenger.** According to the Holy Qur'an, unless the usurious person repents, they are considered at war against Almighty Allah and His Prophet (peace be upon him). If they repent, they can retrieve only their principal amount — "These are the good souls who do not inflict injury on others nor are they harmed by others." ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-07.txt]

3. **Riba exploits the poor.** Those who lend money on interest "suck the blood" of borrowers, making the rich man richer and the poor man poorer. Usury reduces a person to wretchedness. ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-07.txt]

4. **The whole structure of Capitalism rests on riba**, and its prohibition is one of Islam's definitive economic reforms. Hoarding essential commodities for forty days to make huge profits is itself a form of reluctance toward Allah. ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-07.txt]

### Riba in the Pre-Islamic and Islamic Context

In *Ethics in Islam*, Qaradawi notes that Pre-Islamic Arab societies allowed usury ("and it seems that Jews were the first to promote it and then it became a social norm"). Islam eradicated this practice alongside other social evils such as gambling and tribalism. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-02.txt]

### Riba in Muslim Economy

In *Economy in the Light of the Sunnah*, Qaradawi places riba among the core market transactions that the Sunnah regulates:

> "Traditions command justice, forbid injustice, enjoin seeking Halal (lawful) earning, and warn strongly against unlawful earning, particularly interest, gambling or hoarding (of goods)." ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

He groups the Prophetic traditions on economic matters into categories including "usury (interest) and loan, partnership and stock exchange." ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### Riba and Unlawful Wealth in Zakat Calculation

In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi addresses whether income derived from riba-based sources is still subject to zakat. He rules that interest-bearing bonds, though their interest component is prohibited, do not exempt the holder from zakat on the principal — "The fact that interest is prohibited is no reason for exempting these bonds from zakah, because a person who commits what is prohibited must not be given any privilege." ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-10.txt]

### Comprehensive Social Reform

Qaradawi's treatment of riba is not limited to a fiqh ruling on financial interest. He places it within a comprehensive Islamic critique of exploitation:

- In *Ethics in Islam*, he argues that fair wages alone are insufficient in a society "rife with corruption, monopoly, usury, and dictatorship," where people accumulate debt they can never repay. ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-06.txt]
- In *Economic Security in Islam*, he describes the trap that riba creates: when a person in need is forced to take a loan, "it would not be given to him without charging interest on it," and when calamity strikes, he is "compelled to live on loans" and becomes "a person under debt." ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-07.txt]
- In the same work, he cites the Qur'anic story of Qarun (28:81) — a wealthy man who denied God's blessings and "usurped the rights of his community," for which God "caused the earth to swallow him and his dwelling-place." ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-02.txt]

## Evidence Base

### Quranic Verses

1. **Surah al-Baqarah 2:275-279** — The foundational verses on riba prohibition: "Those who devour usury will not stand except as stands one whom the Devil has driven to madness... Allah has permitted trade and forbidden usury... O you who believe! Fear Allah and give up what remains of your demand for usury, if you are indeed believers."
2. **Surah Al 'Imran 3:130** — "O you who believe! Devour not usury, doubled and multiplied; but fear Allah, that you may prosper."
3. **Surah al-Rum 30:39** — Comparing riba unfavorably with zakat: "That which you lend in riba for increase throughout the poverty of other people will have no increase with God."
4. **Surah al-Nisa 4:161** — Condemning the People of the Book: "And of their taking usury though they were forbidden."

### Prophetic Traditions

1. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "A hoarder who hoards essential commodities for forty days for making huge profits shows his reluctance to Allah, and in turn Allah will show His reluctance to him." (Ahmad) ^[raw/extracted/economic-security/ch-07.txt]
2. The comprehensive farewell hadith on riba: "Every instance of riba from the pre-Islamic period is abolished. You shall have your principal amounts — do not commit injustice and no injustice shall be committed against you."
3. The hadith on the seven destructive sins includes: "Consuming riba." (Bukhari and Muslim) ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-05.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

*Detailed comparison with madhhab positions pending.*

Key areas of scholarly consensus and differences:

- **Consensus (ijma'):** All four Sunni madhhabs agree that riba is categorically prohibited (haram). Denying this prohibition is kufr.
- **Riba al-Nasi'ah:** Unanimously prohibited by all schools.
- **Riba al-Fadl:** Prohibited by all schools, but there are differences in application (e.g., the Shafi'i school's "six ribawi items" framework vs. the Hanafi broader interpretation).
- **Modern bank interest:** The majority of classical and contemporary scholars (including Qaradawi) consider conventional bank interest to fall under riba al-Nasi'ah and thus categorically prohibited.

## Contemporary Relevance

Qaradawi's work highlights several dimensions of riba's relevance today:

1. **Debt traps and global poverty:** The riba-based financial system creates perpetual debt cycles, especially for the poor and developing nations — precisely the condition Islam sought to eliminate.
2. **Islamic banking alternatives:** The prohibition of riba necessitates the development of profit-and-loss sharing (mudarabah/musharakah) models that align with Islamic economic ethics.
3. **Zakat vs. Riba:** In the modern Muslim world, the obligation of zakat and the prohibition of riba constitute two sides of the same economic ethic — wealth must circulate justly, not accumulate through exploitation.
4. **Social justice:** Qaradawi's holistic approach insists that abolishing riba alone is insufficient without establishing zakat, ensuring fair wages, and eliminating monopolistic hoarding. The Islamic social system must be "applied in its totality, for it does not function if any of its components are left out." ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-06.txt]
