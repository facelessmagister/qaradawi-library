# Purity (طَهَارَة)

## Definition

**Ṭahārah** (طَهَارَة) — literally "purity, cleanliness, purification" — is the condition of ritual and spiritual cleanliness required for the validity of acts of worship in Islam. It encompasses both **physical purification** (removal of actual impurities, *najāsah*) and **ritual purification** (removal of Hadath, the ritual impurity state), and extends metaphorically to the **purification of the soul** from moral vices. The Prophet said: **"Purity is half of faith"** (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim).

In fiqh, *ṭahārah* is the gateway to all worship: no prayer (*ṣalāh*), no circumambulation (*ṭawāf*), and no touching of the Qur'an is valid without it. It is the precondition without which the other pillars remain inaccessible. The category includes *wuḍūʾ* (ablution), *ghusl* (full-body bath), *tayammum* (dry ablution), and the removal of *najāsah* (physical impurities).

Beyond the ritual dimension, *ṭahārah* in Qaradawi's corpus carries a spiritual and ethical meaning: the Qur'an describes the Prophet's mission as one of **purification** — **"It is He who has sent among the unlettered a Messenger from themselves, reciting to them His verses and purifying them and teaching them the Book and wisdom"** (Q 62:2) — making *taẓkiyah* (spiritual purification) inseparable from *ṭahārah* (ritual purification).

## Qaradawi's Position

Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi treats purity across multiple dimensions:

1. **Purification is a Prophetic mission, not merely a ritual rule.** The Qur'an repeatedly states that the Prophet was sent to "recite, purify, and teach" (Q 2:129; 3:164; 62:2–4), and Qaradawi explains that this "purifying" (*yuzakkīhim*) means spiritual cleansing — ridding people of the evils of ignorance (*jāhiliyyah*), polytheism (*shirk*), hypocrisy (*nifāq*), and other moral diseases — and then building their moral character with true faith, genuine monotheism, honesty, and virtue. He cites the Qur'anic verses: **"He has certainly succeeded who purifies himself"** (Q 87:14) and **"He has succeeded who purifies it [the soul]"** (Q 91:9). ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-04.txt]

2. **Ritual purification carries moral and economic dimensions.** Even *wuḍūʾ* (ablution) is not purely ritual. Qaradawi cites the hadith of Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, where the Prophet saw Saʿd performing ablution and said: **"Is there wastefulness even in this?"** When Saʿd asked whether there could be wastefulness in using water for ablution, the Prophet replied: **"Yes, even if you are taking water from a running river."** This applies the Islamic ethic of avoiding waste (*isrāf*) even in acts of worship that require water. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

3. **Concessions in purification reflect Islam's principle of ease (*takhfīf*).** The Prophet prescribed *tayammum* in place of *wuḍūʾ* and *ghusl* when water is unavailable or its use would cause harm. The famous incident of ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, who fell into *janābah* (major ritual impurity) on a severely cold night and performed *tayammum* rather than risk a harmful bath, illustrates this principle. When other Companions complained to the Prophet, he accepted ʿAmr's reasoning, which cited the Qur'anic verse: **"And do not kill yourselves — surely God is ever-merciful to you"** (Q 4:29). ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

4. **The Prophet warned against pedantry and excess even in purification.** The incident of the wounded man who was given a bath against medical necessity and died as a result prompted the Prophet's rebuke: **"They killed him! May God kill them! Why do they not ask when they do not know? For the only cure for ignorance is asking a question."** This demonstrates that over-scrupulousness (*waraʿ ghayr muʿtamad*) in ritual purity that leads to harm is itself contrary to the Sharīʿah. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

5. **Spiritual purification (*taẓkiyah*) and moral purification are interdependent.** Moral values are closely tied to acts of worship: prayer "prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Q 29:45), charity "purifies them and causes them to increase" (Q 9:103), and fasting produces *taqwā* (Q 2:183). Purity in Islam is therefore not merely ritual cleanness but encompasses the whole transformation of the self — **"Truly he succeeds that purifies it, and he fails that corrupts it!"** (Q 91:9–10). ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-00.txt]

6. **Islam rejects the view that the human body is impure or that physical matter is base.** Against materialist doctrines that reduce man to "a clot of clay" and make "purity, decency, striving against one's vain desires, sacrifice, and seeking Allah's Pleasure strange and unnatural," Islam holds the body in honor. The call to purity does not denigrate the body but dignifies it — the believer purifies the body as a vessel for worship, and the soul as a dwelling for God-consciousness. ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-01.txt]

### Prophetic Hadiths

- **"Purity is half of faith"** (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim) — the foundational hadith establishing *ṭahārah* as central to Islam.
- **"Is there wastefulness [in this]?" / "Yes, even if you are taking water from a running river"** — the hadith of Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ on economy in ablution. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]
- The hadith of ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ performing *tayammum* in severe cold — accepted by the Prophet on the basis of preventing harm. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]
- The hadith of the wounded man whose well-meaning Companions forced him to take a bath, causing his death — **"They killed him! May God kill them! Why do they not ask when they do not know?"** ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]
- **"God is beautiful and He loves beauty"** and **"God loves to see traces of His favors on His servant"** — the broader Prophetic context in which concessions in purification and other rites are situated. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

### On the Concession of Tayammum

| Position | School | View |
|----------|--------|------|
| **Tayammum replaces both *wuḍūʾ* and *ghusl*** | Ḥanafī | Tayammum is a full substitute when water is unavailable or harmful to use. |
| **Tayammum only replaces *wuḍūʾ*** | Some Shāfiʿī variants | Restrict tayammum to minor impurity only. |
| **Qaradawi / mainstream consensus** | — | Tayammum replaces both, based on the evidence of ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ's use of tayammum in *janābah* and the general Qur'anic text (Q 4:43; 5:6). |

### On Over-Scrupulousness in Purification

| Position | View |
|----------|------|
| **Extreme *waraʿ*** | Some ascetic tendencies led to excessive caution in purification (e.g., washing three, five, or seven times beyond what is required). |

## Key Sub-Topics

### Wuḍūʾ (Ablution)

The ritual washing of the face, forearms, head, and feet before prayer, using clean water. Qaradawi emphasizes that even *wuḍūʾ* carries ethical obligations — the prophetic prohibition of wasting water applies even at a running river, integrating economic ethics into ritual practice. ^[raw/extracted/education-economy-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### Ghusl (Full-Body Bath)

Required after *janābah* (major ritual impurity following sexual intercourse or seminal discharge), menstruation, and childbirth. The Prophet's acceptance of ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ's *tayammum* in place of *ghusl* when water use would cause harm establishes the principle that **preservation of life and health takes precedence over full ritual observance**.

### Tayammum (Dry Ablution)

The substitution of clean earth (*ṣaʿīd ṭayyib*) for water when water is unavailable or its use would be harmful. Qaradawi presents this as part of Islam's broader principle of *takhfīf* (alleviation of burden): **"God desires for you ease and He does not desire for you hardship"** (Q 2:185). The concession is not an exception but a designed feature of the law.

### Najāsah (Physical Impurity)

The removal of physical impurities from the body, clothing, and place of prayer. This category deals with tangible filth (blood, urine, excrement, etc.) as distinct from ritual states (*ḥadath*).

### Taẓkiyah (Spiritual Purification)

The purification of the soul from moral vices — polytheism, hypocrisy, arrogance, greed, envy — and its cultivation with virtues. Qaradawi presents this as the ultimate goal of the Prophetic mission: **"He sent among them a Messenger… reciting to them His verses and purifying them"** (Q 62:2). The believer's supplication reflects this: **"O Allah! Grant me the sense of piety and purify my soul, as You are the Best to purify it. You are its Guardian and its Protecting Friend."** ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-04.txt]

## Contemporary Relevance

### Conservation and Environmental Ethics
The Prophetic prohibition against wasting water during ablution — "even if you are taking water from a running river" — has direct bearing on contemporary concerns about water scarcity and environmental stewardship. Islam's ritual purification system, far from encouraging waste, embeds conservation within worship itself.

### Health and Hygiene
The *ṭahārah* system's integration of personal hygiene with spiritual practice has been recognized as a public health contribution. The regular washing of exposed body parts (*wuḍūʾ*), full-body bathing (*ghusl*), and dental hygiene (*siwāk*) contribute to both physical and spiritual well-being.

### Moderation vs. Extremism
The Prophet's rejection of over-scrupulousness in purification (the hadith of the wounded man) and his principle of ease are a direct corrective to both extreme ritualism and casual neglect. Qaradawi's emphasis on the wasaṭiyyah (middle path) applies here: purity is essential, but its pursuit must not become obsessive or harmful.

### Purity as Dignity
Islam's rejection of the view that the human body is base or impure — and its insistence on purification as dignification rather than denigration — counters both materialist reductionism and ascetic self-abasement. The body is purified to honor it, not to demean it. ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-01.txt]
