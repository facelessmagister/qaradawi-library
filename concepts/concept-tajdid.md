# Tajdid / Renewal and Revival (تَجْدِيد)

## Definition

**Tajdid** (تَجْدِيد) — "renewal, revival" — refers in Islamic thought to the periodic revitalization of the Muslim community's understanding and practice of their religion, as foretold in the hadith: "God will send to this Community at the head of every century one who will renew for it its religion" (Abu Dawud, authenticated by multiple scholars). Qaradawi provides a decisive clarification: tajdid does not mean altering Islam or creating a "new edition" adapted to the age. Rather, it means "the reversion of it to where it was in the epoch of the Messenger and his Companions, and of those who followed them with sincerity. It means: the revival of ijtihad in it, and recourse to its original wellsprings, liberation from inflexibility and imitativeness (taqlid) and examination of the legacy with a critical eye." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Tajdid revives the original, not a new version.** Using the analogy of an old building, Qaradawi argues that renewal means "repairing everything that decay has done to it" — not "demolishing it and erecting a prestigious building in the latest style in its place." The religion itself is immutable; what is renewed is the community's understanding of it. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

2. **Tajdid requires the revival of ijtihad.** Renewal is inseparable from independent juristic reasoning. "The revival of ijtihad" and "recourse to its original wellsprings" are the two pillars of tajdid. Without ijtihad, the community falls into taqlid (blind imitation); without returning to the original sources, ijtihad becomes innovation (*bid'ah*). ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **Tajdid includes renewing the faith itself.** Beyond intellectual renewal, Qaradawi cites the hadith: "The faith wears out inside you, as the robe becomes worn out — so beg God that He renew the faith in your hearts." This spiritual renewal — reviving conviction, devotion, and commitment to the religion's "precious values and principles" — is integral to tajdid, not separate from it. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]
