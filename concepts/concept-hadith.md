# Hadith (حَدِيث)

**Arabic:** حَدِيث · **Domain:** Hadith Methodology · **Related:** [[concept-sunnah]]

## Definition

Hadith (حَدِيث, lit. "report, narrative") is the recorded saying, action, or tacit approval (taqrīr) of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, transmitted through a chain of narrators (isnād, pl. asnād). Together with the Qur'an, hadith forms the textual foundation of Islamic law and guidance. The term is closely tied to the broader concept of [[concept-sunnah|Sunnah (سُنَّة)]] — the Prophetic way — but hadith specifically refers to the individual reports that transmit and preserve that way.

The Sunnah functions, in Qaradawi's formulation, as "the Qur'an interpreted and Islam embodied." God said: *“We have sent down to you the Remembrance so that you make clear to humankind what has been sent down for them”* (al-Naḥl, 16:44). Through the Prophet's sayings, actions, and acceptances (taqrīr), the Sunnah is the practical exegesis of the Qur'an.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Qaradawi's Position

### The Sunnah as Second Source of Legislation

Qaradawi affirms the Sunnah, transmitted through hadith, as the second source of Islamic jurisprudence and legislation after the Qur'an. Al-Awzāʿī (d. 157 AH) stated: "The Book is more in need of the Sunnah than the Sunnah is of the Book," because the Sunnah clarifies the Qur'an by detailing what is summary in it, qualifying what is absolute, and particularizing what is general. Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal expressed this relation with precision: "The Sunnah is the exposition of the Book" — neither above it nor independent of it.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

There is no dispute among the jurists — whether ahl al-ḥadīth or ahl al-raʾy — that the Sunnah is a source of legislation. Al-Shawkānī said: "The need for the Sunnah is established, and its independence as a source for the legislation of the injunctions is a religious necessity. No one disagrees on that except one who has no share in the religion of Islam."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### All Jurists Refer to the Sunnah

Qaradawi documents that all the jurists of the Muslims, from different schools and cities, have been in agreement on acceptance of the Sunnah and appeal to it in making rulings. The books of the Ḥanafī school (which exemplifies the school of raʾy) abound in hadiths used as proofs. He refutes the claim that Abū Ḥanīfah accepted only seventeen hadiths, attributing this to a misreading of Ibn Khaldūn, who actually explained that Abū Ḥanīfah was severe in his criteria for narrating and assessing hadiths, and reduced his narrations not from neglect but from strictness.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

Al-Shāfiʿī famously said: "When I report from God's Messenger a ṣaḥīḥ hadith and I do not accept it, then I attest to you that my mind has definitely gone!" He also instructed: "If you find in my book an opposition to the Sunnah of God's Messenger then maintain a view according to the Sunnah and leave what I say."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### The Necessity of Linking Hadith and Fiqh

A distinctive emphasis in Qaradawi's methodology is the necessity that jurists master hadith science and that hadith specialists master fiqh. He calls this a long-standing gap that must be closed:

- **Jurists** who do not master the disciplines of jarḥ and taʿdīl (critical scrutiny of narrators) often rely on weak hadiths or hadiths with no known source in their legal rulings.
- **Hadith specialists** who lack fiqh knowledge become mere conveyers (nuqāl) without understanding the legal implications of what they transmit.

Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah said: "If this matter were in our hands, we would beat with a palm-rod every muḥaddith who did not busy himself with fiqh, and every faqīh who did not busy himself with hadith."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### The Problem of Weak Hadiths in Fiqh Books

Qaradawi identifies a serious problem: many books of fiqh contain hadiths that are weak, extremely weak, fabricated, or even without any source (muʿallaq — "hanging, unsupported"). He provides specific examples from his own research on the fiqh of zakah:

| Hadith | Status |
|--------|--------|
| "There is no ṣadaqah on vegetables" | Challenged |
| "ʿUshr and kharāj do not combine" | Challenged |
| "There is no duty on wealth besides zakah" | Very weak; a copyist's error inverted the meaning |

The last hadith originally read "There *is* a duty on wealth besides zakah" but a copyist's error in a particular transcription of Ibn Mājah inserted "is not" (laysa), and the error circulated and persisted.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

He notes that Ibn Ḥajar, in his source-critiques of the Ḥanafī textbook al-Hidāyah (in al-Dirāyah) and the Shāfiʿī textbook Sharḥ al-Wajīz (in Talkhīṣ al-Ḥabīr), pronounced many of the hadiths on which the legal arguments rest as weak — despite being a Shāfiʿī himself. "The truth has more right to be followed than one's school."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### The Duty of Scholarly Revision

Qaradawi calls for a systematic re-examination of the fiqh inheritance in light of hadith science and its principles. It is a point of agreement that a weak hadith cannot support a legal injunction (ḥukm) establishing obligations in matters of the lawful and the unlawful. Injunctions based solely on weak hadiths must be uncovered and reassessed.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

He provides the examples of:
1. **Bloodwit for non-Muslims (dhimmīs)** — The majority hold it is half that of Muslims, based on hadiths that do not attain the rank of ṣaḥīḥ. Al-Thawrī, al-Zuhrī, Zayd ibn ʿAlī, and Abū Ḥanīfah held it equal to that of the Muslim.
2. **Bloodwit for women** — The majority hold it is half that of men, based on a hadith that al-Bayhaqī said has an isnād "not established." There is not a single established or demonstrated ṣaḥīḥ hadith on the specifics of differential penalty.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### Three Destructive Threats to Hadith

Qaradawi cites a Prophetic warning: hadith reports that knowledge of Prophethood would be targeted by three dangers:

1. **The distortion of extremists (taḥrīf al-ghulāt)** — Turning the religion away from its characteristic ease, facility, and moderation toward excess, burdening people with obligations God never made incumbent. The Prophet said: "Beware yourselves against extremism in the religion, for certainly those before you were destroyed by extremism in the religion."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

2. **The deviations of falsifiers (talyīn al-kādhibīn)** — Introducing into the Prophetic pattern what is not of it. Since the Qur'an was preserved in hearts and written copies, falsifiers targeted the Sunnah. The community's scholars responded by establishing isnād criticism — scrutinizing narrators from birth to death. "The isnād is part of the religion."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **The interpretation of the ignorant (ta'wīl al-juhhāl)** — Deforming priorities, putting back what should come forward, and bringing forward what should be put back. Wrong understanding is "the root of every heretical innovation and error growing up within Islam."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Methodological Principles for Hadith

Qaradawi outlines three cardinal principles for approaching the Sunnah through hadith:

### 1. Verifying the Firmness of the Sunnah (taḥarrī ṣihḥat al-sunnah)

Verification of both isnād (chain) and matn (text), whether the report is of speech, deed, or acceptance. This requires recourse to the recognized assayers of hadith. The scholars established for hadith the science of uṣūl al-ḥadīth (principles of hadith) or muṣṭalaḥ al-ḥadīth (hadith terminology), which holds for hadith the place that uṣūl al-fiqh holds for fiqh. Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ expanded it to 65 kinds; al-Suyūṭī numbered up to 93 kinds.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

Key sub-topics in this science include:
- **Ziyādat al-thiqah** — Additions by a reliable narrator: how far to accept additional material?
- **Taqwiyat al-ḥadīth bi-taʿaddud al-turuq al-ḍaʿīfah** — Strengthening a hadith by multiple weak routes.
- **Ḥadīth mawqūf** — Where the chain stops at a Companion; when can it be treated as marfūʿ (from the Prophet)?
- **Matn criticism** — Study of the content, where criteria acceptable to earlier scholars may not satisfy modern standards.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### 2. Proficiency in Understanding the Sunnah (al-iṭṭilāʿ ʿalā fahm al-sunnah)

Understanding a Prophetic text in harmony with:
- The meanings indicated by language
- The general intent (maqhā) of the hadith and its particular circumstance
- The Qur'anic and Prophetic texts in context
- The general principles and totality of the purposes (maqāṣid) of Islam

This requires distinguishing what came by way of the preaching of the Message from what did not, and within the legislative Sunnah, what is of general and permanent import from what is particular and time-bound — a distinction articulated by Shāh Walī Allāh al-Dihlawī and by Shaykh Maḥmūd Shaltūt.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### 3. Assuring the Text Is Safe from Contradiction by What Is Stronger (salāmat al-naṣṣ min muʿāraḍat mā huwa aqwā minhu)

A text must be checked against what is stronger: a Qur'anic verse, more abundant or sounder hadiths, consonance with uṣūl, or the general purposes of the Law derived from an assemblage of texts. This connects to the legal issue of al-taʿāruḍ wa al-tarjīḥ (contradiction and preference on balance of evidence).^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Hadith Classification

Qaradawi addresses the classification of hadith based on authenticity:

| Classification | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **ṣaḥīḥ** (authentic) | Narrated by a narrator of known probity and completeness of preserving, with a connected isnād to the Prophet, safe from irregularities (shādhdh) and defects (ʿillah) |
| **ḥasan** (good) | Acceptable; its higher level is close to ṣaḥīḥ, its lower level close to ḍaʿīf |
| **ḍaʿīf** (weak) | Cannot support a legal injunction (ḥukm) establishing obligations of the lawful and the unlawful |
| **maḍṭūʿ** (fabricated) | Falsely attributed to the Prophet; its narration is prohibited except to expose its falsehood |

There is scholarly agreement that hadiths used in legal injunctions must be ṣaḥīḥ or ḥasan. Disagreement exists regarding hadiths related to merits of actions (faḍāʾil), invocations, and encouragement (targhīb/tarhīb), where some early scholars were more relaxed — but Qaradawi warns that this relaxation was abused, leading to the circulation of spurious material.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## The Isnād System

Qaradawi emphasizes that Islamic civilization pioneered the scientific methodology of historical source criticism through the isnād system. Scholars did not accept any hadith without verifying the complete chain from beginning to end, scrutinizing each narrator's:
- Teachers, associates, and students
- Trustworthiness and fear of God (taqwā)
- Exactitude in preserving what he heard
- Consonance with trusted reports
- Life history from birth to death^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### Fabricated Hadiths

Regrettably, the community also circulated false hadiths without source or isnād. Examples include:
- "The burial alive of daughters is among the honorable actions"
- "Consult with [women], then oppose them"
- "If one of you has believed firmly in a stone, it will surely benefit him" — Violates tawḥīd

Scholars who compiled books of fabricated hadiths include al-Ṣaghānī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Qārī, Ibn ʿIrāq, al-Shawkānī, al-Laknawī, and al-Albānī.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### Rejection of the Ṣaḥīḥ Equals Acceptance of the Fabricated

Qaradawi makes a striking parity argument: just as accepting fabricated hadiths and attributing them to the Prophet is an offence and a danger, so too is rejecting authenticated ṣaḥīḥ hadiths out of caprice, pride, or presumption. "Acceptance of falsified hadiths enters into the religion what is not of it; rejection of authenticated hadiths drives out of the religion what is of it. Both are reproached and censured alike."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

He illustrates this with three case studies of hadiths rejected due to misunderstanding:
1. **The hadith of ʿĀʾishah on intimacy during menstruation** — Rejected by someone who thought it contradicted the Qur'an, but actually it clarifies the meaning of iʿtizāl (keeping apart).
2. **The hadith "O God, have me live as a miskīn"** — Rejected by someone who understood maskanah as material poverty, whereas Ibn al-Athīr explained it means "lowliness and humility before God."
3. **The hadith "Islam is founded on five"** — Rejected by some because jihad is not listed, but Ibn Taymiyyah explained the five are universal individual obligations, whereas jihad is a collective obligation (farḍ kifāyah) contingent on circumstances.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

### On the Relationship Between Hadith and Fiqh

Qaradawi's insistence on linking hadith science and fiqh echoes themes found across the madhāhib, but he gives it particular urgency for the modern period. Where earlier scholars had identified the gap (e.g., Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah's call to "beat" specialists who neglect the other discipline), Qaradawi provides detailed documentation of the consequences: weak hadiths embedded in authoritative fiqh texts, and source-critiques by scholars like Ibn Ḥajar (a Shāfiʿī correcting Shāfiʿī texts) and al-Bayhaqī (critiquing his own colleagues) demonstrate that the problem cuts across all schools.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### On Abū Ḥanīfah and Hadith

Qaradawi corrects a widespread misconception attributed to Ibn Khaldūn that Abū Ḥanīfah accepted only seventeen hadiths. He quotes Ibn Khaldūn's full passage, which actually explains that Abū Ḥanīfah was severe in narrating criteria and that his students later relaxed those criteria, increasing their narrations. The passage, far from disparaging Abū Ḥanīfah, explains his rigorous standards — and that his reduced narration was due to strictness, not neglect.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### On Weak Hadiths in Preaching

On the permissibility of using weak hadiths for encouragement and admonition (targhīb/tarhīb), Qaradawi takes a stricter position than the authors of books on preaching and Sufism. He notes that many such books contain hadiths that are not merely weak but fabricated, without any source or isnād, and that they "contradict and belie God's Messenger." He criticizes in particular the defense of fabricated hadiths offered by the author of Rūḥ al-Bayān, who attempted to justify lying "for" the Prophet by citing Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām out of context. Qaradawi rebuts: Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām himself stated that when the same praiseworthy objective can be achieved by both truth and lying, lying is prohibited — and the saḥīḥ and ḥasan hadiths can achieve the same objectives.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### On Source-Critique (Takhrīj) Works

Qaradawi catalogs the major works of hadith source-critique on fiqh texts, including:
- **Al-Zaylaʿī**, *Naṣb al-Rāyah li-Aḥādīth al-Hidāyah* (Ḥanafī)
- **Ibn Ḥajar**, *al-Dirāyah fī Takhrīj Aḥādīth al-Hidāyah* (abridgement of the above)
- **Ibn Ḥajar**, *Talkhīṣ al-Ḥabīr* (on al-Rāfiʿī's commentary on al-Ghazālī's al-Wajīz; Shāfiʿī)
- **Al-Albānī**, *Irwāʾ al-Ghalīl fī Takhrīj Aḥādīth Manār al-Sabīl* (Ḥanbalī)
- **Ibn al-Jawzī**, *al-Taḥqīq fī Takhrīj al-Taʿalluq*^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

## Contemporary Relevance

### The Crisis in Understanding the Sunnah

Qaradawi identifies the crisis in thought facing Muslims today as "most clearly represented by the crisis in insight into the Sunnah and in application of it." Among some Islamic re-awakening movements, the Sunnah is reduced to "outward shows and formalisms, without penetrating to the understanding of the wisdom of the Prophetic pattern."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### Enemies of the Sunnah: Old and New

- **Old enemies** — Heretics and innovators who raised doubts about hadith, refuted by scholars like al-Shāṭibī and Ibn Qutaybah.
- **New enemies** — Missionaries, orientalists, and those influenced by them, who attack hadith books, narrators, and methods. Qaradawi notes that contemporary scholars have responded with "overwhelming truths and telling arguments."^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### The Claim That the Qur'an Suffices Without the Sunnah

Qaradawi addresses the argument that since the Qur'an claims to be "a clarification of everything" (al-Naḥl, 16:89), the Sunnah is unnecessary. His response:
1. The Sunnah is the clarification of the Qur'an — without it, the details of prayer, fasting, zakah, pilgrimage, and other obligations would be unknown.
2. The Qur'an itself enjoins obedience to the Messenger alongside obedience to God.
3. Al-Shāṭibī demonstrated that the preservation of the Qur'an vouchsafes that of the Sunnah, because the latter is the exposition of the former — preserving what is clarified necessarily entails preserving what clarifies it.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### Conjecture (Ẓann) and the Hadith

Al-Shāṭibī refuted the claim that hadith is merely "conjecture" (ẓann) and therefore unreliable (citing al-Najm, 53:23). He distinguished three types of conjecture, showing that an isolated report (khabar al-wāḥid) with an authenticated isnād, dependent on a definitive principle of the Law, must be accepted absolutely.^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Classical Scholarly Context

- **Imam al-Shāfiʿī** — Insisted on the supremacy of hadith over personal opinion: "If you find in my book an opposition to the Sunnah of God's Messenger then maintain a view according to the Sunnah and leave what I say."
- **Ibn Khaldūn** — Frequently misquoted on Abū Ḥanīfah; his full passage actually defends the Imam's rigorous standards.
- **Ibn al-Qayyim** — Identified wrong understanding as "the root of every heretical innovation and error."
- **Al-Shāṭibī** — Refuted the "conjecture" argument against hadith in *al-Muwāfaqāt*; also explained that the preservation of the Qur'an vouchsafes the preservation of the Sunnah.
- **Ibn Qutaybah** — Composed *Ta'wīl Mukhtalif al-Ḥadīth* refuting Muʿtazilite objections to hadith.
- **Al-Tahāwī** — Composed *Mushkil al-Athār* reconciling apparently contradictory hadiths with reason.
- **Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī** — Performed source-critique of both Ḥanafī and Shāfiʿī texts, correcting hadiths in his own school's textbooks.
- **Al-Albānī** — Compiled source-critiques of the Sunan; Qaradawi acknowledges his contributions while noting the need for broader scholarly revision.
