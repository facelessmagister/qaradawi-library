# Obligation / Wajib and Fard (وُجُوب / فَرْض)

## Definition

**Wajib** (وَاجِب) and **Fard** (فَرْض) denote that which is obligatory in Islamic law. The two terms converge in meaning — "that which is required by the Lawgiver" — though the Hanafis distinguish between *fard* (established by definitive proof, *qat'i*) and *wajib* (established by probabilistic proof, *zanni*). Qaradawi uses both terms to describe the binding nature of central Islamic duties: zakah is described as "a religious obligation, fixed and eternal, one of the pillars of Islam" whose denial constitutes apostasy. The concept of obligation is fundamental to the entire halal/haram framework, as the five legal categories (obligatory, recommended, neutral, disapproved, prohibited) are anchored by the twin pillars of *wujub* (obligation) and *tahrim* (prohibition). ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Zakat is a fixed, eternal religious obligation.** Qaradawi emphasizes that zakah is not a voluntary charity or state tax but a "religious obligation, fixed and eternal, one of the pillars of Islam." The first Caliph Abu Bakr waged war against those who differentiated between prayer and zakat, treating its obligation as inseparable from the faith itself. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

2. **Obligations admit of dispensations under constraint.** The Shariah does not impose unbearable burdens. Qaradawi accepts the principle that genuine inability or constraint can suspend obligations — the sick person's concessions in fasting, the traveler's shortened prayers, and the poor person's exemption from zakat all reflect this. ^[raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-03.txt]

3. **The obligation of zakat extends beyond the classical categories.** Qaradawi's distinctive position is that the obligation of zakat is not limited to the four classical asset types but applies to "every substantial growing asset" based on the general Qur'anic obligation. The obligation is firm; only the categories and rates require ongoing ijtihad. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]
