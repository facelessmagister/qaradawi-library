# Ikhlas / Sincerity in Worship (إِخْلَاص)

## Definition

**Ikhlas** (إِخْلَاص) — "sincerity, purity, exclusiveness" — denotes the purification of one's intention and action from all motives other than seeking Allah's pleasure. It is the inner counterpart to the outward practice of religion: every act of worship, charity, or service must be directed solely toward God, free from the taint of showing off (*riya'*), social reputation, or worldly gain. The Qur'an commands: "And they were not commanded except to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion" (Q 98:5). In Qaradawi's corpus, ikhlas appears as the spiritual foundation that validates all outward action — without it, even the most faithful compliance with ritual and law becomes empty form. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Ikhlas is the criterion for acceptance of deeds.** Qaradawi insists that the worth of any action in Islam is measured not by its outward magnitude but by the sincerity of the intention behind it. This is grounded in the well-known hadith: "Actions are but by intentions, and every person will have but that which they intended." ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-03-05.txt]

2. **Ikhlas and ijtihad must go together.** In *Approaching the Sunnah*, Qaradawi links sincerity to the methodology of the early generations: they "excelled in understanding the religion, and in conveying it to the people with wisdom, sincerity and moderation." A jurist's ijtihad must be motivated by sincere pursuit of truth, not partisanship or self-promotion. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **Zakat requires ikhlas in its payment.** In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi notes that zakat is "paid out of sincerity on the part of the believer for the sake of God," distinguishing it from mere tax compliance. Without ikhlas, the spiritual purpose of zakat — purification of wealth and soul — is lost. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]
