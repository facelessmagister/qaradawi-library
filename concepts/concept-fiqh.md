# Fiqh (فِقْه)

**Arabic:** فِقْه  
**Transliteration:** *fiqh*  
**Domain:** Usul Al-Fiqh (Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence)

## Definition

Fiqh (فِقْه) is Islamic jurisprudence — the human scholarly discipline of understanding, deriving, and applying the rulings of the Shari'ah (شَرِيعَة) from its revealed sources. Linguistically, *fiqh* means "deep understanding" or "thorough comprehension" (from the root f-q-h). In its technical usage, it denotes the body of legal rulings derived from the Qur'an, Sunnah, ijtihad, and other legislative sources, covering all aspects of worship, transactions, personal status, and criminal law. Fiqh is the *product* of human reasoning applied to the *divine* law of Shari'ah — where Shari'ah is the God-given ideal, fiqh is the scholarly interpretation and elaboration of it.

The distinction is fundamental: Shari'ah (شَرِيعَة) is the unchanging divine law revealed by God; fiqh is the fallible, human attempt to understand and apply that law. Differences among the madhhabib (schools of law) are differences of fiqh, not of Shari'ah.

## Qaradawi's Position

Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi is one of the foremost contemporary scholars of fiqh. His distinctive approach is marked by several core commitments:

### 1. The Necessity of Linking Hadith and Fiqh

Qaradawi argues insistently that the two disciplines of hadith science and fiqh must be reunited. The gap between muhaddithun (hadith specialists) and fuqaha' (jurists) has led to serious problems: jurists relying on weak or fabricated hadiths, and hadith specialists lacking the juristic skill to derive rulings properly. He quotes Sufyan ibn 'Uyaynah: "If this matter were in our hands, we would beat with a palm-rod every muhaddith who did not busy himself with fiqh, and every faqih who did not busy himself with hadith." Qaradawi calls this gap-closing a duty of the learned community. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### 2. Scholarly Revision of the Fiqh Legacy

A central theme in Qaradawi's work is the obligation — "a duty of the learned community in our time" — to go back over the inherited fiqh tradition in light of sound hadith knowledge combined with penetrating juristic reasoning. Many rulings in the established fiqh books rest on weak hadiths that cannot sustain legal obligations. He documents specific cases:
- The hadith "There is no sadaqah on vegetables" is weak (al-Tirmidhi: "its isnad has no sahih"), yet it has been used to restrict zakah on agricultural produce.
- The hadith "'Ushr and kharaj do not combine" lacks proper authentication.
- The hadith "There is no duty [right] on wealth besides zakah" contains a well-known copyist's error (the interpolation of *laysa*), reversing its meaning from "There is a duty on wealth besides zakah" to its opposite. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

### 3. Understanding the Sunnah in Light of the Qur'an

Qaradawi holds that when jurists or commentators differ on what they derive from a sunnah, "the best of them and the one to be favored is the one supported by the Qur'an." He demonstrates this principle with the zakah on agricultural produce: while Malik, Shafi'i, and Ibn Hanbal restricted zakah to a limited set of crops (grains, dates, raisins), Abu Hanifah extended it to all edible produce, which Qaradawi argues aligns better with the Qur'anic verse: "Eat of its fruit when it bears fruit and yield up its due on the day of its harvesting" (al-An'am, 6:142). The Qur'anic text is general and does not restrict liability to specific crop types. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-03.txt]

### 4. Expansion of Zakah Scope (Fiqh al-Zakah)

Qaradawi's magnum opus *Fiqh al-Zakah* (فقه الزكاة) is one of the most comprehensive modern treatments of any single fiqh topic. His key methodological commitments include:
- **Using the condition of growth (*nama'*) as a criterion** for zakatability: if an asset is actually growing or has the potential for growth, it is zakatable. This allows fiqh to address modern financial instruments not known in the classical period.
- **Refusing to change the zakah rates**: against some modern proposals to increase zakah rates, Qaradawi argues that the rates are fixed by clear textual evidence (nusus) and ijma', and changing them would undermine the religious character of zakah as an act of worship rather than a state tax.
- **Preferring the nisab of gold over silver** for paper currency, on grounds that the silver nisab has become extremely low relative to other nisab thresholds and would impose injustice on zakah payers. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

### 5. All Jurists Refer to the Sunnah

Qaradawi insists that the divide between ahl al-hadith and ahl al-ra'y was never about *whether* the Sunnah is a source of legislation, but about practical criteria for accepting hadiths and applying them. He documents that even the Hanafi school — the paradigm of ahl al-ra'y — abundantly cites hadiths in its major textbooks. Abu Hanifah's limited hadith transmission was due to his strict acceptance criteria, not neglect of the Sunnah, as Ibn Khaldun clarified. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

## Evidence Base

### Qur'anic Verses Cited

- **al-Nahl 16:44** — "We have sent down to you the Remembrance so that you make clear to humankind what has been sent down for them" (the Sunnah as exposition of the Qur'an)
- **al-Nisa' 4:92** — Equal bloodwit for accidental killing regardless of religion (used in Qaradawi's argument for revising the fiqh on dhimmi bloodwit)
- **al-An'am 6:142** — "Eat of its fruit when it bears fruit and yield up its due on the day of its harvesting" (general principle for zakah on all agricultural produce)
- **al-Baqarah 2:233** — Mutual consultation, used to reject a spurious hadith contradicting the Qur'an

### Key Hadiths Discussed

- **"The Sunnah is the exposition of the Book"** — Ahmad ibn Hanbal's formulation of the relationship between Sunnah and Qur'an
- **"There is no sadaqah on vegetables"** — Weak; al-Tirmidhi: "not one thing is authenticated from the Prophet on this topic"
- **"There is a duty on wealth besides zakah"** — The original form; reversed by a copyist's error to "There is *no* duty…"
- **"A Muslim is not zakatable for his mare or for his servant"** — Foundation for the principle that personal-use assets are exempt from zakah
- **"Wealth never decreases as a result of sadaqah"** — Supporting the growth condition for zakatability

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

### Zakah on Agricultural Produce

| School | Position | Basis |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Maliki** | Restricted to staples used as food | Hadith restrictions on crop types |
| **Shafi'i** | Restricted to nutritious food crops | Nutritional criterion |
| **Hanbali** | Restricted to measured, storable grains and dates | Hadith on measures and nisab |
| **Hanafi** | All edible produce is zakatable | Qur'anic generality (6:142) + Prophetic "in what the sky waters, a tenth" |
| **Qaradawi** | Agrees with Hanafi position; extends via growth principle | Qur'anic text + Sunnah + condition of nama' |

Qaradawi cites Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi's critique of all three restrictive positions (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) as weak, and favors Abu Hanifah's approach as most consistent with the Qur'an's general command.

### Bloodwit for Non-Muslims (Dhimmi)

| School | Position |
|--------|----------|
| **Majority** | Half the bloodwit for Muslims |
| **Al-Thawri, al-Zuhri, Zayd ibn 'Ali, Abu Hanifah** | Equal bloodwit |
| **Qaradawi** | Agrees with the minority (equal bloodwit), arguing that none of the hadiths on either side attain the rank of sahih; recourse must then be to the general Qur'anic text (4:92) which makes no distinction |

### Bloodwit for Women

Qaradawi notes that the hadith "The bloodwit for a woman is half the bloodwit for a man" has a flawed isnad (al-Bayhaqi: "An isnad like its isnad is not established"), and that there exists no sahih hadith establishing different penalties for injuries to women vs. men. This represents his broader methodological commitment: when hadith evidence is weak, fiqh rulings built upon it must be revisited.

### Nisab for Paper Money

| View | Scholars |
|------|----------|
| Use silver nisab (200 dirhams) | Common practice in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Gulf states, India, Pakistan |
| Use gold nisab (20 dinars / 85 grams) | Abu Zahrah, Khallaf, Hasan; preferred by Qaradawi |
| Use livestock nisab as fallback | Qaradawi's secondary proposal: half the value of 5 camels or 40 sheep |

## Contemporary Relevance

Qaradawi's fiqh methodology is directly relevant to several contemporary debates:

1. **Zakah on modern financial instruments**: The principle of growth (nama') as a criterion for zakatability allows zakah to be extended to stocks, mutual funds, retirement accounts, and other instruments not known to classical jurists.

2. **Revising inherited rulings**: His call for scholarly revision of the fiqh legacy means that rulings based on weak hadiths — on issues such as the bloodwit for non-Muslims, the bloodwit for women, zakah scope on produce, and many others — must be re-examined in light of authentic hadith criticism and the general objectives of Shari'ah (maqasid).

3. **Methodology over conclusions**: Qaradawi models a fiqh approach that prioritizes sound hadith criticism, Qur'anic primacy, and the objectives of the law (maqasid) over taqlid (uncritical following) of any single school. He draws from all four madhhabib but does not bind himself to any exclusive school.

4. **Fiqh al-aqalliyyat** (jurisprudence of Muslim minorities): Though this topic falls outside the current corpus, Qaradawi's methodological principles — wasatiyyah (moderation), taysir (facilitation), and linking text with context — form the basis for this area of contemporary fiqh.
