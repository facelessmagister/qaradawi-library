# Hudud / Fixed Punishments (حُدُود)

## Definition

**Hudud** (حُدُود) — literally "boundaries, limits" — refers in Islamic jurisprudence to the fixed, divinely prescribed punishments for specific serious crimes: theft (*sariqah*), banditry (*hirabah*), adultery/fornication (*zina*), false accusation (*qadhf*), and alcohol consumption (*shurb al-khamr*). The term also carries the broader Qur'anic meaning of "the limits set by Allah" (Q 2:229) — boundaries between the lawful and the prohibited that must not be transgressed. In Qaradawi's treatment, hudud embodies the seriousness with which Islam protects the moral and social order while simultaneously insisting on the strictest evidentiary standards before application, reflecting the principle that it is better to err in forgiveness than in punishment.

## Key Positions

1. **Hudud are Allah's boundaries, not human prerogatives.** The Qur'an states: "These are the limits of Allah; do not transgress them" (Q 2:229). Qaradawi emphasizes that hudud derive from divine legislation, making their promulgation and suspension matters of religious, not secular, authority. ^[raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-04.txt]

2. **Strict evidentiary requirements prevent misuse.** The hadith "Ward off the hudud from the Muslims as much as you can; if there is a way out, set the accused free" reflects the Prophetic insistence that hudud not be applied on the basis of doubt. Qaradawi aligns with this principle, treating the high burden of proof as integral to the hudud system rather than as an external check on it.

3. **Hudud operate within the broader halal-haram framework.** As discussed in *The Lawful and the Prohibited*, the Qur'an sets "boundary limits between lawful and unlawful things (*hudud*) which you should not violate" (Q 65:1, hadith of Abi Tha'labah). The fixed punishments are one expression of these boundaries; the moral exhortation is their broader expression. ^[raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-04.txt]
