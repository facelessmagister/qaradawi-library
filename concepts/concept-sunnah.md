# Sunnah (سُنَّة)

**Arabic:** سُنَّة  
**Transliteration:** Sunnah  
**Domain:** Hadith Methodology · Usul al-Fiqh

## Definition

The Sunnah (سُنَّة) is the way, method, or pattern (طريقة، منهج، نمط) prescribed by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — comprising his sayings (أقوال), actions (أفعال), and tacit approvals (تقريرات). It is the second source of Islamic law after the Quran and serves as the practical exegesis of the Quranic revelation. As Qaradawi states, "the Sunnah is the Quran interpreted and Islam embodied" — the living, applied form of the divine message.

The Quran is the supreme, preserved source; one never argues from secondary sources to validate it. The Sunnah follows alongside the Quran and makes it clear, as God said: *{"We have sent down to you the Remembrance so that you make clear to humankind what has been sent down for them"}* (al-Nahl, 16:44). The term *sunnah* itself means 'way,' 'method,' or 'pattern' and represents the Wisdom (الحكمة) revealed alongside the Book: *{"And remember what is recited in your houses of the signs of God and the Wisdom"}* (al-Ahzab, 33:34).

## Qaradawi's Position

Qaradawi devotes his book *Approaching the Sunnah: Comprehension and Controversy* (كيف نتعامل مع السنة) to a comprehensive treatment of the Sunnah's nature, authority, and proper method of engagement. In Chapter 1, he identifies four defining characteristics of the Prophetic Sunnah:

### 1. A Comprehensive Pattern (منهج شامل)

The Sunnah comprehends the whole of human life across three dimensions:
- **Length (temporal):** From birth to death, indeed from the embryonic stage to what comes after death.
- **Breadth (horizontal):** All spheres of life — in the home, the marketplace, the mosque, on the road, at work; in relations with God, with oneself, with family, with Muslims, non-Muslims, and humankind generally, with animate creatures and inanimate things.
- **Depth (interior):** Body as well as mind and spirit, the outward as well as the inward, speech and action as well as intention.

Qaradawi criticizes those who reduce the Sunnah to "keeping the beard long and the robe short, and using the siwak from the arak tree," forgetting the comprehensiveness in which everyone can find scope for something to serve as their model. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### 2. A Balanced Method (منهج متوازن)

The Sunnah steers between extremes — between spirit and body, mind and heart, this world and the hereafter, the ideal and the actual, theory and practice, freedom and responsibility, individualism and collectivism. It is a moderate pattern for a moderate community (*ummmatan wasatan*). The Prophet ﷺ disapproved of those who inclined towards extremes. When three men resolved to fast perpetually, stand in vigil all night, or abstain from marriage, he said: *"Whoever prefers [something else] above my sunnah is not one of mine."* On seeing excess in worship from Abd Allah ibn Amr, he returned him to moderation: *"Indeed, your body owns a right over you, your eyes own a right over you, your family own a right over you, and your visitor owns a right over you."* ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### 3. An Integrative Way (منهج جامع)

The Sunnah integrates within itself:
- **Faith with intellection** — revelation and reason, so that both flow as "light upon light" (al-Nur, 24:35).
- **Legislation and moral instruction** — moral instruction is of little avail without legislation, and legislation is of little avail without moral guidance. The Prophet was responsible for both.
- **Might and right** — the authority of state with the call to religion. God restrains by political authority what He does not restrain by the Quran alone.
- **Leadership and the people** — the leader is not an angelic being but a human among humans, sharing in their sorrows and joys.

Qaradawi contrasts this with the Israelite model (a prophet guiding in religion while a king administers the state) and the Christian model (partitioning life between God and Caesar). The Islamic model unifies both under the Prophet's leadership, citing *{"My prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for God, the Lord of the worlds"}* (al-An'am, 6:163-64). He quotes Ibn Taymiyyah: "People must have a book to guide, and iron to support." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

### 4. A Realistic Method (منهج واقعي)

The Sunnah does not treat people as winged angels but as human beings — with dispositions, passions, necessities, and needs alongside elevated spiritual aspirations. It makes allowance for human weakness by:
- **Widening the circle of the permitted** and narrowing that of the forbidden, as in the hadith: *"What God has made lawful in His Book, that is lawful; and what He has forbidden, that is forbidden; and what He is silent about, that is exempt. So accept from God His latitude."*
- **Permitting necessities** among things normally restricted, even the forbidden in dire circumstances — e.g., the Prophet permitted two Companions to wear silk due to skin ailment.
- **Keeping the door of repentance open**: *"God spreads out His hands through the night so that He may accept repentance for the offences of the day; and He spreads out His hands through the day so that He may accept repentance for the offences of the night — until the sun rises in the west."*
- **Accommodating human variation:** The Messenger would answer a single question with multiple answers, tailoring responses to different conditions and capacities.

As the Prophet ﷺ reassured Hanzalah — who feared hypocrisy because his spiritual state fluctuated between devotion in the Messenger's presence and normalcy at home — *"If you were able to endure in the state you are in with me, angels would be shaking hands with you on the roads. But, O Hanzalah, there is a time for this and a time for that."* ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

The question of the Sunnah's authority and nature is addressed across all four Sunni madhahib:

- **Hanafi school:** Distinguishes between *sunnah mu'akkadah* (emphasized, near-obligatory) and *sunnah ghayr mu'akkadah* (non-emphasized, recommended). The Hanafis give priority to the Quran and restrict Sunnah's legislative scope where it appears to contradict apparent Quranic meaning.
- **Maliki school:** Gives special weight to the practice of the people of Madinah (*amal ahl al-Madinah*) as a source of Sunnah alongside transmitted hadith, considering the living practice of the Prophet's city a form of Sunnah preservation.
- **Shafi'i school:** Al-Shafi'i's *al-Risalah* established the most influential framework for Sunnah's authority, arguing that the Sunnah is an independent legislative source second only to the Quran and never contradicting it.
- **Hanbali school:** Considers the authentic Sunnah binding in all cases, giving it the strongest weight among the four schools; Imam Ahmad is reported to have said, "Whoever rejects the Sunnah is destroyed."

Qaradawi's position aligns most closely with the mainstream Shafi'i-Hanbali consensus that the Sunnah is an authoritative, binding source second only to the Quran. However, his distinctive contribution is the **framework of four characteristics** (comprehensive, balanced, integrative, realistic) which goes beyond the classical treatment of Sunnah as primarily a legal/textual source and instead presents it as a total way of life. He also emphasizes — consistent with his middle-path (*wasatiyyah*) methodology — that the Sunnah must be understood holistically, not reduced to a checklist of outward observances.

## Contemporary Relevance

Qaradawi's four-characteristic framework for understanding the Sunnah addresses several contemporary distortions:

1. **Reductionism:** Those who reduce the Sunnah to a narrow set of external markers (beard length, robe length, siwak) while ignoring its comprehensiveness across all dimensions of life — economic, social, political, spiritual.
2. **Extremism:** Those who take one aspect of the Sunnah to an extreme, whether asceticism or political radicalism, violating the Sunnah's own principle of balance (*tatarruf* in either direction contradicts the Prophetic pattern).
3. **Secularism:** Those who partition life into religious and secular domains, which contradicts the Sunnah's integrative principle — the Prophet led in prayer and on the battlefield; the spiritual and the temporal were unified in his person.
4. **Idealism disconnected from reality:** Those who demand angelic perfection from believers, ignoring the Sunnah's realistic accommodation of human weakness, its widening of the permitted, and its perpetually open door of repentance.

The Sunnah as Qaradawi presents it is simultaneously comprehensive enough to cover all of life, balanced enough to avoid extremes, integrative enough to unite the spiritual and temporal, and realistic enough to accommodate human frailty — making it a living, applicable guide for Muslim communities facing modern challenges.
