# Sabr / Patience and Perseverance (صَبْر)

## Definition

**Sabr** (صَبْر) — "patience, steadfastness, endurance" — is one of the most frequently commanded virtues in the Qur'an, appearing over ninety times in various forms. It encompasses three dimensions: patience in obedience to Allah (*sabr 'ala ta'ah*), patience in abstaining from sin (*sabr 'an al-ma'siyah*), and patience in the face of trials and afflictions (*sabr 'ala al-musibah*). Qaradawi treats sabr as both a spiritual discipline and a social necessity: the Muslim must be patient not only in personal hardship but in the long struggle for justice and the dawah. The Qur'an pairs patience with prayer: "Seek help through patience and prayer" (Q 2:45), and declares that Allah is with the patient (Q 2:153). ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Patience is indispensable but not passive.** Qaradawi, citing the Prophetic correction of those who went to extremes in worship, warns against a false patience that tolerates injustice silently. "Patience does not forbid one from speaking the word of truth." True sabr is active endurance combined with principled action. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

2. **Patience is a natural virtue refined by Islamic training.** In *Ethics in Islam*, Qaradawi (following the Islamic ethical tradition) holds that patience must become "natural, consistent, and effortless" — not merely occasional forbearance. The Companion who was praised for his natural patience was told by the Prophet: "You have two traits that Allah likes: patience and dignity." ^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-01.txt]

3. **Hope and patience are inseparable.** In *Faith and Life*, Qaradawi connects sabr to hope (*raja'*): the patient believer does not despair, for "the necessity of hope in life" is a companion conviction. Patience without hope degenerates into fatalism; hope without patience into wishful thinking. ^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-04.txt]
