# Halal (حَلَال)

**Arabic:** حَلَال  
**Transliteration:** *halal*  
**Domain:** Usul al-Fiqh / Fiqh al-Mu'amalat / Fiqh al-Ibadat

## Definition

*Halal* (حَلَال) denotes that which is lawful, permitted, and allowed in Islamic law. It encompasses all actions, objects, and transactions that a Muslim may freely engage in without incurring sin. The term is the direct antonym of [[concept-haram|Haram (حَرَام)]] and, together with it, forms the fundamental binary of moral categorisation in the Shariah.

Qaradawi anchors the definition of halal in the **principle of original permissibility** (*al-ibahah al-asliyyah* — الإباحة الأصلية): everything created by Allah is presumed lawful unless a clear, authentictext from the Qur'an, the Sunnah, or the consensus (*ijma'*) of the Muslim scholars declares it otherwise. ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

## Qaradawi's Position

Qaradawi is one of the foremost contemporary champions of the principle of original permissibility. He argues that:

1. **Allah created everything on earth for human benefit.** The Qur'an states: *"God is the One Who created for you all that is on the earth"* (Sura 2:29). The phrase "for you" (*lakum*) is decisive: it signals that the default ruling for all worldly things is permissibility. ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

2. **Only an explicit proof can shift a thing from halal to haram.** The three legitimate sources of prohibition are: (a) a clear Qur'anic verse, (b) an authentic, unambiguous hadith, and (c) the consensus of Muslim scholars. Absent any of these, the thing remains halal. A weak or ambiguous hadith is insufficient to declare something forbidden. ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

3. **What Allah deliberately did not mention is forgiven, not prohibited.** Qaradawi cites the well-known hadith reported by Al-Hakim on the authority of Abu al-Darda': *"What Allah made permissible in His Book is lawful (halal), and what He forbade is unlawful (haram), and what He, deliberately, did not mention is forgiven."* The Prophet then recited: *"And your Lord is never forgetful"* (Sura 19:64). Darqutni's parallel narration on the authority of Abi Tha'labah al-Khashni was elevated to the rank of *hasan* by al-Nawawi and Abu Bakr al-Sama'ani. ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

4. **Over-prohibition is as religiously dangerous as over-permissiveness.** Declaring what Allah has made lawful to be unlawful is, in Qaradawi's reading, an encroachment on divine legislative authority. This mirrors the Qur'anic censure of those who forbade what Allah had allowed (Sura 5:87, Sura 7:32).

5. **Necessity (*darurah*) validates the temporarily prohibited.** Even if an item is haram, constraint or necessity can render it temporarily permissible — Allah Himself states: *"When He has explained to you that which He has forbidden you, except if you are constrained to it"* (Sura 6:119). This further underscores the breadth of halal in Islamic law.

## Evidence Base

### Qur'anic Verses

| Verse | Text | Relevance |

| 2:29 | *"God is the One Who created for you all that is on the earth"* | Foundation of original permissibility |
| 5:87 | *"O you who believe, do not prohibit the good things which Allah has made lawful for you"* | Forbidding over-prohibition |
| 7:32 | *"Say: Who has forbidden the adornment of Allah which He has brought for His slaves?"* | Rebuttal of unwarranted prohibition |
| 6:119 | *"When He has explained to you that which He has forbidden you, except if you are constrained to it"* | Necessity clause; clarity of prohibition boundaries |
| 19:64 | *"And your Lord is never forgetful"* | What Allah omitted is deliberate, not an oversight |

### Prophetic Hadiths

1. **Hadith of Abu al-Darda'** (Al-Hakim, authenticated): *"What Allah made permissible in His Book is lawful, and what He forbade is unlawful, and what He, deliberately, did not mention is forgiven."*
2. **Hadith of Abi Tha'labah al-Khashni** (Darqutni, graded *hasan*): *"Allah enjoined certain duties (fara'id) which you should follow, and set boundary limits between lawful and unlawful things (hudud) which you should not violate. There are things which He deliberately did not mention by Mercy from Him, for He is never forgetful. Therefore, do not try to investigate into them any further."*

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

| School | Position on Original Permissibility | Notes |
|--------|-------------------------------------|-------|
| **Hanafi** | Strongly affirms *al-ibahah al-asliyyah*; default ruling for all worldly matters is permissibility (*ibahah*) until proven otherwise. | Closest to Qaradawi's formulation. |
| **Maliki** | Accepts original permissibility but emphasises *maslaha mursalah* (public interest) as a basis for rulings where text is silent. | Qaradawi incorporates this approach. |
| **Shafi'i** | Affirms permissibility in *mu'amalat* (transactions) but is stricter in *ibadat* (worship), where only what is textually supported is permitted. | Qaradawi's scope is broader for entertainment/arts. |
| **Hanbali** | Generally affirms original permissibility but some Hanbali positions (especially on music/arts) tend toward greater restriction. | Qaradawi directly critiques this tendency. |

Qaradawi's distinctive contribution is the application of the principle of original permissibility to **modern entertainment and arts** — a domain where classical schools often imposed stricter limitations. In *The Diversion and Arts in Islam*, he uses the halal/haram framework to argue that singing, music, and leisure activities are halal by default and require clear textual proof to be deemed otherwise. ^[raw/extracted/diversion-arts/ch-04.txt]

## Contemporary Relevance

- **Over-restriction vs. over-permissiveness:** Many contemporary Muslim communities err toward over-prohibition, declaring entertainment, art, and cultural practices haram without solid textual basis. Qaradawi's principled defence of *al-ibahah al-asliyyah* provides a corrective.
- **Muslim minorities in non-Muslim societies:** The halal category is especially expansive for minorities navigating unfamiliar environments — the default is permission, not suspicion.
- **Islamic finance:** The principle of original permissibility underpins modern Islamic banking, where financial instruments are valid unless they contain a clearly prohibited element (e.g., [[concept-riba|Riba (رِبَا)]]).
- **Digital media and entertainment:** The framework Qaradawi applies to singing and music extends naturally to film, gaming, and social media — all are halal by default unless specific elements make them haram.

## Classical Scholarly Context

The principle *al-asl fil-ashya' al-ibahah* ("the default in all things is permissibility") was articulated by the majority of jurists, with variations in scope across madhhabs. Qaradawi's treatment innovates primarily in his application of this principle to cultural and artistic domains, and in his polemic against the tendency to expand the sphere of haram beyond what the texts warrant. His work serves as a bridge between classical usul and contemporary Muslim life.
