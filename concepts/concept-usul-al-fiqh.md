# Usul al-Fiqh / Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence (أُصُول الفِقْه)

## Definition

**Usul al-fiqh** (أُصُول الفِقْه) — literally "the roots of jurisprudence" — is the discipline that establishes the sources, methods, and principles by which Islamic legal rulings are derived from the primary texts. It encompasses the hierarchy of sources (Qur'an, Sunnah, ijma', qiyas), the rules of textual interpretation (general vs. specific, absolute vs. qualified, abrogating vs. abrogated), and the methodological principles governing ijtihad, taqlid, and the weight of evidence. Qaradawi treats usul al-fiqh not as an abstract academic discipline but as the necessary framework for every jurist who engages in ijtihad. He notes that "the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) are not lacking in hadiths" and that mastery of usul is prerequisite to sound juristic reasoning. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **The four-source hierarchy governs all ijtihad.** Qaradawi follows the classical order: Qur'an, then Sunnah, then ijma', then qiyas. "Only one thing limits all scholars. That is the unmistakable words of the Book of God or the tradition of His Messenger." Where definitive texts exist, ijtihad has no room; where texts are probabilistic (*zanni*), juristic reasoning operates. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

2. **Usul al-fiqh is applied, not merely theoretical.** Throughout *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi demonstrates usul al-fiqh in action: adopting general texts unless restricted by sound specific texts, accepting genuine consensus but being skeptical of claimed consensus, accommodating third opinions, and applying correct analogy (*qiyas*) based on a shared effective cause (*'illah*). ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-01.txt]

3. **The hadiths themselves embody usul al-fiqh principles.** Qaradawi argues that "the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) are not lacking in hadiths," meaning that many foundational legal maxims — "No harm and no reciprocating of harm," "The muslims are bound by their conditions" — are themselves derived from Prophetic statements. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-02.txt]
