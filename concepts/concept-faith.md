# Faith / Iman (إِيمَان)

**Arabic:** إِيمَان  
**Transliteration:** Iman  
**Domain:** Aqeedah (Islamic Theology and Creed)

## Definition

*Iman* (إِيمَان) — faith or belief — is the foundational concept of Islam, denoting a believer's firm conviction in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree (*qadar*). In Qaradawi's treatment, iman is far more than mere intellectual assent; it is a living, transformative force that elevates the human being from the lowest estimation of materialism to the highest station of honor and dignity. Iman encompasses conviction of the heart, declaration of the tongue, and action of the limbs — a comprehensive orientation of the entire person toward Allah.

Qaradawi defines faith practically and existentially: it is the lens through which the believer understands his entire existence, the spring from which moral character flows, and the source of true happiness that material wealth and worldly pleasure can never provide.

## Qaradawi's Position

### 1. Faith Restores Human Dignity

Qaradawi's *Faith and Life* (Chapter 1: "Iman and the Dignity of Man") opens with a decisive contrast between the materialist view and the creedal view of man. Materialism reduces man to "a piece of flesh, blood, nerves, systems, glands, and cells" whose elemental worth is negligible. Against this, the Islamic faith affirms man's supreme position: Allah created him in the best of molds (*aḥsan taqwīm*, Quran 95:4), breathed into him of His Spirit, commanded the angels to prostrate before him, and made him His vicegerent (*khalīfah*) on earth.

The dignity of being a believer (*karāmat al-mu'min*) surpasses even the dignity of being human (*karāmat al-insān*). Qaradawi cites: "You are the best of peoples, evolved for mankind" (Al `Imran: 110), and "But honor belongs to Allah and His Messenger, and to the believers" (Al-Munafiqun: 8).^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-01.txt]

### 2. Faith Is the Source of True Happiness

In *Faith and Life* Chapter 2 ("Iman and Happiness"), Qaradawi argues systematically that material wealth, social welfare, scientific achievement, and worldly pleasure cannot produce true happiness. He cites the example of Sweden, where comprehensive social services failed to prevent widespread despair and suicide. Similarly, he shows that children, experimental science, and inner psychological satisfaction — absent iman — cannot fill the spiritual void. True happiness flows from the contentment that faith provides: "Whoever whose intention is directed to the Hereafter, Allah makes his richness in his heart" (hadith narrated by at-Tirmidhi on the authority of Anas ibn Malik).^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-02.txt]

### 3. Faith Generates Love and Benevolence

In *Faith and Life* Chapter 3 ("Iman and Love"), Qaradawi connects faith directly to love — love of Allah, love of nature, love of life, and love of people. A true believer "does not envy or harbor hatred against anyone." Faith transforms the human heart from selfishness to altruism, from isolation to communion.^[raw/extracted/faith-and-life/ch-03.txt]

### 4. Faith, Ethics, and Worship Are Inseparable

In *Ethics in Islam* (Section 1.3), Qaradawi argues that faith, moral character, and worship form an indivisible whole. The Quran states: "Righteousness is not that you turn your face toward the east or the west, but [true] righteousness is [in] one who believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets and gives wealth… establishes prayer and gives Zakat; [those who] fulfill their promises… and are patient in poverty and hardship" (2:177). This verse links creed, ritual, and ethics into a single organic reality.^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-01-03.txt]

### 5. Moral Obligation Stems from Faith

In *Ethics in Islam* (Section 3.1), Qaradawi adopts Dr. Draz's analysis that moral obligation in Islam has one ultimate source: Allah. The faithful do not hesitate to obey Allah's commandments. "It is not for a believing man or a believing woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decided a matter, that they should [thereafter] have any choice about their affair" (Quran 33:36). Faith generates moral obligation, which in turn generates moral action.^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/section-03-01.txt]

### 6. Faith and the Islamic Framework

In *Ethics in Islam* (Chapter 2), Qaradawi argues that the Islamic moral system must be built entirely on Islam — creed, concepts, attitudes, morality, and laws. He demonstrates that Islamic punishments (for theft, adultery, etc.) cannot be applied in isolation from the broader Islamic framework that ensures social justice, economic security, and moral education through faith.^[raw/extracted/ethics-in-islam/ch-02.txt]

## Comparison with Classical Scholars

- **With the Ash'ari school**: Qaradawi affirms the dual nature of man (body + spirit) and divine decree, consistent with Sunni theology, though his focus is pastoral rather than dialectical.
- **With al-Ghazali**: Qaradawi draws from Ghazali's *Ihya'* on the theme of harmony between the servant and the Lord through adopting divine attributes and the transformative power of faith.
- **With Ibn al-Qayyim**: He draws on Ibn al-Qayyim's insights on how faith dispels anxiety, generates contentment, and changes how a person experiences both prosperity and adversity.
- **With Dr. Draz**: In *Ethics in Islam*, Qaradawi adopts Draz's analysis of moral obligation as the pivot of any ethical system, grounding it firmly in divine commandment rather than secular philosophy.

## The Three Dimensions of Faith

Qaradawi's corpus reveals three dimensions of faith:

1. **Faith as creed (*ʿaqīdah*)** — the set of foundational beliefs described at length in *Faith and Life* Chapter 1: belief in Allah, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree.
2. **Faith as lived experience** — the practical consequences of belief, including dignity, happiness, love, moral character, and social engagement.
3. **Faith as moral obligation** — the ethical imperative that flows from belief, generating duties toward Allah, toward others, and toward oneself.

## Contemporary Relevance

1. **Crisis of meaning**: In an age of materialist philosophy, Darwinian reductionism, and existentialist despair, Qaradawi argues that faith alone restores human dignity by anchoring man in divine purpose.
2. **Happiness and mental health**: The epidemic of depression and suicide in wealthy nations demonstrates that material prosperity without faith produces emptiness.
3. **Social cohesion**: Faith generates love, altruism, and mutual care — the social bonds that materialism and individualism erode.
4. **Moral framework**: Without faith as its foundation, ethics becomes arbitrary; with faith, moral obligation has a transcendent anchor.
5. **Integral Islam**: Faith, ethics, and law cannot be separated; the Islamic social system functions only when creed, character, and legislation operate as a unified whole.
