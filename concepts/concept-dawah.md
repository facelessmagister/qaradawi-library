# Dawah / Islamic Propagation (دَعْوَة)

## Definition

**Dawah** (دَعْوَة) — literally "invitation, call, summons" — denotes the act of inviting others to Islam, whether non-Muslims to embrace the faith or Muslims to deepen their practice and renew their commitment. In Qaradawi's treatment, dawah encompasses both the content of the message (the call to *tawhid*, worship, and righteous conduct) and the method of its delivery, which must embody wisdom, sincerity, and moderation. Dawah is rooted in the Qur'anic command: "Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and argue with them in a way that is best" (Q 16:125). It is not merely proselytization but a comprehensive process of religious renewal that includes education, spiritual cultivation, and the revival of ijtihad. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

## Key Positions

1. **Dawah must renew understanding, not alter the religion.** Qaradawi links dawah to *tajdid* (renewal): the caller invites people back to the original religion as practiced by the Prophet and Companions — not to a "new edition" of Islam. Renewal means "revival of ijtihad in it, and recourse to its original wellsprings, liberation from inflexibility and imitativeness." ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

2. **Dawah pursues the middle way.** The Prophetic model of dawah rejects both extremism and laxity. Qaradawi emphasizes that the Sunnah itself is a "moderate pattern" that the caller must embody, citing the Prophet's correction of those who inclined toward excess in worship. ^[raw/extracted/approaching-the-sunnah/ch-01.txt]

3. **Dawah includes defending Islam against misrepresentation.** Propagation of Islam (as addressed in *The Lawful and Prohibited*) includes exemption from certain normal prohibitions when necessary for conveying the message, indicating that dawah has a recognized urgency in Islamic law. ^[raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-03.txt]
