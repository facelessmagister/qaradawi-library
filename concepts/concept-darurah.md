# Darurah / Necessity (ضَرُورَة)

## Definition

**Darurah** (ضَرُورَة) — "necessity, constraint, compelling need" — is a foundational fiqh principle stating that prohibitions are temporarily lifted under dire necessity. The maxim *al-darurat tubih al-mahzurat* ("necessity permits the prohibited") governs situations where a Muslim faces genuine peril to life, faith, or essential well-being. Qaradawi invokes this principle in *The Lawful and the Prohibited*: "When He has explained to you that which He has forbidden you, except if you are constrained to it" (Q 6:119). Necessity does not abolish the haram ruling; it merely suspends it to the extent and duration required by the emergency.

## Key Positions

1. **Necessity validates the temporarily prohibited.** Qaradawi cites Allah's own words: "When He has explained to you that which He has forbidden you, except if you are constrained to it" (Q 6:119) as the Qur'anic foundation that darurah is an exception to the rule, not an abrogation of it. ^[raw/extracted/halal-haram/ch-04.txt]

2. **Necessity is proportionate and constrained.** The permission extends only to the minimum needed to remove the constraint. In *Fiqh al-Zakah*, Qaradawi applies this principle to zakat dispensations — for instance, the poor are exempt from zakat precisely because necessity overrides obligation in their case. ^[raw/extracted/fiqh-al-zakah/ch-03.txt]

3. **Darurah safeguards the principle of original permissibility.** Together with *al-ibahah al-asliyyah* (original permissibility), darurah ensures that Islamic law does not impose impossible burdens — reinforcing Qaradawi's broader theme that the Shariah is a system of ease, not hardship.
